# Mid-sequence carrier control, 1750–1754 — 26 August 2026

This note deliberately separates **carrier/collation security** from **frontispiece composition identification** for the three still-open middle years 1750, 1752 and 1754. The surrounding annual sequence is increasingly well controlled at image level: 1747, 1748, 1749 and 1751 carry Type C; 1755, 1756 and 1757 also carry Type C. The three years below therefore matter chiefly as missing composition checks inside an otherwise dense Type C run.

## 1750

A surviving luxury copy described by Venator & Hanstein establishes at copy level:

- the 1750 annual host;
- an engraved frontispiece;
- an engraved medal plate;
- a full-page engraved colour key;
- 108 full-page armorial copperplates with verso text;
- 74 numbered double leaves with 135 genealogical tables;
- copy-specific colouring and gold/silver heightening of the armorial plates;
- additional coloured coats of arms pasted onto otherwise blank pages;
- an armorial binding associated with Maria Josepha of Saxony, Dauphine of France.

A second present-market copy independently reports that its frontispiece has been pasted onto the front pastedown (`Frontispiz auf Vorsatzspiegel verklebt`). This is useful copy-history evidence but does not identify the composition.

**Status:** frontispiece presence is secure at physical-copy level; composition remains unclassified from currently indexed imagery.

## 1752

Institutional carrier is secured:

- Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, `Geneal. 46-1752`;
- URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10428150-0`;
- Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek exposes the record as a digitisation of the 1752 Weigel annual.

The reconstructed 1760 catalogue of the Reichsstift Gandersheim independently contains the historical entry:

`Historia Genealogia Heraldica de An. 1751 et 1752. S. 26, N. 6`

The modern reconstruction marks that old-catalogue item as not identified. It is nevertheless significant programme-level reception evidence and makes 1752 a particularly high-value image check.

A surviving 1752 armorial engraving (`Fürstl. Nassau-Saarbruckisches Idsteinisches und Gräffl. Weilburgisches Wappen`) is separately preserved/reproduced on Wikimedia Commons, confirming circulation of the annual's engraved heraldic apparatus but not the threshold frontispiece.

**Status:** host and programme-level historical reception are secure; frontispiece composition remains unclassified.

## 1754

Institutional and free digital carriers are secured:

- Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, `Geneal. 46-1754`;
- URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10428151-5`;
- Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek exposes the BSB digitisation;
- Google Play Books exposes a free 412-page carrier with ID `7JVAAAAAcAAJ`.

This is therefore not a bibliographic-discovery problem. The only useful next step is direct inspection of the opening image(s).

A frequent market-search false friend must be excluded: the Jasper52/Lot-Art lot headed `1754 COATS of ARMS...` is a **1755 calendar** (the photographed title page reads 1755; the imprint is [1754]). It is valid evidence for the 1755 Type C witness and must not be recycled as a 1754 composition control.

**Status:** carrier fully secured; frontispiece composition remains unclassified.
