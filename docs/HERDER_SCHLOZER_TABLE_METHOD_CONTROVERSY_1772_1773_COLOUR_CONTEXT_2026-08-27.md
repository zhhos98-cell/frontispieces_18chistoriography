# Herder–Schlözer 1772–1773: table, strict history, and the first controversy surface for the colour-method branch

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: active controlled controversy map

## 1. Why this branch must be separated from the Gatterer–Schlözer quarrel

The current colour-method research has produced two adjacent but distinct Göttingen controversy surfaces.

### Herder–Schlözer, 1772–1773

This controversy directly concerns the adequacy of Schlözer's Universalgeschichte as historical knowledge and, in the surviving polemical vocabulary, reaches questions of:

- `Fakta`;
- `strenge Wissenschaft`;
- historical correctness and determinacy;
- `Deklamation` versus learned groundwork;
- `Tabelle`;
- pictorial / chronological representation;
- pedagogical and mnemonic delegation.

Schlözer's 1773 defence of `gemahlte chronologische Tabellen` belongs here.

### Gatterer–Schlözer, 1773

The `Species Facti` quarrel concerns:

- teaching competition;
- priority and alleged plagiarism;
- ownership of a `Plan` or method of Universalgeschichte;
- institutional/professional claims.

Colour or table media have not yet been shown to be an explicit object of this second polemic.

Therefore:

`Herder–Schlözer representation dispute`
≠
`Gatterer–Schlözer priority dispute`.

The two can later be connected, but only after their distinct claims are reconstructed.

## 2. Herder's 1772 attack: strict history versus rhetorical Universalgeschichte

Herder's review appeared in the *Frankfurter gelehrte Anzeigen*, no. 60 (1772), pp. 473–478. Modern scholarship reproduces several diagnostically important passages.

Herder demands more `Fakta` and history in place of declamatory chapters. He asks how such rhetorical `Schaumblasen` could function as a `Grundriß zur strengen Wissenschaft, der Historie`.

The polemical contrast is not simply empirical Herder versus philosophical Schlözer. Herder temporarily occupies the position of the strict learned historical discipline and praises German historical textbooks for `Richtigkeit` and `Bestimmtheit`, even where they are dry or meagre. He attacks Schlözer for repeating precisely the `süße Fehler` he associates with Voltaire.

A modern historiographical reconstruction summarizes the punch line of the review with Herder's formula:

`Universalhistorie endlich! – und die ist eine Tabelle!`

The exact primary page behind this sentence should still be directly controlled before quotation in an article, but the secondary locus is stable enough to define the source task.

## 3. Why `Tabelle` matters here

The word `Tabelle` is not a neutral description in this controversy. It concentrates an argument about what happens when historical knowledge is compressed into an overview.

The problem can be reconstructed as:

`historical particulars / Fakta`
→ selection and compression
→ table / outline / system
→ risk of losing factual density, groundwork or historically determinate content.

Herder's complaint therefore provides a negative pressure test for graphical/synoptic method before colour is introduced at all.

This matters because the colour branch should not ask only whether pigment is cognitively useful. It should ask whether a chromatic operation amplifies an already contested act of abstraction/compression.

In other words:

> **Before asking what colour does inside a table, ask what the table has already done to history.**

## 4. Schlözer's 1773 reply: graphical delegation is explicitly defended

Schlözer's response is printed in *Vorstellung seiner Universal-Historie*, part II (Göttingen/Gotha: Dieterich, 1773), pp. [225]–416, under the title *Hrn. Johann Gottfried Herders ... Beurteilung ... mit August Ludwig Schlözers Anmerkungen über die Kunst, Universalhistorien zu beurteilen*.

Institutional carrier:
- ULB Sachsen-Anhalt, DOI `10.25673/95815`;
- VD18 `90090861`;
- shelfmark `AB 22 A 9/k, 26 (2)`.

A modern study quotes p. 327, where Schlözer refuses to dismiss `gemahlte chronologische Tabellen`, at least in children's instruction. Solomon's temple, the Egyptian pyramids and Homer may be painted together on one sheet. Through visual co-presence the objects become vivid, while the proposition of their simultaneity (`Synchronismus`) is fixed through `Association der Ideen`.

This is not a defence of colouring historical facts. It is a defence of **delegating a relation among facts to a graphical/pictorial arrangement**.

Operational sequence:

`accepted historical particulars`
→ `pictorial co-presence`
→ `synchronistic relation made perceptually available`
→ `association / memory`.

The exact p. 327 wording remains strong secondary quotation control until the institutional scan page is directly inspected.

## 5. The 1775 illumination note makes the response chromatic

A separate modern quotation attributes to Schlözer's 1775 revised *Vorstellung der Universal-Historie* the proposal that five world-historical periods be placed into five temporal compartments and their unequal `Länge und Dauer` made visible `durch Illumination der Tabelle`.

This is the chromatic continuation of the table problem, but it is bibliographically distinct from p. 327.

The operation is:

`historical periodisation`
→ `tabular scale`
→ `illumination`
→ `duration becomes visible`.

Thus colour enters **after** the controversial abstraction into table form and further increases the visibility of temporal structure.

This suggests a nested method problem:

1. may historical particulars legitimately be compressed into a table/system?
2. which relations may the table encode?
3. may pigment or illumination amplify those encoded relations?
4. does the visual gain preserve or distort factual warrant?

## 6. Relation to Schlözer's 1772 `Facta färben`

The 1772 first part gives the colour branch its most precise negative control.

Schlözer assigns `Kritik` the recovery/testing of facts from annals and monuments; he mocks `Voltaires` for making facts or at least colouring them; `Zusammenstellung`, by contrast, belongs to the historian.

The Herder controversy therefore exposes a productive tension inside Schlözer's own method:

- facts must not be coloured;
- facts must nevertheless be selected and combined;
- graphical co-presence can legitimately make their synchronism memorable;
- tabular illumination can, at least in the 1775 proposal, make temporal proportion visible.

The issue is **jurisdiction**, not iconoclasm.

## 7. Two controversies, one possible method field

The current Göttingen map should be modeled as two intersecting axes rather than one colour quarrel.

### Axis A — representation / historical adequacy

Herder ↔ Schlözer

Questions:
- fact versus declamation;
- strict history versus abstraction;
- narrative / chapter / table;
- graphical or mnemonic delegation;
- whether overview costs historical particularity.

### Axis B — ownership / professional method

Gatterer ↔ Schlözer

Questions:
- who owns the `Plan` of Universalgeschichte;
- priority / borrowing;
- teaching competition;
- institutional value of a method;
- possibly different material implementations of overview.

Colour can later connect the axes if primary sources show that the same graphical operations, tables or ordering techniques were treated as part of the contested method.

Until then, keep the link as a research programme rather than a conclusion.

## 8. The strongest colour-method formulation after this controversy map

The colour question is now embedded inside a more general history of historical compression and delegation.

A useful hierarchy is:

`Fakta`
→ `criticism`
→ `selection`
→ `Zusammenstellung / Plan`
→ `table / graphical disposition`
→ `colour / illumination`
→ `inspection / memory`.

Each transition has its own possible failure:

- invented or coloured facts;
- weak criticism;
- arbitrary selection;
- excessive abstraction;
- table-induced flattening;
- chromatic overstatement;
- mnemonic clarity mistaken for evidentiary certainty.

The project-specific question therefore becomes:

> **How were graphical and chromatic operations authorized after historical facts had been secured, and when did the gain in overview become vulnerable to the charge that history had been reduced to a table, picture or rhetorical construction?**

## 10. Current judgment

The strongest present claim is **not** that there was one eighteenth-century Göttingen `colour controversy`.

It is:

> **A cluster of Göttingen historiographical disputes made the jurisdiction of historical operations contestable: what criticism may do to facts, what arrangement may do to particulars, what tables may do to history, and what graphical or chromatic media may do to already organized relations. Colour is the sharpest test case because it can cross every one of these boundaries.**
