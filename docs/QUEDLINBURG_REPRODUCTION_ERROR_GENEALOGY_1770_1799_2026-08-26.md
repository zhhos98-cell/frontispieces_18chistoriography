# Quedlinburg gravestone: reproduction-error genealogy, 1770–1799 — 26 August 2026

## Status

**Strong source-chain reconstruction; 1770 and 1799 full-view carriers identified. Exact image-level comparison remains a bounded manual inspection task.**

The Quedlinburg case should no longer be treated merely as an anecdote showing that Johann Christoph Gatterer used visual evidence. It supplies a longitudinal control for how an evidentiary argument could be transformed, damaged, copied and deliberately repaired across media.

The current chain is:

`1770 artifact/drawings → engraved + typographic publication → typographic deformation`

`→ 1783 didactic re-engraving → new graphic omission`

`→ 1787 antiquarian re-documentation → newly visible details`

`→ 1799 Gatterer republication → explicit correction of the earlier typographic failure + renewed engraving`

The analytical object is therefore not just the gravestone image. It is the **reproduction-error genealogy** of an evidentiary operation.

## I. 1770: the artifact enters a diplomatic expert opinion

Gatterer's article:

`J. C. Gatterers diplomatisches Responsum den Streit über König Heinrichs des Finklers Grabmal, welches man vor kurzem in Quedlinburg gefunden haben will, betreffend, nebst denen dazu gehörigen Actenstücken und Zeichnungen`

appeared in *Allgemeine historische Bibliothek* 15 (Halle: Johann Justinus Gebauer, 1770), pp. 3–30.

Two full-view Google carriers are currently open:

- `https://play.google.com/store/books/details?id=-Qb0dslpe8UC`
- `https://play.google.com/store/books/details?id=wjRPSOtAacQC`

BSB carrier: shelfmark `H.misc. 115-13/15`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10931826-6`.

Araújo's image-level collation places the relevant 1770 engraved plate **between pp. 4 and 5** in the BSB copy.

### Historical operation

Gatterer transfers diplomatic procedure to a monumental inscription. The reconstructed diagnostic sequence is:

1. **graphics / script morphology** — the letter forms are classed as neo-Gothic and associated with his `Genus Thulemarium`, excluding a tenth-century royal tomb;
2. **semiotics / heraldry** — comparison with engravings in Anton Ulrich von Erath's *Codex diplomaticus Quedlinburgensis* identifies the arms with the Hoym family;
3. **iconography** — the represented figure is interpreted as a knight rather than a king;
4. conclusion — the stone belongs to Friedrich von Hoym rather than Henry the Fowler.

The case is important because the evidentiary claim depends not only on verbal testimony about what the stone says, but on **formal distinctions that must remain visually discriminable**.

## II. The 1770 printing failure: visual evidence becomes unverifiable

The first publication did not faithfully preserve all the graphic distinctions on which Gatterer's dating depended.

Araújo's comparison shows that the Halle printer lacked adequate metal types for some of the neo-Gothic forms. In particular, the same type could be used where Gatterer's argument required distinct forms of `E`; other unusual letters were substituted with unrelated signs.

Gatterer later described the failure explicitly in *Praktische Diplomatik* (1799), p. 152. He states that the 1770 article contained major printing errors and that the neo-Gothic letters decisive for dating had been badly deformed. His examples are unusually concrete:

- neo-Gothic `C` and `D` were represented using calendar signs for phases/quarters of the moon;
- neo-Gothic `N` was represented by the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet.

This is not a generic complaint about bad printing. The substitution changes the **diagnostic feature** itself.

Operationally:

`artifact morphology`
→ `expert classification`
→ `required visual distinction`
→ `printer lacks fitting sorts`
→ `substitute glyph`
→ `reader can no longer reproduce the expert inference from the printed page`.

This is a particularly strong control for the repository's distinction between an evidentiary operation and its carrier. The inference can be methodologically valid while its printed instantiation becomes non-replicable.

### Production control

Araújo also cites Gatterer's letter to Gebauer of 6 October 1770, Stadtarchiv Halle, Verlagsarchiv Gebauer & Schwetschke, `A 6.2.6 Nr. 10753 (Kartonnr. 42)`. Gatterer warned that material being sent for volume 15 came from a scholar with difficult handwriting and required an attentive, practiced corrector if printing errors were to be avoided.

The surviving error therefore belongs to a documented production problem, not merely a modern conjecture about the printed result.

## III. 1783: Gruber converts the case into didactic apparatus and introduces a new error

Gregor Maximilian Gruber's Viennese *Lehrsystem einer allgemeinen Diplomatik*, a three-volume handbook published in 1783–84, reused Gatterer's Quedlinburg case in its practical component.

Gruber explains at pp. 293–94 that he selected the example because one could rely on the thoroughly systematic Gatterer and because Gatterer had resolved the dispute methodically according to the diplomatic handbook on which Gruber himself relied.

Araújo's inspected witness:

- Universitätsbibliothek der Freien Universität Berlin, shelfmark `17 E 86-2`.

The 1783 plate places excerpts of medieval diplomata around a newly engraved reproduction of the Quedlinburg gravestone.

But the new engraver **omitted one letter in the centre of the upper inscription**.

This produces a second, distinct mode of corruption:

- 1770 = **typographic substitution** inside the explanatory argument;
- 1783 = **engraved omission** inside a didactic visual surrogate.

The case therefore prevents a simple opposition between unreliable movable type and reliable engraving. Both media can alter the evidence, but in different ways.

## IV. 1787: Voigt re-documents the gravestone

Gottfried Christian Voigt's *Geschichte des Stiftes Quedlinburg* (1787) supplied another engraving of the gravestone and revealed details not represented in the earlier visual chain.

Araújo analyses four metal engravings of the object dated **1770, 1783, 1787 and 1799**. His argument is important for this corpus because the later 1787 image did not overturn Gatterer's 1770 identification, yet it exposed how every surrogate selected and omitted formal information.

Relevant inspected Göttingen witness for Voigt's plate:

- SUB Göttingen, `8 H SAX PR 6575:2`, plate between pp. 96 and 97.

A useful future comparison is therefore not only `same object / different accuracy`, but:

`which visible traits did each reproduction choose to preserve?`

## V. 1799: Gatterer republishes, repairs and reframes the case

### Gatterer's explicit correction statement

The 1799 statement is currently controlled through Araújo's exact transcription and should be checked directly in the full-view carrier before promotion to `verified_primary` quotation status.

Its evidentiary content is already secure:

1. Gatterer identifies the prior venue and pages;
2. calls attention to major printing errors in the Halle edition;
3. singles out the deformation of neo-Gothic letters as particularly serious because **dating depended mainly on them**;
4. names the substituted moon-calendar and Hebrew signs;
5. gives those errors as a reason a repeated, improved printing was necessary.

This closes the methodological loop:

`visual morphology used as historical test`
→ `reproduction damages morphology`
→ `damage recognized as epistemically consequential`
→ `later publication corrects the carrier so the test can again be followed`.

## VI. Strong article-level consequence

The Quedlinburg chain supports a stronger formulation than “Gatterer used images as evidence.”

> **For Gatterer, some historical claims depended on reproducible graphic distinctions. A surrogate failed as evidence when the distinctions required for expert judgment could not survive the production chain.**

This means the historical method extended beyond source criticism narrowly defined. It also required control over:

- drawing;
- engraving;
- type supply;
- compositor substitution;
- proofreading/correction;
- page layout;
- repeated reproduction;
- the trained reader's ability to compare formal traits.

The relevant unit is therefore not simply `source → interpretation`, but:

`historical artifact → graphic diagnostic → surrogate production → material carrier → readerly verification`.

This aligns unusually well with the repository's provenance-first distinction between artifact, representation, witness, copy and afterlife.

---

## VII. Guard against overclaiming

1. Do not treat the 1770 engraving as the unmediated gravestone. Gatterer's access was already mediated by drawings and reports.
2. Do not collapse typographic and engraved failures into one generic `copy error`; the mechanism of alteration differs.
3. Do not say the 1787 new details refuted Gatterer. The current secondary reconstruction says they did not change the identification.
4. Do not infer that the 1799 engraving reused the original 1770 copperplate. Araújo describes the later image as a replica / newly produced reproduction and notes differences requiring image-level control.
5. Keep the exact 1799 p.152 quotation at `verified_secondary_transcription` until the primary page is manually inspected.
