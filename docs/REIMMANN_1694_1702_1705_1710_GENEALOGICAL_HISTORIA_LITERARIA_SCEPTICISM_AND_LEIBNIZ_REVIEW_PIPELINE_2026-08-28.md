# Reimmann 1694–1710: genealogy as historical-literary object, genealogical scepticism, and Leibniz's review pipeline

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE PREHISTORY — 1694 FIRST CONTROLLED GENEALOGY-HISTORY PRINT NOW RECOVERED; 1702 LATER TITLE-FORM/EDITION CONTROLLED BUT EXACT EDITION RELATION REQUIRES COLLATION; LEIBNIZ 1701 DIRECT CORRESPONDENCE CONTROLLED; 1705 SCEPTICISM PUBLICATION LOCUS CONTROLLED; 1710 COLLECTED BIBLIOGRAPHY CONTROLLED

## Correction / result

A previous repository checkpoint incorrectly treated Reimmann's 1702 *Historia literaria de fatis studii genealogici* as the beginning of his historical-bibliographic treatment of genealogy.

That chronology is now corrected.

Jacob Friedrich Reimmann had already published at Halberstadt in **1694**:

`De Fatis studii genealogici, apud Hebraeos, Graecos, Romanos, Germanos, exercitatio parergica ...`

The Russian State Library / National Electronic Library catalogue controls a surviving 1694 copy, printed at Halberstadt by the Hynitzsch press, with an extent recorded as 72 leaves/pages in quarto. The *Deutsche Biographie* independently identifies Reimmann's genealogy history as an early work of **1694** and records later editions through 1710.

The chronology must therefore be rewritten as:

`1694 genealogy already historicized as a learned field`
→ `1701/1702 a later Reimmann genealogy-history version enters Leibniz/Eckhart's recension workflow`
→ `1705 explicit scepticism in genealogical study`
→ `1710 bibliography of common/rare, printed/manuscript, published/unpublished genealogical books + collected scepticism`.

This correction is consequential for the Leibniz comparison.

The controlled Leibniz short library classification is dated `1695 bis Frühjahr 1698 (?)`. Reimmann 1694 therefore **precedes** that short scheme. Chronology no longer rules out Reimmann → Leibniz influence.

But direct influence remains entirely **unproved**.

A related Leibniz `fusior` library scheme is dated `Um 1693 (?)`, which appears to precede Reimmann 1694. The exact place of Genealogica/Heraldica in that earlier Leibniz scheme remains unresolved.

The high-value research problem is now:

> Did two independent classificatory projects develop in parallel, or did Reimmann's 1694 genealogy history enter Leibniz's information environment before the 1695–98 short library schema was formulated?

Working status:

`CHRONOLOGICAL INFLUENCE WINDOW OPEN — DIRECT RECEPTION BEFORE 1701 NOT YET FOUND`.

`De Fatis studii genealogici, apud Hebraeos, Graecos, Romanos, Germanos, exercitatio parergica, qua musarum statores, patronos, fautores, amicos, quin omnes, qui rei literariae ponunt pretium, ad actum oratorium ... invitat Jacob Friderich Reimmann`.

Publication control:

- **1694**;
- quarto;
- Russian State Library record `01003097296` / National Electronic Library object `000199_000009_003097296`;

The *Deutsche Biographie* independently describes this as Reimmann's early comprehensive history of genealogy and gives the work as:

`Exercitatio parergica de fatis studii Genealogici ... 1694`, with later editions through 1710.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-OBJECT CATALOGUE CONTROLLED + AUTHORITATIVE MODERN BIO-BIBLIOGRAPHY CONTROLLED`.

## 2. 1694 is earlier than the controlled Leibniz short classification

The Akademieausgabe dates Leibniz's:

`Idea Leibnitiana bibliothecae ordinandae contractior`

to:

`1695 bis Frühjahr 1698 (?)`.

The later primary print of that scheme places:

`Historia civilis`
→ `Universalis`
→ `Geographia`
→ `Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica`.

The corrected chronology is therefore:

`Reimmann genealogy-history print = 1694`

before

`Leibniz short schema = 1695–spring 1698 (?)`.

The older checkpoint had incorrectly used 1702 to argue that Reimmann could not be a source for the Leibniz short schema. That negative argument is withdrawn.

New rule:

- `Reimmann → Leibniz` is **chronologically possible**;
- it is **not evidenced**;
- no direct pre-1698 Leibniz citation, acquisition, review, letter or library record for the 1694 print has yet been controlled.

Do not promote beyond:

`POSSIBLE CHRONOLOGICAL PREDECESSOR / DIRECT RELATION UNPROVED`.

## 3. The c.1693 Leibniz `fusior` remains a decisive boundary control

The Akademieausgabe dates the longer:

`Idea Leibnitiana bibliothecae publicae secundum classes scientiarum ordinandae, fusior`

to:

`Um 1693 (?)`.

That date apparently precedes Reimmann 1694.

A later bibliographic summary of the longer Leibniz arrangement lists under `Historia civilis` universal history, geography, ancient history with antiquities, medieval history, modern history, histories of peoples and histories of particular matters, without mentioning genealogy/heraldry in the summary.

This **cannot** prove absence from the manuscript.

The discriminating test is now unusually sharp:

1. inspect the c.1693 `fusior` directly;
2. determine whether `Genealogica & Heraldica` are present;
3. compare with the 1695–98 short schema;
4. if they appear only in the later schema, ask whether the addition belongs to Leibniz's internal revision, wider learned circulation, or a specific bibliographic stimulus such as Reimmann 1694.

Status:

`HIGH-PRIORITY INTERNAL-LEIBNIZ REVISION TEST`.

## 4. 1702: later Reimmann title-form / edition, not first genealogy history

The 1702 object remains real and important:

`Jacobi Friderici Reimanni Historia Literaria de Fatis Studii Genealogicii apud Hebraeos, Graecos, Romanos & Germanos, in qua Scriptores harum gentium potissimi enumerantur & Totus Genealogiae Cursus ab orbe condito ad nostra usque tempora deducitur`.

Controls:

- Aschersleben / Quedlinburg;
- Gottlob Ernst Strunz;
- 1702;
- BSB `bsb10208675`;
- VD18 `1462589X-001`;
- Google Books/Play ID `7rRNAAAAcAAJ`;
- 120 printed pages in the Google carrier metadata.

Its title makes the historical-literary operation more explicit:

- principal Hebrew, Greek, Roman and German writers are enumerated;
- genealogy's course is carried from the creation of the world to the present.

However, the exact bibliographic relation between the 1694 `Exercitatio parergica` and 1702 `Historia Literaria` requires direct collation.

Modern bio-bibliographic sources disagree about the numbering of later editions: *Deutsche Biographie* records a fourth edition in 1710, while a modern family-history bibliography calls 1710 a third enlarged edition. The repository therefore must **not** assign an edition number to 1702 until copies/title pages/prefaces are compared.

Current label:

`1702 = LATER RECAST / EXPANDED TITLE-FORM; EDITION NUMBER UNRESOLVED`.

## 5. 1 November 1701: the 1702 form enters Leibniz and Eckhart's review workflow

The critical edition controls:

`Leibniz to Johann Georg Eckhart, Berlin, 1 Nov. 1701`, no. 42.

In a passage about recension work, Leibniz lists several works whose treatment may wait until he is present, including:

`Reimanni de fatis Genealog. studii`.

The editors identify this item as Reimmann's 1702 *Historia literaria de fatis studii genealogici*.

This now means something more precise than the previous checkpoint allowed.

It does **not** mark Leibniz's first possible encounter with Reimmann's genealogy-history project, because that project had existed in print since 1694.

It does prove that the **1702 title-form/version** entered Leibniz/Eckhart's planned recension workflow by 1 Nov. 1701.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-DIRECT CORRESPONDENCE TEXT IN CRITICAL EDITION`.

Safe relation:

`LEIBNIZ / ECKHART ↔ REIMMANN 1702 VERSION = DIRECT_RECEPTION / PLANNED_RECENSION_PIPELINE`.

No completed review has yet been found.

## 6. The likely review organ is the *Monathlicher Auszug*, but publication remains unresolved

The IdRZ/GJZ18 periodical profile controls the:

`Monathlicher Auszug aus allerhand neu-herausgegebenen nützlichen und artigen Büchern` (Hannover, 1700–1702)

as:

- edited by Johann Georg Eckhart through Sept. 1702;
- with Leibniz as a contributor;
- a periodical in which Eckhart wrote many reviews from Leibniz's drafts.

The critical-edition note to the 1 Nov. 1701 letter further states that another work named in the same review-workflow sentence, Schilter's *Renuntiationes*, was reviewed in the *Monathlicher Auszug* in December 1701, pp.71–75.

This makes the *Monathlicher Auszug* the strongest current candidate for the review mechanism implied by `recenseri`.

But current GJZ18/title searches have not located a published review of Reimmann's 1702 version.

Therefore retain:

`REVIEW ORGAN = STRONGLY INDICATED`

and

`PUBLISHED REIMMANN REVIEW = NOT YET FOUND`.

The next decisive control is the complete 1702 volume, monthly tables of contents and/or full text.

## 8. 1710: bibliographic corpus construction and collected scepticism

The later genealogical bibliography is catalogued as:

`Historiae literariae exotericae & acroamaticae particula sive De libris genealogicis vulgatioribus & rarioribus commentatio`.

Its title says that it reviews genealogical works that are:

- common / rare;
- printed / manuscript;
- published / unpublished;

and supplies material toward constructing a:

`bibliotheca scriptorum genealogicorum`.

It also includes:

`Disquisitio historica de necessitate scepticismi in studio genealogico`.

BnF-derived cataloguing dates this assembled form `[1710]` from the preface.

A Braidense record exposes confusing inferred dates for component parts, so component-issue history remains unresolved until physical/digital collation.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-TITLE / CATALOGUE CONTROLLED`.

## 10. Reimmann and Zschackwitz 1724

Zschackwitz 1724 has been primary-controlled as:

- making genealogy dependent on history / Reichshistorie;
- demanding authors and sources (`Autores und fontes`);
- valuing documents, older tables, citations and source-grounded testimony;
- judging Gladov/Hahn through coherence, error and source apparatus.

The structural similarity to Reimmann's genealogy-history / scepticism / bibliography is strong.

But full-text search of the HAB Zschackwitz transcription has not found `Reim-` or `Scept-`.

Current relation remains:

`STRONG STRUCTURAL PREHISTORY — DIRECT REIMMANN → ZSCHACKWITZ RECEPTION UNPROVED`.

## 11. Hübner 1729 remains a high-value explicit-uptake candidate

Johann Hübner the younger's *Bibliotheca genealogica* (Hamburg, 1729) systematizes old and new genealogical books for users interested in political knowledge.

Modern bibliographic scholarship explicitly treats Reimmann's genealogy works as predecessors of Hübner's bibliography, and one modern account states that Hübner followed Reimmann's path.

A later *Catalogus bibliothecae Bunavianae* practically classifies:

- Reimmann 1702 under `Historia Studii Genealogici`;
- Reimmann's genealogical-books bibliography and Hübner 1729 together under `Bibliothecae Scriptorum Genealogicorum`.

That is later classification evidence, not proof of Hübner's direct borrowing.

Primary Hübner search remains necessary.

- bibliographically organized;

## 15. Current strongest article-level proposition

> **Genealogy's critical reorganization predates the eighteenth century proper. Reimmann had already made the fortunes of genealogical study into a printed historical problem in 1694; a later form of that project entered Leibniz and Eckhart's recension workflow in 1701; explicit genealogical scepticism followed in 1705; and by 1710 Reimmann organized common and rare, printed and manuscript, published and unpublished genealogical books as a searchable corpus. Because the 1694 work now precedes Leibniz's 1695–98 short library schema, the relation between early genealogical historia literaria and Leibnizian classification becomes an open reception question rather than a chronologically impossible one.**

No direct Reimmann → Leibniz → 1726 plate transmission is currently demonstrated.
