# Deep web sweep — 21 August 2026

This note records the second pass of the 21 August sweep. It concentrates on three questions left open by the first pass: the Gabriel Müller attribution, the pre-Gatterer handbook problem, and a parallel Köhler numismatic corpus.

## 1. Gabriel Müller attribution: externally corroborated

The strongest new source is British Museum object **1910,0906.2**, an illustrated copy of the 1740 *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender*.

British Museum object record:
https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/P_1910-0906-2

The institutional catalogue describes the frontispiece as:

- print made by **Martin Tyroff**;
- **after Gabriel Müller**;
- published by **Heirs of Christoph Weigel (?)**;
- carrying the title `Historia Genealogia Heraldica / testes temporum et veritatis`;
- showing an angel, Time, and two putti looking at genealogical trees and books of heraldry;
- approximate page/sheet size 198 × 115 mm.

This independently corroborates the Müller–Tyroff attribution in the project draft. Evidence level remains `verified_catalogue`, not `verified_primary`, because the present pass has not yet inspected a high-resolution signature on the plate itself.

British Museum authority record for the Weigel heirs also describes the business succession through the widow, Barbara Sibylla, Martin Tyroff, and later Tyroff heirs:
https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/term/BIOG148663

## 2. Müller–Tyroff–Köhler is a recurring production constellation

The frontispiece attribution is not isolated.

### 1733: Altdorf dissertation

DDB/BSB records *Volvntarivm Imperii Consortivm Inter Fridericvm Avstriacvm Et Lvdovicvm Bavarvm Avgg.* (Altdorf, 1733; VD18 14392267) with Johann David Köhler as praeses. Its illustrations include copperplate vignettes, two explicitly catalogued as **after Gabriel Müller by Martin Tyroff**.

DDB example:
https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/YLWTABFR6HLD4ZYUXQ7SLTHJCMFPBMM2

This matters as network evidence: Köhler, Müller and Tyroff are documented together in historical-political visual production only a few years after the target Clio design enters the calendar run. It does **not** imply that the dissertation uses the Clio plate.

### 1756: portrait of Köhler

Rijksmuseum object **RP-P-2021-2604** is a 1756 portrait of Johann David Köhler, made by Martin Tyroff after a painting by Gabriel Müller:
https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Portret%2Bvan%2BJohann%2BDavid%2BK%C3%B6hler--879d4081c65fbfb607bcdbff9a0fe105

The Müller–Tyroff association therefore persists across at least the 1730s–1750s and directly intersects Köhler both as scholar/editor and as sitter.

## 3. A pre-Gatterer handbook branch has surfaced

A previously unmodelled object is:

> *Bequemes und zum Zeitungs-Lesen sehr dienliches genealogisches und heraldisches Hand-Buch. Worinnen aller jetzigen Europäischen Potentaten Stamm-Tafeln und Wappen enthalten.*

The volume is undated and copy descriptions conflict sharply.

### Dating evidence

**Biblioteca Nacional de España** gives the work the range **[between 1725 and 1744]**. The BNE linked-data entry is currently exposed through the heraldry authority page and work link:
https://datos.bne.es/persona/XX5295461.html

**Das Konversations-Lexikon / ZVAB**, seller inventory SB-17124, dates a copy **c.1725**. The argument is internal: the volume includes Pope Benedict XIII (1724–1730). This copy is described with 106 armorial copperplates and 107 genealogical tables, followed by an alphabetical description, a register, and a list of copper-illustrated books published by Christoph Weigel the Elder:
https://www.zvab.com/Bequemes-Zeitungs-Lesen-sehr-dienliches-genealogisches-heraldisches/21506746882/bd

**Honterus Antikvárium**, auction 85, lot 295, attributes the book to **Johann David Köhler**, dates its copy **c.1740**, and records **one copper title/frontispiece**, 81 full-page engraved arms on 116 leaves, with genealogical tables on versos:
https://www.honterus.hu/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/85.-aukcios-katalogus.pdf

Recent Darabanth/Axioart descriptions date incomplete copies c.1750 and show substantially different surviving plate/leaf counts.

### Corpus decision

Do **not** assign this work a single date or stable collation yet. Enter it as a `witness_cluster` with copy-level descriptions. The divergences may reflect:

- multiple issues/states;
- recomposition from annual Wappenkalender sheets;
- incomplete or later rebound copies;
- later cataloguing assumptions.

The copper title/frontispiece reported by Honterus is a priority inspection target. Its identity as `Historia Genealogia Heraldica` is currently **unresolved**.

### Analytical significance

Even before the frontispiece is identified, the title itself is important. It presents genealogical/heraldic apparatus as `zum Zeitungs-Lesen sehr dienlich`: a reference technology for following current political news. This demonstrates a non-annual reference-book logic inside the Weigel-widow/Bieling orbit decades before Gatterer's 1759 *Handbuch*.

The old binary sequence `calendar → Gatterer handbook` is therefore inadequate. We may instead be dealing with repeated **de-serialisation and re-serialisation** of the same armorial/genealogical materials between annual, reference, and cumulative formats.

## 4. Köhler's *Historische Münz-Belustigung* becomes a parallel corpus

Köhler's *Wöchentlich herausgegebene Historische Münz-Belustigung* begins in Nürnberg in 1729 under the Weigel publishing network and runs through 22 annual parts to 1750 in serial catalogues.

A numismatic auction bibliography describes the first part (1729) with a **frontispiece**, preface, 440 pages, illustrations and register. A nearly complete run is described as Nürnberg 1729–1765 with later register material; register dates vary between catalogues and should not be normalised without direct collation.

Solidus catalogue:
https://www.solidus-numismatik.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Download/A_99_Literatur.pdf

The series is methodologically attractive because it repeatedly converts coins and medals into engraved, textualised and chronologically situated historical witnesses. It shares Köhler and the Weigel network with the calendar, but the comparison should remain procedural rather than assume a common doctrine.

Questions for the parallel census:

1. How is the coin/medal positioned relative to historical narrative?
2. How are rarity, authenticity, dating and provenance verbalised?
3. What recurrent page-opening or caption structures make comparison possible?
4. How do weekly issues accumulate into annual volumes and later indexes?
5. Does the same vocabulary of `Zeugen`, `Wahrheit`, `Prüfung`, chronology or comparison recur?

## 5. New model of the object ecology

The corpus now points to at least five interacting forms rather than one serial and its later handbook:

1. **annual historical-memory frontispiece** — 1724 negative/earlier regime;
2. **annual Wappenkalender with reusable Clio threshold** — positive by 1726;
3. **non-annual genealogical/heraldic handbook for news reading** — undated, current catalogue bracket 1725–1744;
4. **weekly numismatic serial** — Köhler, 1729–1750;
5. **later handbook / continued calendar / cumulative Wappenbuch** — Gatterer and Seyfart phases, 1759 onward.

The promising historical problem is therefore less a linear evolution of genres than the repeated movement of visual-tabular material between publication rhythms: annual, weekly, handbook/reference, and cumulative armorial.
