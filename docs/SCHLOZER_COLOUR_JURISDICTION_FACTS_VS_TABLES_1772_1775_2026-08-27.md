# Schlözer: colour jurisdiction between facts and tables, 1772–1775

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: active primary-control note

## 1. Finding

The colour-method branch now has an actor-level distinction internal to Schlözer himself. The relevant opposition is not `Schlözer = anti-colour` versus `Gatterer = pro-colour`.

The stronger distinction is:

`colouring the Facta`
≠
`colouring / illuminating the arrangement through which relations among Facta are displayed`.

In 1772 Schlözer places the first operation on the illegitimate side of historical method. In the 1775 revised edition he explicitly entertains the second as a legitimate didactic and mnemonic operation.

This supplies direct historical support for the repository's working category of **epistemic jurisdiction of colour**: the legitimacy of chromatic intervention depends on where in the historical knowledge chain it occurs and what work it performs.

## 2. Schlözer 1772: `Facta färben` belongs on the wrong side of criticism

In *Vorstellung seiner Universal-Historie* (1772), pp. 44–45, Schlözer compares historical facts to the small coloured stones of a mosaic. The historian's problem is to distribute and order them so that a whole `Gemälde` appears.

The sequence is explicit enough to reconstruct methodologically:

`Annalen / Denkmäler`
→ `Kritik`
→ `Facta`
→ `Zusammenstellung`
→ `System / Gemälde`.

`Kritik` recovers or tests the factual units. `Zusammenstellung` is the historian's operation. Schlözer's aside against the `Voltaires` places making or at least colouring the facts (`färben sie wenigstens`) at the wrong point in that chain.

The point is not that historical representation must remain visually colourless. The target is an operation performed on the evidentiary content before or instead of warranted historical arrangement.

Thus:

`colour_at_fact_layer`
→ suspect / unauthorized alteration or rhetorical loading.

The same passage makes arrangement itself legitimate and methodologically constitutive. The particular mode of `Zusammenstellung` is what gives Universalgeschichte its method.

## 3. Schlözer 1775: colour is licensed at the table/display layer

The second, revised edition of *Vorstellung der Universal-Historie* appeared at Göttingen with Dieterich in 1775. ULB Sachsen-Anhalt provides an open institutional copy, DOI `10.25673/95805`, shelfmark `AB 22 A 9/k, 26 (1)`, VD18 `11056576`.

A detailed modern study of early-modern historical images quotes two passages from Schlözer's 1775 material that materially change the interpretation of his colour vocabulary.

### A. Painted synchronism

Schlözer discusses `gemahlte chronologische Tabellen` in connection with the teaching of children. He proposes placing objects such as Solomon's temple, the Egyptian pyramids and Homer together on one painted sheet. The image should imprint the objects more vividly than discourse alone, while the fact of their simultaneity — `Synchronismus` — becomes durable through `Association der Ideen`.

The operative chain is:

`historical units already accepted`
→ `co-presence on painted sheet`
→ `association`
→ `memorised synchronism`.

Colour/image is therefore not manufacturing the factual units. It is delegated work at the **relation/display/memory layer**.

### B. `Illumination der Tabelle`

The same scholarship quotes a short note from Schlözer's 1775 revised edition in which the five principal periods of world history are placed in a table divided into corresponding compartments. He proposes that their unequal lengths and durations can be made visible to the young eye through the `Illumination der Tabelle`.

This is exceptionally important for the current project because the referent of the colour is not an object's literal hue. Colour is assigned to a **temporal relation / duration structure** in order to make quantitative-historical difference visible.

Operationally:

`periodisation + duration`
→ `tabular spatialisation`
→ `illumination`
→ `duration made visually inspectable`.

This belongs to:

- `colour_role = relational_syntax`;
- `colour_operation = mark_relation; discriminate; support_overview; memorise`;
- epistemic layer = `arrangement / display / pedagogy`.

## 4. Same actor, two colour jurisdictions

Taken together, 1772 and 1775 make the key distinction actor-level rather than merely analytical.

### Illicit jurisdiction

`source / monument`
→ `Kritik`
→ **Factum**
→ historian colours Factum
→ unsupported alteration / rhetorical loading.

### Legitimate jurisdiction

`critically constrained historical units`
→ `selection / periodisation / synchronistic relation`
→ `table / painted sheet`
→ illumination or pictorial co-presence
→ rapid inspection / association / memory.

The governing question becomes:

> **Not whether history may be coloured, but what colour is authorized to do to historical knowledge.**

A compact formulation remains:

> **Where may colour enter without colouring the fact?**

The 1775 evidence now supplies the positive side of that boundary from Schlözer himself.

## 5. Consequence for the Gatterer comparison

The previous binary must be rejected:

`Schlözer = conceptual/textual arrangement`
versus
`Gatterer = material/coloured arrangement`.

Schlözer himself authorizes material graphical delegation when it makes durations or synchronistic relations visible and memorable. The comparison with Gatterer must therefore become more exact.

### Shared problem

Both seek to turn a mass of historical units into a surveyable relation/system. Both can license graphical operations at the arrangement/display layer.

### Possible difference to test

Gatterer's *Synopsis* does not merely recommend illumination pedagogically; surviving copies materially instantiate colour as part of a synchronistic historical instrument. His broader auxiliary-science practice also subjects graphic codes and surrogates to evidentiary tests.

The stronger comparison is therefore not pro-colour versus anti-colour, but:

- what relation is delegated to colour;
- whether colour is necessary, redundant or auxiliary;
- how the colour assignment remains accountable to historical evidence;
- whether colour is standardized across copies/users;
- how the medium participates in the construction rather than merely recollection of the historical whole.

## 6. Relation to the 1773 controversy

This finding does **not** establish that the Gatterer–Schlözer polemic of 1773 was explicitly a dispute about pigment or colour.

It does establish something more useful: Schlözer's own methodological programme has an explicit boundary between impermissible colouring of facts and permissible graphical/illuminated treatment of historical relations. Colour can therefore be used as an actor-grounded discriminator inside the larger controversy over `Plan`, `Zusammenstellung`, `Methode`, `Übersicht` and Universalgeschichte.

The next polemical-text test should search for delegation vocabulary rather than only literal colour words:

`Tafel`, `Gemälde`, `Karte`, `Strich`, `Auge`, `sichtbar`, `Übersicht`, `Zusammenstellung`, `Plan`, `Methode`, `Synchronismus`, `Illumination`, `Farbe/färben`.

## 7. Ontology consequence

The current colour ontology already separates the relevant layers. This finding strengthens the need to encode an explicit `epistemic_location` or equivalent relation for every colour operation:

- `source_object`;
- `surrogate_encoding`;
- `fact_proposition`;
- `classification`;
- `arrangement_system`;
- `display_inspection`;
- `memory_pedagogy`;
- `copy_finishing`.

A colour operation cannot be evaluated from its visible appearance alone. The same material act of adding pigment can be methodologically different according to what proposition or relation it modifies.
