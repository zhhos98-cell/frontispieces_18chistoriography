# Duchesne 1621: genealogy and proof corpus as separated navigational zones

## Result

André Duchesne's *Histoire de la maison de Chastillon-sur-Marne* (Paris, 1621) can now be controlled not only as the method Gatterer criticizes in 1769, but as a materially structured book in which genealogical/historical exposition and documentary `Preuves` occupy **distinct navigational zones**.

This matters because Gatterer's 1769 criticism of the Duchesne method is specifically about `Evidenz`: reproducing proof passages is not enough when genealogical propositions and proofs remain too far apart for their agreement to be perceived quickly.

A page-level institutional control now shows that this problem has a concrete bibliographic basis.

For the Jeanne de Châtillon branch, Biblissima indexes Duchesne's:

- historical/genealogical discussion at **pp. 114–119**, Gallica frame **f152**;
- corresponding `Preuves du livre III` at **pp. 68–82** of the proofs section, Gallica frame **f854**.

The relation is therefore not one of locally adjacent claim and proof. The reader moves between a narrative/genealogical zone and a separately paginated documentary proof zone.

The safe conclusion is:

> **Duchesne's book can contain both genealogical propositions and documentary proofs while requiring substantial intra-volume navigation to compare them.**

This gives a physical control for Gatterer's later distinction between evidentiary material being present and the warrant relation being immediately inspectable.

## I. Primary carrier and institutional control

Google Books exposes a 1621 Lyon Public Library digitisation of Duchesne's *Histoire de la maison de Chastillon-sur-Marne* and Google Play exposes a free 1092-page carrier (`QtdDAAAAcAAJ`).

BnF cataloguing independently identifies the same 1621 work as consultable on Gallica and gives its full programmatic title: the genealogies and arms are `justifié[s] par chartes, titres, arrêts et autorités des plus fidèles historiens`.

At title level, therefore, the work explicitly combines:

`genealogy + arms + historical narrative`
with
`charters + titles + judgments + trusted historians as proofs`.

The issue for Gatterer is not absence of proof. It is how the proof is disposed relative to the proposition it warrants.

## II. Jeanne de Châtillon as a page-level separation control

Biblissima's record for Jeanne de Châtillon provides exact Gallica locators into the 1621 book:

- Duchesne, main discussion: **pp. 114–119**, Gallica `bpt6k318896w/f152`;
- `Preuves du livre III`: **pp. 68–82**, Gallica `bpt6k318896w/f854`.

This is especially valuable because Biblissima is not merely citing Duchesne generically. It maps one historical person to both the main exposition and the separate proof corpus.

Operationally, the reader's task is approximately:

`genealogical/historical statement in main text`
→ `identify relevant proof section`
→ `move to separately paginated Preuves`
→ `locate documentary passage`
→ `compare back to the proposition`.

That is precisely the kind of navigational burden that Gatterer's 1769 local-adjacency method attempts to reduce.

Do not convert the frame-number distance (`f152` → `f854`) into a literal cognitive-distance metric. The carrier includes front matter, tables and separately paginated sections. The important controlled fact is the **sectional separation**.

## III. The `Preuves` are genuine documentary source space, not decorative appendix

External scholarly/institutional uses of Duchesne's `Preuves` confirm their function as a documentary corpus.

The Telma/Chartes database, for example, records a 1290 charter and identifies its source as an `édition ancienne` in:

`A. DUCHESNE, Histoire de la maison de Chastillon sur Marne, 1621, Preuves, p. 159.`

Modern medieval scholarship likewise cites `preuves du livre III, p. 71-72` for documentary evidence concerning the counts of Blois.

Thus the separated `Preuves` section is not merely a bibliography or note apparatus. It contains transcribed documentary evidence reusable as a source edition.

The controlled Duchesne architecture is therefore:

`genealogy / narrative / arms`
+
`separate documentary proof corpus`.

This is a strong evidentiary architecture, but not yet Gatterer's 1769 architecture of one proposition placed immediately beside its proof passage.

---

## IV. Relation to Gatterer's 1769 critique

Gatterer praises the method associated with Duchesne for reproducing proof passages verbatim and recognizes its broad adoption. His criticism is a second-order one: genealogical propositions remain compressed in Stammtafeln, multiple propositions are gathered together, and propositions cannot be brought sufficiently close to their corresponding proofs for identity to be perceived rapidly.

The new page-level Duchesne control makes the distinction concrete:

### Duchesne

`proof passage preserved`
+
`proof collected in dedicated proof zone`
+
`reader navigates between genealogical exposition/table and proof corpus`.

### Gatterer's 1769 ideal

`one genealogical proposition`
+
`its reproduced Beweisstelle`
+
`immediate local co-location`
→ `agreement inspectable mit einem Blick`.

The change is therefore not simply from `no evidence` to `evidence`.

It is a transformation in **proof localization and addressability**.

## V. Relation to Gierl's comparative sequence

Martin Gierl's illustration list identifies Duchesne 1621 as:

`Genealogie, Wappen, Chronik`, with a `Stammtafel` around pp. 68f.

He then places beside it:

- Köhler 1733: `Stammtafel und Probationes`, p. 5 / Tafel 1;
- Sprengel / Gattererian method: `Tafel und Erläuterungen`, p. 19ff.

The present control makes the first term in that sequence more analytically precise.

Duchesne should be coded as:

- source/proof reproduction: **present**;
- dedicated proof corpus: **present**;
- local claim–proof adjacency: **not the organizing principle demonstrated by the current page-level control**;
- intra-volume navigation between exposition and proof: **demonstrably required in at least the Jeanne de Châtillon case**.

This yields a meaningful baseline for comparison with Köhler and Sprengel.

## VI. Variables for the comparative layout census

For each format, record separately:

1. `claim_granularity` — person/relation/cluster;
2. `proof_granularity` — one charter passage, several documents, narrative authority cluster;
3. `proof_location` — same line/page/opening, nearby pages, separate section/appendix;
4. `addressing_system` — number, letter, name, heading, marginal reference;
5. `proof_mode` — verbatim transcription, excerpt, paraphrase, citation only;
6. `reader_navigation` — local glance, page turn, section jump, volume-level lookup.

The Duchesne control currently supports:

`proof_location = separate proof section`

and, for a mapped individual branch,

`reader_navigation = main exposition ↔ separately paginated Preuves`.

The remaining variables require direct page inspection.

## VII. Relevance to the frontispiece corpus

Duchesne's architecture clarifies a more general problem in historical print.

Evidence can be **present in the same volume yet functionally distant** from the visual or textual proposition it warrants.

For frontispieces and plates, this suggests recording not only whether explanatory evidence exists somewhere in a host book, but:

- whether the plate has local labels or references;
- whether its historical interpretation depends on distant prose;
- whether later rebinding/reuse increases proof distance;
- whether an image survives after the textual apparatus that once made its evidentiary role inspectable has disappeared.

This is a way to historicize the relation between image and proof without treating all illustration as evidence by default.
