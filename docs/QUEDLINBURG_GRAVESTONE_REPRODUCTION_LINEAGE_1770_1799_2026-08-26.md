# Quedlinburg gravestone reproduction lineage, 1770–1799 — 26 August 2026

## Status

**Four distinct metal-engraving moments are now separated: 1770, 1783, 1787 and 1799.**

The earlier project shorthand `1770 plate → 1783/1799 replicas` is incomplete. André de Melo Araújo's reconstruction identifies four engravings of the same Quedlinburg gravestone, produced in different editorial and evidentiary situations. Their differences are methodologically useful because they expose how a historical object changed when redrawn, engraved, recombined for teaching, locally re-observed, and authorially remediated.

This is not a `same_plate` lineage. The safe relation is a sequence of **derived visual surrogates / re-engravings / renewed representations**, each of which must be controlled at image level.

---

## 1. 1770 — Gatterer's expert opinion and the first printed surrogate

Johann Christoph Gatterer, `J. C. Gatterers diplomatisches Responsum den Streit über König Heinrichs des Finklers Grabmal ... betreffend, nebst denen dazu gehörigen Actenstücken und Zeichnungen`, *Allgemeine historische Bibliothek* 15 (Halle: Johann Justinus Gebauer, 1770), pp. 3–30.

### Evidentiary operation

Gatterer had not inspected the Quedlinburg stone in person. Drawings and a verbal description travelled to Göttingen. His diplomatic analysis of graphic/script form, heraldic signs and iconography established that the stone could not be Henry the Fowler's tenth-century royal tomb and associated it instead with Friedrich von Hoym.

The printed engraving then allowed readers to inspect a surrogate of the object alongside the published expert opinion. The sequence is therefore:

`material object → drawing / description sent to Göttingen → expert analysis → engraved surrogate → distributed expert opinion`.

This is a particularly clean case of **remote historical judgment mediated through visual surrogates**.

## 2. 1770 textual reproduction failure — the argument exceeds the printer's type repertoire

The image was not the only vulnerable carrier. Gatterer's palaeographical reasoning depended on distinctions between neo-Gothic letter forms.

When the Responsum was set at Gebauer's Halle shop, the compositor could not preserve all those graphic distinctions. In the 1799 republication Gatterer explicitly complained that serious printing errors had entered the 1770 version and that the neo-Gothic letters decisive for dating had been badly deformed. He specifies that the compositor substituted inappropriate signs for several neo-Gothic forms.

The methodological point is sharper than generic `print error`:

> **A historical inference can depend on formal distinctions that ordinary typography is technically unable to transmit.**

The evidentiary chain can therefore fail after the historian's analysis, at the reproduction stage visible to readers.

## 3. 1783 — Gruber turns Gatterer's case into a teaching plate and introduces a new visual error

Gregor Maximilian Gruber, *Lehrsystem einer allgemeinen Diplomatik vorzüglich für Oesterreich und Deutschland ...*, vol. II, practical part (Vienna: Johann Paul Kraußische Buchhandlung, 1783).

Araújo shows that Gruber selected the Quedlinburg case because Gatterer's systematic diplomatic procedure could be relied upon and because the analysis followed the *Elementa artis diplomaticae universalis* that Gruber himself used as a methodological foundation.

### Transformation of the carrier

The new plate does not merely reproduce the 1770 image in the same editorial situation. Gruber places a new reproduction of the gravestone **inside a didactic plate together with excerpts of medieval diplomata**.

The object has therefore changed function:

`case-specific surrogate in a controversy`
→ `portable exemplar inside a practical diplomatic teaching system`.

### New error

Araújo identifies a concrete copying failure: the artist commissioned in 1783 **omitted a letter in the centre of the upper inscription**.

This gives the project a rare controlled example in which repeated visual circulation is itself generative of new documentary error.

Safe relation:

`1770 gravestone representation → derived 1783 teaching representation with omission`

not `same_plate_as`.

The copy used by Araújo is Freie Universität Berlin, shelfmark `17 E 86-2`.

## 4. 1787 — Voigt re-observes / re-describes the stone and supplies a different visual record

Gottfried Christian Voigt, *Geschichte des Stifts Quedlinburg*, vol. II (Leipzig, 1787), pp. 90–96, with plate inserted **between pp. 96 and 97**.

Fast manual route:

`https://books.google.com/books?id=PsYGAAAAcAAJ&pg=PA90`

### Why this is not simply another replica

Araújo treats the 1787 engraving separately from the two explicit replicas of the 1770 image (Gruber 1783 and Gatterer 1799). Voigt's account incorporates **previously unavailable details** of the same material object and promises/provides a more faithful drawing, especially with regard to the script.

The 1787 state therefore represents a renewed encounter with the object's information rather than only a downstream copy of Gatterer's first engraving.

This creates an important branching structure:

`stone`
→ `1770 remote drawings → Gatterer plate`
→ `1783 didactic replica of Gatterer carrier`

but also:

`stone / renewed local observation`
→ `1787 Voigt representation with additional detail`.

The lineage is therefore not a single linear chain.

## 5. 1799 — Gatterer deliberately remediates the case

Johann Christoph Gatterer, *Praktische Diplomatik: nebst 15, gröstentheils in Kupfer gestochenen Tafeln* (Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1799), pp. 132–152.

### Authorial correction is explicit

At p.152 Gatterer explains why the Responsum needed to be printed again: the 1770 Halle version contained serious errors, above all deformation of the neo-Gothic letters on which the dating depended.

The 1799 version therefore performs two related corrections:

1. **textual/typographic remediation** — the diagnostic letter forms are printed more adequately;
2. **visual remediation** — the 1770 gravestone plate is replicated again as a newly produced engraving rather than simply carried forward as an unchanged matrix.

Araújo notes that although the 1770 and 1799 plates can initially look like impressions from the same matrix, closer comparison brings differences into view. Treat the 1799 plate as a **new derived engraving** until image-level plate-state evidence proves otherwise.

## 6. Revised four-stage model

The best current reconstruction is not:

`original → increasingly accurate copies`.

It is:

`1770 controversy / remote expert surrogate`
→ `1783 didactic reuse / new omission error`

alongside

`1787 renewed local information / independent visual revision`

followed by

`1799 authorial remediation / corrected typography + newly replicated plate`.

This is analytically stronger because each reproduction performs a different historical operation:

| year | carrier | operation | transformation risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1770 | Gatterer, *AHB* 15 | remote expert judgment + public verification | diagnostic script distinctions partly lost in typesetting |
| 1783 | Gruber, *Lehrsystem*, II | didactic extraction and recombination | a letter omitted in the new engraving |
| 1787 | Voigt, *Geschichte des Stifts Quedlinburg*, II | renewed local description / added object detail | new representation must be controlled against the stone and earlier surrogates |
| 1799 | Gatterer, *Praktische Diplomatik* | authorial republication and remediation | replica remains a mediated reconstruction, not the object itself |

## 7. Article-level consequence

The case should not be used merely to say that eighteenth-century historians became attentive to visual evidence.

A stronger formulation is available:

> Gatterer's diplomatic judgment depended on **preserving discriminating visual differences across a chain of representations**. The 1770, 1783, 1787 and 1799 carriers show that reproduction did not simply transmit evidence: it could erase distinctions, introduce new errors, add newly observed information, and become the target of deliberate scholarly correction.

This links the Quedlinburg material directly to the project's larger distinction:

`object history ≠ representation history ≠ plate history ≠ host-publication history ≠ evidentiary function`.

It also supplies a counterweight to the Clio/frontispiece material. In Clio, an inherited image can remain stable while publication regimes change. In Quedlinburg, the represented object remains nominally the same while the **visual carrier and the actionable information it preserves change repeatedly**.

---

## 8. Immediate image-level control

The highest-value manual check is a side-by-side comparison of:

1. 1770 plate, between pp.4–5;
2. 1783 Gruber teaching plate;
3. 1787 Voigt plate, between pp.96–97;
4. 1799 plate, between pp.146–147.

Record for each:

- full inscription and line disposition;
- position/form of the diagnostic neo-Gothic letters;
- the letter omitted in 1783;
- coat-of-arms form;
- figure/knight details;
- framing lines and plate dimensions;
- engraver/draughtsman signatures;
- whether the stone is isolated or embedded among other diplomatic specimens.

Do not normalize discrepancies. The discrepancies are the evidence.
