# Leibniz → Reimmann reception boundary, 1693–1703: pre-1694 genealogy/insignia classification, narrowed influence window, and first controlled contact

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE NEGATIVE/BOUNDARY RESULT — LEIBNIZ c.1693 PRE-REIMMANN CLASSIFICATION ALREADY CONTROLLED; REIMMANN 1694 PRIMARY OBJECT CONTROLLED; LEIBNIZ-SIDE RECEPTION CONTROLLED BY 1 NOVEMBER 1701; SURVIVING DIRECT CORRESPONDENCE CONTROLLED FROM 10 DECEMBER 1703; PRE-1698 DIRECT RECEPTION NOT FOUND IN THIS PASS

## Result

This pass closes one previously open causal possibility and narrows another.

The repository had already established from the Akademieausgabe draft sequence N.78–N.81 that Leibniz's c.1693 library-ordering work contains genealogy together with arms/insignia before Jacob Friedrich Reimmann's 1694 *De Fatis studii genealogici* appeared.

The decisive pre-1694 sequence is:

- N.78: `Historia familiarum seu Genealogica, huc insignia et arma`;
- N.79: compressed family-history / genealogy + insignia wording;
- N.80: `Historia Genealogica ubi et de insignibus`;
- N.81: `Historiae migrationum, item Genealogiarum simul et insignium`.

Therefore:

> **Reimmann 1694 cannot explain the first insertion of genealogy and heraldic-sign material into Leibniz's historical classification. That inclusion is already present in Leibniz's c.1693 drafting sequence.**

This closes the broad hypothesis:

`Reimmann 1694 → Leibniz first introduces genealogy/heraldry into historical classification`.

A narrower question remains chronologically possible because the later Leibniz *contractior* is dated by the critical edition to `1695 bis Frühjahr 1698 (?)` and the posthumous print tradition exposes the formula:

`Historia civilis. ... Geographia. Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.`

The interval is therefore:

`Leibniz c.1693 Genealogica / Genealogiarum + insignia / arma`
→ `Reimmann 1694 genealogy-history print`
→ `Leibniz 1695–spring 1698 (?) contractior: Genealogica & Heraldica`.

But this pass found **no direct evidence** that Reimmann's 1694 print caused the terminological or classificatory change. The surviving direct Reimmann–Leibniz correspondence begins only in December 1703; direct Leibniz-side knowledge of Reimmann's genealogy-history is securely controlled by November 1701.

The appropriate relation is now:

`REIMMANN 1694 → LEIBNIZ CONTRACTIOR TERMINOLOGICAL/COMPRESSIVE REVISION = CHRONOLOGICALLY POSSIBLE, DIRECT RECEPTION UNPROVED`.

The stronger historical model is currently:

`independent Leibnizian genealogy/insignia classification already present c.1693`
+
`independent Reimmann genealogy-history programme in print 1694`
→ `later documented convergence/contact by 1701–1703`.

This is a more discriminating result than either an influence claim or a generic parallelism claim.

## 4. Direct Leibniz-side knowledge is secure by 1 November 1701

Leibniz's letter to Johann Georg Eckhart of 1 November 1701, Akademieausgabe I,20 N.42, places:

`Reimanni de fatis Genealog. studii`

inside an explicit recension workflow. Leibniz lists it together with several other books whose reviewing can be deferred until he is present.

The editors identify the item as Reimmann's *Historia literaria de fatis studii genealogici* in its 1702 publication form.

This is the earliest **controlled direct Leibniz-side reception** located in the present pass.

It proves:

`Leibniz/Eckhart information environment ↔ Reimmann genealogy-history project by Nov. 1701`.

It does not prove:

- first encounter in 1701;
- reception of the 1694 physical edition specifically;
- any effect on the 1695–98 *contractior*.

## 6. The critical edition identifies Johann Andreas Schmidt as the contact broker in 1703

The introduction to Akademieausgabe I,22 states that the correspondence with the Halberstadt polyhistor **begins in that volume** and concerns above all historical sources and historical projects.

It further reconstructs a concrete mediation path:

`Johann Andreas Schmidt`
→ puts Leibniz and Reimmann in contact
→ pre-correspondence requests concerning Halberstadt historiography pass back and forth
→ Reimmann's first letter, N.424, develops information and questions about Martin von Rhebock
→ Reimmann offers unpublished manuscript chronicles potentially useful to Leibniz
→ a Halberstadt chronicle copy later enters Leibniz's *Scriptores rerum Brunsvicensium* work.

This is stronger than merely knowing that they later corresponded.

The first controlled direct-contact ecology is:

`broker / Schmidt`
→ `historical question`
→ `manuscript chronicle locator`
→ `source offer`
→ `copy transmission`
→ `historical edition / project use`.

## 9. Consequence for the frontispiece argument

The significance of Reimmann changes slightly but productively.

The repository no longer needs Reimmann as the source from which Leibniz acquired genealogy as a historical category. The c.1693 Leibniz sequence already demonstrates an indigenous Leibnizian ecology in which:

`family history / genealogy / insignia / arms`
+
`antiquities / coins / inscriptions`
+
`manuscripts / diplomas / monument criticism`
→ historical organization and proof.

Reimmann's importance lies elsewhere:

- genealogy becomes an object with its own historical *fata*;
- writers and traditions can be bibliographically ordered;
- genealogical study becomes explicitly susceptible to sceptical criticism;
- by 1701 this programme is inside Leibniz/Eckhart's review environment;
- by 1703 Reimmann and Leibniz participate directly in the same source-procurement ecology.

Thus the pre-1726 line is increasingly best described as **converging organizational operations**, later joined by documented social and textual contact, rather than a single transmission chain.

Working formulation:

> **Before Reimmann's genealogy history appeared, Leibniz had already classified genealogy together with insignia and arms inside a historical-evidentiary apparatus. Reimmann independently historicized genealogy as a learned field in 1694. His programme entered Leibniz's review environment by 1701, and direct correspondence from 1703 developed through manuscript-source exchange. The safe genealogy is therefore convergence followed by documented contact, not Reimmannian origin of Leibniz's classificatory bundle.**
