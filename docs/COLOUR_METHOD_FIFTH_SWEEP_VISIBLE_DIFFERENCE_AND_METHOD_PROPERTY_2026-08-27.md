# Colour as method — fifth sweep: visible difference, inference jurisdiction, and method-property

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: active synthesis

## 1. Main result

The latest Araújo control and a renewed prior-art sweep force two simultaneous corrections.

First, the Gatterer–Schlözer `Federkrieg` is already established in scholarship as a substantive epistemological dispute, not merely a competition for students, lecture fees or reputation. Araújo reconstructs the contrast between Gatterer's `vierfache Beschäftigung` and Schlözer's `vierfache Methode`, explicitly treating the conflict as one over valid epistemological access to universal-historical representation and the connection of parts.

Second, the visual/optical dimension of Gatterer's historical method is also strong prior art. Daniel Fulda has explicitly interpreted Gatterer's project under the heading `The Primacy of the Optical`, including synchronistic tables as a response to simultaneous spatial activities that sequential narrative cannot adequately display, and connects this to Gatterer's theory of Plan, vividness and historical Evidenz.

Therefore novelty cannot be located in any of the following claims:

- the 1773 `Federkrieg` had epistemological stakes;
- `Plan` involved selection and arrangement;
- Gatterer wanted world history to make connections visible;
- synchronistic tables solved a problem of simultaneity;
- Gatterer linked historical representation to optical/perceptual Evidenz;
- visual clarity had a scientific role.

The remaining opening is narrower and stronger:

> **How was permission to infer from visible or chromatically encoded difference distributed across distinct epistemic tasks, and when did a representational operation become part of an appropriable historical method?**

## 2. Federkrieg: the property bundle is now precisely bounded

Araújo points to the decisive primary loci.

In Schlözer's `Species Facti` (1773), pp. 414–415, the priority question is framed through `Erfindungen`, `Aenderungen`, `Plündernde` and `Geplünderte`.

In Gatterer's *Antwort auf die Schlözersche Species Facti* (1773), pp. 37–38, Gatterer is quoted as claiming that Schlözer learned from his lectures and publications:

`meinen ganzen Plan der Universalhistorie, meine ganze universalhistorische Denkungsart, Methode, Entdeckungen u.s.f.`

The current property bundle is therefore:

`Plan`
+ `Denkungsart`
+ `Methode`
+ `Entdeckungen`
+ lecture transmission
+ publication corpus.

This is strong secondary quotation control with exact primary loci. It remains `secondary_scholarship / probable` until the page images are directly inspected.

The project-specific missing bridge is no longer whether method could be treated as intellectual property. It plainly could. The missing question is:

> **Did graphical implementation — synchronistic tables, disposition, overview, visual arrangement — belong to this appropriable bundle?**

## 3. The bridge moves backward to 1761

Araújo supplies an important prehistory in Gatterer's *Handbuch der Universalhistorie* (1761), pp. 61–62.

Gatterer's method distinguishes chronological narration of each people/state from a second rule requiring the states flourishing at the same time to be narrated in `synchronistischem Zusammenhange`.

Araújo further reports that Gatterer explicitly judged Köhler's `synchronistische Tabellen` useful for clarifying the `Zusammenhang und die Verbindung gleichzeitiger Völker und Reiche`.

This matters because tabular synchronism is therefore not a late decorative supplement to a conceptual Plan. It belongs to Gatterer's pre-1773 attempt to solve the problem of world-historical simultaneity and connection.

However, the inference must remain bounded:

`table belongs to Gatterer's historical method context before 1773`

DOES NOT YET ESTABLISH

`table was explicitly part of the method-property alleged to have been plagiarised in 1773`.

Direct control of *Handbuch* 1761 pp. 61–62 and *Antwort* 1773 pp. 37–38 is required before connecting those propositions historically.

## 4. Strong prior art: Gatterer's optical historiography

Daniel Fulda's chapter in *Rethinking Lessing's Laocoon* already treats Gatterer's history as an optical problem.

Fulda reconstructs `Plan` as a contemporary concept involving selection and arrangement of materials and notes that Gatterer was dissatisfied with a predominantly diachronic order forced by linguistic/narrative succession. Synchronistic tables are presented as a device for spatially simultaneous activity that narrative cannot display at once.

Fulda also reconstructs Gatterer's historiographical Evidenz as combining:

- reliability / scientific persuasiveness;
- vividness / illusion of presence;
- an eyewitness-like perceptual ideal transferred from historical source to later reader.

This is a major novelty guard.

Do not claim:

`epistemic warrant versus perceptual Evidenz` as though no one has noticed that the two meanings coexist in Gatterer.

The project can still ask something Fulda's optical-history framing does not by itself answer:

> **When the medium makes a distinction perceptually forceful, what licenses the specific historical inference drawn from that distinction?**

That question requires moving from general optical vividness to task-specific authorization.

## 5. Gatterer human colour: not adoption then rejection, but task-specific jurisdiction

Araújo's reconstruction of Gatterer 1771–1789 corrects a simpler chronology.

### 1771: essential/genealogical restriction already exists

In *Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie*, pp. 61–62, outward bodily differences are reported as `zufällige` rather than `wesentliche Unterscheidungszeichen`, and as `blose Varietäten`.

Thus Gatterer had not simply treated skin colour as essential in 1775 and rejected that view later. The restriction precedes the proposed anthropography.

### 1775: descriptive use remains available

In *Abriß der Geographie*, pp. 4–5, Gatterer nevertheless projects a `Menschen- und Völkerkunde (Anthropographia und Ethnographia)` including a `Geographie der Menschenkörper nach Gestalt und Hautfarben`.

Visible bodily difference can therefore still organize a descriptive anthropography without being granted essential or genealogical force.

### 1789: language outranks skin colour for origin inference

Araújo reports that in *Kurzer Begriff der Geographie*, vol. 2, p. 606, peoples are classified decisively by language because language — not skin colour — is a more solid proof of origin. Skin colour can persist when there is insufficient precise and reliable linguistic information.

The correct trajectory is therefore not:

`colour adopted -> colour rejected`.

It is:

`visible bodily colour`
→ legitimate for descriptive discrimination
→ insufficient for essential difference
→ weaker than language for genealogical/origin inference
→ available as fallback under data scarcity.

## 6. A new distinction: observation, classification and inference

The Gatterer case now permits three levels to be separated.

### `perceptual_discriminability`

Can a difference be seen or reliably reported?

### `classificatory_utility`

Can that difference be used to group or distinguish objects/people for a specified descriptive task?

### `inferential_warrant`

What stronger historical proposition may be drawn from the classification — origin, essence, cause, relation, duration, importance?

These levels do not entail one another.

A colour difference can be:

- perceptually real;
- descriptively useful;
- genealogically weak.

This is a stronger test than the familiar distinction between true and false representation.

## 7. Three negative colour jurisdictions now visible

### A. Schlözer 1772 — fact-layer overreach

`colour -> modifies Factum`

Failure: rhetorical alteration of factual proposition.

### B. Gatterer 1771–1789 — inference-layer overreach

`visible Hautfarbe -> essential/genealogical conclusion`

Failure: a real perceptual difference lacks sufficient warrant for the stronger inference.

### C. graphical/chromatic display — display-to-evidence overreach

`clear table/colour contrast -> apparent historical certainty`

Failure: perceptual salience may exceed the warrant of the prior classification or relation.

These failures are structurally distinct and should not be collapsed into a single `colour error`.

## 8. Positive jurisdictions remain equally important

The project is not a history of prohibitions.

Colour can be legitimate when the task matches its warrant:

- heraldic tincture as source/object property;
- hatching as conventional surrogate decoder;
- Synopsis pigment as system-assigned relational syntax;
- Schlözer's proposed illumination as perceptual display of already organized duration;
- Gatterer's Hautfarbe as descriptive bodily distinction, if not overextended to essential/genealogical inference.

The key object is therefore **jurisdiction**, not pro-colour versus anti-colour attitudes.

## 9. Revised novelty boundary

Already occupied by prior art:

- Gatterer and visual cognition;
- optical historiography;
- synchronistic tables and simultaneity;
- Plan as selection/arrangement;
- the substantive epistemological dimension of the Federkrieg;
- Gatterer's historical Evidenz and vividness;
- colour as cognitive semantics in the Synopsis.

Still open, subject to primary closure:

> **A cross-domain history of colour's inferential jurisdiction: the distinction between a visible property, a coded relation and the historical inference each is permitted to carry; the differential warrants for those operations; and the conditions under which visual or chromatic clarity exceeds, substitutes for, or remains subordinate to source, classificatory and genealogical warrant.**

This formulation can connect heraldry, universal-history tables and human classification without asserting one transferred `colour method` across domains.

## 10. The method-property question after the prior-art correction

The 1773 dispute remains useful, but its role changes.

It is no longer evidence that visuality itself was controversial. Rather, it asks whether a historian's **operational package** could become a priority-bearing possession.

Working sequence:

`problem of universal simultaneity`
→ `synchronistic rule`
→ `table / graphical externalization`
→ `colour as one optional relation-marking layer`
→ `Plan / Methode as professional property?`

Only the first three links are presently strongly controlled by scholarship and bibliography. The final property link remains unresolved.

## 12. Current strongest formulation

> **The history of colour as historical method is not a history of whether colour was used. It is a history of what could legitimately be inferred from a visible or chromatically encoded difference. A colour might be true as an observation, useful as a classifier, weak as evidence of origin, powerful as a mnemonic relation, or dangerous when perceptual clarity was mistaken for historical warrant.**

Short form:

> **When may a visible difference become a historical difference?**
