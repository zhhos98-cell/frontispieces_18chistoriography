## 3. Six-person commission: negative search result must remain explicit

Thomas Fuchs's 2002 public summary directly states that in 1686 a six-member censorship commission met for several months, consisting of three professors from Marburg and three from Gießen, and fundamentally revised the Chronicle. The public summary does **not** name them.

The current open-web sweep searched combinations of:

- `Winckelmann / Winckelmanns Chronik`;
- `1686 / 1687`;
- `Zensurkommission / Zensoren / Revision / Commissarien`;
- `Marburg / Gießen`;
- Fuchs-specific vocabulary and title references.

No source was recovered that directly names the six members.

Therefore:

`SIX-MEMBER STRUCTURE = CONTROLLED`

`INDIVIDUAL SIX NAMES = UNRESOLVED`.

## 4. Faculty-roster guardrail

A 1686 Marburg or Gießen faculty roster may be used only to generate **search candidates**.

It must not be used as identity proof.

Required promotion rule:

`candidate professor in office in 1686`
+
`direct documentary/scholarly link to Winckelmann revision commission`
→ `commissioner identity can be promoted`.

Without the second term, the candidate remains a search lead only.

This guardrail matters because Fuchs characterizes the censors as younger professors shaped by early-Enlightenment standards; that description is analytically suggestive but insufficient for prosopographical identification.

## 5. Tollner guardrail

Karl Ludwig Tollner (1660–1715) is an important **later** node in the history of Hessian historiography: after Winckelmann's death Landgrave Karl I commissioned him to continue/work on Hessian history, and Johann Hermann Schmincke later succeeded him in that historiographical line.

The current sweep found **no direct evidence** that Tollner was one of the 1686 six censors.

Do not infer:

`later Hessian historiographer`
→ `1686 censor`.

This is precisely the kind of retrospective institutional continuity that must be separated from item-level commission membership.

## 6. Fuchs access boundary

The Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek / DNB record for Thomas Fuchs, *Traditionsstiftung und Erinnerungspolitik* (2002; Potsdam Habilitationsschrift 2000/2001), reports that only the table of contents is digitally available through that record.

A recent scholarly treatment by Peter Rabeler explicitly sends the detailed account of Winckelmann's revisions/censorship back to Fuchs, pp. 246–305.
