## Modern locator control: common origin, migration, climate

A 2026 chapter by Simone De Angelis independently places the crucial sequence at Zimmermann 1778 pp.114–115:

- p.114: Caucasus as the proposed origin / dispersal centre, tied to the elevation and radiating mountain systems;
- p.115: population growth, family migration across large regions, gradual accommodation to climates, and climatic transformation into geographically named human groups.

Source:

Simone De Angelis, “In the Course of Time”. The Formative Drive and “Dégénération” in Johann Friedrich Blumenbach’s Varieties of Humankind (2026), Springer Nature Link:

## Modern locator control: colour and taxonomic vocabulary

Michael Wallaschek’s 2018 historiography supplies a second, more granular locator map for Zimmermann 1778:

- pp.53ff., 77ff.: human groups described particularly through bodily size and skin colour, with geographical distribution represented on the zoological world map;
- pp.54, 59: `Aus-/Abartungen` and `Varietäten` are reported as used without Kant’s tighter definitional separation;
- pp.54, 101: `Racen`, `Menschengattungen`, `Gattung`, `Art`, and `Geschlecht` are reported as taxonomically loose / co-level usages rather than a stable hierarchy;
- p.107: migrations and resulting mixtures are reported as causal to the present distribution of human `Racen`;
- pp.114ff.: Zimmermann’s migration / `Anartung` account is again flagged as a major comparator for Kant.

Source:

Michael Wallaschek, *Beiträge zur Geschichte der Zoogeographie. 11* (Halle, 2018), especially the Anthropogeographie section, PDF:
https://d-nb.info/1174478888/34

The relevant Wallaschek pages were visually checked in the PDF, not taken only from a search snippet. They remain secondary evidence for Zimmermann until the cited 1778 pages are collated.
