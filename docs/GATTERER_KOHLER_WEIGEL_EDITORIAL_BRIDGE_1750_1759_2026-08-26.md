# Gatterer–Köhler–Weigel editorial bridge, 1750/1756–1759 — 26 August 2026

## Status

**Gatterer's contribution to the posthumously completed twenty-second part of Köhler's `Wöchentliche Historische Münz-Belustigung` is securely attributed in modern bibliographic scholarship and the complete primary volume is available full-view. The nominal volume date is 1750; the relevant final material appeared only in 1756 after Johann David Köhler's death. Exact title-page imprint, editorial wording in Gatterer's preface, and contribution-level page images remain bounded primary checks.**

This discovery materially changes the mid-century transition model. Gatterer did not first enter the Weigel/Köhler print ecology with the 1759 genealogy/heraldry handbook. Before that handbook, he was already contributing to the posthumous closure of Köhler's numismatic serial and supplying a biographical preface on Köhler himself.

---

## I. Seven Gatterer contributions in part 22

Martin Gierl's bibliography attributes the following items in part 22 to Johann Christoph Gatterer. Gierl retains the series' nominal date `1750` but explicitly notes that these items appeared in **1756**.

1. `[Gatterer] 1750a` — `Ein überaus schöner Medaillon auf den König in Pohlen Sigmund August vom Jahr 156– (vermuthlich 1564.)`, no. 51, pp. 401–408.
2. `[Gatterer] 1750b` — `Eine rare Medaille König Stephans in Pohlen auf die Eroberung Lieflands vom Jahr 1582`, second supplement sheet, pp. 425–432.
3. `[Gatterer] 1750c` — `Gedächtnißmünze auf die Ehe-Jubelfeyer Herrn Jobst Lazarus Königs ... vom Jahr 1750`, no. 50, pp. 393–400.
4. `[Gatterer] 1750d` — `König Sigmunds III. in Pohlen überaus rarer und schöner Thaler vom Jahr 1630`, third supplement sheet, pp. 433–444.
5. `[Gatterer] 1750e` — `König Stephans in Pohlen rare Gedächtnismünze auf die Wiedereroberung der Stadt Poloczko vom J. 1579`, no. 52, pp. 409–416.
6. `[Gatterer] 1750f` — `König Stephans in Pohlen Thaler auf das wiedereroberte Liefland und die Stadt Poloczko`, first supplement sheet, pp. 417–424.
7. `[Gatterer] 1750g` — unpaginated `Vorrede, in welcher von des seeligen Herrn Prof. Köhlers Leben und Schriften zuverlässige Nachricht mitgetheilet wird`.

Gierl's illustration list also reproduces Gatterer's Sigismund III item at p. 433 as a concrete example of his work inside Köhler's publication.

A separate modern history of eighteenth-century numismatics independently states that Gatterer contributed **seven pieces** to the last volume of the `Münzbelustigungen`, nominally for 1750 but published in 1756.

## III. Collaborative posthumous closure, not a single-author completion

Do not write that Gatterer single-handedly completed Köhler's final volume.

The bibliographic evidence is more complicated:

- Gatterer demonstrably supplied six numismatic pieces plus the biographical preface;
- Johann Tobias Köhler is also credited in biographical/catalogue literature with completing and publishing at least the final pieces of the 1750 year after his father's death;
- one modern reconstruction says the publisher requested Gatterer's help in bringing the final material to publication.

The safe formulation is therefore:

> **The posthumous closure of part 22 was collaborative. Gatterer was a substantial contributor and biographical framer of Köhler, while Johann Tobias Köhler also participated in the final completion/publication.**

Until the opening preface and terminal issues are inspected page by page, keep `editor`, `completer`, `publisher-requested continuator`, and `contributor` as distinct roles.

## IV. Why this changes the 1753–1759 transition

The repository already controls the following sequence:

- **1753** — Martin Tyroff actively extends the inherited Weigel heraldic plate stock with the first supplement to the Wappenbuch complex;
- **1755** — Gatterer's `Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum` is produced in Nürnberg with Tyroff among its engravers; the annual Wappenkalender is still issued for `C. Weigels Erben`;
- **1755** — Johann David Köhler dies;
- **1756** — Gatterer contributes six numismatic articles and a Köhler biographical preface to the delayed posthumous final part of the `Münz-Belustigung`;
- **1759** — Gatterer's first `Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik` is still literally published `Im Verlag Christoph Weigels, des ältern, seel. Erben` and carries the inherited Clio pretitular plate `HISTORIA GENEALOGIA HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORVM ET VERITATIS`;
- **1761–1762** — the handbook imprint shifts to Raspe.

This produces a much more concrete transition than a generic intellectual genealogy:

`Köhler's Weigel numismatic serial`
→ `posthumous closure with Gatterer contributions / Köhler biography (1756)`
→ `Gatterer takes over the Weigel genealogy-heraldry publication as Handbuch (1759)`
→ `later Raspe phase`.

The key continuity is **editorial, publishing and material**, not merely thematic.

## V. Consequence for the Clio frontispiece

This bridge changes the host-context history of the inherited Clio plate.

Before this control, one could describe Gatterer's 1759 handbook as a later scholarly publication that happened to reuse an older Weigel/Köhler frontispiece. The 1756 evidence now shows that Gatterer had already entered the same broader Köhler–Weigel serial ecology before the handbook transition.

A stronger safe formulation is:

> **The Clio plate passes into a Gatterer handbook within a publication ecology in which Gatterer had already participated in the posthumous continuation of Köhler's numismatic work.**

This supports a succession/history-of-use argument, but it does **not** prove:

- that Gatterer designed or commissioned the Clio plate;
- that he adopted Köhler's exact interpretation of `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica`;
- that the plate's inscription should be read through Gatterer's later theory of `Evidenz`;
- that all Weigel-heirs titles were editorially coordinated by the same people.

The value of the bridge is that it removes the need for a speculative intellectual-transfer story where a direct print-world succession can be documented.

## VI. Relation to the 1769 genealogy method

This discovery does not yet establish that Gatterer's 1769 `Von der Evidenz in der Genealogie` directly cites Köhler's 1733 genealogical method.

Gierl's modern comparison places:

- Duchesne 1621;
- Köhler 1733 `Stammtafel + Probationes`;
- Sprengel's 1769 example of Gatterer's method

in a comparative visual sequence. That comparison remains historiographical until a direct Gatterer → Köhler citation is located in the 1769 essay or its worked example.

The two relations must therefore stay separate:

1. **1756 direct publishing/editorial relation** — established at contribution/bibliographic level;
2. **1769 methodological genealogy from Köhler's table/proof format** — plausible comparator, direct textual acknowledgment not yet found.
