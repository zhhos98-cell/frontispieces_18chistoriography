## Closure result

Round 3 asked what Zimmermann 1778 already permits colour, climate, geography, migration and human variation to do before Meiners 1785/1793.

The primary-controlled answer is now sufficiently bounded.

### 1. Common origin / diffusion

Zimmermann does present a conjectural route from one human `Stamm` / homeland through population growth, family migration, geographical branching and gradual climatic adaptation/transformation. The pp.114–115 reconstruction is explicitly marked an `Einfall`, so its epistemic status is plausible historical reconstruction rather than mathematical certainty.

### 2. Colour jurisdiction

Colour is a serious descriptive and comparative bodily variable. Zimmermann nevertheless does not let visible colour alone carry the natural division of humankind. At p.109 he explicitly says the `Abtheilung des Menschen` / `Zerfällung in verschiedene Racen` should pay greater attention to `Klima`.

Colour therefore has classificatory importance but not self-sufficient taxonomic essence at the controlled locus.

### 3. Taxonomic vocabulary

The controlled pages do not support a stable Zimmermann hierarchy equivalent to Meiners's later `Geschlecht / Stamm / Race / Varietät / Spielart` sequence.

Zimmermann instead uses a mobile operational vocabulary:

- p.59 explicitly coordinates `Abartungen oder Varietäten`;
- p.59 contrasts `größten Varietäten` with `mindern oder untern Grade der Ausartungen`;
- pp.100–105 allow family traits plus isolation/endogamy to generate a `Race` / `Menschenrace`;
- p.101 uses `Menschengattungen` and common `Stamm` within a possible monogenetic derivation;
- pp.106–111 use `Race`, `Racen`, `Hauptracen` inside competing classificatory systems;
- migration and mixture complicate stable race partition.

The vocabulary is therefore best represented as operationally mobile and problem-relative, not simply as an undeveloped version of Meiners's later hierarchy.

### 4. Intensity / degree

Primary control establishes three distinct intensity regimes:

- environmental intensity: `Grade der Hitze und Kälte`, thermometric differences, pp.31–34;
- bodily colour gradation: `Nüancen`, `Stufenfolge`, and hypothetical temporal increase of `Schwärze`, pp.106–108;
- magnitude of variation: `mindern oder untern Grade der Ausartungen`, p.59.

These are connected in Zimmermann's causal architecture but remain semantically distinct.

### 5. Zimmermann ↔ Meiners boundary

The difference is not adequately described as:

- climate absent → climate present;
- non-taxonomy → taxonomy;
- environmentalism → heredity;
- colour classification → anti-colour classification.

Zimmermann already measures environment, models long-duration bodily plasticity, grades human variation, permits historically generated races, treats migration/mixture as classification-changing, and gives climate direct classificatory jurisdiction.

Meiners's later difference is stronger at the level of **rank stabilization and inferential regulation**:

- a more explicit rank sequence;
- explicit rejection of colour as sole/principal sign;
- explicit restriction on inference from isolated bodily parts;
- specimen-provenance controls;
- sharper passage-level separation between acquired hardening and group-attributed hardness while retaining variable climate causation.

Canonical boundary formulation:

> **Zimmermann 1778 already possesses a historical-geographical machinery for producing, grading and classifying human variation, but its vocabulary and evidential levels remain mobile; Meiners subsequently makes rank relations and admissible bodily inference more explicit and more regulated.**

at p.51.

Primary page-image inspection proves the sentence is on **printed p.59**. The canonical delta records this correction.
