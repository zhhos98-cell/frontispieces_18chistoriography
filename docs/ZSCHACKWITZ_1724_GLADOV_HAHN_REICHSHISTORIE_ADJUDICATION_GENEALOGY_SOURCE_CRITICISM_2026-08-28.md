# Zschackwitz 1724: Gladov–Hahn afterlife, Reichshistorie as genealogy infrastructure, and source-critical adjudication

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE PRIMARY RECEPTION BRIDGE — HAB INSTITUTIONAL FULL-TEXT TRANSCRIPTION DIRECTLY CONTROLLED; PAGE-IMAGE CHECK STILL DESIRABLE

## Result

Johann Ehrenfried Zschackwitz's *Historisch-Genealogischer Schau-Platz* (1724) does much more than list Friedrich Gladov, Simon Friedrich Hahn, Johann Peter Ludewig and Nicolaus Hieronymus Gundling among consulted authors.

Its preface/body directly evaluates the recent German `Reichs-Historie` field and thereby supplies a previously missing afterlife for the Gladov–Hahn controversy.

The sequence is explicit:

`Genealogie requires Historie`
→ `German genealogy therefore requires Reichshistorie`
→ `Halle credited with putting Reichshistorie into proper order`
→ `Ludewig + Gundling named as exemplary programme-builders`
→ `Gladov judged as a partly well-arranged but composite/error-prone work`
→ `Hahn praised for superior learning, structure and primary-source/testimony control`.

This is not merely a bibliography. It is an actor-level methodological adjudication inside a genealogical handbook.

Working relation label:

`PRIMARY-DIRECT CRITICAL RECEPTION / METHODOLOGICAL ADJUDICATION`.

---

## 1. Genealogy is explicitly made dependent on history

Zschackwitz opens the relevant discussion by arguing that genealogies cannot be learned by themselves and require the assistance of history. He immediately specifies that a practitioner must therefore concern himself especially with German imperial history.

The important relation is:

`Genealogie`
→ cannot operate autonomously
→ requires `Historie`
→ specifically requires `Teutsche Reichs-Historie` for German dynastic work.

This is a strong pre-1726 control for the later visual co-presence of `HISTORIA` and `GENEALOGIA`.

It shows that the relation was not only classificatory, as in library/science taxonomies. It was presented as a **working dependency in genealogical practice**.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-DIRECT INSTITUTIONAL TRANSCRIPTION CONTROLLED`.

Source locus: HAB digital transcription around transcript lines 579–581 / page anchors `ID00038` onward.

## 2. Zschackwitz credits Halle with reorganizing Reichshistorie

After criticizing older German historical writing as incomplete, confused, overly Roman-centered or inattentive to the utility of history for German public law, Zschackwitz states that the University of Halle deserves the credit for first bringing German Reichshistorie into a proper order and stripping away what was unnecessary.

He then names:

- **Johann Peter Ludewig**;
- **Nicolaus Hieronymus Gundling**;

and praises their outlines for a complete German Reichshistorie, wishing that they could expand them fully.

This is a crucial control for the Gladov/Gatterer line because it confirms, from 1724, the same Halle founders whom Gatterer would retrospectively identify in 1767 as founders of the large historical tendency he called `juridical`.

The evaluation differs sharply:

### Zschackwitz 1724

`Halle / Ludewig / Gundling`
→ positive reorganization of Reichshistorie
→ useful relation to German public law.

### Gatterer 1767

`Ludewig / Gundling juridical tendency`
→ historically important reform
→ but danger that history becomes subordinated to professional jurisprudence / Staatsrecht.

Therefore the later Gatterer critique can now be set against a contemporary 1724 witness that regarded the Halle reorganization as a methodological advance.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-DIRECT FOR ZSCHACKWITZ; GATTERER COMPARISON STRUCTURAL UNTIL AHB p.29 IS PRIMARY-TRANSCRIBED`.

## 3. Gladov: arrangement partly praised, textual constitution distrusted

Zschackwitz turns directly to the recently published Gladov Reichshistorie.

His judgment is mixed but substantively critical.

He grants the work a reasonably good organization. He then says that its textual constitution visibly appears not to have flowed from one pen but to have been collected from different `Discursen`. He additionally faults the work for very numerous printing errors and for false or distorted principles.

This is a major reception edge because it maps closely onto the 1718–19 controversy without simply repeating the juridical label `plagiarism`.

The critical categories are instead:

- `Einrichtung` / organization;
- unity or plurality of textual production;
- compilation from discourses;
- printing error;
- false principles.

Working status:

`CRITICAL_RECEPTION OF GLADOV — PRIMARY-DIRECT`.

Important caution:

Zschackwitz does **not** in this controlled passage explicitly state that Gladov stole from Hahn. The phrase about multiple discourses is highly relevant to the earlier controversy but should be coded as a **methodological/compositional judgment**, not silently converted into a plagiarism verdict.

## 4. Hahn: an explicit positive counter-model

Zschackwitz's judgment of Simon Friedrich Hahn is markedly stronger.

He calls Hahn's emerging German Reichshistorie exceptionally good and treats it as evidence of the author's:

- diligence;
- extensive reading;
- special knowledge of German events;
- understanding of the necessity of historical citations/support (`allegata`);
- use of strong passages and testimonies drawn from the source fountains themselves.

This last point is the most important for the repo.

Zschackwitz explicitly converts the quality difference between competing Reichshistorie works into an **evidentiary criterion**:

`good historical work`
→ `allegata are necessary`
→ `authorities/testimonies should be taken from Brunnquellen`
→ stronger warrant for historical claims.

This is directly continuous with the repository's existing 1724 Zschackwitz record on:

- documentary support;
- correction;
- certainty;
- comparison;
- the rejection of unsupported or erroneous genealogies.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-DIRECT INSTITUTIONAL TRANSCRIPTION CONTROLLED`.

## 5. The 1718–19 controversy is transformed into a 1724 quality hierarchy

Earlier checkpoint:

`Gladov 1717`
→ Hahn's 1718 accusation of borrowing from dictata/discourses
→ publisher-side defense based partly on shared Halle pedagogical knowledge
→ Herbst 1718 detailed critique
→ 1719 Gladov defense.

Zschackwitz 1724 does something different.

He does not reproduce the polemical legal/moral question:

`who owns the lecture knowledge?`

Instead he evaluates the products through historiographical performance:

`How coherent is the work?`

`Are its principles reliable?`

`Does it provide necessary citations?`

`Are its testimonies drawn from sources?`

This gives a useful transition:

`priority / plagiarism controversy`
→ `methodological reception`
→ `source-critical hierarchy of historical works`.

That transformation is unusually valuable for the present project because it shows how a controversy can survive not as repeated accusation but as **criteria of scholarly judgment**.

## 6. Zschackwitz is not neutral between Gladov and Hahn

The author list alone made Gladov and Hahn look like coexisting authorities in one consultation corpus.

The body text now corrects that flat reading.

They are both consulted, but they are **not assigned equal epistemic standing**.

Current relation:

- Gladov = usable/relevant Reichshistorie, but composite and error-prone;
- Hahn = superior model of source-supported Reichshistorie.

The corpus should therefore distinguish:

`AUTHOR_LIST_CO-PRESENCE`
from
`BODY_TEXT_EVALUATIVE_HIERARCHY`.

This is an important general rule for the repo: bibliographic inclusion does not equal endorsement.

## 7. Direct connection to the frontispiece problem

Gladov's 1717 frontispiece visually stages Historia as a temporal/pragmatic guide. The book title presents the work as a pragmatic account supported by trustworthy testimony and as an introduction to German public law.

Zschackwitz 1724 now gives an early external test of the **host work's actual epistemic performance**.

He effectively separates:

`programmatic title/frontispiece claim`
from
`critical judgment of textual execution`.

That is a highly productive comparison for this repo.

A frontispiece can stage:

- temporal orientation;
- historical utility;
- evidentiary authority;

while a contemporary reader can still challenge:

- compilation structure;
- principles;
- errors;
- source control.

Safe article formulation:

> **The Gladov volume's visual and verbal programme promised pragmatic, testimony-supported Reichshistorie; Zschackwitz's 1724 reception shows a contemporary reader testing that promise against the work's compositional unity, principles, errors and source apparatus.**

Do not claim that Zschackwitz comments on the frontispiece itself.

## 8. A stronger prehistory for `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA`

The sequence before the 1726 Type-A plate is now richer:

### 1690s Leibniz

classification relation:

`Historia civilis → Geographia → Genealogica & Heraldica` in the 1695–98 short scheme.

### Reimmann 1710

explicit bundle:

`Historia Civilis → Chronologia / Geographia / Genealogia / Heraldica / Numismatica / Antiquaria`.

### Gladov 1717

visual/pragmatic relation:

`Historia + Chronos → Reichshistorie → Staatsrecht / temporal orientation`.

### Zschackwitz 1724

working dependency:

`Genealogie requires Historie, especially Reichshistorie`
+
`historical quality requires allegata and source-grounded testimonies`.

### Type A from 1726

visual compression:

`HISTORIA + GENEALOGIA + HERALDICA + TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`.

The prehistory is therefore not only one of disciplinary names. It includes a working claim that genealogy depends on history and a source-critical vocabulary for ranking historical authorities.

## 9. Relation to Gatterer's later `juridical sect` critique

Zschackwitz's 1724 passage makes the later Gatterer critique more historically precise.

Zschackwitz explicitly values the improvement of Reichshistorie partly in terms of what it can contribute to `Jus Publicum` and praises Halle's Ludewig/Gundling programme.

Gatterer, in 1767, later credits Ludewig and Gundling with transforming German historical writing while objecting to the tendency for imperial history to become subordinate to jurisprudential training.

Thus the tension can now be formulated as:

`1724: relation to public law = methodological usefulness`

versus

`1767: excessive subordination to public law = jurisdictional danger`.

This is exactly the sort of historical change the repo needs to capture.

The same relation between history and jurisprudence can shift from **positive utility** to **boundary problem** without requiring either side to be inconsistent.

## 13. Current strongest article-level proposition

> **By 1724 the relation between history, genealogy and evidentiary authority was already operational rather than merely taxonomic. Zschackwitz argued that genealogy depended on Reichshistorie, credited Halle's Ludewig–Gundling programme with reorganizing that field, and then ranked competing Reichshistorie works by compositional coherence, principles, citation and access to source-grounded testimony. The Gladov–Hahn plagiarism dispute had thus acquired a methodological afterlife: the question of who owned historical knowledge was translated into a judgment about how historical claims should be assembled and warranted.**

This gives the 1726 `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS` programme a much denser immediate context than a simple genealogy-of-disciplines account.
