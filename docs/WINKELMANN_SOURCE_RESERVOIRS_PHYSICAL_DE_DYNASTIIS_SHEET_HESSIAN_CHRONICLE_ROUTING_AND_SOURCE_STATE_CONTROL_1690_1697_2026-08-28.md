# Winkelmann source reservoirs: physical `De dynastiis` sheet, Hessian-chronicle routing, and source-state control, c.1690–1697

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE SOURCE-ARCHITECTURE CHECKPOINT — ONE 1690 WINKELMANN EXTRACTION UNIT PHYSICALLY RECONSTRUCTED; A SEPARATE HESSIAN-CHRONICLE / GEOGRAPHICA RESERVOIR IS REFERENCED FROM MULTIPLE PROBLEM FILES; ITS SURVIVING PHYSICAL PACKET IS NOT YET IDENTIFIED; 1694-DATING / 1697-PRINT RELATION REQUIRES SOURCE-STATE DISCIPLINE

## Result

The current Winkelmann evidence should no longer be treated as one undifferentiated set of notes.

At least **two distinct source streams / reservoirs** are now visible.

### A. `De dynastiis` source stream

Two June 1690 Leibniz excerpts from Johann Just Winkelmann's lost manuscript `De dynastiis` survive as Hannover NLB `Ms 23, 47(1) Bl. 124–125`.

The official catalogue and Akademie edition establish a physical fact:

- Bl. 124 is cut from Bl. 125;
- the lower cut edge of Bl. 125 matches the upper cut edge of Bl. 124;
- Leibniz originally wrote the two excerpts on **one sheet**;
- both are filed under `Insignia`.

This is a directly reconstructed **physical extraction unit**.

### B. Hessian-chronicle / description source stream

A different body of Winkelmann/Hessian material is repeatedly invoked through formulas such as:

`Winkelman ex excerpt. Heßischer Chronick. liegen bey Geograf.`

and

`Excerpta ex Winkelm. Hass. so liegen bey Geogr.`

These references occur from at least two different problem files:

- `Insignia` (ID 7139);
- `Antiquissimi habitatores` (ID 14091).

A third note, ID 69098, is itself filed `Linguae | Geographica` and sends the reader to an `excerpt. ex Winkelm.` concerning `Hess / Heßen / Catti`.

This makes a **Geographica-side Winkelmann source reservoir** highly plausible at the level of retrieval practice.

But its physical surviving packet has not yet been reconstructed.

The central architectural distinction is therefore:

`source reservoir / extraction packet`
≠
`problem-oriented jurisdiction file`.

A source batch can be kept in one place while multiple problem files point to it.

## 1. Physical reconstruction: Bl. 124–125 were one sheet

Official Leibniz-Katalog records:

### ID 5959

`Ms 23, 47(1) Bl. 124`

Title:

`[Exzerpt aus Winkelmann, De dynastiis]`

Date:

`> 1690-06`

Ordering mark:

`Insignia ("Insignia")`.

The note ends:

`Aus H. Winckelmans Mso de dynastiis.`

The catalogue remarks:

- watermark remnant: crown / shield with lion;
- Bl. 124 is cut from the lower part of Bl. 125.

### ID 4561

`Ms 23, 47(1) Bl. 125`

Title:

`[Exzerpt aus Winkelmann, De dynastiis]`

Date:

`> 1690-06`

Ordering mark:

`Insignia ("Insignia tit.")`.

The catalogue explicitly states that the lower cut edge matches the upper cut edge of Bl. 124 and concludes that Leibniz wrote the two pieces on one sheet from the same now-lost Winkelmann manuscript.

Sources:

- official catalogue query: https://leibniz-katalog.bbaw.de/de/extended-search?ordnungsvermerk_normiert=Insignia
- Akademie edition, `Ms 23,47`, N.113–114: https://leibnizp1.bbaw.de/fileadmin/Webdateien/Dateien/Ms_23_47.pdf

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY PHYSICAL-WITNESS RELATION CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL CATALOGUE + CRITICAL EDITION`.

### Consequence

This is stronger than source identity or chronological coincidence.

The sequence can be reconstructed materially as:

`one sheet`
→ `two contiguous Winkelmann De dynastiis excerpts`
→ `later cut into Bl.124 + Bl.125`
→ both retained under `Insignia`.

This is currently the clearest physical example in the Winkelmann material of a **source-generated extraction packet**.

## 2. What the `De dynastiis` sheet actually does

The two excerpts are not primarily descriptions of coats of arms.

They concern:

- ducal titulature;
- the historical escalation and accumulation of titles;
- counties and lordships incorporated into Brunswick-Lüneburg rule;
- documentary formulas in charters and letters;
- comparison with older juridical/historical authorities.

Yet Leibniz files the sheet under `Insignia` / `Insignia tit.`.

This broadens `Insignia` once again.

Here the jurisdiction reaches:

`titles / dignities / dynastic territorial accumulation`
+
`documentary titulature`
+
`heraldic-juridical status`.

The file is not reducible to visual blazon.

## 3. A separate Hessian source reservoir is independently referenced twice

### ID 7139 — from `Insignia`

`Ms 23, 47(1) Bl. 21r`, June 1690.

Ordering mark:

`Insignia`.

The note compares the two walking lions of the Counts of Diez with Brunswick forms and concludes:

`Winkelman ex excerpt. Heßischer Chronick. liegen bey Geograf.`

### ID 14091 — from `Antiquissimi habitatores`

`LH 12,1,2 Bl.98`.

Ordering mark:

`Antiquissimi habitatores`.

The note concerns large stone columns near Miltenberg and Zwingenberg and concludes:

`davon vid. Excerpta ex Winkelm. Hass. so liegen bey Geogr.`

These are independent problem contexts:

`heraldic/dynastic sign`

and

`ancient monumental remains`.

Both point to Winkelmann/Hessian excerpts located with `Geographica`.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH CROSS-REFERENCE FORMULAS CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL CATALOGUE`.

### Consequence

The phrase `liegen bey Geogr.` is no longer an isolated local instruction.

It identifies a recurrent **retrieval destination** for Winkelmann/Hessian material.

The safest label is:

`GEOGRAPHICA-SIDE WINKELMANN SOURCE RESERVOIR — PHYSICAL PACKET NOT YET RECONSTRUCTED`.

## 5. The 1694 / 1697 source-state problem

Several Leibniz-Katalog records assigned to the 1694 historical-working complex contain material that the editors identify against Winkelmann's printed:

`Gründliche und Wahrhafte Beschreibung der Fürstentümer Hessen und Hersfeld`, Bremen 1697.

Important examples:

### ID 11623

`LH 12,1,3 Bl.112`

Date in catalogue:

`1694` with uncertainty marker `?`.

Ordering mark:

`Nostra sub Romanis`.

Opening:

`Ex W. Hassicae Chron. p. 6.`

The note then cites pages 56, 59 and 96, among other material. The catalogue comments that passages in the 1697 print match or help identify Leibniz's notes.

### ID 12220

`LH 12,1,3 Bl.123`.

Date in catalogue:

`1694` with uncertainty marker `?`.

Ordering mark:

`Saxones sub Romanis`.

The catalogue identifies the extracted passage with Winkelmann's 1697 print, p.400.

This chronology must be handled carefully.

It is **not safe** to say:

`Leibniz read the 1697 printed book in 1694`.

The evidence instead establishes two separate controls:

1. the Leibniz working papers are assigned by the catalogue/edition to a 1694 historical complex, with explicit uncertainty;
2. their textual content can be identified or paralleled in the later 1697 print.

Winkelmann's Hessian chronicle had a long pre-publication history. Modern scholarship describes the work as the result of decades of revision before printing, and Winkelmann had been commissioned to work on Hessian history long before the 1690s.

Therefore a manuscript/pre-publication source state is historically plausible.

But until correspondence or a surviving manuscript transmission is controlled, the exact source state remains open.

Current label:

`1694-WORKING-PAPER ATTRIBUTION + 1697-PRINT TEXTUAL IDENTIFICATION; PREPUBLICATION SOURCE STATE PLAUSIBLE, NOT YET DIRECTLY PROVED`.

Sources:

- Leibniz-Katalog `Sub Romanis` query;
- H-Soz-Kult report on Winkelmann's Hessian chronicle as a long-revised work before print;
- Kalliope control of Winkelmann–Leibniz correspondence, 14 Oct 1693–16 Nov 1698, LBr 1006.

## 6. The correspondence window makes manuscript transmission testable

Kalliope controls a surviving correspondence complex:

`Briefwechsel Johann Just Winkelmann mit Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz`

Shelfmark:

`LBr. 1006`

Date span:

`14.10.1693–16.11.1698`

Extent:

`36 Blatt`.

The 1694 Akademie volume alone contains multiple exchanges, including:

- Winkelmann → Leibniz, 13 May 1694;
- Leibniz → Winkelmann, 18 June 1694;
- Winkelmann → Leibniz, 25 July 1694;
- Winkelmann → Leibniz, 10 October 1694;
- Leibniz → Winkelmann, second half October 1694;
- Winkelmann → Leibniz, 28 October 1694;
- Winkelmann → Leibniz, 30 December 1694.

The correspondence therefore overlaps exactly with the period assigned to the Hessian extracts.

A direct transmission test is now feasible:

> Did Winkelmann send, lend, quote, describe or promise manuscript sections of his Hessian chronicle to Leibniz before the 1697 print?

This is the decisive source-state question.

## 7. Winkelmann's own programme explains why one source could feed many jurisdictions

Leibniz-Katalog ID **5924**, April 1691, records Winkelmann's projected writings.

Among them are:

- Hessian chorography/topography;
- `Ethographia`;
- `Hierographia`;
- `politographia`;
- histories of extinct illustrious families in Hesse and Saxony;
- other historical-political and regional works.

Winkelmann's source production itself was therefore cross-domain.

This matters because routing the same author into:

`Insignia`

`Geographica`

`Linguae | Geographica`

`Antiquissimi habitatores`

`Nostra / Saxones sub Romanis`

need not imply classificatory confusion.

The source object already bundled topographical, dynastic, ethnographic, linguistic and political-historical matter.

Leibniz's archive disaggregated it by problem.

## 8. The refined architecture: source reservoir vs problem jurisdiction

The previous checkpoints established `jurisdiction by question`.

The Winkelmann case now adds a necessary second layer.

### Layer 1 — source reservoir

A source-generated batch can be extracted and stored together:

`Winkelmann De dynastiis sheet`

or plausibly:

`Winkelmann / Hessian chronicle excerpts kept with Geographica`.

### Layer 2 — problem jurisdiction

Research notes invoke parts of that source reservoir from:

- `Insignia`;
- `Antiquissimi habitatores`;
- `Linguae`;
- `Sub Romanis` problems;
- person/dynasty-specific dossiers.

### Layer 3 — project dossier

Other checkpoints already show materials moving into projects such as:

- `Origines Estenses`;
- Brunswick/Este complexes;
- other dynastic historical work.

The emerging archival model is therefore:

`SOURCE RESERVOIR`
↕
`PROBLEM JURISDICTION`
↕
`PROJECT DOSSIER`.

Cross-references allow movement without requiring every piece of evidence to be copied into every problem file.

This is a major refinement of the earlier routing triangle.

## 9. Why this matters for library classification

Formal classifications appear more stable than the working archive because they compress the routing ecology.

The archive asks:

`where is the source batch?`

`which problem file should call it?`

`which project dossier needs the result?`

The library taxonomy later asks:

`where does this kind of knowledge belong?`

Thus the movement:

`source reservoir → problem jurisdiction → formal category`

is not a simple rise of disciplines.

It is a shift from **retrieval architecture** to **knowledge architecture**.

The `Geographia. Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.` formula becomes especially interesting in this light because `Geographica` can function both as a formal field and, in the working papers, as a destination for source material used by several other problems.

No direct derivation from one archive packet to the formal formula has been demonstrated.

## 10. Why this matters for the frontispiece

The frontispiece spatially separates and coordinates:

`HISTORIA`

`GENEALOGIA`

`HERALDICA`

and their witnesses.

The Winkelmann case shows what this visual stability conceals.

Before knowledge is personified into apparently distinct fields:

- one physical extraction sheet can contain multiple status/dynastic problems;
- one Hessian source reservoir can be invoked from heraldic, antiquarian, linguistic and ethnogenetic files;
- the same source can be redistributed without losing its source-level unity.

The longer transformation can therefore be formulated:

`source packet / reservoir`
→ `problem-oriented routing`
→ `taxonomic compression`
→ `visual differentiation and coordination`.

This may be more historically precise than describing the image as simply representing already-established auxiliary sciences.

## 12. Strongest article-level proposition

> **The Winkelmann material exposes a two-layer architecture beneath Leibniz's disciplinary labels. One source stream can be reconstructed materially: two 1690 excerpts from the lost `De dynastiis`, both filed under Insignia, were originally written on the same sheet and only later cut apart. A second Hessian source reservoir is repeatedly retrieved from Geographica by otherwise different problem files. The archive therefore distinguishes source-level storage from problem-level jurisdiction. The same reservoir can feed heraldic, antiquarian, linguistic and ethnogenetic inquiries, while formal classification later compresses these mobile retrieval relations into more stable categories.**

This is currently the strongest controlled mechanism in the repository for moving from source materiality to classificatory architecture.
