## Result

The sixth-part transmission history can now be separated into at least three materially different textual states:

`late authorial sixth-part state around 1699`
≠ `heir-controlled posthumous manuscript reservoir c.1701–1704`
≠ `1754 reconstructed publication state`.

Peter Rabeler's recent synthesis, explicitly citing Thomas Fuchs, states that the sixth part was **largely completed** (`weitgehend abgeschlossen`) when Johann Just Winckelmann died in 1699, but that when the text was finally published in 1754 **a large part of the original manuscript could no longer be found and had to be replaced by texts of the Hanau archivist Johann Adam Bernhard (1688–1771)**.

Rabeler cites:

- Fuchs, *Traditionsstiftung und Erinnerungspolitik* (2002), pp. 20–21, 305;
- Johann Just Winckelmann / Johann Adam Bernhard, sixth part, Kassel 1754.

## 1. `completed at death` and `reconstructed in 1754` are not contradictory

The Staff correspondence already controls that in November 1701 the heirs said the missing sixth book was `achevé` — finished/completed — and demanded a substantial sum to hand it over.

Rabeler/Fuchs now gives the longer source-state control:

- Winkelmann's sixth part was **largely complete at death**;
- by 1754 a **large part of the original manuscript was no longer recoverable**;
- the missing textual mass had to be **replaced by Bernhard's texts**.

Therefore:

`COMPLETION STATUS AT c.1699/1701`
≠ `SURVIVAL STATUS IN 1754`.

A manuscript can be substantially complete and later become dispersed, withheld, lost, fragmented, or otherwise unavailable.

This directly resolves a possible false contradiction in the corpus.

## 2. The 1701–1704 heir chain now has a plausible downstream consequence

The repository already controls:

### 1701 — Nathanael von Staff

- heirs claim the sixth book is finished;
- heirs ask a substantial sum to hand it over;

### 1702 — Leibniz

- Winkelmann's son had come to Hanover concerning his father's affairs;
- the son's `hautes prétensions` were so high that Leibniz expected the matter to be laid aside.

### 1704 — Gerhard von Mastricht

- identifies the son as **Anton Günther Winkelmann**;
- reports `varia Manuscripta` of the late historiographer in the son's possession;

This does **not** establish a direct causal chain:

## 3. Bernhard is not merely a later cataloguing attribution

Rabeler's bibliography explicitly lists the 1754 object as:

`[Bernhard, Johann Adam]: Johann Just Winckelmanns Sechster Theil ...`

and the surrounding discussion states that the absent Winckelmann manuscript had to be replaced by Bernhard's texts.

Independent biographical controls place Johann Adam Bernhard (1688–1771) in precisely the institutional position that makes such intervention plausible:

- after Hesse-Kassel inherited Hanau-Münzenberg in 1736, Landgrave Wilhelm VIII placed Bernhard in charge of the Hanau archive;
- he served as Hessen-Hanau historiographer and archivist;
- he reorganised and worked intensively through the Hanau archival holdings.

This supports the label:

`BERNHARD = EDITORIAL / REPLACEMENT-TEXT PRODUCER IN THE 1754 SIXTH-PART STATE`.

Still unresolved:

- whether Bernhard himself prepared the entire printer's copy;
- whether another Kassel editor mediated his materials;
- how much surviving Winckelmann text vs Bernhard replacement text appears in each section;
- whether Bernhard's additions are marked internally;
- whether the register was his work;
- whether he supplied only Hanau-relevant material or reconstructed broader sections.

## 6. The evidentiary formula itself changes status in 1754

The phrase:

`Aus glaubwürdigen Documenten und Scribenten ordentlich verfasset`

appears in a publication whose underlying text is no longer purely one author's surviving manuscript.

This gives the phrase a new analytical function.

In 1697 it operates as Winkelmann's public evidentiary self-presentation after decades of revision and source criticism.

In 1754 the formula covers a reconstructed text combining:

- surviving Winkelmann material;
- later Bernhard textual replacement / supplementation;
- a new publication/editorial environment.

Therefore the same evidentiary formula can survive across a major change in textual agency.

Working distinction:

`EPISTEMIC / PROJECT FORMULA CONTINUITY`
≠ `TEXTUAL-AUTHORIAL CONTINUITY`.

## 7. A striking Leibniz-side continuity requires direct textual comparison

The Leibniz-Katalog currently notes that a preprint Winkelmann excerpt used by Leibniz aligns with several pages of the **1754 sixth part**, including pp. 10–12, 40–41, 54–56, 59, 69, 96 and probably more.

This is potentially crucial.

It means at least some textual matter accessible to Leibniz before the 1697 publication window may survive through the posthumously reconstructed 1754 text.

But this creates a mixed-state problem:

`LEIBNIZ PREPRINT EXCERPT`
↔ `1754 PRINT PASSAGE`

can identify continuity of particular passages without proving the whole 1754 text is Winkelmann-authored.

Immediate method:

1. map every Leibniz excerpt to the 1754 print;
2. identify passages with strong lexical continuity;
3. compare those sections against any surviving Winkelmann manuscript;
4. treat unmatched 1754 sections as possible Bernhard/editorial candidates, not automatic Bernhard text;
5. search Bernhard manuscripts for phrase-level matches.

This can eventually yield a **textual stratigraphy** of the sixth part.
