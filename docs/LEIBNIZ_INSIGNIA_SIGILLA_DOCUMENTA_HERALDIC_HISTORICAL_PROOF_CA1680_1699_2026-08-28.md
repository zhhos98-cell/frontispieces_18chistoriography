# Leibniz c.1680–1699: `Insignia`, seals, diplomas, and documentary antiquity as heraldic historical proof

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE OPERATIONAL PREHISTORY — LEIBNIZ AUTOGRAPH EXCERPTS CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL MANUSCRIPT CATALOGUE / AKADEMIEAUSGABE LOCATORS; SOURCE-MANUSCRIPT PAGE IMAGES NOT YET DIRECTLY INSPECTED

## Result

A cluster of Leibniz autograph notes establishes a substantially stronger prehistory for the repository's `HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS` problem than a generic history of heraldry as an auxiliary science.

Between material catalogued after 1680, notes associated with work after June 1690, and a dense 1699 extraction campaign from Caspar Sagittarius/Christian Schurzfleisch-type historical materials, Leibniz repeatedly treats heraldic insignia as **historical propositions whose antiquity must be tested against documentary and material carriers**.

The controlled operations include:

- doubting retrospective reconstructions of old Welf arms;
- comparing claims made from old books and Wappenbücher;
- inspecting/recording seals attached to dated diplomas;
- comparing shield and banner figures across successive seals;
- locating documentary carriers in named monastic archives/tabularia;
- rejecting an asserted early lion insignia because there is `nullo ... verae antiquitatis documento`;
- rephrasing the same problem as absence of `antiquitatis testimonium` or `antiquitatis documenta`;
- filing separate autograph slips under `Insignia` and `Genealogica`.

The central working relation is therefore:

`heraldic claim / insignia`
→ `dated seal / sigillum`
→ `diploma / archival carrier`
→ `chronological comparison`
→ `documentary antiquity threshold`
→ `historical acceptance / doubt`.

This is highly relevant to the later frontispiece ecology because it makes Heraldry neither decorative nor merely classificatory. Arms and insignia can function as historically testable signs whose claims depend on material-documentary witnesses.

Working relation label:

`OPERATIONAL HERALDIC SOURCE CRITICISM / DOCUMENTARY-ANTIQUITY CONTROL`.

No direct Leibniz → Weigel/Köhler transmission has been demonstrated.

## 1. An early `Insignia | Arma` notebook extracts and criticizes Welf heraldic traditions

The official Leibniz catalogue records autograph item:

`Ms 23, 47(1) Bl. 143`

catalogue ID `42859`, Akademieausgabe V `1230.132`.

The catalogue gives the chronological control as:

`ca. > 1680`,

i.e. after 1680 rather than a securely dated 1680 composition.

Leibniz's source is Johann Heinrich Hoffmann's manuscript *Ehrenkleinod des von Uhrankunfft Fürstlichen Hertzogen-Hausses Brunswig-Lüneburg*, today Hannover NLB Ms XXIII 39–40.

Leibniz files the excerpt under:

`Insignia ("Insignia | Arma")`.

The note begins with the proposition that coats of arms were initially mutable:

`Wappen anfangs veranderlich`.

It then assembles competing claims about:

- lions as Welf insignia;
- lilies attributed to earlier Welfs;
- Heinrich der Löwe;
- later leopards;
- books and visual genealogical/heraldic authorities.

Crucially, Leibniz inserts his own explicit doubt about one retrospective reconstruction, saying in substance that he places little trust in it and suspects that an allegedly ancient Welf lily device was fabricated backwards from the more recent arms of the kings of France because the Welf ancestor was thought to be of Frankish stock.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH EXCERPT + LEIBNIZ CRITICAL INTERPOLATION CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL CATALOGUE/EDITION`.

This is a direct example of heraldic source criticism:

`claimed old armorial sign`
→ `inspect source genealogy / chronology`
→ `detect possible retrospective projection`
→ `withhold trust`.

## 2. The same note compares heraldic claims with seals and synchrony

The long Hoffmann excerpt does not stop at armorial-book claims.

Leibniz records:

- seals associated with Heinrich der Löwe;
- civic seals;
- the lion in Heinrich's later/smaller seal;
- the lion in the major seal of his son Heinrich around 1200;
- later seals of descendants;
- chronological changes in the use of lions, eagles and other heraldic figures.

He also inserts a synchrony check around Helmold and Heinrich der Löwe.

This matters because the heraldic inquiry already operates through:

`sign`
+
`seal carrier`
+
`datable person/event`
+
`chronological comparison`.

The evidentiary object is not the blazon in isolation.

Status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH OPERATION CONTROLLED`.

## 3. `Insignia` slip: seal evidence is tied to dated diplomas and named archives

A second especially important autograph note is:

`Ms 23, 333 Bl. 132 [4. Notiz]`

catalogue ID `68466`, Akademieausgabe V `4700.117 / 4`.

Leibniz files it under:

`Insignia ("insign")`.

The excerpt records two major seals of Heinrich der Löwe in which an equestrian figure carries banner and shield but neither bears a figure, and contrasts them with a smaller seal showing the lion.

It then links seal forms to documentary carriers:

- a diploma associated with a monastery and a seal;
- a seal hanging from a diploma dated 1191;
- a document preserved in the `Tabularium Walkenredense`;
- a diploma dated to 1200 in which Heinrich, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Saxony, bears a simple lion on both banner and shield.

The key operation is therefore:

`seal iconography`
→ `diploma`
→ `archive/tabularium`
→ `date`
→ `heraldic chronology`.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH EXCERPT CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL CATALOGUE/EDITION`.

This is one of the strongest pre-1700 controls in the repository for heraldic imagery being evaluated through diplomatics/material documentary evidence.

## 4. A parallel Heinrich-der-Löwe note compares three seals as a temporal series

The immediately related item:

`Ms 23, 333 Bl. 132 [5. Notiz]`

catalogue ID `68467`, Akademieausgabe V `4700.117 / 5`, is filed under:

`Henricus Leo ("Henrici Leonis")`.

It compares three seals:

- two larger seals, one representing a younger and one an older Heinrich as mounted figure;
- in both, banner and shield are blank;
- the smaller seal contains a lion and no human/equestrian figure.

This is not a generalized heraldic rule. It is a **serial comparison of object states across a person's documentary seals**.

Status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH OBJECT-SERIES COMPARISON CONTROLLED`.

The methodological implication is important:

> Heraldic history can be reconstructed from variation among dated documentary impressions rather than from later armorial tradition alone.

## 5. A Henninges excerpt already uses a seal attached to a diploma as armorial evidence

Another autograph strip is:

`Ms 23, 47(1) Bl. 98`

catalogue ID `4222`, Akademieausgabe V `1230.090`, catalogued after June 1690 and filed under:

`Insignia ("Insignia")`.

Leibniz extracts from Hieronymus Henninges's *Theatrum Genealogicum* an example concerning Emperor Charles IV and the Walkenried privileges.

The note focuses on a seal attached to a diploma and uses the sequence of imperial seals to discuss when a particular form of the imperial eagle appears.

Thus by this working period the note structure is already:

`genealogical authority / Henninges`
→ `diploma`
→ `seal`
→ `serial historical comparison of insignia`.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH EXCERPT CONTROLLED`.

This is a direct bridge between `Genealogica`, `Insignia` and documentary carriers.

## 6. 1699 Schurzfleisch extraction: asserted antiquity of lion insignia fails for lack of `documentum`

In 1699 Leibniz made numerous autograph extracts from Schurzfleisch's *Disputationes historicae civiles* (Leipzig, 1699), using his own copy now recorded as Hannover NLB `Gc-A 644`.

The most important slip for the present project is:

`Ms 23, 47(1) Bl. 48`

catalogue ID `4496`, Akademieausgabe V `1230.042`.

Leibniz extracts the claim:

`Leonis insignia quaedam referunt ad Henricum superbum ... nullo ... verae antiquitatis documento.`

The evidentiary logic is explicit:

`claim: lion insignia already belong to Heinrich the Proud`
→ `test: documentary evidence of true antiquity`
→ `result: none`.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH EXCERPT CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL CATALOGUE/EDITION`.

This is not a modern reconstruction of Leibniz's heraldic method. The note itself frames the heraldic proposition through documentary antiquity.

## 7. Leibniz repeats the same test in `testimonium` and `documenta` language

Two related autograph slips from the same Schurzfleisch discussion sharpen the vocabulary.

### `Ms 23, 181, 2, 6 Bl. 32`

Leibniz notes that Schurzfleisch denies that it is established by:

`antiquitatis testimonio`

that Heinrich the Proud was already called `Leo`.

### `Ms 23, 181, 2, 6 Bl. 45`

A parallel note states that Schurzfleisch denies it is established:

`ex antiquitatis documentis`

that Heinrich the Proud was already called `Leo`.

These variant extractive formulations create a useful evidentiary equivalence environment:

`verae antiquitatis documentum`
≈ `antiquitatis testimonium`
≈ `antiquitatis documenta`.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH EXCERPT VOCABULARY CONTROLLED`.

This cluster is directly relevant to the repository's larger question of how `testes`, `documenta` and historical truth become operationalized.

The note concerns Schurzfleisch's citation of Methodius/Irenicus as evidence that Saxons already used a lion image in antiquity.

This is significant because the evidentiary question itself is being sorted into Leibniz's historical working archive as `Insignia`.

## 12. Consequence for the 1726 frontispiece

The later programme:

`HISTORIA`
+
`GENEALOGIA`
+
`HERALDICA`
+
`TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`

can now be placed against a controlled prehistory in which genealogy and heraldic insignia were already materially entangled with witnesses of time and proof.

The strongest safe relation is:

> **By the late seventeenth century, at least in Leibniz's working historical practice, heraldic signs were propositions about the past that could be confirmed, dated or rejected through seals, diplomas, archival carriers and other documentary testimony.**

This gives a concrete operational meaning to the later juxtaposition of `HERALDICA` and `TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`.

But the relation remains:

`OPERATIONAL / EVIDENTIARY PREHISTORY`.

Do not claim:

`Leibniz's insignia notes directly caused or supplied the 1726 frontispiece programme`.

No Weigel/Köhler reception edge has yet been demonstrated.

---

## 13. Strongest article-level proposition

> **Heraldry entered historical knowledge not only as a taxonomy of arms but as a testable evidentiary practice. Leibniz's working notes compare claimed ancient insignia against dated seals, diplomas and archive locations; he rejects assertions for lack of `verae antiquitatis documentum`, recasts the same problem through `antiquitatis testimonium` and `documenta`, and files the resulting extracts under `Insignia` and `Genealogica`. The later Historia–Genealogia–Heraldica frontispiece can therefore be situated within a pre-existing world in which arms and genealogies were already coordinated with material witnesses and documentary proof.**

This is one of the strongest currently controlled prehistories for the plate's evidentiary ecology.
