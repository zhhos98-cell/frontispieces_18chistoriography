# Tyroff–Gatterer–Weigel transition, 1753–1762 — 26 August 2026

This pass isolates the unusually dense mid-century production window around Martin Tyroff, the elder Weigel house, Johann Christoph Gatterer, the old Wappenkalender, and the first Gatterer handbooks. It is designed to test—not assume—the possibility that the Clio frontispiece acquired a new plate/state around 1755.

## 1. 1753: Tyroff is actively extending the inherited Wappenbuch stock

Martin Tyroff issued the first supplement to the inherited Siebmacher/Fürst/Helmers/Weigel Wappenbuch in 1753. Later descriptions of the supplement sequence identify this as the first of the Tyroff supplements and retain Köhler's prefatory material. The important point for the present plate-history problem is operational: by 1753 Tyroff was not merely holding old Weigel copperplates; he was actively extending a large heraldic plate corpus.

Control:
- Göttinger Digitalisierungszentrum indexes the 1753 Weigel Wappenbuch/supplement complex under the Nürnberg Tyroff / Weigel / later Raspe publication genealogy.
- Later contemporary/near-contemporary descriptions of the supplement sequence attribute Supplement I to Martin Tyroff.

This makes new engraving, supplement production, plate maintenance and reconfiguration in the Tyroff workshop immediately plausible before 1755.

## 2. 1755 Wappenkalender: complete physical copy with frontispiece

A detailed physical-copy collation in Katalog 37 records the 1755 issue as the **thirty-third** newly enlarged and improved *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender*:

- literal imprint: Nürnberg, Andreas Bieling for `C. Weigels Erben` (printed 1754 for calendar year 1755);
- engraved frontispiece present;
- engraved colour-explanation plate;
- one engraved coin plate;
- **109 full-page engraved armorial plates**;
- 79 numbered double leaves with genealogical tables;
- publisher advertisements;
- described as complete.

Source: Katalog 37, item 35, lines corresponding to the 1755 calendar description in the digitised catalogue.

This is important because a frontispiece is demonstrably present at exactly the point around which a later Gatterer copy records `M. Turoff fec. 1755`.

**Current status:** frontispiece identity remains image-unverified. Do not yet call the 1755 Wappenkalender frontispiece the dated 1755 Tyroff Clio plate.

## 3. 1755 Gatterer, *Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum*

Gatterer's major Nürnberg genealogy appeared in the same year through Johann Joseph Fleischmann. The MDZ/BSB object record is unusually rich:

- one frontispiece;
- one engraved title;
- eighteen engraved plates (some folding);
- multiple illustrators, explicitly including **Martin Tyroff**, alongside Georg Daniel Heumann, Johann Jakob Paul Küffner, Michael Rößler, Johann Jacob Schwarz and Johann Michael Seligmann;
- 886 scans in the BSB/MDZ carrier (`bsb11197769`).

A separate physical-copy catalogue likewise records an engraved frontispiece, engraved title with broad rocaille border, eighteen copperplates, six engraved vignettes and genealogical tables.

Specific later scholarship identifies at least one Holzschuher plate (Table V) as a Martin Tyroff engraving. Thus Tyroff was certainly engraving for Gatterer in 1755.

**Guard:** none of this establishes that Tyroff engraved the Holzschuher frontispiece, still less that it is the Clio composition. The value of this object is production-context evidence: Gatterer and Tyroff are directly linked through engraved historical-genealogical work in the exact year under investigation.

## 4. 1759 Gatterer handbook: Clio is explicitly present under the old Weigel-heirs imprint

The first Gatterer *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik* is still published by the elder Weigel heirs and printed by Andreas Bieling:

`Im Verlag Christoph Weigels, des ältern, seel. Erben. Gedruckt, bey Andreas Bieling. 1759.`

A detailed surviving-copy description explicitly identifies the pretitular copper as:

`HISTORIA GENEALOGIA HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORVM ET VERITATIS`

Other 1759 physical copies independently record an engraved frontispiece.

Therefore the Clio composition demonstrably crosses from the old annual Wappenkalender ecology into Gatterer's handbook **before** the imprint has shifted to Raspe.

## 5. 1761–1762: imprint shifts to Raspe; 1762 frontispiece is dated 1755

By 1761 the handbook imprint is the Raspische Buchhandlung; 1762 is likewise published by the Raspische Handlung.

The Finnish National Library copy of the 1762 handbook records:

- engraved frontispiece;
- signature read by the cataloguer as **`M. Turoff fec. 1755`**;
- numerous armorial illustrations.

This dated signature is now the highest-information unresolved plate-state clue in the mid-century chain.

## 6. Competing hypotheses

### H1 — 1755 re-engraving / second plate of the Clio composition

Possible sequence:

`1726 visible Clio`
→ `1740 BM witness: Martin Tyroff after Gabriel Müller`
→ `1755 Tyroff re-engraving / new plate state`
→ `1759 Gatterer handbook: explicit Clio`
→ `1762 Gatterer handbook: M. Turoff fec. 1755`

This model fits the chronology and the active Tyroff engraving/heraldic workshop of 1753–55.

### H2 — the 1762 dated 1755 frontispiece is a different Tyroff composition

If the 1762 image is not Clio, then the Gatterer series changed frontispieces between the explicit Clio witness of 1759 and the 1762 copy.

That outcome would also be historically important: Gatterer's handbook would have a changing threshold apparatus rather than simple uninterrupted Clio reuse.

### H3 — catalogue date/signature reading requires correction

Image inspection could show that `1755` or the name has been misread, or that the date belongs to another element. This remains possible until the plate itself is inspected.

## 7. Required image test

Compare directly:

1. 1740 British Museum Clio witness (`1910,0906.2`);
2. complete 1755 Wappenkalender frontispiece;
3. 1759 Gatterer pretitular Clio copper;
4. 1762 Finnish National Library frontispiece signed/read `M. Turoff fec. 1755`.

Record:
- composition;
- exact inscriptions;
- signature wording and position;
- plate dimensions;
- frame/border geometry;
- line-level differences, retouching and plate wear;
- placement relative to title page.

Only then may the corpus promote the relation to `same_copperplate_as`, `reengraving_of`, `same_design_new_plate`, or `different_frontispiece`.

## 8. Analytical consequence already secure

Even before the final image test, the production chronology has changed materially. The Gatterer transition is not simply:

`old Weigel calendar → new Raspe handbook`.

It is at minimum:

`elder Weigel/Tyroff plate workshop + active heraldic supplementation (1753)`
→ `1755 calendar frontispiece + Gatterer/Tyroff genealogical engraving activity`
→ `1759 Gatterer handbook still under Weigel heirs, carrying Clio`
→ `1761/62 Raspe handbook, with a 1762 copy carrying a Tyroff frontispiece catalogued as dated 1755`.

The plate history and the publisher-imprint transition therefore overlap rather than coincide cleanly.
