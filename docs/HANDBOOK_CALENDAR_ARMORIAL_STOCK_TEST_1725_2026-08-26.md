# 1725 calendar ↔ *Bequemes Hand-Buch* armorial stock test — 26 August 2026

## Purpose

This note upgrades the old aggregate `85 plates / 85 plates` hypothesis into an object-level comparison programme. It does **not** yet claim identical copperplates.

The relevant question is no longer whether the 1725 Weigel *Wappenkalender* and the undated *Bequemes und zum Zeitungs-Lesen sehr dienliches genealogisches und heraldisches Hand-Buch* merely contain similar kinds of heraldic material. Both now have surviving object-level evidence showing the same physical information architecture:

`named polity/house → engraved arms → directly paired genealogical table`.

The next test is therefore same polity against same polity, image against image, rather than whole-book plate counts.

## 1. Direct handbook image now recovered

The current SB-17124 copy, dated by its seller to c.1725 from the inclusion of Pope Benedict XIII, is described as containing:

- 106 armorial copper engravings;
- about 42 coloured or partly coloured;
- 107 genealogical tables directly associated with the corresponding arms;
- an alphabetical armorial description section;
- a register;
- a terminal list of Christoph Weigel the Elder's copper-illustrated publications.

The seller's gallery now yields an actual internal spread (`sb-17124_6.jpg`). It visibly contains, among others:

- `Churfürstl. Pfaltz-Bayerisches Wappen`;
- `Bischöffl. Augspurgisches Wappen`;
- `Churfürstl. Trierisches Wappen`;
- Schönborn-related arms;
- Speyer and other episcopal arms.

Direct image:
`https://www.konversations-lexikon.de/img/angebote/sb-17124/sb-17124_6.jpg`

Seller record:
`https://www.konversations-lexikon.de/sb-17124/bequemes-und-zum-zeitungs-lesen-sehr-dienliches-genealogisches-und-heraldisches-hand-buch`

This is the first current-pass image-level control of the handbook's internal armorial layout.

## 2. Matching 1725 calendar polity set is independently attested

A detached 1725 Weigel calendar leaf is catalogued as:

`Churfürstl. Pfältzisches Wappen, Bischöffl. Augspurgisches Wappen / Churfürstl. Trierisches Wappen`.

The seller explicitly assigns it to the 1725 *Curieuser Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender*, gives a sheet size of approximately 20 × 24 cm, and reports a printed genealogical Stammtafel on the verso.

Record:
`https://www.buchfreund.de/de/d/p/93011704/pfalz-augsburg-trier-churfuerstl-ffaeltzisches`

Other surviving 1725 detached leaves independently show the same calendar page technology:

- Stablo / Löwenstein;
- Pfalz-Zweibrücken / Pfalz-Sulzbach;
- Konstanz / Passau-Lamberg;
- Hessen-Darmstadt.

These are approximately 20 × 24 cm sheets and are repeatedly described with genealogical tables on the verso.

## 3. What this upgrades

The overlap is now more specific than `same genre` or `same publisher orbit`.

At least the following are directly controlled on both sides:

1. the same class of current ruling polities/houses;
2. named armorial copper engravings;
3. an armorial-image → genealogical-table pairing;
4. overlapping named entries including Pfalz/Augsburg/Trier;
5. Weigel elder-widow / Bieling production context for the handbook and Weigel calendar stock immediately around 1725.

This justifies promoting the research relation from a generic similarity to:

`direct_stock_family_test_candidate`.

It does **not** yet justify:

- `same_copperplate_as`;
- `same_impression_as`;
- `same complete plate stock`;
- dating the SB-17124 copy exactly to 1725;
- identifying the handbook's frontispiece as the Clio plate.

## 4. Decisive next comparison

Compare the handbook gallery's Pfalz-Bayern / Augsburg / Trier material directly against the 1725 calendar leaf for the same polities.

Record:

- exact wording and spelling of captions;
- calligraphic hand / engraved letterforms;
- shield orientation and relative scale;
- mitres, croziers, supporters, helmets and mantling;
- incidental line breaks and small numbering;
- plate border / trimming;
- plate dimensions if recoverable;
- verso Stammtafel typography and line structure;
- scratches, wear, repairs or re-engraving.

A close match in heraldic content alone is insufficient because heraldic designs are conventional. A match in **engraved caption hand + composition + micro-layout + plate defects** would support same-plate reuse.

## 5. Why the copy-state problem remains central

The handbook family is materially unstable across surviving copies:

- Hirsch 1930 lot 72: title copper + 85 armorial plates;
- SB-17124: 106 armorial copper engravings + 107 genealogical tables;
- Honterus: title/frontispiece + 81 armorial plates;
- Babel: title/frontispiece + 60 armorial plates, incomplete;
- Geneva 2013: frontispiece + 105 arms / 107 descriptions;
- Darabanth 2026: original title/frontispiece and title page missing; later owner inserted a foreign frontispiece and calendar leaves.

Therefore even a confirmed same-plate match would demonstrate a **shared plate-stock relation between specified witnesses**, not prove that every copy of the handbook used one fixed calendar-derived stock.

## Working interpretation

The strongest current model is material rather than bibliographic:

`annual calendar stock ↔ extractable armorial/genealogical leaf units ↔ handbook recomposition`

This remains a testable model. The newly recovered handbook interior image makes the test executable at the level of individual armorial leaves.
