# Köhler's five-copy collation of the Helmers armorial — 1734 control, 26 August 2026

## Status

A previously underused passage attributed to Johann David Köhler's **1734 preface** to the Weigel reissue of the Siebmacher/Fürst/Helmers armorial materially strengthens the project's copy-level method. Köhler reports that **five Helmers copies were compared against one another** and that no two agreed in the condition and number of the coats of arms.

The wording is presently controlled through Gustav Adelbert Seyler's later quotation of Köhler, not yet by direct inspection of the 1734 preface page. Treat the substance as **secondary-transmission control of a named primary passage**, below `verified_primary` until the digitised 1734 preface itself is page-checked.

Primary work: *Das grosse und Vollständige anfangs Siebmacherische, hernacher Fürstische und Helmerische, nun aber Weigelische Wappen-Buch*, Nürnberg, Christoph Weigel the Elder's widow / Lorenz Bieling, 1734, with a preface by Johann David Köhler. Public digital carriers exist at BSB/MDZ and Google Books; the Göttingen GDZ also preserves later carriers of the work.

Secondary transmission: Gustav Adelbert Seyler, *Geschichte der Heraldik. Wappenwesen, Wappenkunst, Wappenwissenschaft*, in *J. Siebmachers großes Wappenbuch*, Band A, Nürnberg 1885–1889/1890, p. 549.

## The reported Köhler passage

Seyler quotes Köhler as saying that, on exact comparison (`bey genauer Zusammenhaltung`) of **five Helmers copies**, none agreed with another in the `Beschaffenheit und Anzahl der Wappen`. Köhler then explains the divergence materially: some arms had been removed and corrected replacements inserted; empty shields had been filled; and new arms had been inserted or added in different parts.

The important point is not the exact diplomatic spelling, which still requires the 1734 page. The operational structure is already clear:

`five nominal copies of the same armorial`
→ `exact side-by-side comparison`
→ `different contents / different number of arms`
→ `substitution + correction + filling + insertion + addition`
→ `copy configuration cannot be inferred from title/edition identity alone`.

## Why this matters for the corpus

This is unusually close to the repository's present witness discipline. Köhler is not merely observing bibliographical variation in the abstract. He describes a **comparative inspection procedure across multiple physical exemplars** and connects the resulting discrepancies to interventions in a reusable graphic corpus.

The passage supports four distinctions already central to the project.

### 1. Work/edition identity does not determine copy contents

Five objects belonging to the same Helmers armorial tradition could differ in both the **condition** and **number** of represented arms. Aggregate title-level bibliography is therefore insufficient for reconstructing the actual visual corpus available to a particular reader.

### 2. Plate-stock history is intervention history

Köhler's reported verbs describe a stock that could be actively reconfigured:

- an arm could be removed;
- a corrected arm could be substituted;
- a previously empty shield could be filled;
- a new arm could be inserted;
- further arms could be added elsewhere.

This is stronger than generic `new edition` language. The material corpus is treated as something editable at the level of individual heraldic units.

### 3. Correction and augmentation are materially entangled

The same production ecology supports both **error correction** and **corpus growth**. A changed copy need not represent only a later editorial text-state; it can embody a mixed history of replacement, completion and supplementation.

This gives a useful control for later Wappenkalender and Seyfart/Wappenbuch cases where a numerical plate count by itself cannot identify the state of the apparatus.

### 4. Comparative bibliography is already an actor practice

Most importantly, `genaue Zusammenhaltung` makes comparison itself explicit. Köhler's knowledge of the armorial's history arises from putting several exemplars into relation rather than privileging one nominally authoritative copy.

That is a historical analogue of the repository rule:

`publication record ≠ witness occurrence ≠ copy configuration`.

Do not claim that Köhler possessed a modern bibliographical theory of issue/state. The narrower and safer claim is that **he used multi-copy comparison to diagnose material variation in a serially revised engraved reference work**.

## Relation to the 1723 Wappenkalender and the 1772 publisher retrospective

The passage creates a useful middle control between two existing project nodes.

### 1723

The 1772 publisher retrospective says Köhler's Wappenkalender was undertaken in 1723 as a remedy for problems in older large armorial compilations: small figures, unstable tincture coding, lack of descriptive space and temporal change in arms. That remains retrospective evidence until Köhler's 1723 opening statement is directly controlled.

### 1734

Köhler's own armorial preface, eleven years after the calendar launch, shows him treating the inherited Siebmacher–Fürst–Helmers corpus as a **materially unstable multi-copy object** requiring exact comparison. Whatever the still-unread 1723 programme says, this establishes that copy discrepancy and graphic correction were part of Köhler's own working horizon by 1734.

### 1772

The later publisher genealogy can now be read against an actor-side methodological control rather than standing alone. The connection should remain bounded:

`1772 retrospective: older armorial architecture had technical limits`
+
`1734 Köhler: inherited armorial copies materially disagree and must be collated`
→
`strong evidence that armorial publication was understood as a problem of correction, updating and configuration, not merely decorative reproduction`.

This does **not** prove that the four specific defects listed in 1772 were already the explicit reasons Köhler gave in 1723.

## Consequence for current plate-stock tests

This passage raises the evidentiary bar for several live comparisons in the repository.

- The 1725 calendar/handbook `85/85` plate-count coincidence cannot establish identical stock.
- A recurring annual armorial count cannot establish an unchanged configuration.
- A reissued title cannot be assumed to contain the same individual arms or the same corrected states.
- Copy-specific omissions, substitutions, inserted plates and filled/empty shields are historically plausible production phenomena, not merely modern binding accidents.

For the data model, a useful relationship vocabulary remains:

`removed / substituted / corrected / filled / inserted / added`

with each relation attached, wherever possible, to a named copy or directly controlled publication state.

## Evidence guards

1. The exact Köhler wording is currently known through Seyler's nineteenth-century quotation; direct 1734 page inspection is still needed for quotation-level `verified_primary` status.
2. Do not infer that all five Helmers copies were different editions; Köhler's point is precisely that nominally comparable exemplars could diverge materially, but their exact bibliographical status must be reconstructed from the primary page and surviving copies.
3. `ausgeschlagen` in this context should not be overtranslated into a specific modern printing-shop operation without material control of the relevant leaves/plates.
4. The passage establishes multi-copy collation and copy variation. It does not by itself identify which physical copper matrices were reused, altered or replaced.
5. The 1734 preface belongs to the large Weigel/Siebmacher armorial project; do not silently transfer every observation to the annual Wappenkalender.
