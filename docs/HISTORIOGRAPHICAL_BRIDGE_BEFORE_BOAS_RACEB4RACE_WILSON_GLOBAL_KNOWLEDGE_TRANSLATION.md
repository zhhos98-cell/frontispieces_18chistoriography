# Historiographical bridge — *Before Boas*, RaceB4Race/PCRS, Wilson, and globally distributed knowledge

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: framing note; **not a new empirical closure branch**

## 0. Why this bridge is necessary

The colour work has exposed a larger historiographical problem.

A conventional origin story can make late-eighteenth-century Göttingen appear as a site at which modern Western historiography, ethnology, anthropology and comparative human science were intellectually generated. The primary evidence now makes that picture too self-contained.

The safer distinction is:

`site of synthesis / institutionalisation / inferential authorization`

≠

`exclusive provenance of observations, categories, objects, languages, testimony and local knowledge`.

The relevant problem is therefore not whether Göttingen was important. It was. The problem is what kind of importance it had.

Provisional formulation:

> Göttingen should be studied as a processing and authorization site in a globally distributed knowledge system: a place where heterogeneous observations, reports, objects, vocabularies and local histories were made comparable, causally ranked and authorised to bear historical, ethnological, anthropological and racial meanings.

## 1. Vermeulen: ethnography is already a field-to-study translation problem

Han F. Vermeulen's *Before Boas* is indispensable because it breaks the fiction of an autonomously German or Göttingen genesis.

His reconstruction locates the early ethnographic program in the Russian exploration of Siberia. Gerhard Friedrich Müller, working within the Second Kamchatka Expedition, systematised:

- descriptions of peoples and customs;
- interviews and informant relations;
- language/vocabulary collection;
- maps and drawings;
- object acquisition for the Kunstkamera;
- archival research;
- comparison among peoples.

Vermeulen also records Müller's concern that interpreters could distort information and that Cossack intermediaries were feared by local people. The production of ethnographic evidence was therefore already conditioned by translation, coercion, trust, intermediary position and imperial infrastructure.

The operational chain is not:

`European scholar → observation → science`.

It is closer to:

`local person / community / practice / language / object`
`→ encounter`
`→ informant relation / interpreter / imperial intermediary`
`→ questionnaire / vocabulary / drawing / map / collected object / note`
`→ archive / museum / travel or expedition report`
`→ comparison`
`→ general description of peoples`.

This is already a Wilson-style chain of material and social transformations.

## 2. Vermeulen's separation thesis creates the exact junction this repo studies

Vermeulen insists that eighteenth-century ethnology and physical anthropology largely developed in separate domains:

- ethnology / `Völkerkunde`: peoples and nations; history, geography, languages; broadly `historia civilis`;
- physical anthropology: varieties/races; natural history, medicine, anatomy; broadly `historia naturalis`.

This distinction should be preserved rather than flattened.

But Vermeulen also identifies a small group of scholars who linked the two domains, including:

- Gatterer;
- Meiners;
- Blumenbach;
- Georg Forster;
- Herder and others in adjacent configurations.

This repo is concentrated precisely on these **cross-domain junctions**.

The project therefore need not dispute Vermeulen's field genealogy. It can extend it by asking:

> What operations allowed evidence generated for one domain — travel description, ethnography, geography, natural history, genealogy, morphology — to cross into another domain and acquire new inferential jurisdiction?

This changes the unit of analysis from `discipline` to `operation + evidence carrier + inference target`.

## 3. Meiners 1792 exposes the junction at actor level

The now fully primary-direct colour essay is unusually diagnostic.

Pp.611–612 explicitly ask whether colour can serve as a reliable sign of `Racen und Abkunft`, while simultaneously asking whether observed colour is produced by climate/place or descent.

P.613 then makes the upstream evidence problem explicit:

- travellers use unstable colour vocabularies;
- languages cannot precisely capture innumerable gradations;
- credible observers can contradict one another;
- reported colour changes with the observer's implicit comparison population;
- similar visible effects can arise from different causes, especially climate and mixture.

Thus the race/descent inference is downstream of a globally distributed and unstable report system:

`remote embodied encounter`
`→ colour naming`
`→ travel/report circulation`
`→ source contradiction`
`→ source/comparison-frame discrimination`
`→ causal discrimination`
`→ racial/genealogical inference`.

This matters historiographically because the supposedly metropolitan concept cannot be separated from the non-metropolitan encounters that supplied, destabilised and constrained it.

## 4. The key asymmetry: distributed knowledge production, concentrated inferential jurisdiction

The strongest current formula is:

`distributed production of knowledge ≠ distributed jurisdiction over what that knowledge is allowed to mean`.

Local and non-European actors can supply:

- names;
- linguistic distinctions;
- genealogical traditions;
- environmental knowledge;
- descriptions of bodies and practices;
- objects;
- routes and places;
- testimony;
- comparative terms.

But a later institutional site may control the transformation of those inputs into:

- `people`;
- `nation`;
- `race`;
- `descent`;
- `variety`;
- `civilisation`;
- `historical stage`;
- causal history.

The asymmetry is therefore not adequately described as either autonomous European invention or simple extraction/borrowing.

A better object is **translation plus jurisdiction**.

Suggested fields:

- `knowledge_provenance`;
- `local_category_retained_or_erased`;
- `intermediary_chain`;
- `translation_layer`;
- `capture_medium`;
- `comparison_frame`;
- `institutional_processing_site`;
- `inferential_jurisdiction_holder`;
- `category_shift_after_transfer`;
- `political_or_administrative_reuse`.

## 5. RaceB4Race / Premodern Critical Race Studies changes the periodisation problem

The recent PCRS field is directly relevant, but not because this repo should simply project a modern race concept backward.

Important landmarks include:

- RaceB4Race, founded in 2019 as a cross-institutional community for premodern critical race studies and explicitly designed to centre and support Black, Indigenous and other scholars of colour;
- the 2021 *Literature Compass* special issue `Race Before Race: Premodern Critical Race Studies`;
- Geraldine Heng's *The Invention of Race in the European Middle Ages* (2018) and the subsequent debate over race before modern race terminology;
- Noémie Ndiaye, *Scripts of Blackness* (2022);
- Ndiaye and Lia Markey, eds., *Seeing Race Before Race: Visual Culture and the Racial Matrix in the Premodern World* (2023);
- recent work linking PCRS to book history, print, mapping, performance and Critical Indigenous Studies.

The methodological contribution for this repo is not a yes/no question — `did race already exist?`

It is a shift toward **race-making operations**:

- figuring;
- mapping;
- performing;
- printing;
- collecting;
- naming;
- comparing;
- genealogising;
- spatialising;
- making embodied difference administratively or historically legible.

This is compatible with actor-sensitive chronology because `race-making` can be studied without assuming that every premodern distinction is already the modern race concept.

## 6. Why *Seeing Race Before Race* is particularly close to this project

The 2023 volume/exhibition treats visual culture capaciously: manuscripts, printed books, maps, travel books, objects and works produced by Europeans and Indigenous peoples are analysed as active media of racial formation.

That is close to the frontispieces repo because the central question is likewise not merely representational content.

It is:

> What operation does a material or visual carrier make possible, and what difference becomes newly legible or inferentially usable because of that operation?

Thus a map, table, frontispiece, colour term, skull, drawing, travel description or collected object belongs to the same analytical family when it changes:

- comparability;
- visibility;
- portability;
- memory;
- categorisation;
- evidentiary weight;
- jurisdiction of inference.

## 7. Wilson is the bridge between global knowledge history and race-making history

Georgina Wilson's methodological contribution in the current repo is the recursive question:

> How does a difference become available to history in the first place?

Applied to globally distributed human knowledge, this becomes:

`local material / embodied / linguistic difference`
`→ conditions of perceptual and social availability`
`→ capture medium`
`→ transport / storage`
`→ surrogate / transcription / translation`
`→ comparative legibility`
`→ category`
`→ inference`.

The Wilson move prevents two simplifications at once.

### Against autonomous-intellectual-history simplification

A concept is not generated independently of the carriers and encounters that make its objects available.

### Against simple-source-recovery simplification

Recovering a non-European or local source is not enough. One must reconstruct what happened to its epistemic status during transfer.

The central Wilson-style question is therefore:

> Which material and institutional transformations turned locally situated knowledge into evidence for a supposedly general history of humankind?

## 8. Where this extends *Before Boas*

Vermeulen is strongest on:

- disciplinary and terminological genealogy;
- expeditionary infrastructure;
- fieldwork practices;
- the Russian/German institutional network;
- ethnography/ethnology as studies of peoples;
- the separation and later interaction of ethnology and anthropology.

The repo can extend this by shifting attention to **epistemic transformations inside the network**:

`field knowledge → reportable datum`
`→ transportable datum`
`→ comparable datum`
`→ causal datum`
`→ genealogical/racial/historical datum`.

This makes it possible to ask not merely where ethnology was invented, but:

> At what transfer point did a statement about a particular person, community, body, language or place become authorised as evidence about a people, race or historical order?

## 9. Where PCRS can correct or supplement *Before Boas*

Do not force a polemical opposition. But note the difference of emphasis.

A disciplinary-origin history can still leave indigenous/local actors visible mainly as `informants`, `peoples described`, or `objects of study`.

PCRS and Critical Indigenous Studies make it harder to leave that epistemic hierarchy uninterrogated.

The relevant questions become:

- Which categories originated locally rather than with the metropolitan classifier?
- Which local distinctions survived translation?
- Which were flattened into European equivalence classes?
- Which intermediaries altered testimony?
- Which objects retained meanings unavailable to the collecting institution?
- When did collection or transcription sever a datum from the conditions under which it was warranted?
- When did European comparison create a new claim that no local source itself made?

This is not merely an ethics supplement. It changes the history of method.

## 10. Provisional historiographical thesis

> Modern Göttingen historical and human-scientific method should not be narrated as the autonomous Western invention of procedures subsequently applied to a non-Western world. Its comparative operations were built inside transregional knowledge systems whose observations, languages, objects and testimony were globally distributed and often produced under imperial conditions. Göttingen's distinctive role lay partly in transforming these heterogeneous inputs into portable and comparable evidence and in concentrating jurisdiction over the historical meanings they could bear. The resulting categories — people, descent, race, variety, civilisation, stage — were therefore neither simple European projections nor transparent condensations of local knowledge. They were products of asymmetric translation through material carriers, institutional processing and cross-domain inference.

Short form:

`global encounter`
`→ asymmetric capture`
`→ material/linguistic translation`
`→ institutional comparison`
`→ concentrated inferential jurisdiction`
`→ universalising historical/racial category`.

## 11. Stop rules

- Do not convert every non-European datum into `indigenous knowledge`; identify the actual source relation where possible.
- Do not treat imperial context as proof that every scholarly operation was administratively caused by empire.
- Do not erase Russian, German, local and indigenous distinctions by collapsing them into a binary Europe/non-Europe model.
- Do not infer local epistemic agency merely from the presence of a local informant; reconstruct what contribution is recoverable.
- Do not use PCRS to erase actor terminology or chronology.
- Do not use actor terminology to declare race-making analytically absent before a stabilized modern race concept.
- Keep `knowledge provenance`, `capture`, `translation`, `comparison`, and `inference jurisdiction` analytically separate.
