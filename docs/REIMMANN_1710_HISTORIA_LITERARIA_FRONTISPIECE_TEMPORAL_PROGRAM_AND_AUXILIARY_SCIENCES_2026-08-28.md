# Reimmann 1710: *Historia literaria* frontispiece, temporal programme, and the prehistory of historical auxiliary sciences

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE PREHISTORY / SERIAL-FRONTISPIECE NODE — HOST VOLUME CONTROLLED; ICONOGRAPHY CONTROLLED FROM OPEN-ACCESS MODERN DIRECT ANALYSIS + PUBLIC-DOMAIN IMAGE DERIVATIVE; ORIGINAL INSTITUTIONAL PLATE STILL TO BE INSPECTED; AUXILIARY-SCIENCE TAXONOMY PRIMARY-TITLE CONTROLLED

## Result

Jacob Friedrich Reimmann's multi-part *Versuch einer Einleitung In die Historiam Literariam Insgemein und derer Teutschen insonderheit* (Halle: Renger, 1708–1713) supplies a previously uncoded early-eighteenth-century comparison point for the frontispiece corpus.

The most important image for the present article is the frontispiece to **vol. 3,2 = Tomus IV / the fourth part-volume (1710)**. Daniel Fulda's open-access 2022 analysis identifies it explicitly as the frontispiece to `vol. 3,2`, and the public-domain Wikimedia derivative is likewise catalogued as:

`1710 J. F. Reimmann, Versuch einer Einleitung in die Historiam Literariam, vol.3,2.png`.

Its programme makes **Historia literaria** a torch-bearing guide for the learned world and organizes the scene as a choice between two temporal-epistemic directions:

`Historia literaria + Sapientia + Philologia + Philosophia`
→ `light / ordered cultivation / forward direction`

versus

`Stultitia + Critica literalis + Philosophia scholastica`
→ `clouds / barren ruined terrain / backward direction`.

The frontispiece is therefore a strong prehistory for the repository's concern with images that do not merely decorate historical books but **spatialize the organization, direction and use of knowledge**.

An adjacent 1710 volume, **vol. 3,3 = Tomus V**, is even more important textually: its title explicitly classifies

`Chronologia, Geographia, Genealogia, Heraldica, Numismatica, Antiquaria`

as `Neben-Wissenschafften` belonging to `Historia Civilis`.

This configuration predates the repository's 1726 `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA` composition family.

The safe proposition is therefore:

> **The disciplinary configuration later visualized through Historia, Genealogia, Heraldica and material witnesses already had a printed taxonomic prehistory in Halle by 1710.**

This is a structural prehistory, **not yet a transmission claim** from Reimmann to Weigel/Köhler or to Gatterer.

## 3. Iconographic programme of the 1710 vol. 3,2 frontispiece

Fulda's direct modern analysis provides a precise iconographic reading.

### Forward / enlightened side

- the learned discipline **Historia literaria** appears as a **torch** or torch-bearing guiding force;
- it leads the knowledge of the faculties toward light and a landscape of ordered constructions;
- **Philologia** and **Philosophia** appear as two vigorous horses pulling the learned cart in this direction;
- **Sapientia** governs/steers the productive direction;
- the left side of the composition opens into brighter sky and cultivated, ordered space.

### Backward / barren side

At the back of the cart:

- two donkeys embody **Critica literalis** and **Philosophia scholastica**;
- **Stultitia** drives/spurs them;
- their direction points toward clouded, rocky, barren terrain;
- ruins occupy this side of the image;
- a medieval-looking ruined castle gives the barren landscape a temporal index.

The inscriptions articulate the choice as:

- `ad astra` versus `ad rastra`;
- `Der Weisheit Weg` versus `Der Torheit Steg`.

Thus the plate simultaneously codes:

`truth / wisdom / philology / philosophy / cultivation`

against

`folly / literal criticism / scholastic submission / sterility`.

But it also introduces a diachronic vector:

`ruined authoritative past`
↔ `present learned cart`
↔ `ordered possible future`.

Evidence status:

`SECONDARY-DIRECT ICONOGRAPHIC ANALYSIS; PUBLIC-DOMAIN DERIVATIVE IMAGE IDENTIFIED; ORIGINAL PLATE NOT YET DIRECTLY INSPECTED BY REPOSITORY`.

Do not treat Fulda's interpretive vocabulary as a diplomatic transcription of every plate label until the original scan is checked.

## 4. The plate is more than a generic light/dark emblem

Fulda's strongest claim is not simply that the image uses light as a metaphor for truth.

He argues that the image constructs a **historical direction**:

- ruins and medieval architecture mark the side toward which sterile authority-knowledge would regress;
- the alternative direction points toward ordered, prosperous and cultivated conditions enabled by better knowledge;
- the learned cart occupies the present decision-point between the two.

The image is therefore useful for this repository as a case of:

`knowledge taxonomy`
+
`historical direction`
+
`visualized practical consequence`.

This is structurally close to the Gladov 1717 frontispiece without being compositionally identical.

### Reimmann 1710

`present scholarly apparatus chooses between epistemic-temporal directions`.

### Gladov 1717

`Historia writes on Chronos between a knowable past and uncertain but traversable future`.

Both images make the relation between historical knowledge and temporal orientation visible in early-eighteenth-century Halle-related learned print culture.

Working comparison label:

`PARALLEL HALLE TEMPORAL-EPISTEMIC VISUALIZATION — NO DIRECT IMAGE DERIVATION YET SHOWN`.

## 5. A useful textual-image lock: Reimmann had already described Historia as the first light / torch of learning

Fulda cites Reimmann's first volume (1708) as already using the cognitive term `Auffklärung unsers Verstandes` and describing historia/historia literaria through illumination.

In Fulda's reconstruction, the 1710 frontispiece therefore does not simply borrow a free-floating emblem of light. It develops a textual programme in which Historia / Historia literaria organizes the field of learning and makes further acquisition of knowledge possible.

The safe formulation is:

> **The torch frontispiece externalizes a programme already articulated textually in Reimmann's series: historical knowledge serves as an orienting technology for the acquisition, discrimination and extension of learning.**

Evidence status:

`SECONDARY-DIRECT TEXTUAL CONTROL`; original 1708 pages remain a primary transcription target.

Do not make a corpus-level claim that Reimmann invented the cognitive sense of `Aufklärung`; Fulda places it among very early usages, but broader Begriffsgeschichte control is outside the present checkpoint.

## 6. Tomus V (1710) gives an explicit prehistory of the later auxiliary-science configuration

The title of Reimmann's **vol. 3,3 / Tomus V** is exceptionally important for the repository.

It states that the volume treats:

`Historiæ Civilis und derer dazu gehörigen Neben-Wissenschafften`

and then names those auxiliary/subsidiary sciences:

- `Chronologiæ`;
- `Geographiæ`;
- `Genealogiæ`;
- `Heraldicæ`;
- `Numismaticæ`;
- `Antiquariæ`.

This is a primary bibliographic formulation, not a later historian's reconstruction.

The configuration can therefore be coded:

`Historia Civilis`
→ `Chronologia`
→ `Geographia`
→ `Genealogia`
→ `Heraldica`
→ `Numismatica`
→ `Antiquaria`.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-TITLE DIRECT / INSTITUTIONAL DIGITAL RECORD CONTROLLED`.

## 7. Consequence for the 1726 `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA` composition family

The repo's existing 1726–1765 composition family should now be preceded by a **non-genealogical disciplinary configuration node**.

Before this checkpoint, the image genealogy could too easily suggest that the co-presence of Historia, Genealogia and Heraldica emerged first as a visual programme in the 1726 Type-A plate.

Reimmann changes the baseline.

By 1710 a printed Halle work already groups:

`Historia Civilis`
+
`Chronology`
+
`Geography`
+
`Genealogy`
+
`Heraldry`
+
`Numismatics`
+
`Antiquities`.

Therefore the later plate's personification of `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA` can be investigated against a **pre-existing disciplinary taxonomy** rather than treated as an iconographic invention ex nihilo.

This does **not** prove:

`Reimmann → Weigel/Köhler`.

Nor does it prove:

`Reimmann → Gatterer`.

Current relation:

`DISCIPLINARY CONFIGURATION PREHISTORY / TRANSMISSION UNPROVED`.

## 8. Why Numismatica and Antiquaria matter especially

The Reimmann list is broader than the later named trio.

It binds Genealogy and Heraldry to:

- chronology;
- geography;
- numismatics;
- antiquities.

This is directly relevant to the repository's later Gatterer material because the Göttingen programme increasingly treats historical knowledge as an apparatus coordinated across:

- temporal ordering;
- spatial ordering;
- lineage;
- arms/signs;
- coins;
- antiquities / material remains;
- diplomatics and documentary evidence.

The new research question is therefore not merely:

`Where did the Historia/Genealogia/Heraldica trio come from?`

but:

> **Why did a larger early-eighteenth-century auxiliary-science bundle become selectively compressed into particular personified visual combinations, and how was that compression later reopened by Gatterer's wider evidentiary apparatus?**

This is a strong article-level question because it distinguishes:

`disciplinary taxonomy`
from
`visual selection`
from
`institutional re-expansion`.

## 9. Reimmann's series appears to use multiple distinct frontispiece programmes

Modern scholarship and institutional metadata indicate that Reimmann's six-part work did not rely on one stable frontispiece repeated unchanged.

At minimum:

- vol. 3,2 (1710) has the torch/cart `ad astra / ad rastra` programme controlled above;
- vol. 3,3 (1710) has its own engraved frontispiece according to institutional catalogue metadata;
- vol. 3,4 / Tomus VI (1713) also has an engraved frontispiece;
- the first volume (1708) likewise had a frontispiece.

A 2006 modern study distinguishes these as a serial visual programme, but direct image-by-image primary inspection is still required before all individual motifs are entered as verified corpus facts.

This creates a new high-priority task:

`REIMMANN_FRONTISPIECE_SERIES_CENSUS 1708–1713`.

The correct object of study may be the **sequence of changing plates across the series**, not only the famous 1710 wagon image.

## 10. Secondary-reference conflict and current resolution rule

One modern bibliographic presentation of *Historia literaria* (2007) associates a Reimmann frontispiece with `Drittes Hauptstück`, while later scholarship discussing the well-known torch/cart image explicitly identifies it as `vol. 3,2` / the fourth volume.

Because Reimmann's adjacent volumes each carry frontispieces, generic references such as `Reimmann 1710, Frontispiz` are unsafe.

Current rule:

1. encode the torch/cart plate as `vol.3,2` on the strength of Fulda 2022 + the public-domain image catalogue + institutional host metadata;
2. keep `vol.3,3` as a separate frontispiece-bearing object;
3. do not assign 3,3 iconography from a secondary caption without direct plate control;
4. compare physical/digital witnesses of 3,2 and 3,3 to determine whether any plate reuse occurred across copies/issues.

## 11. Relation to Gatterer

No direct Gatterer citation of Reimmann has yet been found in this sweep.

However, the historiographic context is structurally significant:

`early Historia literaria`
→ `disciplinary/chronological ordering of all learning`
→ `history as an orienting and discriminating apparatus`
→ `auxiliary-science bundle around Historia civilis`

followed later at Göttingen by:

`Gatterer universal history + historical auxiliary sciences + collections + institute + review infrastructures`.

A modern history-of-knowledge study independently describes Göttingen historiography as emerging between *historia literaria* and the rise of disciplines, and points specifically to Gatterer's universal-historical works, Institute and systematic bibliographic practices as part of this transition.

This supports a **genre/infrastructure bridge**, not personal influence.

Working relation:

`REIMMANN / HISTORIA LITERARIA → GATTERER = STRUCTURAL PREHISTORY; DIRECT RECEPTION UNPROVED`.

## 15. Current strongest article-level proposition

> **The visual organization of historical knowledge around Gatterer had a deeper prehistory than the portable Clio composition alone suggests. In Reimmann's Halle *Historia literaria*, a 1710 frontispiece already makes historical learning a torch-bearing technology that directs the whole learned world between rival epistemic futures; in an adjacent 1710 volume, Historia civilis is explicitly surrounded by Chronology, Geography, Genealogy, Heraldry, Numismatics and Antiquities as its auxiliary sciences. The later personified triad of Historia, Genealogia and Heraldica therefore selected from a disciplinary bundle already available in print, while Gatterer's programme would later expand historical coordination again across textual, visual, material and institutional apparatuses.**

The middle relation is structural, not yet genealogical: no direct Reimmann → 1726 plate or Reimmann → Gatterer transmission has been demonstrated.
