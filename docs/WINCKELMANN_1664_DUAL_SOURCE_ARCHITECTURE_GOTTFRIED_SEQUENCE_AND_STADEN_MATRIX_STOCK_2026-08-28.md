# Winckelmann 1664: dual-source architecture — Gottfried/Merian sequencing versus surviving Staden matrix stock

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE VISUAL-GENEALOGY CHECKPOINT — MODERN RECEPTION SCHOLARSHIP STATES THAT WINCKELMANN DID NOT KNOW STADEN'S 1557 ORIGINAL EDITION, FOLLOWED GOTTFRIED IN TEXT AND IMAGE ORDER, AND INSERTED SOME REDISCOVERED ORIGINAL STADEN WOODBLOCKS AT ARBITRARY LOCATIONS. ICONOGRAPHIC/SEQUENTIAL GENEALOGY AND PHYSICAL MATRIX GENEALOGY ARE THEREFORE DIRECTLY DIVERGENT.

## Result

The visual genealogy of Johann Just Winckelmann's 1664 *Der americanischen Neuen Welt Beschreibung* is not adequately described as:

`Staden 1557 → Winckelmann 1664`.

Franz Obermeier's reconstruction of Staden reception gives a materially more complex architecture.

For Winckelmann's Staden section:

- the **text and arrangement** follow Johann Ludwig Gottfried's *Historia antipodum / Newe Welt und Americanische Historien* in text **and image**;
- Winckelmann, according to Obermeier's reading of his own statement, **did not know the original Staden edition**;
- **some** rediscovered original Staden woodblocks were nevertheless physically available and inserted into the 1664 host;
- those old blocks were placed at `beliebigen Stellen` / in a `willkürlichen Anordnung`, so their original text-image relation was not preserved.

Obermeier's wording in the Martius-Staden-Jahrbuch discussion is explicit:

`Text und Anordnung von Stadens Buch folgen bei Winckelmann der Ausgabe von Gottfried in Text und Bild, die Originalausgabe war Winckelmann nach eigenen Angaben nicht bekannt. Einige der von Winckelmann wiederentdeckten Originalholzschnitte Stadens sind beigegeben, der Textbezug tritt in ihrer willkürlichen Anordnung aber hinter der illustrativen Bebilderung zurück.`

A related iconographic study states:

`Winckelmann standen auch die Platten einiger Holzschnitte der editio princeps Stadens zur Verfügung, die er in seinem Text an beliebigen Stellen eingefügt hat.`

Sources:

- Martius-Staden-Jahrbuch, Obermeier discussion, searchable lines around pp.47–48 of the article: https://www.yumpu.com/de/document/view/9269431/martius-staden-jahrbuch
- Franz Obermeier, *Brasilien in Illustrationen des 16. Jahrhunderts*, searchable public text: https://dokumen.pub/brasilien-in-illustrationen-des-16-jahrhunderts-9783954879748.html

This yields a direct repository-level distinction:

`TEXTUAL / ICONOGRAPHIC MODEL`
≠ `PHYSICAL MATRIX SOURCE`.

---

## 1. Two genealogies coexist inside one publication

### A. Programme / sequencing genealogy

`STADEN MATERIAL`
→ later reception / Gottfried-Merian organization
→ `WINCKELMANN 1664 TEXT + IMAGE ARRANGEMENT`.

Obermeier specifically says Winckelmann follows Gottfried in both text and image arrangement.

Thus the Staden narrative as encountered by the 1664 reader is mediated through a later seventeenth-century compilation/reception structure rather than reconstructed from the 1557 princeps.

### B. Matrix genealogy

At least some physical woodblocks originate in the 1557 Staden printing stock and survive into Winckelmann's 1664 production environment.

Thus:

`1557 PHYSICAL BLOCK`
→ storage / survival / rediscovery
→ `1664 IMPRESSION`.

The two lineages meet only at the moment of 1664 production.

This is a particularly clean example of:

`composition / arrangement history`
≠ `matrix history`.

---

## 2. Winckelmann can possess an original matrix without possessing the original book

This is the strongest conceptual payoff.

If Obermeier's reconstruction is correct, Winckelmann could have:

- physical access to surviving sixteenth-century image matrices;
- no direct access to the 1557 publication that originally coordinated those matrices with text;
- a later Gottfried/Merian textual/iconographic model for reconstructing the story;
- freedom to insert surviving old blocks at places determined by the new host rather than the old one.

Therefore:

`ACCESS TO ORIGINAL REPRODUCTION TECHNOLOGY`
≠ `ACCESS TO ORIGINAL PUBLICATION CONTEXT`.

An old matrix can outlive the bibliographic/ecological knowledge needed to interpret its original placement.

This is directly relevant to the repository's plate-stock ecology model.

---

## 3. Physical authenticity and contextual authenticity diverge

The same image occurrence can be:

`PHYSICALLY AUTHENTIC`

in the sense of being an impression from a sixteenth-century Staden block,

while simultaneously being:

`CONTEXTUALLY DISPLACED`

because it no longer accompanies the passage it originally illustrated.

Thus matrix authenticity does not preserve:

- chapter assignment;
- sequence;
- narrative referent;
- evidentiary function;
- authorial/eyewitness framing.

Working distinction:

`MATRIX AUTHENTICITY ≠ CONTEXTUAL AUTHENTICITY`.

This is a stronger formulation than generic `image reuse`, because the material witness itself survives while its semantic coordinates are reconstructed from another textual tradition.

---

## 4. Gottfried/Merian and Staden stock operate at different production layers

The 1664 host should therefore be decomposed into at least four layers:

1. **textual mediation** — Staden rewritten/paraphrased through the Gottfried reception tradition;
2. **iconographic sequencing** — image arrangement follows Gottfried rather than the unknown-to-Winckelmann princeps;
3. **matrix stock** — some surviving original Staden woodblocks physically re-enter production;
4. **new/mixed apparatus** — non-Staden images and a separate Columbus threshold plate enter the same host.

The book is thus neither a facsimile nor a simple derivative edition.

It is a recomposition assembled from media whose historical depth differs.

---

## 5. This helps explain the bulk-provenance problem

The JCB/Open Library catalogue currently describes 32 Staden-section images as impressions from original 1557 blocks, whereas Obermeier repeatedly says only `einige` / `ein Teil` of the original block stock survived.

The dual-source architecture shows why a bulk assumption is hazardous.

Because Winckelmann's image sequence itself follows Gottfried/Merian, **composition identity with Staden-related imagery cannot be used as a proxy for block identity**.

For any one 1664 image there are at least four possibilities:

- original Staden block reused;
- later block copying a Staden composition;
- Gottfried/Merian-derived composition represented by another matrix;
- unrelated/non-Staden stock absorbed into the programme.

Therefore matrix promotion must remain item-level.

---

## 6. A broader print-stock ecology becomes visible

Obermeier's surrounding discussion is useful comparatively.

He notes that:

- original Staden woodblocks were carefully retained by the first printer and reused in a same-year 1557 reissue;
- minor irregularities in the blocks were recut;
- more than a century later part of the stock reappeared in Winckelmann;
- de Bry copperplates likewise circulated among heirs, were lost in part through fire, and in some later travel collections surviving original plates coexisted with newly recreated derivative images.

Thus Winckelmann's case belongs to a larger early-modern ecology:

`matrix preservation`
→ `inheritance / storage / loss`
→ `reactivation`
→ `recutting / state change`
→ `new textual hosting`
→ `partial substitution when stock is missing`.

This is very close to the repository's later Weigel/Tyroff/Raspe plate-stock questions, but a century earlier.

The next primary task is therefore to locate **Winckelmann's own statement** in the 1664 text and transcribe it directly.
