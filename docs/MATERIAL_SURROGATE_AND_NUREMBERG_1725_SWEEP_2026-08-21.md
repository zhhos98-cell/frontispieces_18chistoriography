# Material surrogates and Nürnberg 1725 — 21 August 2026

This pass sharpens the corpus around two linked but distinct problems: how antiquarian images functioned as transportable substitutes for objects, and what kinds of object-centred historical work were present in Nürnberg during the unresolved 1725 Clio transition.

It does **not** claim a direct Sagittarius → Schlegel → Eccard → Weigel/Köhler influence chain. The purpose is to record demonstrable operations and documentary edges so that future transmission evidence can be added without retrospectively manufacturing it.

## 1. 1680s: Sagittarius and the double problem of visual surrogates

Martin Mulsow's reconstruction of Christian Schlegel's numismatic work preserves an unusually concrete description of Johann Christfried Sagittarius's earlier bracteate project.

Sagittarius planned a work on roughly **600 bracteates**. According to the report quoted by Mulsow, coins were represented both from **original specimens** and from **drawings received from elsewhere**; **497 images** were converted into woodcuts and printed on **four full sheets**.

Schlegel later criticised the visual mediation itself. He judged the draughtsman insufficiently knowledgeable because names had been omitted and letters were sometimes transposed. He nevertheless inferred a clear scholarly function for the images: Sagittarius had them cut so that he could communicate to other people what his collection contained.

A Gotha witness preserves the later checking stage. Schlegel wrote short identifications beneath the printed coin images:

- Forschungsbibliothek Gotha, **Ch. A 1205**;
- catalogue title reported by Mulsow: *Joan. Christfrid. Sagittarii ectypa numorum cavorum cum perbrevi explicatione Christiani Schlegelii manu adscripta*.

This creates a useful prehistory for the corpus's visual-evidence model:

`original coin / received drawing → woodcut surrogate → remote scholarly circulation → copying error → expert correction and annotation`

The important point is not that this caused the Clio frontispiece. It shows that by the 1680s–1690s antiquarian workers already understood both sides of reproduced evidence: images could make objects mobile, but the act of reproduction could also alter the evidence.

Source: Martin Mulsow, “Die erste Münzgeschichte des mittelalterlichen Sachsen. Christian Schlegels ungedruckte Historiae Saxonicae Antiquioris Veritas ex Nummis Demonstrata von 1697,” *Neues Archiv für sächsische Geschichte* 89 (2018), 159–205, DOI 10.52411/nasg.Bd.89.2018.S.159-205.

## 2. Schlegel: manuscript and plates acquire different documentary lives

Schlegel's *Historiae Saxonicae Antiquioris Veritas ex Nummis Demonstrata* makes the relation between historical claim, material evidence and engraved apparatus programmatic in its title.

The modern reconstruction also matters materially. Working shelfmark information extracted from Mulsow identifies:

- **Ch. B 1739** — rough/draft state;
- **Ch. B 1740** — fair-copy manuscript.

The project envisaged **eight folio copperplate tables** representing more than two hundred coins. The manuscript and its visual apparatus subsequently became separated. Mulsow reconstructs a later documentary life for the plates in a **1723 Salomon Franck** publication context.

This is an unusually strong control for a central corpus rule: a plate can have a history that is not identical with the history of the text or book for which it was first produced.

The shelfmark mapping remains `verified_catalogue` / scholarly reconstruction until checked against the current Gotha manuscript catalogue and, ideally, the manuscripts themselves.

## 3. A bounded transmission edge: Tentzel → Eccard

The corpus now has one more documentary edge that should be kept narrow.

A modern bibliographic reconstruction of Veldeke/Eneas print transmission reports that Eccard's *Historia genealogica principum Saxoniae superioris* (1722), col. 331, prints a group of *Eneas* verses **after Wilhelm Ernst Tentzel's 1702 Supplementum Historiae Gothanae**.

This proves a textual-use relation from Tentzel to Eccard for that material. It does **not** establish that Eccard borrowed Tentzel's numismatic method.

Köhler, independently, treats a 1700 commemorative medal of Tentzel in the 1743 *Historische Münz-Belustigung*. Tentzel therefore becomes a genuine documentary node touching both Eccard and Köhler, but the two edges must remain separately typed.

## 4. 1725 Nürnberg: Eccard turns a cabinet object into historical explanation

A major local comparator surfaced in the exact year of the unresolved Weigel title copper.

Johann Georg von Eccard published in Nürnberg with **Peter Conrad Monath**:

*Erklärung eines alten Kleinodien-Kästleins, aus dem Ebnerischen Cabinete zu Nürnberg, worauf die Verlöbniß Heinrichs Hertzogen von Sachsen und Pfaltzgrafen bey Rhein mit Agnesen Pfältzis. Erb-Princeßin vorgestellet ist: dadurch auch einiger massen die Braunschweig-Lüneburgische Historie, nebst einigen alten dahin gehörigen Müntzen, erläutert wird* (1725).

Stable institutional anchors:

- VD18 **14444186**;
- BSB **4 Bavar. 601**, `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10332488-3`;
- another BSB copy **4 Asc. 954#Beibd.1**, `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10625348-2`.

Institutional metadata gives `[6] Bl., 56 S., [2] Bl.` and copper-engraved illustration. Historical and current copy descriptions sharpen the visual collation:

- Joseph Baer & Co., catalogue 776 (1931), item 683: **3 Kupfertafeln**;
- a current Jeschke/Barnebys description: **3 copperplates, one folding; 1 text copper; a historiated initial, ornamental rule and woodcut vignette**.

The title itself establishes the object relation at catalogue level: an old jewel/casket from the **Ebner cabinet in Nürnberg**, depicting a dynastic betrothal, is used to illuminate Brunswick-Lüneburg history together with related old coins.

This is extremely close operationally to the evidentiary procedure described in Eccard's 1721–22 correspondence, but the present pass has not yet inspected the argument page by page. The safe formulation is therefore: **a 1725 Nürnberg printed comparator in which a cabinet object, coins and multiple engravings are explicitly assembled around dynastic historical explanation**.

No direct production relation to Weigel, Köhler, Müller or Tyroff has yet been found. Monath is a different publisher. The value of the object is chronological and urban: Eccard's material-genealogical practice is physically present in Nürnberg print culture in the same year as the unresolved Weigel title copper.

## 5. Hirsch 1930: the 1725 calendar and handbook become a plate-stock test

Antiquariat Emil Hirsch's catalogue no. 18 (4 Nov. 1930) places two unusually useful objects next to one another:

### Lot 71 — 1725 Weigel Wappenkalender

The historical copy description records:

- `Mit Titelkupfer`;
- twelve portraits;
- approximately one hundred old-coloured arms distributed across **85 copperplates**;
- a colour-explanation copperplate.

This confirms that the 1725 issue had a title copper while leaving its identity unresolved.

### Lot 72 — undated genealogical-heraldic handbook

Immediately following, Hirsch catalogues the Weigel-widow *Genealogisches und heraldisches Hand-Buch* as `(1725)` with:

- `Mit Titelkupfer`;
- **85 engraved armorial plates**.

The coincidence is striking: adjacent catalogue entries, same approximate dating, both with title copper, both with **85 armorial copperplates**.

It is **not** evidence that the books use the same impressions or even the same complete plate stock. It creates a precise testable hypothesis. Direct comparison should record:

1. armorial captions and numbering;
2. plate dimensions and layout;
3. verso Stammtafeln and their typography;
4. ordering and omissions;
5. plate defects, scratches and wear;
6. colour state;
7. title-copper identity.

If substantial same-plate reuse is established, the handbook/calendar relation can be modelled materially rather than inferred from genre labels.

## 6. Seyfart: repeated frontispieces persist after the Gatterer transition

The terminal side of the corpus has also strengthened.

A Venator & Hanstein description of a four-volume Seyfart set, *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch* (Nürnberg: Raspe, **1767–1776**), records:

- **3 repeated engraved frontispieces** (`3 wh. gest. Front.`);
- 567 of 571 armorial plates;
- volume I additionally carrying the title *Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender auf das Jahr 1767 oder jährliches Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik*.

A separate 1771 second-volume copy is described with:

- `(4), 194 Bl.`;
- **Titelkupfer**;
- 193 engraved armorial plates.

This rules out a simple disappearance of the frontispiece apparatus after Gatterer. What remains unresolved is more interesting: what image was repeated, in which three of the four volumes, and whether that repeated design belongs to the Müller–Tyroff Clio family.

No catalogue located in this pass identifies the repeated Seyfart frontispiece as `Historia Genealogia Heraldica`. It remains `identity_unchecked`.

## 7. Revised high-information queue

The most valuable next inspections are now narrowly defined:

1. **1725 BSB/MDZ `bsb10428127`** — extract the opening leaves and identify the title copper.
2. Compare 1725 Wappenkalender armorial plates with the Hirsch-described handbook family, starting with the shared count of 85 plates.
3. Inspect the three/four visual plates in Eccard's 1725 *Kleinodien-Kästlein* and identify engraver/signatures, depicted object views and coin arrangement.
4. Search Monath / Ebner / Eccard Nürnberg networks for any documented contact with Weigel–Köhler, without assuming one.
5. Identify the repeated Seyfart frontispiece across 1767–1776; start with 1767, 1768 and 1771 digital witnesses.
6. Verify Schlegel shelfmarks and the exact Franck 1723 plate reuse from current Gotha/Weimar object records.
7. Keep every actual transmission edge (`Tentzel → Eccard textual use`, `Köhler → Tentzel commemorative notice`) distinct from operational parallels.

The project is increasingly best described as a history of **evidentiary infrastructures and their visual/material reconfiguration**, with the Clio frontispiece serving as one durable threshold object inside that wider system.
