# Blumenbach 1788–1797: modular publication lag — causal integration before the public race gate

Date: 2026-08-27

## Status

This note refines the previous `private uptake → public adoption` model. The chronology now distinguishes multiple separable publication processes:

1. **private uptake of a categorical criterion**;
2. **public reorganisation of causal natural history**;
3. **public discussion of intergenerational propagation and variable constancy**;
4. **public adoption of necessary inheritance as a race/variety persistence gate**.

The key result is that these processes did **not** move together.

## 1. Starting point: the 1788 private working copy

Blumenbach's hand-copy of the third edition of the *Handbuch der Naturgeschichte* contains handwritten notes summarising Kant's distinction between necessarily inherited race characters and non-necessarily inherited varieties.

Current controlled reconstruction:

`common descent compatible → inheritance test → necessary / non-necessary → Race / Varietät`.

This is `private_uptake`, not yet a claim about the wording of the printed 1788 edition.

See:
- `docs/BLUMENBACH_1788_PRIVATE_PERSISTENCE_GATE_AND_PUBLIC_ADOPTION_LAG.md`.

## 2. 1790–1791: public causal integration without public race terminology

Two public-text controls make the lag more precise.

### 1790 *Beyträge zur Naturgeschichte*

Blumenbach publicly organized the human section as the division of humankind into five **Spielarten**, not five races. The first edition's table of contents gives:

- `VI. Die Ausartung der organisirten Körper`;
- `VIII. Ausartung ... des Menschen`;
- `XII. Eintheilung des Menschengeschlechts in fünf Spielarten`.

The terminological pattern is compatible with continued reluctance to stabilize `Race/Rasse` as the public category.

### 1791 fourth edition of the *Handbuch*

The official Blumenbach Online work-level editorial description identifies a real change from editions 1–3: in §§ 13–15, pp.15–20, variations in the operation of the `Bildungstrieb` are now explicitly used to explain altered or new biological forms through `Ausartungen / Degeneration`.

The same Blumenbach Online anthropology guide describes the human section, pp.52–56, as a division into five **Varietäten**.

Norbert Klatt's edition-history discussion supplies the stronger negative terminological control: the term `Race` is absent from the 1791 fourth edition, even though it had appeared once in each of the first three editions. Klatt also notes that Blumenbach's 1790 *Beyträge* uses `fünf Spielarten`.

This produces a more exact sequence:

`1788 private Kantian category note → 1790/1791 public causal reorganisation + Spielart/Varietät language → later public propagation regime → later necessary-inheritance gate`.

## 3. What 1790–1791 does — and does not — show

It is tempting to read the 1791 causal changes as a direct publication of the Kantian notes. That inference is too strong.

The safe claim is narrower:

- Blumenbach privately possessed the Kantian hereditary distinction by 1788;
- by 1790–1791, his published natural history was being reorganized around a more explicit `Bildungstrieb → Ausartung/Degeneration` causal architecture;
- the public human classification nevertheless continued to use `Spielart/Varietät`, and the fourth edition did not publicly stabilize `Race`;
- therefore **causal integration and categorical/terminological adoption were temporally decoupled**.

The causal revision may draw on Blumenbach's own Bildungstrieb trajectory, Buffonian degeneration, Kantian interaction, or several sources at once. Chronological adjacency is not transfer proof.

## 4. 1795: public propagation regime with variable constancy, still not the 1797 gate

The third edition of *De generis humani varietate nativa* (1795) was heavily revised. Blumenbach Online describes it as a major recasting and notes the first use of the familiar names `Caucasia`, `Mongolica`, `Aethiopica`, `Americana`, and `Malaica` for the five **varietates**.

Primary Latin closure now changes the interpretation of this intermediate state.

### §33, p.85

Blumenbach places `degeneratione in varietates` among the principal ways in which the formative drive can deviate from its normal direction. Reproduction/formative continuity and degeneration into varieties therefore belong to the same causal architecture.

### §39, p.106

He asks explicitly whether deformations or mutilations imposed accidentally or deliberately, especially if repeated through long series of generations, can in the course of time become something like a second nature, so that an artificial effect becomes congenital conformation.

Kant's 1785 intervention is cited as an example of the negative side of this dispute.

### §39, p.108

Blumenbach refuses to settle either the affirmative or negative position. But he pressures the negative position by pointing out that other `notae gentilitiae`, including features of the nose, lips and eyebrows, do propagate in families through **more or fewer generations** with **greater or lesser constancy**, as do some organic diseases and defects of speech/pronunciation.

This yields an explicit 1795 model:

`familial / gentilitial character → variable generational propagation → greater / lesser constancy`.

### §40, pp.109ff.

He then warns that causal reconstruction of degeneration is complicated by:
- several causes reinforcing one another;
- causes working in contrary directions;
- different susceptibility and resistance of organic bodies/organs (`pronitas / renisus`);
- different immediate and mediated causal effects.

The continuation of §40 also treats persistence as trait-sensitive: some colour changes persist differently under climatic transfer, while persistent skull forms and eye colours can depend on more mediated and still partly unknown causes.

Therefore 1795 is no longer best coded as merely:

`anthropological recasting + named varietates + degeneration framework`.

It should be coded as:

`public degeneration framework + explicit intergenerational propagation problem + variable constancy + acquired-to-congenital conversion question + causal pluralism + organ/trait-specific susceptibility`.

What remains absent from the controlled 1795 passages is an explicit equivalent of the 1797 rule:

`necessarily inherited through generation → Rasse / lacking that necessity → Spielart`.

The decisive distinction is:

`propagation exists ≠ propagation is necessary ≠ persistence determines category membership`.

## 5. 1797: public categorical adoption

The fifth edition of the *Handbuch* supplies the closure established in the downstream control:

- explicit distinction of `Rassen und Spielarten (varietates)`;
- race-level status tied to necessary inheritance through reproduction;
- explicit credit to Kant's 1788 distinction;
- added direction to Girtanner's 1796 systematic treatment.

## 6. Revised chronology: modular rather than linear adoption

The old linear model:

`private uptake → publication`.

The evidence now warrants a finer modular model:

`public concept availability`
`→ private categorical uptake (1788)`
`→ public causal reorganisation (1790–1791)`
`→ public variable-propagation / causal-pluralism regime (1795)`
`→ explicit public necessary-inheritance race/variety gate (1797)`.

Or, by module:

- `causal architecture`: publicly revised earlier;
- `human grouping terminology`: remains Spielart/Varietät through the intermediate phase;
- `intergenerational propagation`: publicly explicit by 1795;
- `propagation constancy`: explicitly variable in 1795;
- `necessary hereditary persistence as category jurisdiction`: publicly explicit in 1797;
- `Kant/Girtanner attribution for the categorical distinction`: publicly explicit in 1797.

This is better described as **modular publication lag** than as one undifferentiated adoption lag.

The strongest transition is now:

`variable propagation phenomenon → necessary persistence threshold`.

## 8. Consequence for the Meiners comparison

The relevant contrast is no longer:

`Meiners had no persistence tool / Blumenbach later acquired one`.

Nor is it:

`Blumenbach had the whole 1797 system already in 1788`.

The stronger controlled comparison is:

- Blumenbach privately recorded the Kantian necessary-inheritance distinction in 1788;
- Blumenbach publicly reorganized causal natural history by 1790–1791 while still withholding the race terminology/gate;
- Meiners was independently engaging Kant and by 1790 was using descent/hereditary permanence inside a ranked plural causal field;
- Blumenbach in 1795 publicly discussed variable familial propagation, possible acquired-to-congenital conversion, and organ-specific causal susceptibility without yet using necessary inheritance as a category gate;
- Blumenbach in 1797 promoted necessary reproductive persistence into explicit categorical jurisdiction.

The divergence is therefore one of **causal weighting, epistemic caution, persistence strength, and inferential jurisdiction**, not simple chronological access.

## 9. Stop rules

- Do not infer direct Kant → 1791 wording merely from chronology.
- Do not backdate the 1797 public definition into the printed 1788 or 1791 editions.
- Do not treat `Degeneration` as equivalent to the Kantian race criterion.
- Do not treat use of `Varietät` as evidence that heredity was irrelevant.
- Do not treat absence of `Race` in 1791 as absence of causal change.
- Do not describe heredity/propagation as absent from the 1795 third edition.
- Do not equate `propagari` or greater/lesser constancy in 1795 with necessary inheritance.
- Do not turn Blumenbach's open question about inherited artificial modifications into an affirmative doctrine.
- Do not promote the 1795 propagation regime to the 1797 categorical gate.

## 11. Strong current formulation

> Blumenbach's adoption of the Kantian persistence criterion was modular rather than instantaneous. The criterion entered his private working apparatus in 1788. In 1790–1791 he publicly strengthened the causal architecture of degeneration while retaining Spielart/Varietät. By 1795 propagation itself had become an explicit public problem: Blumenbach asked whether repeated artificial changes could become congenital, pointed to familial and gentilitial characters transmitted through more or fewer generations with greater or lesser constancy, and insisted that degeneration involved interacting causes and organ-specific susceptibility. The decisive 1797 change was therefore not the arrival of heredity. It was the promotion of a stronger form of persistence — unavoidable or necessary inheritance through reproduction — from a variable causal phenomenon into a categorical gate separating race from variety. Causal integration, propagation, persistence strength, terminology, category jurisdiction, and attribution moved on different publication clocks.
