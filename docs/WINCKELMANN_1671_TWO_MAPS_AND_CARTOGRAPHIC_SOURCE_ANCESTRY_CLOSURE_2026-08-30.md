# Winckelmann 1671 — two maps and cartographic source ancestry closure

Date: 2026-08-30  
Status: **TWO MAP OBJECTS IDENTIFIED; REGIONAL MAP PRODUCTION LINE CONTROLLED; CITY-PLAN AUTHORSHIP/SOURCE ROLES REMAIN EXPLICITLY SPLIT**

## Result

The two `Kt. (Kupferst.)` in the Landesbibliothek Oldenburg collation can now be resolved as two different spatial objects rather than one undifferentiated map count:

1. a regional map of the counties of Oldenburg and Delmenhorst with Jever and Kniphausen, published in the Winkelmann Chronicle after p.38, produced by **Gerhart Muntinck / Johann Nutzhorn**;
2. the large bird's-eye / plan hybrid **`Eigentliche Abbildung der Hochgräflichen Residenz Statt und Festung Oldenburg`**, separately preserved as a map object and attributed in later specialist cataloguing to **Henrich van Lennep**, while the Oldenburg archival map series also preserves a **Peter Bast** source/draughtsman attribution for the Winkelmann-plan tradition.

This closes the Priority-2 `identify the two maps` task at object level and materially sharpens their source genealogies.

## 2. Regional map — `Tabula Noua Comitatus Oldenburgici et Delmenhorstani...`

Niedersächsisches Landesarchiv, Abt. Oldenburg, gives an unusually strong direct control:

**NLA OL, K-ZE, Best. 298 Z Nr. 1465**

Title:

`Tabula Noua Comit(atus) Oldenburgici et Delmenhorstani, cum Dynastys Jeuerense et Kniphusana`

Archive description explicitly says:

`entnommen aus der Winkelmann-Chronik 1671, nach S. 38`.

Controlled metadata:

- scale: c. 1:210,000;
- sheet: 43.1 × 60.4 cm;
- image with border: 38.4 × 56.9 cm;
- north is left;
- date/state: `1654 (1671)`;
- **Gerhart Muntinck — Vermesser/Zeichner**;
- **Johann Nutzhorn — Kupferstecher**;
- index object: `Winkelmann Chronik`.

Archive route: `NLA OL, K-ZE, Best. 298 Z Nr. 1465`.

### Production ancestry

The archive itself points back to **Best. 298 Z Nr. 3482**, a 1650 copper engraving with the same `Tabula Noua...` title, c. 1:200,000, 37.6 × 57.7 cm, naming Muntinck and Nutzhorn and noting a likely hand-drawn source under **Best. 298 Z Nr. 2643**.

The latter source-control environment is tied to the older survey/map tradition of the counties. The relevant records include:

- `Best. 298 Z Nr. 2643`, *Accurata Descriptio Comitatus Oldenburgici et Delmenhorstani...*, with Muntinck / Musculus-related archival metadata and a manuscript/parchment source in Hannover;
- `NLA HA, Kartensammlung, Nr. 70 OL/3 pg`, c.1648 manuscript map on parchment, scale c. 1:160,000–1:190,000, explicitly cross-referenced in the catalogue to Winckelmann's *Oldenburgische Friedens-*.

The exact personnel labelling in the intermediate archival descriptions is not perfectly uniform: one record's title field names Gerhart Muntinck while its person index also retains Johann Conrad Musculus. Preserve that inconsistency rather than silently harmonizing it.

### Safe production chain

Current controlled sequence:

`court / survey manuscript-map tradition`
→ `c.1648 manuscript/parchment county map`
→ `1650 printed Tabula Noua state (Muntinck/Nutzhorn environment)`
→ `1654 / 1671 Chronicle state, Muntinck draughtsmanship + Nutzhorn engraving`
→ `inserted after p.38 in Winckelmann 1671`.

This is a substantially stronger result than the former generic `2 maps` census.

## 3. City / fortress bird's-eye plan — independent surviving map object

The second map-class object is:

**`Eigentliche Abbildung der Hochgräflichen Residenz Statt und Festung Oldenburg`**.

Landesbibliothek Oldenburg / Stadtmuseum Oldenburg separately catalogues a surviving sheet:

- shelfmark **KP51**;
- date `[um 1670?]`;
- c. **1:3,200**;
- west at top;
- wind rose upper left;
- allegorical title cartouche lower left;
- explanatory legend below;
- digital object: `https://digital.lb-oldenburg.de/lbolmapo/content/titleinfo/1754647`;
- direct 2.48 MB PDF: `https://digital.lb-oldenburg.de/download/pdf/1754647.pdf`.

The surviving sheet is therefore not only a book illustration; it has a separate map-object afterlife.

Specialist sale/catalogue literature identifies the same subject as an anonymous copper engraving from Winckelmann 1671, about 33 × 47 cm, and cites Eckhardt/Elerd/Gäßler for an attribution to **Henrich van Lennep**. It calls the image the first Oldenburg city plan to include the surrounding area.

### Direct image inspection

The separate KP51 digital scan confirms a large west-oriented bird's-eye/plan hybrid with:

- fortified city and castle;
- extensive suburban / garden / water landscape;
- compass rose;
- large allegorical/floral cartouche;
- long numbered legend along the lower margin.

This is materially and functionally distinct from the county `Tabula Noua`.

## 4. Peter Bast versus Henrich van Lennep — do not collapse the roles

The Oldenburg archival map sequence introduces an important provenance complication.

It contains:

- **Best. 298 OL B Nr. 112 M** — Pieter/Peter Bast's 1598 `Wahrhafftige Contrafactur der gräflichen Stadt Oldenburg`, scale c.1:2,200;
- **Best. 298 OL B Nr. 113 M** and **Best. 298 OL A Nr. 113 a** — `Eigentliche Abbildung der hochgräflichen Residenzstadt Oldenburg (nach Winkelmanns Chronik)`, dated 1671–1721, scale c.1:2,700, with **Peter Bast indexed as Zeichner**.

At the same time, specialist print-map literature treats the 1671 Chronicle sheet as anonymous engraving with **Henrich van Lennep attributed** as engraver/producer.

The correct evidentiary model is therefore not:

`Bast OR Lennep`.

Instead preserve:

`earlier/source-plan or cartographic model attribution = Bast tradition`

versus

`1671 engraving / production attribution = Lennep (attributed)`.

Direct plate-signature or production-account evidence would be needed to collapse these roles further.

The 1598 Bast view is a directly attested predecessor: it is signed `Petr. Bast au. et sculp. 1598` and was published in Hamelmann's 1599 *Oldenburgisch Chronicon*. Modern museum scholarship notes that Bast likely depended on earlier local cartographic surveys rather than conducting all measurement himself.

Thus the 1671 city image belongs to a longer local city-plan ecology, not an ex nihilo illustration programme.

## 6. Analytical consequence

The 1671 apparatus no longer looks like a generic decorative copperplate programme. Its spatial layer joins two distinct information infrastructures:

1. **territorial surveying and county cartography**, with identifiable survey/drawing/engraving roles and pre-publication map states;
2. **urban/fortress visualisation**, with a long predecessor-plan ecology and an independent afterlife outside the book.

This strengthens the repository's larger claim:
