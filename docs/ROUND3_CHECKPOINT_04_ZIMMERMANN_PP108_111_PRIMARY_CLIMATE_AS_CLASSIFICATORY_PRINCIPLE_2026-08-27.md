## Evidence basis

User-supplied full-page image pairs of printed pp.108–109 and pp.110–111 were visually inspected. The images preserve the immediate context around Zimmermann's explicit statement about how the human species should be divided into `Racen`.

Secondary locator control independently confirms the exact p.109 sentence and places pp.106ff. inside Zimmermann's survey of attempts by Linné, Blumenbach, Erxleben and Kant to bring human differences into classes or `Hauptracen`. Secondary sources are used only as locator/context control; the p.109 claim below is primary page-image controlled.

## p.109 — decisive classificatory sentence

Zimmermann writes:

> `Es wäre also der Natur der Sache weit mehr angemessen, bey der Abtheilung des Menschen, oder bey seiner Zerfällung in verschiedene Racen mehr auf das Klima Acht zu geben.`

This sentence materially changes the Round 3 model.

`Klima` is not confined here to the role of an external cause that explains bodily differences after races have already been constituted. Zimmermann explicitly introduces climate into the act of **division itself**:

- `Abtheilung des Menschen`;
- `Zerfällung in verschiedene Racen`;
- `mehr auf das Klima Acht zu geben`.

The wording therefore licenses a primary-direct statement that climate has **classificatory jurisdiction**, not merely causal jurisdiction, in Zimmermann's anthropogeography.

## Immediate Blumenbach comparison on p.109

Zimmermann immediately continues:

> `Dies hat allerdings Herr Professor Blumenbach in seiner vorzüglichen Abhandlung über den Menschen zum Theil beobachtet. Er sezt gleichfalls vier verschiedene Hauptracen unsers Geschlechts fest.`

This establishes several points at once:

1. Zimmermann explicitly reads Blumenbach as a relevant comparator for the problem of human racial division;
2. he regards Blumenbach's procedure as only partially (`zum Theil`) conforming to the climate-oriented principle he has just stated;
3. `Race` and `Hauptrace` are explicit actor categories in this classificatory discussion;
4. the passage is therefore not merely a retrospective projection of later race terminology onto Zimmermann.

The exact relation between Zimmermann's own preferred climatic ordering and Blumenbach's four-race scheme still requires reconstruction from the entire pp.106–113 survey, but the existence of the comparison is primary-direct.

## pp.108–109 — colour and the jurisdiction problem

The p.109 conclusion occurs after a run of observations concerning visible bodily difference, including skin colour and its geographical/climatic distribution. Modern scholarship has correctly treated this context as Zimmermann's challenge to attempts to make a simple geographical or phenotypic colour scheme carry too much taxonomic weight.

The primary-page sequence supports the bounded reading:

> visible colour is an important descriptive datum, but Zimmermann does not allow colour alone to determine the natural division of humankind; he redirects the classificatory operation toward climate.

This is stronger and more precise than saying simply that Zimmermann was a climatic determinist. The issue is an evidential jurisdiction problem: **what observable character may authorize a division, and what causal/geographical relation should organize that division?**

## Answer to Round 3 Question 2 — colour jurisdiction

At least one primary locus now resolves the question sufficiently for the Round 3 stop condition.

### Primary-direct result

- bodily/skin colour participates in Zimmermann's description of human variation;
- colour does not function here as a self-sufficient taxonomic essence;
- Zimmermann explicitly recommends climate as the more appropriate principle to attend to when dividing the human species into `Racen`;
- consequently colour is best treated, at this locus, as a **visible variable whose taxonomic meaning is mediated by environmental/geographical explanation**, rather than as an autonomous race-defining property.

This result should remain bounded to the present page cluster until pp.53–59 are collated, because those earlier pages may show additional descriptive or classificatory uses of colour.

## Relation to pp.114–115

Checkpoint 03 established the later causal sequence:

`Vaterland → population growth → family migration → geographic branching → gradual climatic influence → named human forms / Racen / Varietäten`.

The p.109 passage now supplies the classificatory counterpart:

`human difference → problem of division into Racen → climate as preferred organizing principle`.

Together, pp.109 and 114–115 show that Zimmermann uses climate on **both sides of the operation**:

1. as an explanatory mechanism producing variation through time;
2. as a principle to be attended to in organizing the resulting human differences.

This is one of the strongest Round 3 findings so far.

## Relation to pp.31–34

The architecture of the first volume is now clearer:

- pp.31–34: climatic range is posed and measured as an intensive environmental variable; climate and nourishment are proposed among `Nebenursachen` capable of explaining `Ausartungen`;
- p.109: climate is explicitly promoted into the classificatory act of dividing humankind into `Racen`;
- pp.114–115: migration through climatic space supplies a conjectural historical mechanism for deriving later races/varieties.

Thus Zimmermann's climate concept moves through three linked jurisdictions:

`measurement → classification → historical derivation`.

That sequence should become a canonical comparative control for the broader project.

## Preliminary Zimmermann ↔ Meiners boundary

The comparison with the already closed Meiners material can now be sharpened.

### Zimmermann 1778

- warns, in effect, against letting visible difference carry classification by itself;
- proposes `Klima` as a more adequate principle in the `Abtheilung` / `Zerfällung` into races;
- uses `Race`, `Hauptrace`, `Varietät`, `Abartung/Ausartung`, `Menschengattung`, `Nation`, and `Familie` in a mobile vocabulary whose exact hierarchy is not yet stable;
- links classification to a historical-geographical causal model.

### Meiners 1785/1790/1793 — closed control

- explicitly rejects colour as the sole/principal bodily sign;
- retains a more articulated `Stamm / Race / Varietät / Spielart` hierarchy;
- by 1790 explicitly restricts inference from isolated bodily parts and requires better specimen provenance;
- by 1793 retains hierarchy while making selective local use of Kantian race terminology.

### Current boundary hypothesis

The difference is increasingly unlikely to be `environmentalism versus taxonomy` or `climate absent versus present`.

A stronger formulation is:

> Zimmermann already makes climate govern both the production and ordering of human difference, but Meiners later increases the explicit regulation of taxonomic rank and admissible bodily evidence.

This remains a working boundary until Zimmermann pp.53–59 and p.101 are directly collated.

## Historiographical caution

The p.109 sentence should not be paraphrased as though Zimmermann simply replaces phenotype with latitude. `Klima` in the first volume is a physical/environmental category that need not coincide mechanically with geographical latitude. The earlier thermometric pages and Zimmermann's broader physical-climate usage make this distinction important.

Nor should `mehr auf das Klima Acht zu geben` be modernized into a genetic or ecological population concept. It is an eighteenth-century classificatory-causal instruction within natural history.
