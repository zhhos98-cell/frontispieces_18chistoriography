# Final historiography sweep and novelty boundary — 2026-08-27

## Scope

This is the closing historiographical sweep for the current frontispiece/plate-stock corpus. It is deliberately narrower than a general bibliography of eighteenth-century visual culture. The question is:

> Which propositions already have strong prior art, and what remains defensible once the project is recast at the levels of programme, design, physical matrix/state, witness occurrence, host regime and surviving copy?

The result is a substantial narrowing. Several propositions that were useful during discovery should now be treated as established historiographical ground rather than novelty claims.

## 1. Gatterer, visual evidence and paratext: already occupied

André de Melo Araújo's work now sets a very close boundary around generic claims concerning Gatterer, visual evidence and media.

### Direct controls

- André de Melo Araújo, “The First Page of History: Materiality and the Making of a Visual Argument in Eighteenth-Century Historiography” (2020).
- André de Melo Araújo, “Transmediating Historical Artifacts. Johann Christoph Gatterer’s Works on Diplomatics and the Reproduction of Documentary Evidence for Eighteenth-Century Historical Research,” *Espacio, Tiempo y Forma* 35 (2022): 129–156.
- André de Melo Araújo, “Evidentiary Authority as a System: Johann Christoph Gatterer and the Collective Making of Historical Knowledge in the Eighteenth Century,” *Berichte zur Wissenschaftsgeschichte* 48.3 (2025): 203–226. DOI: `10.1002/bewi.2145`.
- André de Melo Araújo, “Epistemologia paratextual. Prova e autoridade no prefácio à Historia genealogica (1755) de Johann Christoph Gatterer” (2026).

The 2025 article explicitly reconstructs Gatterer's 1755 *Historia genealogica* as a verbal/visual/material information system, including manuscript preparation, drawings, engraved plates, surviving objects, reproduction errors, collaborative production and the trading of authoritative editions. It asks how evidence is conveyed and authenticated through media and treats the engraved frontispiece and plates as epistemically active.

### Claims to demote

Do not present as novelty:

- Gatterer used images as historical evidence;
- copperplate reproduction shaped evidentiary authority;
- verbal and visual information formed an evidentiary system;
- historical proof could be altered by reproduction and format;
- front matter/paratext could organise or validate evidence;
- collaborative artistic/editorial production mattered to historical knowledge.

These belong in the historiographical foundation.

## 2. Long-duration frontispiece design genealogies: Palmer closes the generic claim

Russell Palmer, “Robinson Crusoe Portrait Frontispieces: Models, Variants, and Interpretations, 1719–1799,” *Papers of the Bibliographical Society of America* 119.1 (2025): 39–98. DOI: `10.1086/734344`.

Palmer offers a systematic century-scale census of *Robinson Crusoe* portrait frontispieces, identifies four prototypes, follows successive reuses and variants through full editions, abridgments and chapbooks, and asks how booksellers, engravers and readers reinscribed the meanings of pre-existing designs.

This is a direct comparator for the present Type A/B/C work.

### Claims to demote

Do not claim as novelty:

- reconstructing a frontispiece design family across many decades;
- identifying recurrent prototypes and variants across editions/formats;
- arguing that a reused frontispiece acquires different meanings in different hosts;
- treating booksellers/engravers/readers as agents in the reinterpretation of inherited designs.

The present project must operate at a different object resolution and institutional/problematic setting.

## 3. Reused designs and changing media contexts: Jung closes another broad opening

Sandro Jung, *Eighteenth-Century Illustration and Literary Material Culture: Richardson, Thomson, Defoe* (Cambridge University Press, 2023).

Jung studies transmediation and the reuse/adaptation of illustrations in new material contexts. His explicit analytical point is that reused designs can signify differently depending on host medium and context; the same visual design can be redacted, re-embedded and made to participate in a new relational meaning.

### Claims to demote

Do not make the following do novelty work by themselves:

- the same design can change meaning when moved to a new host;
- re-engraving/redaction creates continuity without material identity;
- illustration should be analysed relationally with its material context.

These are established methodological premises.

## 4. Antiquarian copperplate media ecologies: Heringman and Lake are close prior art

Noah Heringman and Crystal B. Lake, “Antiquarian Media Ecologies in the Eighteenth Century,” *Modern Philology* 120.1 (2022): 1–23. DOI: `10.1086/720632`.

Working from *Vetusta Monumenta* (1718–1906), Heringman and Lake explicitly reconstruct the production and circulation of antiquarian copperplate engravings as a media ecology joining images, texts, textures, manuscript debates, field sketches and preparatory drawings. They emphasise the way artifacts enter and transform visual and discursive networks over time.

This matters because the present project also moves beyond a single book toward a network of printed surrogates and historical objects.

### Claims to demote

Do not claim as novelty:

- antiquarian historical knowledge operated through a visual/verbal media ecology;
- copper engravings circulated inside wider networks of sketches, objects and texts;
- reproduced artifacts could generate both critical and interpretive associations over time.

The opening must be more specific than “antiquarian media ecology.”

## 5. Physical printing plates, custody, repair and reprinting: Betti is exceptionally close

Chiara Betti, “Lost Treasures Resurface: The Untold Story of the Society of Antiquaries’ Printing Plates,” *The Antiquaries Journal* 104 (2024): 304–342. DOI: `10.1017/S0003581524000179`.

This is the strongest newly located material comparator in the final sweep. Betti treats 170 surviving historical printing plates as artifacts and reconstructs their provenance, manufacture, bibliographical use and collection history. Particularly relevant points include:

- copperplates passing from George Vertue to his widow after his death;
- the widow continuing to publish impressions;
- later transfer/purchase of plates by the Society of Antiquaries;
- assessment of rust, cleaning and repair before renewed printing;
- reprinting old plates decades after the engraver's death;
- alteration of lettering and numbering;
- explicit need for catalogue fields recording a plate's `printing history` and `alterations`;
- comparison of print states and papers;
- surviving plate surfaces as evidence not recoverable from impressions alone.

### Claims to demote

Do not claim as novelty:

- copperplates have a post-authorial/post-engraver custody history;
- widows/heirs can preserve and commercially exploit plate stock;
- plates can lie dormant and later be cleaned, repaired and reprinted;
- physical plate changes and impression states should be catalogued separately;
- printing surfaces themselves can be historical objects.

This literature strongly validates the repository's matrix/state ontology, but it also means that ontology alone is not the historical novelty.

## 6. One-plate longitudinal microhistory: Bowen and Imhof set a very high bibliographical standard

Karen L. Bowen and Dirk Imhof, “18,257 Impressions from a Plate,” *Print Quarterly* 22.3 (2005): 265–279; and Bowen and Imhof, *Christopher Plantin and Engraved Book Illustrations in Sixteenth-Century Europe* (Cambridge University Press, 2008).

Their Plantin-Moretus work demonstrates what a genuinely plate-level longitudinal history can achieve when the archive survives. A pictorial title plate used from 1601 to 1658 can be followed through dated payments, multiple physical reworkings, visual states and impression counts. The broader book reconstructs production costs, sales and inventories of copperplate stock.

### Consequence for this repository

The methodological gold standard for a `same_matrix_as` claim is therefore much higher than iconographic continuity. Ideally it combines:

`dated archival custody / payments + physical-state comparison + edition chronology + impression evidence`.

The present Nürnberg case may not preserve this density, but the absence of equivalent account books must be stated as an archival limit rather than used to lower the identity threshold.

### Claim to demote

Do not claim that a long-term history of one physical printing plate, including reworking and states, is itself unprecedented.

## 7. Copy augmentation and extra-illustration: mature scholarship already exists

Key controls include Lucy Peltz, *Facing the Text: Extra-Illustration, Print Culture, and Society in Britain, 1769–1840* (Huntington Library Press, 2017), and the Multigraph Collective's chapter “Thickening” in *Interacting with Print* (2018).

This literature treats pasted/tipped-in prints, drawings, maps, letters and other materials as active readerly remaking rather than mere damage or eccentricity. Extra-illustration can reorder and reinterpret a published text and turn the surviving copy into a distinctive artifact.

### Claims to demote

Do not claim as novelty:

- owners materially remake books by adding visual matter;
- copy-specific augmentation can be interpretive;
- the published book can be transformed into a private visual archive or composite artifact.

### What remains useful in the present corpus

The 1747/1750 Wappenkalender copy augmentations remain important because they can be placed inside a **pre-existing serial armorial information architecture** and compared with publisher-issued armorial plates, rather than treated as free-standing examples of extra-illustration. The object is therefore the interaction between private augmentation and an already modular/recombinable publication apparatus.

## 8. Göttingen is not a generic endpoint: its own historiography is already substantial

Three bodies of scholarship delimit the Göttingen landing point.

### Peter N. Miller

*History and Its Objects: Antiquarianism and Material Culture since 1500* (2017), especially “Material Evidence in the History Curriculum in Eighteenth-Century Göttingen,” treats Göttingen as the site where a professionalised historical curriculum systematised material remains as evidence and helped institutionalise the auxiliary sciences.

### Martin Gierl

*Geschichte als präzisierte Wissenschaft. Johann Christoph Gatterer und die Historiographie des 18. Jahrhunderts im ganzen Umfang* (2012), together with Gierl's work on the Institute of Historical Sciences and the Göttingen Society of Sciences, establishes Gatterer's historical programme as an institutional and methodological project rather than simply an author's oeuvre. Gierl explicitly frames the Institute founded in 1764 against broader changes in the organisation of knowledge at Göttingen.

### Fact-based epistemology

Scholarship on Schlözer and Göttingen has likewise shown that `facts`, source criticism and precision became central to the university's late-eighteenth-century disciplinary formation. This supplies the larger controversy within which the present material history must eventually be interpreted.

### Consequence

The endpoint cannot be phrased as “Göttingen discovered material evidence” or “Gatterer made historical method scientific.” The productive question is one of **translation and capture**:

> What happened when an already existing commercial/serial Nürnberg apparatus for organising genealogical, heraldic and historical information entered the orbit of a Göttingen programme that increasingly specified what counted as inspectable, comparable and critical historical evidence?

That is a narrower and stronger bridge.

## 9. The defensible core after the sweep

The strongest remaining project is not a theory of frontispieces and not a generic history of visual evidence. It is a longitudinal history of **infrastructural capture and re-specification** across two partially overlapping worlds.

### A. Pre-Göttingen apparatus

Before Gatterer's Göttingen institutional programme, Nürnberg calendars, handbooks, armorial sheets, genealogical tables, portraits, historical modules, advertisements and separately printed copperplates already constituted a serial/recombinable apparatus. Its operations included:

- annual updating;
- extraction and recombination;
- cross-format reuse;
- publisher-held plate stock;
- repeated threshold programmes;
- object/image/text comparison;
- copy-level augmentation and loss.

This apparatus should not be retrospectively described as already “Göttingen historical science.” Its organisation has to be reconstructed on its own terms.

### B. Capture and re-hosting

Gatterer's Handbuch and related formats do not start from a blank page. The project can now ask how an inherited Nürnberg apparatus was selected, re-hosted and made to serve new functions as Gatterer's programme moved toward Göttingen's historical sciences.

The 1757 Type C → 1759 Type A re-hosting is especially valuable because it prevents a simple stylistic-progress narrative: the new authorial/institutional regime could select an older graphic design from the available repertoire.

### C. Identity hierarchy as method, not novelty

The ontology

`programme -> design -> matrix -> matrix_state -> impression -> host_issue -> copy`

is the method required to see the historical process. It is not the novelty claim by itself. Its value is that it prevents three historiographical collapses:

1. visual continuity into material continuity;
2. publication continuity into plate custody;
3. one surviving copy into an ideal edition.

### D. Strongest historical proposition to test

A defensible proposition is:

> The Göttingen reorganisation of historical knowledge can be studied not only through methodological texts and curricula but through the selective re-use, re-hosting and re-specification of an older Nürnberg material information infrastructure whose plates, tables, armorial sets and serial formats already made historical materials portable, modular and comparable.

This remains a proposition to test, not a result to assume. The corpus is unusually well suited to testing it because the same threshold programme and related apparatus survive across the pre-Gatterer and Gatterer/Raspe publication regimes.

## 10. Claims hierarchy after closure

### Established historiographical ground

- images and engravings can carry evidentiary authority;
- material/visual mediation changes historical knowledge;
- frontispieces have reception and paratextual functions;
- frontispiece prototypes can recur and vary across decades and formats;
- reused designs can change meaning in new hosts;
- copperplates have custody, repair, alteration and reprinting histories;
- readers can materially remake copies through extra-illustration;
- Göttingen institutionalised material evidence and auxiliary historical sciences.

### Project-specific established object findings

- the Historia/Genealogia/Heraldica programme is realised in at least three distinct designs;
- design recurrence is non-linear;
- Type B is directly secure in 1746 and Type C in 1747, fixing the design transition at 1746/1747;
- Type C persists through the final numbered 1757 Wappenkalender;
- a 1759 Gatterer Handbuch re-hosts Type A;
- later copies/configurations diverge materially;
- the Weigel/Tyroff publishing environment has direct contemporary evidence for reusable `Verlagskupfer` as persistent publishing capital.

### Open project-specific claims

- exact 1725 programme-adoption point;
- 1739/1740 Type B image-level entry and matrix chronology;
- Type A early/late physical matrix continuity;
- Type C matrix/state sequence around the `fec. 1755` signature;
- exact custody path of the threshold plates across Weigel/Tyroff/Raspe;
- late 1767–1776 copy-level frontispiece persistence;
- the exact historical relation between the Nürnberg apparatus and Göttingen's later epistemic/institutional re-specification.

## 11. Freeze decision

The broad historiographical sweep is now **frozen**. Further generic searches for “frontispiece,” “visual evidence,” “plate reuse,” “extra-illustration,” or “Gatterer images” are unlikely to alter the present boundary.

Reopen only for:

- a study directly on the Weigel/Köhler/Gatterer/Raspe publication ecology;
- a study directly on the `Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica. Testes temporum et veritatis` frontispiece lineage;
- evidence of the specific Nürnberg-to-Göttingen transfer/reuse mechanism;
- new scholarship after this sweep that directly overlaps the project-specific proposition above.

## 12. Deferred axis: colour

`Colour` is intentionally excluded from this closing sweep so that it does not destabilise the completed ontology/data-cleaning pass. It should be opened as a separate analytical axis after closure, with a narrower question: whether colour functions as an operation of discrimination, comparison, fidelity, ordering or evidentiary control, and how that operation intersects with the later Göttingen historiographical dispute.
