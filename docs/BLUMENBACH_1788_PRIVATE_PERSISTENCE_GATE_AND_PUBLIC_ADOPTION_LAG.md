# Blumenbach 1788: private persistence gate and public-adoption lag

Date: 2026-08-27

## Status

This note closes a chronological falsification question raised by the Girtanner 1796 / Blumenbach 1797 downstream control:

> Was the `necessary inheritance → race` persistence gate only a late-1790s development, or was it already available in Göttingen before Meiners's 1792 colour essay?

Answer: **the criterion was already present in Blumenbach's private working copy in 1788.**

## 1. Facing p.22: necessary inheritance versus variety

In handwritten additions to his copy of the third edition of the *Handbuch der Naturgeschichte* (1788), Blumenbach noted opposite printed p.22 a summary of Kant's 1788 distinction:

- a property compatible with common descent can be either necessarily inherited or not;
- if necessarily inherited, it constitutes the character of `Race`;
- if not necessarily inherited, it belongs to `Varietät`.

Operational reconstruction:

`compatible with common descent → inheritance test → necessary / non-necessary → Race / Varietät`

This is already the persistence gate later published more explicitly in Blumenbach 1797.

## 2. Facing p.60: persistence as a sign of common descent

Opposite the anthropological discussion around printed p.60, Blumenbach added another note describing `Race` as a radical peculiarity that indicates common descent and permits several persistent inherited characters within the same animal species / stem. He again copied the Kantian formula that a race is the class difference among animals of one and the same stem insofar as that difference is unavoidably hereditary.

The same note also records a Forster reference (`G. Forster ad Cook II p. 296`). This juxtaposition is methodologically important: the private working page brings together a formal hereditary criterion and travel/ethnographic observational material rather than treating them as wholly separate worlds.

Do not overinterpret this as agreement with Forster or as a fully stabilized synthesis. It shows co-presence in Blumenbach's working apparatus.

## 3. Chronological consequence

The previous downstream sequence was too late if stated as:

`Meiners 1792 plural causes → Girtanner 1796 persistence gate → Blumenbach 1797 adoption`.

The chronology must now distinguish **private conceptual uptake** from **published categorical adoption**:

`Kant 1788 published criterion → Blumenbach 1788 private hand-copy uptake → Girtanner 1796 extended/systematic treatment → Blumenbach 1797 explicit published race/variety definition and Girtanner citation`.

This does **not** establish that Meiners knew Blumenbach's private notes.

It does establish that the persistence criterion was locally and conceptually available in Göttingen before 1792.

## 4. New methodological distinction: availability, uptake, publication, circulation

The Prange problem already required:

`available apparatus ≠ adopted apparatus`.

The Blumenbach manuscript requires a parallel conceptual distinction:

`concept publicly available ≠ privately taken up ≠ publicly adopted ≠ disciplinarily circulated`.

Proposed fields:
- `concept_availability`
- `private_uptake`
- `working_copy_integration`
- `published_adoption`
- `cross_actor_visibility`
- `cross_disciplinary_uptake`
- `publication_lag`

A private annotation can prove that a scholar was working with a category without proving that contemporaries encountered that category through the scholar's published work.

## 5. Direct comparison with Meiners 1792

The contemporaneous contrast is now sharper.

### Blumenbach private working apparatus, 1788

`common descent → necessary inheritance test → race / variety distinction`

### Meiners colour argument, 1792 (provisional derivative reconstruction)

`observer reports → colour categories → plural causal partition (blood / climate / mixture / physiology) → cross-trait and provenance triangulation → historical inference`

Therefore the difference cannot be explained simply by saying that a hereditary-persistence criterion did not yet exist.

A stronger research problem is:

> Why did co-located scholars authorize different operations on visible human difference even when some of the relevant conceptual tools were already available in the same intellectual environment?

Possible explanatory dimensions to test, not assume:
- disciplinary task;
- evidence carrier;
- private versus public knowledge state;
- source corpus (specimen/autopsy versus traveller report);
- required inference target;
- publication genre;
- interpersonal transfer.

## 6. Persistence gate and evidence architecture

The 1788 hand-copy also prevents an overly simple opposition between specimen/autopsy and abstract theory. Blumenbach's working book is itself a material apparatus in which printed text, marginal annotation, citations to Kant, and references to Forster/travel material are physically co-located.

This yields another Wilson-compatible operation:

`working copy → juxtaposition of sources/categories → private methodological availability → possible later publication`.

The material book does not merely preserve conclusions; it records a transitional epistemic state between reading, classification and publication.

## 7. Stop rules

- `private uptake` does not establish `public adoption`.
- Blumenbach's hand-copy does not establish Meiners's access.
- co-location in Göttingen does not establish transfer.
- a copied Kantian criterion does not prove that Blumenbach consistently applied it in every anthropological judgment in 1788.
- later 1797 publication should not be backdated wholesale to the 1788 printed edition; only the handwritten working-copy state is earlier.

## 9. Strong current formulation

> By 1788, the Kantian persistence gate separating necessarily inherited race-characters from non-necessarily inherited varieties had already entered Blumenbach's private working apparatus in Göttingen. Its presence there before Meiners's 1792 colour essay means that the later divergence cannot be narrated as a simple succession in which hereditary classification had not yet become thinkable. The more precise problem is one of uneven uptake and jurisdiction: a concept may be publicly available and privately appropriated without becoming the operation through which another scholar, genre, or evidence network organizes visible difference.
