## 3. What remains positively established around this boundary

### Eccard → Döderlein is direct

Döderlein's 1729 Nürnberg *Commentatio Historica de Numis Germaniae Mediae* explicitly uses Eccard's 1725 *Erklärung eines alten Kleinodien-Kästleins*, citing p.45 / Tab. III / Fig. XXVI in a coin identification, while also engaging the older Schlegel numismatic tradition.

Thus a printed bridge exists:

`Schlegel / earlier numismatic comparison`
→ `Eccard 1725 numbered engraved figure`
→ `Döderlein 1729 explicit reuse in numismatic judgment`.

### Köhler begins his weekly serial in the same Nürnberg year, but no citation edge is presently demonstrated

Köhler's *Historische Münz-Belustigung* begins in 1729. The chronological and local overlap is important, but it does not authorize:

`Döderlein → Köhler`

or

`Eccard → Köhler`

as direct textual transmission.

The correct relation remains **parallel / overlapping Nürnberg evidentiary practice**, pending new primary evidence.

### Johann Tobias Köhler → Döderlein is a later social/educational edge

Johann David Köhler's son Johann Tobias was educated at Weißenburg under Döderlein and later participated in the continuation/completion of his father's numismatic publication environment.

This later intergenerational connection is real but must not be retrojected into Johann David Köhler's 1729 launch as proof of borrowing.

## 4. Positive control: Köhler does engage earlier numismatic authorities elsewhere

Modern reconstructions of individual Köhler articles show that he can explicitly work through earlier numismatic literature, including figures such as Tentzel and Schlegel, when reconstructing a coin problem.

That makes the absence of an Eccard/Döderlein hit in the present searchable series apparatus analytically useful: a direct predecessor citation is not something we need to assume merely because the methods or local setting overlap.

## 5. Consequence for the article model

The earlier tempting chain:

`Schlegel → Eccard → Döderlein → Köhler → Gatterer`

is too linear for the surviving evidence.

The safer reconstruction is branched:

`Schlegel / late-17th-c. surrogate-numismatic practice`
→ `Eccard 1725`
→ `Döderlein 1729`

alongside

`Weigel/Köhler serial ecology from 1723/1729`
→ `Köhler's own numismatic comparison practices`
→ `Gatterer enters Köhler/Weigel publication, office, collection and plate infrastructures in the 1750s`.

The branches can converge operationally without a demonstrated direct citation chain.

This is preferable to forcing an intellectual genealogy where the material evidence instead shows **parallel infrastructures, shared city/print environments, later social connections, and multi-carrier succession**.
