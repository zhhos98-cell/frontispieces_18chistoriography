# Winkelmann 1687: Marburg `Ms. 309` as a surviving revision-rules and notes witness

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE ITEM-LEVEL CHECKPOINT — A 1687 MARBURG MANUSCRIPT DIRECTLY CATALOGUED AS FRAGMENTS OF RULES AND NOTES MADE BY THE KASSEL/DARMSTADT COMMISSIONERS FOR REVISION OF THE WINCKELMANN CHRONICLE; CONTENT, HANDS, PERSONNEL, AND RELATION TO KASSEL `2° Ms. Hass. 35[1.2` REMAIN OPEN

## Result

The current revision-regime model can now be anchored to a surviving item-level manuscript witness outside the Kassel shelfmark group.

Kalliope's finding aid for the **Universitätsbibliothek Marburg**, collection/finding-aid context `Neuzeitliche Handschriften` → `Hassiaca`, records:

`Ms. 309`

with the title:

> *Fragmente derjenigen Regeln und sich darauf gründende Noten, welche von denen zur Revision der Winckelmannschen Chronik verordnet gewesenen Fürstlich Casselschen und Darmstädtischen Commissarien zur Beobachtung vorgeschrieben und gemacht worden.*

The record dates the manuscript to **1687**, gives an extent of **180 leaves**, identifies the language as **German**, and classifies it as a manuscript work.

Kalliope record identifier:

`eadDE-611-HS-3888458`

Source:

- Kalliope finding aid, Universitätsbibliothek Marburg, `Neuzeitliche Handschriften`, `Ms. 309`: https://www.kalliope-verbund.info/de/findingaid?fa.enum=35&fa.id=DE-611-BF-85145&lastparam=true

This is a major advance because the catalogue title explicitly distinguishes two linked layers:

`Regeln`
→ `sich darauf gründende Noten`.

The surviving witness is therefore not merely evidence that censorship happened. It indicates a review apparatus in which **rules/criteria were prescribed and notes were generated on the basis of those rules**.

Safe working model:

`commission-level review jurisdiction`
→ `explicit review rules / criteria`
→ `rule-grounded notes`
→ `revision pressure / requested textual change`
→ `later textual stabilization`.

This is the strongest item-level control yet recovered for the operational form of the Winckelmann revision regime.

## 2. Relation to the 1686 six-member commission

The repository already controls through Thomas Fuchs's 2002 synthesis that in **1686** a censorship commission sat for several months and consisted of **six members, three Marburg professors and three Gießen professors**, and that it fundamentally revised the Chronicle in the landgraves' interest.

The new Marburg witness is dated **1687** and explicitly names Kassel/Darmstadt commissioners charged with revision of the Chronicle.

This creates a strong chronological and functional adjacency:

`1686 multi-month commission`
→ `1687 surviving rules + rule-grounded notes witness`.

However, the following equality is **not yet established**:

`1686 six-member Marburg/Gießen commission`
=
`the commissioners whose rules/notes survive in Marburg Ms. 309`.

Reasons for restraint:

- Kalliope's short record does not name the commissioners;
- it does not state whether the 1687 manuscript is an original commission file, a contemporary copy, a later compilation of 1687 material, or a fragment extracted from a larger dossier;
- it does not provide internal foliation/itemization, hands, signatures, or provenance history;
- the exact relation to Thomas Fuchs's Kassel `2° Ms. Hass. 35/1` `Zensurakten` remains unverified.

Current status:

`SAME REVISION REGIME = STRONGLY SUPPORTED`

`SAME COMMISSION PERSONNEL = PROBABLE BUT UNVERIFIED`

`SAME PHYSICAL DOSSIER / COPY RELATION = OPEN`.

## 3. Why `Regeln → Noten` matters epistemically

The wording of the catalogue title is unusually valuable for the repository's larger problem.

It does not describe only objections or corrections. It describes **rules prescribed for observation** and **notes grounded upon them**.

That supports an operational architecture of historical criticism:

`explicit norm / rule`
→ `application to textual passage or claim`
→ `note / judgement`
→ `revision request or correction`.

If direct inspection confirms that this structure is internal to the manuscript, the 1686–87 case could move from a general claim about changing standards of historical knowledge to a reconstructable **criterion-by-criterion review procedure**.

Potential fields include:

- documentary/source requirement;
- source proximity / authority;
- chronological consistency;
- dynastic wording;
- confessional wording;
- territorial/jurisdictional claims;
- genealogical accuracy;
- treatment of legends/fables;
- admissibility of inference;
- narrative style / rhetoric;
- citation or documentary apparatus;
- required deletion, addition, substitution, or qualification.

These fields are hypotheses for extraction, not claims about the manuscript's contents before inspection.

Do **not** normalize these into one shelfmark or one repository dossier.

The productive question is now:

`what is the transmission / copy / extraction / division relation between Kassel 35[1.2 and Marburg Ms. 309?`

Possible relations to test:

- independent surviving commission-side witnesses;
- Marburg copy/extract from a Kassel/Darmstadt dossier;
- Kassel file containing originals while Marburg preserves compiled rules/notes;
- later archival redistribution of originally related papers;
- partial overlap without direct copy relation.

All remain open pending direct inspection and catalogue/provenance concordance.

## 8. Repository-level analytical payoff

The frontispiece repository already distinguishes:

`programme identity ≠ composition identity ≠ plate identity ≠ impression/copy identity`.

The Winckelmann case now gains a more exact textual analogue:

`commission identity`
≠ `review rule identity`
≠ `review-note identity`
≠ `textual target identity`
≠ `revision-state identity`
≠ `archival witness identity`.

A stable printed historical work can therefore be treated as the output of a materially distributed revision apparatus whose norms, notes, princely correspondence, authorial revisions, and later print state survive in different repositories and different documentary forms.

That is directly useful for the repository's larger claim that historical coherence is **produced through coordination of distributed witnesses**, not simply reflected by a finished book, image programme, or disciplinary label.
