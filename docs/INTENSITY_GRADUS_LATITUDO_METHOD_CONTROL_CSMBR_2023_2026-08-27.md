## What the CSMBR framework establishes as prior art

The CSMBR programme explicitly frames premodern intensity through **intensio et remissio formarum** and the paired concepts:

- `latitudo` — a range/latitude within which a quality can vary;
- `gradus` — degrees acquired or lost within that range;
- oppositional poles such as hot/cold, perfection/imperfection, up/down;
- visualisation of intensity by diagrams, lines and geometric relations;
- grading of heat, colour, sound, drug powers, purity/impurity and bodily states.

The programme also stresses that grades/shades could operate simultaneously as **quantities and values**, not merely as modern numerical measurements.

Bigotti's 2022 article provides a tighter historiographical control: fourteenth-century `complexio` could become conceptualised, visualised and quantified through intensity schemes, while diagrams also expressed a hierarchical cosmos ordered by ascending degrees of perfection and nobility.

## Analytical discriminator for this repo

The colour-method branch must now keep at least four distinct operations apart:

`colour category / hue identity`
≠ `colour intensity or shade within a quality`
≠ `taxonomic rank or nesting`
≠ `colour used as relational syntax`.

Examples:

1. `Roth / Blau / Schwarz` in a heraldic key = semantic colour categories, not necessarily intensity.
2. lighter/darker, more/less saturated, brighter/duller, or explicit degrees of one quality = possible **intensive gradation**.
3. `Stamm → Race → Varietät → Spielart` = taxonomic nesting; do not redescribe it as intensity merely because it is hierarchical.
4. Gatterer's applied table colours marking political relations/revolutions = relational syntax; the referent need not possess literal chromatic intensity.

## Relevance to current Meiners Round 2

The new primary collation establishes that Meiners's 1785 p.17 and 1793 pp.59–60 preserve a hierarchy of one species → two Stämme → Racen → Varietäten → Spielarten together with rejection of colour as the sole/principal mark of human similarity and difference.

This should **not** automatically be assimilated to intensity theory.

The CSMBR control instead sharpens the next question:

> Does Meiners ever operationalise bodily colour, hardness, strength or another property as an explicit continuous/degreed variable (`more/less`, shade, degree, scale, latitude), rather than as a categorical mark or taxonomic property?

For `Härte / Abhärtung`, the 1785→1793 revision now shows a distinction between acquired hardening and hardness as an Eigenthümlichkeit of Stämme/Racen. This is not itself enough to call `Härte` an intensive variable. Promotion requires actor-level evidence of degree, continuous comparison, scale or graded variation.

## Relevance to Gatterer and colour-method ontology

The CSMBR material is particularly useful as a guard against collapsing:

- sample/hue classification;
- graphical decoding;
- pigment application;
- arbitrary relational colour;
- intensive shade/degree.

A new possible ontology operation is therefore:

`grade_intensity` — order instances by more/less of a quality or by explicitly articulated shades/degrees.

Do not add this to canonical records merely because colours differ. Require evidence that the historical operation is genuinely intensive or scalar.

## Historiographical guard

Do not claim novelty for:

- the existence of premodern theories of intensity;
- `intensio et remissio formarum`;
- `latitudo` / `gradus` as means of ordering variable qualities;
- diagrams used to visualise degrees/intensities;
- colour/shade as part of premodern graded ordering;
- the conjunction of quantitative and evaluative hierarchy in intensity systems.

The project-specific opening remains narrower:

> When eighteenth-century historical and auxiliary-historical practices operationalised colour or bodily properties, were they treating them as categories, intensive continua, evidentiary marks, taxonomic variables, or graphical syntax, and how did translations between these regimes alter what could be compared or inferred?

## Stop rule

`hierarchy ≠ intensity`.

A hierarchy becomes relevant to intensity history only when the source or apparatus orders a **variable quality by degree**, range, shade, more/less participation, or an equivalent intensive relation.
