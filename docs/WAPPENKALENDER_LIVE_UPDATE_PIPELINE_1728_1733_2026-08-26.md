# Wappenkalender as a live-update publication pipeline — 1728 and 1733 direct controls

## Status

Direct inspection of the ULB Düsseldorf 1728 and 1733 Weigel/Köhler Wappenkalender witnesses now exposes an explicit **information-update workflow** rather than merely an annual publication rhythm.

The evidence comes from two layers in each volume:

1. the opening `Geneigter Leser!`, which explains annual revision, information sources and intended use;
2. a terminal correction/update page carrying information that arrived or changed **while the volume was already in press**.

This permits a much stronger model:

`external information network`
→ `annual review/revision of genealogical tables`
→ `printing begins`
→ `new dynastic events continue to arrive during production`
→ `terminal additions/changes patch the already-set apparatus`
→ `next annual cycle begins from a newly revised state`.

The serial is therefore not simply a calendar issued once a year. It is an **open but deadline-bounded updating system**.

## 1. 1728: annual improvement and augmentation are explicit programme language

ULB Düsseldorf vol. 6, host year 1728, opens its `Geneigter Leser!` by saying that the calendar now appears for the sixth time in its ordinary arrangement and at the same time with the again necessary `Verbesserung` and **`alljährigem Vermehrung`**.

The preface then says the attached **108 Stammtafeln** of the presently flourishing imperial, royal, electoral, ducal and princely houses have been:

- `aufs neue ... übersehen` — reviewed anew;
- `nach den neuesten Nachrichten vermehret` — enlarged according to the newest reports/news;
- prepared so that they may continue to be used with `zuverlässiger Gewißheit` and `guter Bequemlichkeit` in **daily newspaper reading** (`beym täglichen Zeitungs-Lesen`).

The key relation is therefore actor-explicit:

`newest reports`
→ `review + augmentation of tables`
→ `reliable certainty + practical convenience`
→ `daily interpretation of current news`.

This sharply qualifies any treatment of the genealogical tables as static dynastic reference matter. Their advertised value depends on being sufficiently current to decode the persons and houses encountered in contemporary newspapers.

## 2. The 1728 preface also explains selective compression

The same page reminds readers that, because the Stammtafeln are abbreviated on account of limited space, their principal attention is directed toward **currently living** high-ranking persons. Deceased names are retained only where indispensable for knowledge of present history.

This is an information-selection rule:

`finite page space`
→ `compress genealogy`
→ `prioritize living actors`
→ `retain dead predecessors only when needed to understand the present`.

The serial therefore optimizes genealogy for **current informational utility**, not for maximal ancestral completeness.

This is a strong parallel to the colour-key architecture already reconstructed in this repository: finite print space is managed by centralization, abbreviation and cross-channel redundancy rather than by trying to reproduce every possible datum everywhere.

## 3. 1728: information continues to arrive while the book is in press

At the end of the 1728 volume ULB indexes and directly displays a separate page headed:

`Unter währenden Druck eingelauffene Zusätze und Verbesserungen.`

The phrase is decisive. These are **additions and corrections received while printing was underway**.

The page gives concrete table-specific patches, including events dated in June and July 1727:

- a marriage assigned to a particular table/page;
- the death of Franz Adalbert von Salzburg on **18 July 1727**;
- a third marriage of the Duke of Eisenach in **1727**;
- the birth of a prince to the Duke of Sachsen-Hildburghausen on **10 June 1727**;
- the death of a Holstein-Gottorf count/bishop at St Petersburg on **1 June 1727**, with his widow identified.

The important fact is not each dynasty but the publishing operation:

`table already composed / positioned`
+
`new event occurs or reaches Nürnberg`
→
`cannot be fully re-integrated into already-set table architecture`
→
`terminal correction points reader back to exact table/page`.

The end matter becomes a **delta layer** over an earlier state of the volume.

## 4. 1733: the serial defines itself as an annual improvement project

The 1733 `Geneigter Leser!` is even more explicit. It recalls that the *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender* originally appeared for the purpose of the **annual augmentation and improvement of the newest genealogy and heraldry** (`zu alljährlicher Vermehrung und Verbesserung der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldic`).

The preface notes that other books serving the same laudable purpose have since appeared and that scholars are working everywhere toward the completeness of these useful and agreeable sciences. It nevertheless invokes the idea that two people doing the same thing do not thereby produce the same result (`Duo cum faciunt idem, non est idem`).

The Wappenkalender is thus self-positioned inside a **competitive update ecology**, not as a singular invention of genealogy/heraldry.

## 5. 1733: a named type of correspondent supplies regional intelligence

The 1733 preface says that the present annual again appears with necessary improvement and augmentation, **especially in several Italian princely houses**, because a `guter Freund` residing in **Rome** had helpfully sent the relevant materials/information.

This directly identifies an external information route:

`Rome-based correspondent`
→ `Italian princely-house intelligence`
→ `Nürnberg editorial operation`
→ `revised annual genealogical tables`.

The preface then says the attached tables were reviewed anew and changed/enlarged from the **newest and most reliable reports** (`neuesten und sichersten Nachrichten`) so that they could be used with `zuverlässiger Gewißheit` and convenience in daily newspaper reading.

This is stronger than a generic statement that editors used correspondence. It ties:

- a geographic information node;
- a specific type of data;
- a revision operation;
- an epistemic quality claim;
- a reader-use case.

## 6. 1733: the in-press delta layer recurs

The 1733 volume ends with a page headed:

`Unter währenden Druck geschehene Veränderungen.`

It records several genealogical changes that occurred while printing was underway, including deaths dated **9, 10 and 11 June/July 1732** and directs the reader to the exact Stammtafel/page locations affected.

The vocabulary changes slightly from 1728:

- 1728: additions and improvements **received** during printing;
- 1733: changes **that occurred** during printing.

Operationally both pages perform the same function: they patch a corpus whose referents continue changing after composition has begun.

## 7. Three clocks operate simultaneously

The two witnesses reveal three distinct temporalities.

### A. Social/dynastic event time

People are born, marry and die on specific dates.

### B. information-arrival time

News reaches the editor through correspondents and other `Nachrichten` channels after variable delay.

### C. print-production time

Tables and pages are composed, printed and become increasingly expensive or impossible to revise in situ.

The terminal corrections exist because these clocks do not align.

A useful formalization is:

`event occurs`
≠
`editor learns event`
≠
`relevant table is still editable`.

Serial publication is one technical response to that misalignment.

## 8. The annual is a rolling database rather than a sequence of closed books

At work level, the mechanism is:

`year n inherited state`
+
`new reports / correspondence`
→ `review and revise tables for year n+1`
→ `print`
→ `late events become terminal delta`
→ `delta + further reports feed next annual state`.

This suggests a more accurate modern analytical analogy: **versioned rolling dataset**, provided the analogy is clearly marked as analytical rather than actor vocabulary.

The annual issue is a snapshot with a built-in patch mechanism, not an epistemically closed endpoint.

## 9. Relation to Spener 1680

Spener's heraldic programme already showed:

`external submissions`
→ `independent checking`
→ `printing of ready material`
→ `printed sheets circulated for correction`
→ `late additions/errors entered in indexes`
→ `information arriving too late deferred to the next part`.

The Wappenkalender evidence now supplies a directly comparable eighteenth-century serial system:

`newest reports / Rome correspondent`
→ `annual table revision`
→ `print production`
→ `events or information during printing`
→ `terminal additions/changes`
→ `next annual revision`.

No direct Spener→Köhler textual dependency is required for the comparison. The stronger project-level point is that **changing genealogical/heraldic knowledge repeatedly forces print systems to create explicit interfaces between incoming information and already-fixed pages**.

## 10. Relation to the 1772 publisher retrospective

The 1772 Weigel/Raspe `Vorbericht` says that coats of arms change and receive augmentations `von Zeit zu Zeit`, and presents successive armorial formats as responses to problems of current coverage, legibility, coding and description.

The 1728/1733 annuals now show the update problem in operation decades earlier and at much finer temporal resolution. The annual apparatus is not merely capable of yearly renewal; it is already **out of date during printing**, which is why terminal delta pages are needed.

This strongly supports the broader claim:

> **Temporal currency is a material constraint on historical reference publishing.**

The book represents moving social objects with a production technology that freezes pages sequentially. Corrections are not accidental marginalia to an otherwise stable product; they are a recurrent consequence of the medium's temporal mismatch.

## 11. News-reading is an explicit design target

Both 1728 and 1733 connect the revised genealogical tables to `täglichen Zeitungs-Lesen`.

This gives the Wappenkalender a functional location between learned reference apparatus and current political information. The tables do not merely preserve dynastic knowledge. They help readers resolve identities and relations encountered in the news.

A compact relation is:

`newspaper reports person/house/event`
→ `reader consults current Stammtafel / armorial apparatus`
→ `dynastic relation becomes interpretable`.

The annual's need for current information follows directly from this use. A genealogical table that is several deaths/marriages behind can mislead the reader precisely where the book claims practical utility.

## 12. Data-model consequences

Add or preserve the following distinctions:

- `event_date`;
- `information_received_date` — when known;
- `print_stage_at_update`;
- `in_press_addition`;
- `in_press_correction`;
- `terminal_delta_to_table`;
- `source_channel` — correspondence / newspaper / court report / other;
- `source_geography`;
- `target_table_or_page`;
- `rolled_forward_next_issue` — only when demonstrated.

Do not store all terminal updates as generic errata. Some are corrections to previously wrong information; others are genuinely **new events occurring after the table state had been fixed**.

The 1728 heading itself combines `Zusätze` and `Verbesserungen`, while 1733 uses `Veränderungen`; those actor distinctions should be retained.

## Evidence guards

1. ULB Düsseldorf provides direct primary images for both opening programme pages and terminal update pages; these are `verified_primary` at copy/page level.
2. The present pass has not identified the individual Rome correspondent. Do not name or socially position him without additional evidence.
3. `neuesten Nachrichten` does not necessarily mean printed newspapers; the Rome example proves that private correspondence is at least one source channel.
4. `zuverlässiger Gewißheit` is the publisher/editor's epistemic claim, not external proof that every table was accurate.
5. The terminal pages contain both newly occurring events and possible corrections. Preserve their semantic distinction rather than calling every item an `error`.
6. A terminal update's presence does not prove it was incorporated into every later issue; roll-forward must be checked longitudinally.
7. Do not modernize `alljährliche Vermehrung` into database language in quotations. `Rolling dataset` is an analytical model only.

2. **Correspondence identification:** search Köhler/Weigel correspondence and prefaces for the Rome `guter Freund`, especially agents supplying Italian princely-house data.

4. **Later persistence:** inspect 1738, 1744, 1748, 1751 and 1755 terminal matter for `Zusätze`, `Verbesserungen`, `Veränderungen`, `Avertissement` or correction lists.
5. **Cross-format comparison:** test whether the undated newspaper-reading Handbuch and later Gatterer Handbuch preserve the same update logic without an annual host.
