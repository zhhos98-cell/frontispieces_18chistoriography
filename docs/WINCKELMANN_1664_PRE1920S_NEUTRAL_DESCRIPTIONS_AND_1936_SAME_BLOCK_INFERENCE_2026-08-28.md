# Winckelmann 1664: pre-1920s neutral descriptions and the 1936 `same blocks` inference

Date: 2026-08-28

Status: BIBLIOGRAPHIC-CLAIM GENEALOGY UPGRADE — THREE PRE-1920S MARKET/BOOK DESCRIPTIONS NOW PROVIDE NEUTRAL VISUAL-APPARATUS CONTROLS, WHILE THE 1936 SABIN/VAIL ENTRY IS DIRECTLY CONTROLLED AS AN EXPLICIT SAME-PHYSICAL-BLOCK CLAIM WHOSE IMMEDIATE EXPLANATORY BRIDGE IS WINCKELMANN'S OWN RESCUE NARRATIVE RATHER THAN AN EXPOSED PAIRWISE MATRIX CONCORDANCE.

## Result

The genealogy of the proposition

`1664 impressions = same physical woodblocks as Staden 1557`

can now be historicized more precisely.

Three independent pre-1920s descriptions of Winckelmann's 1664 volume notice its woodcuts but do not surface a physical-original-block proposition:

1. Bernard Quaritch, catalogue no.260 (July 1870), item 596: `large folded portrait, and several woodcuts in the text`;
2. James Carson Brevoort remainder-library sale catalogue (1890), lot 1326: `Numerous curious woodcuts`; copy wants one preliminary leaf and the last leaf of the index, with a note that a late London catalogue priced the book at £3 8s;
3. an early-twentieth-century antiquarian/auction record surfaced through Rare Book Hub, lot 2445: `With portrait of the author and 24 quaint woodcuts of aboriginal customs, etc.`.

These are negative controls only. Their silence cannot prove that same-block identity was unknown or false. Their value is narrower: in three independent professional descriptions spread across several decades, the visual apparatus is salient enough to describe, but physical identity with Staden's 1557 matrices is not used as descriptive or commercial language.

By contrast, the 1936 Wynkelmann entry in the completed Sabin/Vail *Bibliotheca Americana*, vol.XXIX, no.105680 states explicitly:

`Woodcuts in the text, printed from the same blocks as those used for the first edition of Hans Staden's "Warhaftige Historia," Marpurg, 1557 ...`

It then immediately adds that Wynkelmann says he himself saved the blocks from destruction when they were discovered near Cassel, citing Winckelmann p.135.

Thus by 1936:

`WINCKELMANN AUTHORIAL RESCUE / PROVENANCE NARRATIVE`
→ `BIBLIOGRAPHIC SAME-PHYSICAL-BLOCK PROPOSITION`

is presented as a settled chain.

What the entry does **not** expose is an intervening item-by-item physical concordance based on dimensions, defects, lettering, frame geometry or wear. It also does not cite Wegner at that point.

This matters because the repository's current JCB/Open Library formulation — thirty-two 1664 text images printed from original 1557 blocks — belongs to a bibliographic environment in which physical identity had already been hardened into ordinary descriptive prose.

## Evidence rule

Do not convert the pre-1920s silence into a claim that scholars or collectors did not know about block survival. Do not convert the 1936 certainty into direct physical proof either.

The controlled distinction is:

`PRE-1920S NEUTRAL DESCRIPTIVE LANGUAGE`
≠ `1930s EXPLICIT SAME-BLOCK BIBLIOGRAPHIC LANGUAGE`
≠ `ITEM-LEVEL MATRIX CONCORDANCE`.

## Immediate unresolved hinge

The strongest remaining bibliographic question is now narrower:

`What exactly do Wegner 1925/1927 p.19 and JCB 1931 pp.122–123 say?`

If either explicitly states same physical blocks, the hardening point can be moved earlier than 1932/1936. If they preserve only Winckelmann's rediscovery narrative or speak of original illustrations more loosely, the 1930s Sabin/Vail formulations become more important as a semantic upgrade.
