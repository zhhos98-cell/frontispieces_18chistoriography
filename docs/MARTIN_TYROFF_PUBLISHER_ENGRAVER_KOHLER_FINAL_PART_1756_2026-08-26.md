# Martin Tyroff as publisher/engraver in Köhler's final part, 1756 — 26 August 2026

## Status

**Primary OCR of the bound twenty-second part directly separates printer, current publisher and scholarly preface. The dedication author calls himself the `nunmehriger Verleger` and says that his own work was involved in most of the engraved coins and medals. The dedication signature is badly corrupted in current OCR but begins `Martin ...`; contextual and independent institutional evidence make Martin Tyroff the strong identification. Keep the publisher identity at `strong_primary_identification_pending_signature_image` until the signature page is visually read.**

This note isolates roles that were previously in danger of being compressed into a generic `Weigel/Köhler/Gatterer` publishing environment.

---

## I. Title page: Andreas Bieling is printer

The title page of the twenty-second part reads, in current OCR:

`Johann David Köhlers ... im Jahr 1750 wöchentlich heraus gegebener Historischer Münz-Belustigung ... Zwei und Zwanzigster Theil ...`

and closes with the explicit printer formula:

`Gedruckt bey Andreas Bieling`.

The same title page announces a reliable account of the deceased Professor Köhler's life and writings `an statt der Vorrede`.

Therefore:

`Andreas Bieling = printer`

must be kept separate from the following dedication's `Verleger`.

---

## II. Dedication: the current publisher also claims engraving labour

Immediately after the title page, a dedication to the Gandersheim coadjutrix states that the writer presents the final part of Köhler's `Historische Münzbelustigung` and describes his own contribution in two linked ways.

Current OCR preserves the key phrases well enough to establish the operation:

- `meine ... Arbeit` in relation to the `in Kupfer gestochenen ... verschiedenen Münzen und Medaillen`;
- this work was involved `größtentheils`;
- the same speaker identifies himself as `nunmehriger Verleger`.

The role combination is therefore direct:

`current publisher`
+
`substantial engraving/graphic labour on the numismatic apparatus`.

This is unusually important for the repository. Here the person responsible for bringing the publication to market is not spatially or occupationally distant from the engraved evidentiary apparatus.

---

## III. Signature identification: Martin Tyroff is very strong, but keep the final image check

The dedication closes immediately before Gatterer's separate biographical preface. Current OCR reduces the signature to approximately:

`Martin ...`

followed by a Gandersheim court/service designation that is also badly corrupted.

Several independent controls make **Martin Tyroff** the strong identification:

1. Martin Tyroff is institutionally catalogued as an engraver and publisher in Nürnberg, and British Museum authority records state that through marriage into the Weigel family he ran the family firm until his death.
2. The twenty-second part contains a newly made **1756 portrait of Johann David Köhler by Martin Tyroff**, after Gabriel Müller's 1734 portrait; independent museum records date and attribute this print directly.
3. The text of the same part explicitly names `Herrn Martin Tyoroffs` when praising emblematic figures made by his skilled hand.
4. Tyroff demonstrably had a visual/patronage connection with the Gandersheim court environment; a surviving portrait of Johann Anton Kroll von Freyen, Oberhofmeister of Reichsstift Gandersheim, bears `Martin Tyroff sculp Norimb.`
5. Martin Tyroff had come into possession/control of the older Weigel art-dealing/publishing firm and is independently documented as publisher/print dealer as well as engraver.

Together these controls make a different `Martin` implausible, but the corpus should still distinguish:

`strong contextual identification`
from
`signature image directly read`.

The final promotion therefore waits for one manual scan check of the dedication's closing page.

---

## IV. The dedication and Gatterer's preface are separate paratexts by separate agents

The dedication ends before a new heading:

`Vorrede, in welcher von des seeligen Herrn Prof. Köhlers Leben und Schriften zuverlässige Nachricht mitgetheilet wird`.

Martin Gierl attributes this biographical preface to Johann Christoph Gatterer.

Therefore do not merge:

- **dedication** — current publisher / likely Martin Tyroff;
- **biographical preface** — Gatterer;
- **printer statement** — Andreas Bieling;
- **posthumous continuation of the weekly sequence** — Johann Tobias Köhler also has a documented role.

A clean role table is now possible:

| Agent | Controlled role in the final part |
|---|---|
| Andreas Bieling | printer |
| Martin Tyroff | strong identification as current publisher + graphic/engraving contributor; signature image still pending |
| Johann Tobias Köhler | posthumous continuator of his father's series |
| Johann Christoph Gatterer | six attributed numismatic pieces + biographical preface on Johann David Köhler |
| Johann David Köhler | deceased originating author / serial founder |

This is the most concrete production-network decomposition yet achieved for the transition.

## V. Gatterer explicitly acknowledges nominal serial time ≠ actual publication time

The opening of Gatterer's biographical preface directly discusses the chronological mismatch produced by the delayed completion.

Current OCR makes the logic clear: the historical `Münz-Belustigungen` left incomplete by the deceased author had to be carried forward `bis auf das 1756ste Jahr`; otherwise readers could reasonably be confused that the life and writings of an author who died in **1755** appeared inside a year-volume carrying the heading **1750**.

This is direct historical-actor evidence for a central repository distinction:

`nominal serial date`
≠
`actual completion/publication time`.

The mismatch is not a modern bibliographic reconstruction alone. It was visible enough in 1756 that Gatterer himself felt obliged to explain it to the reader.

This matters well beyond this volume. It strengthens the rule that annual/weekly nominal dates must not automatically be treated as dates of plate production, textual composition, printing, or binding.

---

## VI. Tyroff is named inside the historical argument, not only in paratext

In the Gatterer-attributed no. 50 article concerning the marriage-jubilee medal for Jobst Lazarus König, the text explicitly states that two related printed pieces were ornamented with excellent emblematic figures in their headpieces by the skilled hand of `Herrn Martin Tyoroffs`.

The significance is methodological as well as prosopographical. Tyroff's work is not invisible craft labour reconstructed only from signatures. The scholarly text itself can identify the printmaker as part of the chain through which historical/commemorative objects were made visible.

This creates a rare explicit relation:

`historical object/event`
→ `printed explanatory text`
→ `named graphic producer`.

---

## VII. Relation to the Clio / Gatterer handbook transition

The mid-century chain can now be stated more concretely:

`Martin Tyroff runs the older Weigel firm and works as engraver/publisher`
→ `1753 extends the inherited heraldic plate stock`
→ `1755 participates as engraver in Gatterer's Holzschuher genealogy; Weigel-heirs Wappenkalender continues`
→ `1755 Johann David Köhler dies`
→ `1756 final Köhler Münz-Belustigung is printed by Bieling, posthumously continued by Johann Tobias Köhler, substantially supplied by Gatterer, and presented by a current publisher strongly identifiable as Martin Tyroff who also claims graphic labour`
→ `1759 Gatterer's Handbuch appears under the literal Weigel-heirs imprint, printed by Bieling, with the inherited Clio pretitular plate`
→ `1761/62 imprint shifts to Raspe`.

This substantially reduces the need for an abstract `influence` claim. The relevant continuity is a **working publication infrastructure with overlapping personnel, plates, editorial roles and serial formats**.

## VIII. Evidence guard

1. Do not quote the dedication signature from OCR; visually inspect it first.
2. Until that image check, write `dedication author/current publisher strongly identifiable as Martin Tyroff`, not `signature directly verified as Martin Tyroff`.
3. Do not call Tyroff the printer of part 22; the title page explicitly names Andreas Bieling as printer.
4. Do not call Gatterer the sole editor/completer of the final part; Johann Tobias Köhler has a documented posthumous continuation role.
5. Do not collapse the 1750 nominal series date into actual publication date. Gatterer's own preface explains the 1756 completion problem.
6. Do not infer that every coin plate in the part was engraved by Tyroff merely because the dedication says his work was involved in most of the engraved coins/medals. Plate-level signatures remain the correct unit.
