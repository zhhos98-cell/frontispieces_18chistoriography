# Bernhard attribution provenance: Strieder guardrail and Kassel `2° Ms. Hass. 50/51` manuscript route

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: ATTRIBUTION-PROVENANCE AND MANUSCRIPT-ROUTING CHECKPOINT — ADB EXPLICITLY ATTRIBUTES COMPLETION OF WINKELMANN'S SIXTH PART TO JOHANN ADAM BERNHARD, BUT ITS REFERENCE TO STRIEDER I.374 DOES NOT BY ITSELF PROVE THAT STRIEDER 1781 CONTAINS THAT COMPLETION ATTRIBUTION. TWO OLD KASSEL BERNHARD MANUSCRIPT SHELFMARKS HAVE STRONG PROBABLE MODERN CONCORDANCES WITH CURRENT `2° Ms. Hass. 50/51`; DIRECT HASSIACA DESCRIPTIONS REMAIN OPEN.

## 1. Attribution-provenance guardrail

The 1875 *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* entry for Johann Adam Bernhard states directly that Bernhard:

`vollendete` Johann Just Winckelmann's *Hessische Chronik*, whose sixth part appeared in Kassel in 1754.

The entry ends with a parenthetical reference:

`(Seine übrigen Schriften findet man bei Strieder, Hess. Gel.-Geschichte I. 374.)`

The grammar matters. `übrigen Schriften` means Bernhard's **other writings**. The Strieder reference therefore cannot be cited, without direct inspection, as the source for the preceding statement that Bernhard completed Winckelmann's Chronicle.

Current safe chronology of explicit attribution:

- **1875 ADB Bernhard** — explicit `vollendete` attribution controlled;
- **1898 ADB Winckelmann** — explicit internal attribution that the continuation from p.377 derives from the Hanau archivist Bernhard;
- **2002 Fuchs / recent Rabeler** — reconstruction of largely completed authorial sixth part, later manuscript loss, and replacement by Bernhard texts.

Potential earlier attribution in Strieder 1781 remains:

`OPEN UNTIL P.374 IS DIRECTLY READ`.

An ONB digital object for Strieder volume I has been located, including a machine-readable IIIF manifest identifier, but the current retrieval environment has not yet exposed p.374 itself. Do not backdate the Bernhard attribution from ADB to Strieder on bibliographic cross-reference alone.

## 2. ADB gives two highly relevant Bernhard manuscript shelfmarks in Kassel

The ADB Bernhard entry identifies two unpublished manuscript works in the Kassel Landesbibliothek:

### `MSS. hess. fol. 50`

`Die Alterthümer des Hochfürstlichen Hauses Hessen in ihrer Wahrscheinlichkeit und dann in ihrer Gewißheit`

ADB states that Bernhard prepared a clean manuscript of this work and presented it to the Landgrave.

This is unusually valuable for the sixth-part problem because it is:

- directly Bernhard;
- explicitly Hessian-dynastic;
- apparently prepared as a high-status presentation manuscript;
- concerned with the evidentiary status of dynastic antiquity (`Wahrscheinlichkeit` / `Gewißheit`).

### `MSS. hess. fol. 51`

`Beschreibung der vormaligen fürstlichen Abtei Hersfeld`

ADB attributes this anonymous work to Bernhard and places it under the adjacent Kassel manuscript number.

This is equally relevant because `Hersfeld` belongs to the territorial scope of Winckelmann's public project title.

Source:

- Karl Bernhardi, `Bernhard, Johann Adam`, *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* 2 (1875), p.458, via Deutsche Biographie.

## 3. Modern UB Kassel holdings control strongly suggests shelfmark continuity

UB Kassel's official 2012:

`Liste der vorhandenen Katalogisate und Digitalisate des Hassiaca-Bestandes`

contains consecutive rows:

- `2° Ms. Hass. 50    x`
- `2° Ms. Hass. 51    x`

The document defines:

`x = im Katalog der Hassiaca`.

Neither row carries the `o` marker for an ORKA modern short record, and neither row shows a digital-surrogate URL in the 2012 list.

This supplies a strong probable concordance:

`ADB MSS. hess. fol. 50`
≈ `current 2° Ms. Hass. 50`

`ADB MSS. hess. fol. 51`

However, exact equality is not yet promoted because the actual Hassiaca catalogue descriptions for 50 and 51 have not been directly inspected.

## 4. Why `Ms. Hass. 50` is now a top-priority Bernhard comparator

The sixth-part textual-stratigraphy problem requires a Bernhard manuscript that is closer than generic biography or printed attribution.

`Die Alterthümer des Hochfürstlichen Hauses Hessen ...`

is particularly promising because its title itself encodes an epistemic movement:

`Wahrscheinlichkeit`
→ `Gewißheit`.

If direct inspection confirms Bernhard's hand and dating, it can be compared against the 1754 continuation for:

- documentary citation formulas;
- distinctions between probable and certain claims;
- treatment of dynastic genealogy;
- use of charters / `Urkunden`;
- source hierarchy;
- syntax and transition formulas;
- regnal chronology;
- heraldic/genealogical vocabulary;
- marginal/reference apparatus.

This could convert the current status:

`P.377ff = HISTORICALLY ATTRIBUTED TO BERNHARD`

toward:

`P.377ff = TEXTUALLY / MANUSCRIPT-COMPARATIVELY CONTROLLED BERNHARD LAYER`.

## 5. `Ms. Hass. 51` supplies a territorial comparator internal to the Winckelmann project geography

The Hersfeld manuscript matters for a different reason.

Winckelmann's major printed work is explicitly a description of:

`Fürstenthümer Hessen und Hersfeld`.

A Bernhard manuscript on the former princely abbey of Hersfeld could therefore reveal whether the mid-eighteenth-century editor/continuator worked within the same territorial archive/problem space using distinguishable source practices.

Questions for direct inspection:

- Does the work identify Bernhard by hand, title page, ownership or catalogue attribution?
- What source types does it use?
- Does it cite documents by archival provenance?
- Does its Hersfeld vocabulary overlap Winckelmann's printed text?
- Does it contain passages later reused in the 1754 sixth part or register?
- Is it contemporary with or earlier/later than the 1754 publication?

## 6. A direct Bernhard manuscript comparator is independently available in Darmstadt

Hessisches Staatsarchiv Darmstadt catalogues:

`102` — old/provisional shelfmark `C 1 Nr. 195/40`

Johann Adam Bernhard,

`Vormalige Beschaffenheit und eigentliche Gestalt des Buseckerthals`,

c.1760.

The archival description controls:

- Bernhard authorship;
- c.1760 date;
- 64 leaves;
- paper manuscript in quarto, fixed boards;
- a territorial-historical deduction for Landgrave Ludwig VIII (?) concerning Hessian sovereignty in the Buseck valley;
- explicit engagement with historiographical and juridical literature concerning medieval legal relations.

This is only about six years after the 1754 sixth part and provides an independent, directly attributed Bernhard manuscript for:

- hand comparison;
- source-reference formulas;
- argumentative syntax;
- documentary/juridical evidentiary practice;
- relation between territorial history and archival proof.

It should be used as a **method/hand comparator**, not assumed to derive from the same printer-copy process.

## 7. Bernhard's 1748 source-supply practice supplies a close operational analogue

Modern scholarship on Bernhard's history of Adolf of Nassau reports that the work was completed in 1748 after extensive source collecting and that the Idstein archivist Hagelgans supplied Bernhard, at his request, with unpublished archival material.

This is close in time to the 1754 Winkelmann continuation and demonstrates an operational Bernhard pattern:

`historical writing problem`
→ `inter-archive request`
→ `unpublished documentary supply`
→ `new historical synthesis`.

This does not prove the same archival route operated for the sixth part. It does make Bernhard's intervention intelligible as part of his normal historical-production practice rather than as a purely literary continuation.

## 9. Repository-level guardrail

The present case adds another identity distinction:

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC CROSS-REFERENCE`
≠ `ATTRIBUTION SOURCE`.

And for archive concordance:

`SAME SHELFMARK NUMBER ACROSS CATALOGUE ERAS`
≠ `VERIFIED SAME MANUSCRIPT`

until title/provenance/extent controls agree.

These distinctions matter because the easiest narrative — Strieder already knew everything; old fol.50 simply equals current 50 — is plausible, but archival work becomes strongest precisely when plausible concordances remain explicitly probabilistic until directly checked.
