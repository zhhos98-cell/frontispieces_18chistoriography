# Gabriel Müller → Martin Tyroff repeated collaboration, 1740–1756 — 26 August 2026

## Object-level controls

The Müller→Tyroff pairing attached to the Type B *Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica* frontispiece was not an isolated collaboration.

### 1740 Wappenkalender frontispiece

British Museum object `1910,0906.2` is catalogued as an illustrated-book/print object published in Nuremberg in 1740, made by **Martin Tyroff after Gabriel Müller** and published by the **Heirs of Christoph Weigel**. This is the Museum-level external control corresponding to the Type B frontispiece family whose 1744 direct image visibly reads `G. Müller del.` / `M. Tyroff sc.`.

### 1756 Johann David Köhler portrait

British Museum object `Bb,13.545`, `Ioannes David Koelerus`, is catalogued as a print made by **Martin Tyroff after Gabriel Müller**, dated **1756**.

An independent portrait-history control preserves the more precise production inscriptions:

`Gabr. Müller ad vivum pinx. 1734`

and

`Martin Tyroff del. et sculp. 1756`.

The portrait was produced for the final/22nd part of Köhler's *Wöchentliche Historische Münz-Belustigungen*, nominally 1750 but completed/published in its final form in 1756. The image therefore belongs directly to the same Köhler/Weigel–Tyroff publishing environment being reconstructed for the Wappenkalender.

Sources:

- British Museum, `1910,0906.2`.
- British Museum, `Bb,13.545`, *Ioannes David Koelerus*.
- British Museum Martin Tyroff authority record `BIOG49071`.
- Independent portrait-history description reproducing/transcribing the 1756 Köhler portrait and its Müller/Tyroff production lines.

## Analytical consequence

The Müller→Tyroff relation should be treated as a **repeated designer/painter-to-engraver collaboration inside the Weigel/Tyroff production network**, not merely as an attribution attached to one allegorical plate.

The controlled minimum span is:

`1740 Wappenkalender frontispiece: Müller → Tyroff`

`1756 Köhler portrait: Müller (1734 painted model) → Tyroff (1756 engraving)`.

This matters in two ways.

First, it strengthens the attributional context of Type B. The 1739 catalogue signature `G. Müller del / M. Tyroff sc.`, the British Museum 1740 attribution, and the directly signed 1744 Type B image all sit inside a real recurring collaboration rather than an isolated catalogue formula.

Second, the 1756 portrait places Tyroff as an active image producer at exactly the late Köhler/Weigel moment when the Wappenkalender Type C repertoire is also directly visible. Tyroff's role in the firm therefore spans multiple image genres: allegorical threshold engraving, portrait production, and publisher/firm management.

## Guardrails

- Repeated collaboration does not establish that Müller designed Type C.
- The 1756 Köhler portrait does not establish any matrix continuity with the frontispiece plates.
- `after Gabriel Müller` / `Gabr. Müller ad vivum pinx.` concerns the source design/portrait; it must not be conflated with copperplate authorship.
- The chronological persistence of the collaboration supports a production-network interpretation, not a claim that the same artistic workflow governed every Weigel/Tyroff engraving.
