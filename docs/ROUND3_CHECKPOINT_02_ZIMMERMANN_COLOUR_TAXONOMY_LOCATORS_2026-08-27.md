## Cluster A — printed p.109: what should govern division into `Racen`?

Simone De Angelis transcribes Zimmermann 1778 p.109 as follows:

> `Es wäre also der Natur der Sache weit mehr angemessen, bey der Abtheilung des Menschen, oder bey seiner Zerfällung in verschiedene Racen mehr auf das Klima Acht zu geben.`

Source status: `secondary scholarly transcription, exact printed-page citation`.

De Angelis places the sentence after discussing Zimmermann's use of reports of white skin among Andean populations to unsettle simple skin-colour/geographical expectations. For Round 3 the important jurisdictional point is narrowly bounded:

- `Race` is explicit actor vocabulary at p.109;
- the actor-language prescription, as transcribed, tells the reader to attend more to `Klima` when dividing humanity into races;
- therefore visible colour cannot presently be treated as Zimmermann's sole or self-sufficient classificatory operator;
- this does **not** yet prove that colour is irrelevant, nor that `Klima` alone defines race, because the surrounding primary pages remain to be inspected.

## Cluster B — printed pp.114–115: `Racen`, `Varietäten`, migration and climate

A modern German history-of-zoogeography compilation reproduces a long Zimmermann passage and marks it `(I: 114f.)`. The transcription begins with the actor framing that the human `Racen` might be derived in an unconstrained/natural way, places the oldest humans on the major inhabitable elevation of eastern Asia, and then says their later `Varietäten` can be naturally derived from that origin.

The same transcription then gives the migration mechanism: population increase forces families to descend from the elevated homeland along several routes; after entry into Africa the human being `artete dem Klima nach und nach an`; and once humans have gradually been transferred into all climates, climatic influence makes them geographically differentiated forms including Georgians, Negroes and Eskimos.

The later summary sentence, reproduced by the same compilation, says the argument has supplied reasons by which all `Abartungen unsers Geschlechts` can be derived `von einem Stamme` and the possible worldwide spread of humanity can be shown from a single part of the earth.

Source status: `secondary/tertiary transcription with exact volume-page marker`; the PDF itself could not be fetched by the current web layer, so this is **not** scan verification.

## Provisional terminology finding — deliberately bounded

Taken together, the page-specific transcriptions make the following primary test much sharper:

- p.109: humanity may be divided into `Racen`, with `Klima` assigned explicit classificatory attention;
- p.114: the argument sets out to derive human `Racen`, then calls the resulting/post-origin differences `Varietäten`;
- p.115 / immediate continuation: climate produces `Ausartung`/`Abartungen`, while all such differences can be derived from one `Stamm`.

This currently looks like a **fluid lexical field** rather than a demonstrated Kant-1785-style technical hierarchy in which `Race` is licensed specifically by hereditary permanence and predictable mixed descent.

That is a hypothesis for primary inspection, not yet a final claim. The scan must decide whether nearby clauses stabilize these terms more strongly than the transcriptions indicate.

## Provisional Zimmermann ↔ Meiners boundary

Hold the closed Meiners controls fixed. If the p.109 and p.114–115 transcriptions survive scan collation, the boundary is likely to be:

### Already present in Zimmermann 1778

- one-stock / one-region dispersal model;
- migration as historical-geographical mechanism;
- climate and nourishment as transformative causes;
- bodily/geographical differences treated as generated rather than requiring multiple local creations;
- `Race`, `Varietät`, `Abartung/Ausartung`, `Stamm` all available in the same explanatory field;
- explicit resistance to treating a simple visible/geographical colour expectation as sufficient classification, insofar as p.109 privileges climate-attention in racial division.

### Still not licensed from Zimmermann 1778 on present evidence

- a stable nested taxonomy equivalent to Meiners's `Stamm > Race > Varietät > Spielart`;
- a rule that race status requires hereditary invariance;
- Meiners's 1790 specimen-provenance / anti-single-body-part constraints;
- the inference that a visible colour mark by itself establishes essential group membership;
- direct Zimmermann -> Meiners textual dependence.

This is the emerging distinction between **causal-generation machinery** and **inferential entitlement / evidential discipline**. Zimmermann may already supply much of the former while Meiners later hardens or redistributes what may be inferred from bodily signs and classificatory terms.
