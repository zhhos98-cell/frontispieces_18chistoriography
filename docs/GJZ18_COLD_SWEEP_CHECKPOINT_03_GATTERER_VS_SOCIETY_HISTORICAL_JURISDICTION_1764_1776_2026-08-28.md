# GJZ18 cold sweep checkpoint 03: Gatterer versus the Göttingen Society of Sciences — historical jurisdiction, 1764–1776

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE INSTITUTIONAL CONTROVERSY — SECONDARY-DIRECT ARCHIVAL QUOTATION CONTROLLED; PRIMARY ARCHIVE NOT YET INSPECTED

## Result

The search for lesser-known controversy surfaces around Gatterer has produced a relation more important than an isolated negative review.

Johann Christoph Gatterer's Historical Academy / Institute entered a **direct jurisdictional conflict with the Königliche Sozietät der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen** over the scope, status and disciplinary reach of the historical sciences.

Martin Gierl reconstructs this conflict from Göttingen University Archive material and explicitly describes the story as a `squabble between institutions`.

The named camps in his reconstruction are:

- **Johann Christoph Gatterer / Historical Institute**;
- on the Society side, **Johann David Michaelis** and **Abraham Gotthelf Kästner**;
- **Christian Wilhelm Büttner** between the two camps;
- an additional `Prof. Murray` appears in the decisive 1767 Senate discussion as one of the persons who had objected that parts of the Institute programme infringed the Society's rights. The exact Murray identity is **not yet normalized in this repository** and must not be guessed.

This is not merely a quarrel over honorific titles. It is a conflict over **what history may legitimately include and coordinate**.

Working relation label:

`DIRECT INSTITUTIONAL POLEMIC / JURISDICTIONAL CONFLICT`.

---

## 1. The conflict begins with two competing academy-like infrastructures

Gatterer founded his historical association in 1764 and originally wanted the stronger designation `Historische Akademie`.

The Royal Society of Sciences already occupied the academy position in Göttingen. Gierl therefore frames the confrontation as one between:

`Society of Sciences / academy umbrella`
versus
`Gatterer's Historical Academy / Institute`.

The conflict included:

- institutional title and rank;
- claim to the royal designation;
- access to funding / scientific independence;
- whether the Institute could operate as a research body rather than only a university teaching institute;
- above all, whether historical science could draw natural history and mathematically/naturally grounded disciplines into its supporting apparatus.

Evidence status: `SECONDARY SYNTHESIS FROM ARCHIVAL MATERIAL`.

---

## 2. Named opponents: Michaelis and Kästner

Gierl's explicit actor map names:

- **Gatterer** on one side;
- **Johann David Michaelis**, orientalist and director of the Society of Sciences;
- **Abraham Gotthelf Kästner**, mathematician;
- Büttner as an intermediary figure.

This is a major correction to a Schlözer-centered controversy map.

Michaelis and Kästner are not merely intellectual comparators. They belong to an institution that actively resisted Gatterer's proposed jurisdiction.

Evidence status: `SECONDARY-DIRECT ACTOR MAP CONTROLLED`.

## 3. The 15 April 1767 Senate discussion: explicit boundary-policing

Gierl reproduces/paraphrases the decisive intervention from:

`Diskussion vor dem Senat`, 15 April 1767, Göttingen University Archive, `Kur 7539, 5`.

Michaelis, speaking as director of the Society, states that Kästner and `Prof. Murray` had already identified provisions detrimental to the Society's rights. He further invokes a ministerial declaration that the Historical Institute was to restrict itself to **history proper** and not bring **natural history** into its sphere.

Michaelis consequently asks the Rector not to confirm the provisions until the Society has deliberated and, if necessary, had time to appeal to higher authority.

Evidence status:

`SECONDARY-DIRECT ARCHIVAL QUOTATION CONTROLLED`.

The repository has **not** inspected `Kur 7539, 5` itself. Do not label this `PRIMARY-DIRECT`.

## 4. The issue was historical jurisdiction, not simply administrative jealousy

Gierl's reconstruction is analytically important because it makes the institutional logic explicit.

The Society's position can be summarized as:

`academy`
→ legitimate scientific umbrella
→ independent research + finance
→ cross-disciplinary jurisdiction.

Whereas the Historical Institute was to remain:

`university institute`
→ history `proper`
→ teaching + historical source work
→ bounded disciplinary jurisdiction.

Gatterer's project was broader. He did not simply seek to annex natural history. His programme treated mathematics, geography and natural-historical knowledge as supporting resources for historical questions.

Thus the controversy concerns the boundary between:

`history as a coordinating science of heterogeneous empirical witnesses`
versus
`history as a bounded narrative/cultural discipline`.

This is directly relevant to the inherited Clio ecology.

## 5. Direct relation to the Clio problem

The inherited Clio frontispiece had already staged:

`HISTORIA`
+
`GENEALOGIA`
+
`HERALDICA`
+
`TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`.

Under Gatterer this visual grammar entered a handbook and was then re-articulated institutionally through:

- diplomatic, heraldic, numismatic and geographical cabinets;
- comparative series of original and reproduced witnesses;
- the Historical Academy/Institute seal combining Historia with auxiliary sciences and material witnesses.

The 1767 Society conflict now adds a new operation:

`Historia + auxiliary / empirical sciences`
→ **claim of disciplinary jurisdiction**
→ institutional objection

This is much closer to the original project question — how Clio becomes a tool in historiographical controversy — than a generic intellectual genealogy.

A safe formulation is:

> **The visual constitution of history represented by the inherited Clio ecology became, under Gatterer, an institutional claim about what kinds of knowledge and witnesses history could coordinate. In 1767 that claim encountered explicit jurisdictional resistance from the Göttingen Society of Sciences.**

Do **not** claim that Michaelis or Kästner attacked the Clio plate itself.

The controversy concerns the **knowledge constitution that the plate can be used to track**, not the image as an explicitly cited pamphlet weapon.

## 7. The conflict may explain the timing of Gatterer's historical theory

Kasper Risbjerg Eskildsen's modern synthesis makes a consequential suggestion: Gatterer's sudden concentration on essays about historical theory in the later 1760s may have been partly driven by the need to justify the Historical Institute's position during these institutional conflicts.

This must remain a causal interpretation, not a demonstrated motive until actor-level correspondence is controlled.

But the chronology is striking:

- 1764: Historical Academy founded;
- 1766: royal/official Institute reconfiguration;
- 1767: explicit Senate jurisdictional conflict;
- 1767: `Vom historischen Plan` and programmatic historical writing;
- 1767 onward: `Allgemeine historische Bibliothek` as Institute publication infrastructure.

Working hypothesis:

`institutional boundary conflict`
→ `need to define historical method/jurisdiction publicly`
→ `theory + review infrastructure`.

Evidence status: `STRONG SECONDARY HYPOTHESIS; ACTOR-LEVEL CAUSATION UNPROVED`.

## 8. A second jurisdictional front: the `juridical sect`

Eskildsen also points to Gatterer's 1767 retrospective criticism of an older German `juridical` historical tradition associated with Ludewig and Gundling.

Gatterer credits this tradition with widening German history beyond emperor-history but complains that it risked submitting historical knowledge to jurisprudential `bread studies`, with imperial history reduced to an auxiliary of German public law.

This is potentially a second, distinct polemical front:

`history as auxiliary to jurisprudence`
versus
`history as autonomous/coordinating science`.

The exact 1767 Gatterer page and any journal responses must be primary-controlled before naming individual jurists as direct opponents.

Status:

`ACTOR-LEVEL POLEMICAL CATEGORY CONTROLLED SECONDARILY; OPPONENT NETWORK UNRESOLVED`.

## 9. The Society wins the institutional boundary — but the field does not become unified

Gierl's reconstruction states that the academy/Society ultimately prevailed in the jurisdictional dispute. The Historical Institute survived in a narrower, recognizably modern form centered on historical sources, editions, journal publication, teaching and regular meetings.

Yet this did not produce a single coherent `Göttingen school`.

The later field remained internally competitive:

- Schlözer became an increasingly direct rival of Gatterer;
- Meiners belonged to both institutional worlds;
- Spittler and Heeren later occupied major historical terrain;
- Heyne and Michaelis shaped antiquity / cultural-historical work from within the Society ecology.

Thus `Göttingen historical school` should be treated as a **competitive field of historical jurisdiction**, not one homogeneous methodological programme.

This strongly supports the cold-sweep strategy.

---

## 10. Heyne as later redefinition, not automatically a 1767 direct opponent

Do not flatten Christian Gottlob Heyne into the initial Michaelis/Kästner camp without specific evidence.

The later development is nevertheless analytically important. In Gierl's reconstruction, Heyne's programme increasingly defines modern historical inquiry through peoples' mentalities, customs, rites, institutions, laws, arts, crafts, products and ideas — a cultural profile in which nature is no longer part of history in the expansive Gattererian sense.

This can be treated as:

`LATER INSTITUTIONAL / CONCEPTUAL REDEFINITION OF HISTORY`,

not yet `DIRECT_POLEMIC`.

The possible article payoff is strong:

Gatterer's broad empirical/natural-cultural historical constitution did not simply triumph. The institutional outcome helped separate cultural history from natural history while the Historical Institute itself persisted.
