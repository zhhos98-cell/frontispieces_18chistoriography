## I. A fourth register of `Evidenz` must be kept distinct

The repository already distinguishes at least three operations in Gatterer's use of `Evidenz`:

1. **source warrant / historical demonstration** — authentic monuments, documents and critically tested testimony ground certainty;
2. **systemic overview / causal arrangement** — relations among events become surveyable as a system;
3. **narrative evidentia / ideal presence** — vivid narration produces an `ideale Gegenwart` and makes the reader, as it were, a spectator.

The 1769 genealogy essay introduces a fourth, unusually concrete operation:

4. **visible proposition–source identity through disposition** — the genealogical proposition and the passage that proves it must be brought into immediate spatial proximity so their agreement can be apprehended rapidly, almost at a glance.

This operation is neither merely source criticism nor rhetorical vividness. It concerns the **editorial and graphic arrangement of already selected proof**.

---

## II. Genealogical truth: agreement between source and proposition

On the transcribed p. 10 Gatterer begins from a distinction between the genealogist's own conviction and the public communicability of that conviction.

If all propositions have been drawn from good sources, the genealogist may personally be convinced of their truth. But the genealogist must also convince others and should raise that conviction to the highest possible degree of intelligibility, `bis zur Evidenz`.

Gatterer's definition is unusually explicit. In the current uncorrected transcription, **genealogical truth consists in the agreement (`Uebereinstimmung`) of the sources with the genealogical propositions drawn from them**.

Operationally:

`good source`
→ `genealogical proposition extracted from source`
→ `agreement of proposition and source`
= `genealogische Wahrheit`.

Truth therefore does not reside simply in possession of an authoritative document. It lies in the controlled relation between a statement and what the document/monument/testimony warrants.

---

## III. Evidenz: make that agreement immediately perceptible

The next page sharpens the distinction from truth to Evidenz.

The current transcription says Evidenz occurs when the agreement, even the `Identität`, of the genealogical propositions and their sources is set in such a light that one can perceive it **immediately and without effort**. Gatterer reformulates the same point visually: it should, as it were, **strike the eye** that what the genealogist says is exactly what a diploma, monument or credible writer says.

The analytical consequence is major:

> **For Gatterer in 1769, an evidentiary relation can be true without yet being evident. Evidenz is produced when disposition makes the warrant relation rapidly inspectable.**

This yields a sequence stronger than `source → claim`:

`source`
→ `genealogical proposition`
→ `verbatim proof passage`
→ `graphic/editorial co-location`
→ `agreement perceived with minimal cognitive travel`
→ `Evidenz`.

The page is therefore part of the evidentiary operation.

---

## IV. Citation is insufficient: reproduce the Beweisstelle

Gatterer draws two practical consequences from the definition.

First, genealogical sources should **not merely be cited**. The relevant proof passages (`Beweisstellen`) should themselves be reproduced verbatim.

Second, the genealogical proposition and the proof passage should be placed **immediately together** so their agreement / identity can be surveyed almost `mit einem Blick`.

This is a direct eighteenth-century methodological statement about information architecture:

`bibliographic pointer to distant source` < `proof text locally reproduced beside proposition`.

The distinction is not decorative. Local reproduction reduces the operations required of the reader to check the claim.

The source surrogate is therefore epistemically useful precisely because it shortens the distance between proposition and warrant.

---

## V. Critique of the Duchesne method: evidence can fail through layout

Gatterer credits the method associated with André Duchesne for reproducing proof passages verbatim and acknowledges its wide approval and imitation.

But he argues that the method still does not raise genealogical truth to Evidenz because **the propositions and their proofs cannot be brought close enough together for their identity to be perceived quickly and easily**.

Two defects are identified in the transcribed passage:

- genealogical propositions are not individually marked out but remain compressed in the Stammtafeln;
- many propositions are crowded together instead of each proposition appearing individually with its own proof.

This is highly important for the repository's media analysis. The failure is not primarily bad source selection. It is a failure of **disposition and navigational distance**.

A method may contain the correct source passages and still be epistemically suboptimal if the reader must travel too far between statement and proof.

---

## VI. Stammtafeln as navigation, not self-sufficient proof surfaces

Immediately before defining genealogical truth, Gatterer discusses how genealogical tables should refer readers into the accompanying historical-genealogical text.

The uncorrected transcription says that the Stammtafeln should indicate the paragraphs or sections where the known historical and genealogical particulars concerning each person are narrated in order.

He explicitly says this arrangement is used in:

- Treuer's genealogical history of the Münchhausen family;
- **Gatterer's own 1755 `Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum`.**

This self-reference creates a direct bridge from the repository's 1755 visual/material corpus to the 1769 methodological articulation.

Safe conclusion:

> By 1769 Gatterer retrospectively presents the 1755 Holzschuher genealogy as an example of a navigational relation between Stammtafel entries and numbered textual locations.

This does not yet prove that every element of the mature 1769 Evidenz method was already fully theorized in 1755. It does show that the earlier publication's table–text cross-reference architecture belonged to the practical genealogy he now endorsed.

---

## VII. The 1755 Holzschuher object now needs to be decomposed into two visual/evidentiary operations

The repository has so far emphasized the 1755 engravings of charters, seals, inscriptions, arms and other artifacts as **reproduced testimony**.

The 1769 self-reference reveals another operation in the same book ecology:

### A. Artifact plates

`historical artifact`
→ `drawing / manuscript intermediary`
→ `engraved surrogate`
→ `warrant / testimony`.

### B. Stammtafel–text navigation

`person / relation in genealogical table`
→ `paragraph/section reference`
→ `historical-genealogical account`
→ `proof apparatus`.

These should not be collapsed into one category of `visual evidence`.

One operation reproduces objects. The other reduces navigational distance among genealogical statements, narrative particulars and supporting material.

The 1769 essay suggests that **location and cross-reference can themselves be methodological variables**.

## VIII. Method followed immediately by a worked example

The material organization of *Allgemeine historische Bibliothek* vol. 12 matters.

Gatterer's essay occupies pp. 3–17. The next item begins at p. 18:

`Beyspiel zur Erläuterung der Gattererschen Methode in der Genealogie`, pp. 18–46.

Martin Gierl's bibliography attributes this worked example to **Matthias Christian Sprengel** and dates the contribution 1768 in the bibliographic convention used for its composition/publication history.

Gierl's list of illustrations explicitly identifies:

- André Duchesne, *Histoire de la maison de Chastillon sur Marne* (1621): genealogy, arms, chronicle;
- Johann David Köhler, *Genealogia familiae augustae Stauffensis* (1733): Stammtafel and `Probationes`;
- Matthias Christian Sprengel, `Beyspiel zur Erläuterung der Gattererschen Methode in der Genealogie`: **Tafel und Erläuterungen**, pp. 19 ff.

This provides a ready-made comparative sequence for the repository:

`Duchesne 1621`
→ `Köhler 1733`
→ `Gatterer/Sprengel 1769 practical method`.

The next high-information task is not another conceptual search. It is to inspect pp. 18–46 and record exactly how proposition, table, proof passage, numbering and explanatory text are spatially coordinated.

## IX. Difference from the 1767 `Vorrede von der Evidenz in der Geschichtskunde`

The two texts must remain distinct even though they share the term `Evidenz`.

### 1767 historical Evidenz

Current controlled registers include:

- source-based historical demonstration / certainty;
- causal/systemic intelligibility;
- vivid narration producing `ideale Gegenwart` and a spectator effect.

### 1769 genealogical Evidenz

The distinctive operation is:

`proposition + reproduced proof passage + immediate adjacency`
→ `visible identity of statement and source`
→ `rapid verification`.

Thus the article should not translate all Gattererian Evidenz into `visual evidence` or `vividness`.

The 1769 case is closer to **inspectability**, **proof localization**, and **page-level verification**.

## X. Relation to frontispiece and threshold apparatus

This essay does not explain the inherited Müller–Tyroff Clio frontispiece and should not be used as a retrospective key to its designer's intention.

It nevertheless alters the host-context in which the old frontispiece circulated under Gatterer.

The inherited threshold image declares:

`Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica. Testes temporum et veritatis.`

Gatterer's mature genealogical method then defines truth and Evidenz operationally through:

- good sources;
- propositions drawn from them;
- reproduced proof passages;
- immediate co-location;
- rapid visual apprehension of agreement.

The safe relation is therefore:

`inherited iconographic equation of disciplines/witnesses`
≠ `Gatterer's authorship of that equation`

but

`inherited threshold image`
+
`later host-publication culture with explicit source/proof/disposition method`
= **historically transformed threshold function**.

## XI. Article-level consequence

A stronger media-division-of-labour model is now available:

1. **frontispiece / threshold image** — makes disciplines and witnesses co-present before reading;
2. **artifact plate** — transports a particular material witness into print;
3. **genealogical table** — compresses kin relations and points toward fuller textual locations;
4. **proposition + Beweisstelle layout** — makes source/claim agreement locally inspectable;
5. **synchronistic table** — coordinates political systems, epochs and forms of historical knowledge at world-historical scale;
6. **vivid narrative** — reconstructs an ideal presence / spectator relation;
7. **diplomatic graphic comparison** — turns morphology into a diagnostic test whose reproduction accuracy must itself be controlled.

The shared problem is not `visuality` in the abstract. It is **which relation historical method delegates to which material/graphic/textual form**.

1. The GenWiki transcription explicitly says it is uncorrected. Do not quote its spelling/punctuation as primary without checking the printed page image.
2. `genealogische Wahrheit = source/proposition agreement` is presently a strong transcription-level claim plus full primary-carrier availability; quotation-level evidence awaits pp. 10–11 inspection.
3. Do not collapse `truth`, `certainty`, and `Evidenz`; in this essay Evidenz is a further condition of rapid perceptibility imposed on a warrant relation already judged true.

5. Do not project the 1769 theoretical vocabulary wholesale back into 1755. Gatterer's own self-reference supports continuity of a table–text navigational device, not identity of the entire conceptual system.
