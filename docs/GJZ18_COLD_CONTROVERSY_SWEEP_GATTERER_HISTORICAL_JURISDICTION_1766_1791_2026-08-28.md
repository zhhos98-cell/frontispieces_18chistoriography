# GJZ18 cold controversy sweep: Gatterer and contested historical jurisdiction, 1766–1791

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: OPEN — discovery/index control with bounded full-text and archival metadata checks

## Research question

Do not reduce the transformation of the inherited Clio frontispiece into a polemical historiographical apparatus to the famous Gatterer–Schlözer dispute alone.

The broader question is:

> After the inherited `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORVM ET VERITATIS` architecture entered Gatterer's Göttingen publication and institutional ecology, where did the resulting programme of historical jurisdiction encounter criticism, rival programmes, review-response chains, imitation, appropriation, or institutional replacement?

This sweep uses GJZ18 primarily as a discovery/index layer. It does **not** equate every negative review with a direct controversy.

## Relation taxonomy / evidence discipline

Use these labels:

1. `DIRECT_POLEMIC` — actor-level attack and reply/response demonstrable.
2. `REVIEW_RESPONSE` — a review and a response/correspondence about that review demonstrable.
3. `CRITICAL_RECEPTION` — negative/ambivalent review, but no response chain yet.
4. `METHODOLOGICAL_RIVAL` — competing historical architecture or jurisdiction, direct comparison/competition demonstrable.
5. `IMITATOR_OR_COMPETING_JOURNAL` — parallel review/institutional infrastructure; no personal hostility required.
6. `APPROPRIATOR_OR_MARKET_COMPETITOR` — adopts a method/terminology/format in the same market; borrowing must still be proved.
7. `SUCCESSOR_OR_INSTITUTIONAL_REPLACEMENT` — later infrastructure occupies terrain formerly held by Gatterer's institute/journals.
8. `SAME_FIELD_ONLY` — proximity without controlled controversy.

## 2. Auserlesene Bibliothek reviews Gatterer's `Historisches Journal` — an external counter-review surface

GJZ18 isolates three reviews in the `Auserlesene Bibliothek der neuesten deutschen Litteratur`:

1. `Historisches Journal, von Mitgliedern des königl. historischen Instituts zu Göttingen. T. 1` — 1774, vol. 5, pp. 575–589.
2. Johann Georg Meusel, `Betrachtungen über die neuesten historischen Schriften` — 1774, vol. 5, pp. 589–593.
3. Gatterer, `Historisches Journal ... T. 2–3` — 1776, vol. 9, pp. 102–112.

For this three-record result set GJZ18 gives the evaluation distribution:

- **positive: 2**
- **negative: 1**.

Evidence: `GJZ18-INDEX CONTROLLED`.

At present the negative record has **not** been isolated article-by-article. Do not claim that either Gatterer review is the negative one until the record or full text is controlled.

### Why this journal is structurally important

The `Auserlesene Bibliothek` is not just another notice sheet. It was built as a selective review organ with its own claims about critical authority and literary selection. Its anonymous editorial/reviewer environment included figures associated with Carl Renatus Hausen, Jakob Mauvillon and later Christian Friedrich Helwing, among others; individual review authorship requires separate attribution control.

If one of the Gatterer `Historisches Journal` reviews is the negative item, this would provide a valuable external judgment on the institute's own review/boundary-policing machine. Even if both Gatterer reviews are positive and Meusel is the negative one, the adjacent treatment of Gatterer and Meusel still maps competing historical-review infrastructures in the same critical venue.

### Primary carrier

Google Books/Google Play exposes vol. 5 (1774) full view; the target pages are 575–593.

Status:

`CRITICAL_RECEPTION SURFACE — HIGH PRIORITY; exact polarity unresolved`.

## 4. Johann Georg Meusel — parallel historical-review infrastructure

GJZ18 profile of `Fortgesetzte Betrachtungen über die neuesten historischen Schriften` controls a large specialist historical-review corpus (494 indexed entries). Its first listed 1774 review is:

- Gatterer, `Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie. T.1.2.`
- `Fortgesetzte Betrachtungen ...` (1774), pp. 3–14.

Evidence: `GJZ18-INDEX CONTROLLED`.

The periodical is associated with Johann Georg Meusel, who served as editor and, by his own later account, was also the principal reviewer. That does **not** yet authorize assigning this specific anonymous Gatterer review to Meusel.

The important structural point is that Meusel built a sustained specialist historical-review infrastructure overlapping the field Gatterer's `Allgemeine historische Bibliothek` and `Historisches Journal` had claimed.

Working status:

`IMITATOR_OR_COMPETING_JOURNAL candidate`.

Next controls:

1. article-level reviewer attribution/signature;
2. exact evaluation of Gatterer's synchronistic programme;
3. preface/programme comparison between Meusel's journal and Gatterer's historical institute journals.

## 7. Spittler + Meiners — late Göttingen successor infrastructure

A later candidate field is the `Göttingisches historisches Magazin` (from 1787), associated with Christoph Meiners and Ludwig Timotheus Spittler, followed by the `Neues göttingisches historisches Magazin`.

This begins after Gatterer's `Historisches Journal` ends in 1781 and may represent a transfer/reallocation of Göttingen historical-review jurisdiction to a younger professorial network.

Do not yet call this a replacement until the opening programme/preface is compared directly with Gatterer's journals.

Working status:

`SUCCESSOR_OR_INSTITUTIONAL_REPLACEMENT candidate`.

Potentially important side control: Spittler's early magazine work includes correction of erroneous geographical/statistical calculations that had migrated into tables and generated false judgments — an operation strongly comparable to Gatterer's own witness/reproduction policing, but not evidence of direct inheritance by itself.

## 8. Existing famous disputes now become controls, not the whole field

### Herder–Schlözer (1772–73)

A dispute over facts, strict history, common measure, commensuration, Plan, table/synopsis and graphical delegation.

### Gatterer–Schlözer (1773)

A dispute over teaching territory, priority/plagiarism and ownership of Universalgeschichte method/Plan.

The cold-sweep objective is to identify lesser-known relations that show Gatterer's historical jurisdiction being evaluated or challenged before, beside and after these famous disputes.

## 9. Emerging field model

The evidence increasingly suggests that `controversy` should be modeled as more than pamphlet exchange:

`Gatterer/Institute defines historical jurisdiction`
→ `reviews competing historical works`
→ `authors correspond/respond`
→ `external review journals evaluate the institute's own output`
→ `parallel specialist historical journals emerge`
→ `methods such as synchronism become shared/competitive market property`
→ `later Göttingen journals and professors occupy/redefine the field`.

This is a much stronger route from inherited Clio to controversy than requiring the Clio plate itself to be explicitly cited in a polemic.

The plate can function as the early portable constitution of an epistemic arrangement; controversy can occur when that arrangement is operationalized as review, method, curriculum, institutional jurisdiction, or critical authority.

- explicit attack + reply;
