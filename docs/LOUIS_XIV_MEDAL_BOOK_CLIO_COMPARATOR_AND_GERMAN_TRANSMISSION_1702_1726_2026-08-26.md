# Louis XIV medal-book Clio comparator and German transmission, 1702–1726 — 26 August 2026

## Status

**Strong comparator; German transmission of the medal-plus-historical-explanation apparatus is directly documented before the Weigel Clio witness, but no direct German copy of the Coypel–Simonneau frontispiece has yet been located in the Nürnberg/Weigel/Müller/Tyroff line. Do not promote to derivation.**

## 1. The 1702 folio frontispiece

The folio *Médailles sur les principaux événements du règne de Louis le Grand* (Paris, Imprimerie royale, 1702) carries a frontispiece designed by Antoine Coypel and engraved by Charles-Louis Simonneau.

Institutional controls:

- Louvre, `L 416 LR/0 Recto`, 1702, Simonneau; engraved after Coypel.
- National Gallery of Art, `Clio Recording the Life of Louis XIV`, 1702 / after 1702, with signatures `Inventé et dessiné par A. Coypel` / `Gravé par Simonneau l'aîné`.
- Musée Médard describes the operation explicitly: Clio records the reign and prepares to entrust the historical account to the medal press.

The composition contains:

- Clio / History with an open book and writing instrument;
- Time beside her;
- Mercury presenting Louis XIV's portrait;
- medals / engraving or minting tools;
- the medal press in the background;
- a program in which historical writing is materially translated into medallic commemoration.

This is a highly relevant comparator for the later Weigel image because it joins `History + Time + writing + portable visual witnesses` in a threshold image.

## 2. Feature comparison with the Weigel/Tyroff Clio

The Weigel/Köhler frontispiece visible by 1726 and securely attested in 1737/1740 instead organizes:

- a winged female figure labelled/functioning through `Historia`;
- Chronos/Time with scythe;
- an open book;
- a genealogical tree / Stammbaum;
- heraldic shield/helmet apparatus;
- the inscription `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`.

The comparison is therefore stronger than a generic `female allegory of history` parallel:

`History + Time + written record + graphic/material witness ecology`

exists in both.

But the scene architecture differs substantially:

### Coypel–Simonneau 1702

`royal portrait + Mercury + Clio + Time + medal press + medals`

The operation is monarchic metallic history: historical narrative is converted into a serial medallic monument.

### Weigel/Müller/Tyroff by 1726

`Historia + Chronos + genealogy + heraldry + family/political witnesses`

The operation is disciplinary/evidentiary redistribution: history is flanked by genealogy and heraldry as witnesses of time and truth.

At current evidence level the Weigel image should be described as **a comparable reorganization of a wider History–Time–witness visual grammar**, not as a copy or adaptation of Coypel.

## 3. German-language transmission is nevertheless direct and early

A German/French adaptation of the French medal corpus appeared in 1704:

*Der berühmten Königlichen Academisten zu Paris Curiöse Schau-Müntzen, vorstellend die vornehmste Thaten Ludwig des Grossen ...* (Schaffhausen, Johann Rudolf Frey, 1704), with another issue/edition at Baden / Jean Louis Baldinger in 1705.

Modern numismatic cataloguing attributes the adaptation to **Lorenz Beger**, director of the Brandenburg-Prussian antiquities collection in Berlin.

The German version is explicitly based on the **compact / Großoktav** form of the 1702 project and reproduces the medal corpus with bilingual historical explanation.

This matters because it proves that before the Weigel Clio appears, the German-speaking antiquarian world had already received the French operational format:

`medal image + caption/iconographic explanation + historical narrative`

through an elite antiquarian mediator.

## 4. Negative visual boundary: the Beger carrier is not yet a Coypel-frontispiece bridge

Surviving descriptions of the 1704 Beger edition collate:

- blank leaf / preliminary leaves;
- 573 pages;
- numerous engraved medal illustrations;
- German/French parallel text.

They do **not** report the large Coypel–Simonneau folio frontispiece.

This fits the bibliographic relation: the Beger version follows the compact edition rather than the lavish folio state.

Therefore the 1704/05 German transmission currently supports:

`French medal-history apparatus → German antiquarian/numismatic publication culture`

but not:

`Coypel Clio frontispiece → German copied frontispiece → Weigel Clio`.

One bounded opening-leaf inspection of the 1705 Google full-view carrier can close this negative boundary visually.

## 5. Additional caution: Franz Ertinger does not by itself establish German reception

The Louvre 1702 album also contains a second frontispiece/related frontispiece engraving by **Franz Ertinger**, catalogued as `Ecole allemande`, after Sébastien Leclerc.

Ertinger was German-born but worked in Paris. His participation in the French production should not be counted as evidence that the Coypel frontispiece circulated through German print culture.

This is an agent-origin fact, not a reception edge.

## 7. Article-level consequence

This comparator is useful even if no direct derivation emerges.

It shows that the Weigel frontispiece enters a European visual environment in which **history could already be represented as a process of converting events into durable graphic/material witnesses**. The distinctive Weigel move is to detach that grammar from a single monarch's medallic commemoration and redistribute evidentiary labor across `Historia`, `Genealogia`, `Heraldica` and their associated witnesses.

The analytically safe contrast is:

`Coypel 1702: history → medallic monument`

versus

`Weigel 1726+: history ↔ genealogy/heraldry ↔ witnesses of time and truth`.
