## Result

This pass sharpens the repository distinction:

`rediscovered old block stock`
≠ `Winckelmann's provenance attribution`
≠ `composition identity with 1557`
≠ `physical same-matrix identity with 1557`
≠ `matrix state`
≠ `narrative placement in the 1664 host`.

Two upgrades matter.

First, Winckelmann's printed p.136 wording shows that his attribution of roughly thirty-four recovered woodcuts to Hans Staden was not based on comparison with a known copy of the 1557 Marburg princeps. Immediately before the rediscovery claim, he says he had not been able to establish whether Staden's description together with the wood figures had ever been printed.

Second, Franz Obermeier's 2024 image-history appendix gives a page-by-page motif list for the Winckelmann visual apparatus. It directly identifies:

- p.168 = `Angriff von Indigenen auf ein Indigenendorf`;
- p.179 = `Schiffbruch in der Nähe von São Vicente`.

This requires a correction to the first-pass p.168 hypothesis. The p.168 image is **not** safely identified as the Igarassu/Garasu scene from the surrounding first-voyage text. Rather, Obermeier identifies it as an attack on an Indigenous village, a motif belonging to the Staden image repertory but not to the immediate Garasu textual episode. P.168 is therefore already a strong control for **image-stock survival plus contextual relocation**.

The result is analytically stronger:

`OLD STADEN IMAGE / POSSIBLY OLD MATRIX`
+
`NEW 1664 NARRATIVE POSITION`
=
`MATERIAL OR COMPOSITION CONTINUITY WITHOUT CONTEXTUAL CONTINUITY`.

## 1. Primary-text control: what Winckelmann says before the 34-block claim

IA full-text OCR, printed p.136, gives the following sequence:

- Staden returned home and, according to Winckelmann's reconstruction, intended to put his adventures into images, have them cut in wood at Kassel, and publish them with the description;
- Winckelmann then says: `Ob aber solche Beschreibung mit den Holzfiguren in Druck seye befordert worden / hab in keine Erfahrung bringen können`;
- he cites Bolduan's *Historica bibliotheca* for a supposed 1593 Frankfurt/de Bry item;
- he notes the Staden narrative in Johann Ludwig Gottfried's *Neue Welt Historien*, 1655, with eight copperplates;
- only then does he report the recent Kassel rediscovery of `bey vier und dreissig Holzschnitt` and say that Staden had `sonder Zweifel` caused them to be designed and cut;
- he frames his own action as rescuing them `von den Untergang` and adorning his renewed narrative with the old wood figures.

The crucial OCR wording is:

> `Demnach aber jüngsthin zu Cassel bey vier und dreissig Holzschnitt / so oftgedachter Hans Staden sonder Zweifel abreissen und ins Holz schneiden lassen / sich ungefehr wieder gefunden. Als / habe ich selbige von den Untergang erretten ... und mit den damaligen geschnittenen Holz Figuren auszieren wollen ...`

The OCR is imperfect, but the evidentiary syntax is clear. `sonder Zweifel` marks an attribution, not a reported matrix concordance against a known 1557 exemplar.

Evidence consequence:

`WINCKELMANN KNEW OLD BLOCK STOCK EXISTED`
+
`WINCKELMANN ATTRIBUTED ~34 BLOCKS TO STADEN`
+
`WINCKELMANN DID NOT CONTROL A PRINTED STADEN + WOODFIGURE EXEMPLAR`

therefore

`P.136 ≠ DIRECT 1557 SAME-MATRIX CONCORDANCE`.

This remains first-rate evidence for how Winckelmann understood, narrated, and legitimized recovered material stock.

Primary source:

- Internet Archive item `deramericanische00winc`: https://archive.org/details/deramericanische00winc
- IA full text: https://archive.org/stream/deramericanische00winc/deramericanische00winc_djvu.txt

Independent scholarly control:

- Franz Obermeier, `Hans Stadens Ikonographie des ersten Brasilienbuchs Warhaftige Historia von 1557 im Kontext der Tradition und ihre Transformation in der frühen Rezeption`, first digitally published 2024. In the Winckelmann appendix he says the p.135 claim that Staden's illustrations had been made in Kassel is `sicher nicht auf authentischen Informationen fußend`, precisely because Winckelmann says the original edition was not known or accessible to him. The same appendix then inventories the 1664 images by page.

This independently validates the distinction between Winckelmann's seventeenth-century provenance attribution and modern composition/matrix reconstruction.

## 2. P.168: corrected from scene-match hypothesis to contextual-relocation control

### Earlier first-pass hypothesis — superseded

IA OCR around printed pp.167–168 places the surrounding text in the first-voyage Garasu/Igarassu siege/provisioning narrative. On textual context alone, the first pass tentatively mapped the image to an Igarassu battle composition.

That inference is now superseded.

### Obermeier's direct motif identification

Obermeier's 2024 appendix lists:

`S.168: Angriff von Indigenen auf ein Indigenendorf`.

His earlier appendix of the 1557 repertory identifies a corresponding motif as Staden first book, chapter 29:

`Angriff von Indios auf ein Indiodorf`.

This belongs to the later captivity narrative rather than the first-voyage Garasu episode.

Therefore p.168 is valuable precisely because the surrounding 1664 text and the inherited Staden motif diverge:

`1664 TEXTUAL POSITION = FIRST VOYAGE / GARASU-IGARASSU SEQUENCE`

while

`1664 IMAGE MOTIF = ATTACK ON AN INDIGENOUS VILLAGE FROM A DIFFERENT STADEN NARRATIVE CONTEXT`.

Safe current status:

`composition/motif identification = SCHOLARLY APPENDIX CONTROLLED`

`contextual relocation = STRONGLY CONTROLLED`

`same physical matrix as 1557 = UNVERIFIED AT ITEM LEVEL`

`JCB-reported disappearance of original names = CATALOGUE CONTROLLED`

`exact removed labels / physical intervention mechanism = OPEN`.

This is a direct example of the repository formula:

`MATRIX OR COMPOSITION AUTHENTICITY ≠ CONTEXTUAL AUTHENTICITY`.

The p.168 pair should now be recovered against the **chapter-29 Indigenous-village attack woodcut**, not against the earlier Igarassu scene.

## 4. A second matrix-state clue appears at p.171

Obermeier's appendix gives:

`S.171: Schiff rund, Fischmaul links nachgeschnitten, so bereits bei Wiederholungen des Holzschnitts in der Erstausgabe.`

Elsewhere in the article he explains that on this reusable ship woodcut the badly cut mouth of the fish at upper left was already recut / completed in repeated impressions within the 1557 first-edition production.

This is potentially very useful for matrix-state work because it gives a specific diagnostic feature:

`ROUND SHIP COMPOSITION`
+
`UPPER-LEFT FISH-MOUTH RECUT STATE`.

However, the logical order remains:

1. verify p.171 composition directly;
2. compare frame, linework, dimensions and defects;
3. establish whether the 1664 impression comes from the same physical block;
4. only then use the fish-mouth state as a chronological/state marker.

A copied composition could reproduce the already corrected fish-mouth form, so state resemblance alone is not enough to prove matrix identity.

## 7. Bibliographic-tradition clue: how attribution hardened

Later English-language Staden editions repeat a stronger rediscovery formulation. Malcolm Letts, for example, says Winckelmann discovered thirty-four of the original wood blocks and used them again.

That suppresses the attribution step visible in Winckelmann's own `sonder Zweifel` wording.

Working genealogy:

`WINCKELMANN 1664: recovered ~34 blocks + Staden attribution ('sonder Zweifel')`
→ `later editorial/bibliographic tradition: 34 original Staden blocks`
→ `modern catalogue shorthand: 32 Staden-section illustrations from original 1557 blocks + 2 provenance exceptions`.

This remains a hypothesis about bibliographic transmission until JCB's cited authorities are directly checked:

1. JCB pre-1675 catalogue 3:122–123;
2. Moraes 944–946;
3. Richard N. Wegner, *Begleitschrift*, p.19;
4. Karl Fouquet, `Die Ausgaben der Wahrhaftigen Historia 1557–1964`, relevant p.32 / facsimile pagination context.

## 9. Repository-level payoff

The 1664 case now has a historical mechanism rather than a simple survival anecdote:

`MATERIAL REDISCOVERY`
→ `PROVENANCE ATTRIBUTION`
→ `RESCUE / RE-PUBLICATION PROGRAMME`
→ `RECONTEXTUALIZED IMAGE ORDER`
→ `LATER BIBLIOGRAPHIC HARDENING OF ATTRIBUTION`
→ `MODERN COMPOSITION/MATRIX/STATE REOPENING`.

P.168 is especially valuable because it shows the distinction at occurrence level: even before matrix identity is settled, the old Staden motif has been detached from its earlier narrative position and redeployed in a different textual sequence.

This is exactly why the repository must keep separate:

`frontispiece / image programme / composition / matrix / matrix state / impression / textual placement / host publication / provenance claim`.
