# Winckelmann 1664 p.136 provenance attribution and p.168/p.179 scene concordance

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: PRIMARY-TEXT UPGRADE + SCENE-LEVEL CONCORDANCE CHECKPOINT — WINCKELMANN'S OWN P.136 WORDING SHOWS THAT HIS ~34-BLOCK STADEN PROVENANCE WAS AN ATTRIBUTION MADE WITHOUT KNOWLEDGE OF A PRINTED STADEN EDITION WITH THE WOOD FIGURES; P.168 AND P.179 CAN NOW BE MAPPED TO SPECIFIC 1557 SCENE CANDIDATES, BUT MATRIX/STATE IDENTITY STILL REQUIRES DIRECT PIXEL-LEVEL PAIRING.

## Result

This pass materially sharpens the repository's distinction between:

`rediscovered old block stock`
≠ `Winckelmann's provenance attribution`
≠ `composition identity with 1557`
≠ `physical same-matrix identity with 1557`
≠ `matrix state`.

The central new primary-source result is Winckelmann's own discussion on printed p.136. Before introducing the rediscovered woodcuts, he explicitly says that he had not been able to ascertain whether Staden's description together with the wood figures had ever been brought into print. He knows instead:

1. a Bolduan bibliographical notice associating Staden with a 1593 Frankfurt/de Bry publication;
2. the Staden narrative in Johann Ludwig Gottfried's *Neue Welt Historien*, 1655, illustrated with eight copperplates;
3. only after this does he introduce the recently rediscovered Kassel block stock.

The crucial p.136 wording in the IA OCR is:

> `Demnach aber jüngsthin zu Cassel bey vier und dreissig Holzschnitt / so oftgedachter Hans Staden sonder Zweifel abreissen und ins Holz schneiden lassen / sich ungefehr wieder gefunden. Als / habe ich selbige von den Untergang erretten ... und mit den damaligen geschnittenen Holz Figuren auszieren wollen ...`

The OCR is imperfect, but the syntax and evidentiary structure are clear enough for the present claim. Winckelmann treats the recovered stock as something Staden had **`sonder Zweifel`** caused to be designed and cut. That is an attribution. It is not a statement that he compared these matrices with impressions in the 1557 Marburg princeps; immediately beforehand he says he had not established whether the description with the wood figures had in fact been printed.

This makes the modern pp.18/74 provenance exception especially important. If two of the thirty-four images are demonstrably not from Staden's original work, the discrepancy is no longer merely `modern bibliography vs authorial statement`; it is direct evidence that Winckelmann's rediscovery narrative compressed a materially mixed or at least imperfectly identified stock into a single Staden provenance class.

Working label:

`REDISCOVERY-BASED PROVENANCE ATTRIBUTION`.

This is stronger and more precise than treating p.136 as evidence for thirty-four verified original Staden matrices.

## 1. Primary-text control: what Winckelmann says before the 34-block claim

IA full-text OCR, printed p.136, gives the following sequence:

- Staden returned home and, according to Winckelmann's reconstruction, intended to put his adventures into images, have them cut in wood at Kassel, and publish them with the description;
- Winckelmann then says: `Ob aber solche Beschreibung mit den Holzfiguren in Druck seye befordert worden / hab in keine Erfahrung bringen können`;
- he cites Bolduan's *Historica bibliotheca* for a supposed 1593 Frankfurt/de Bry item;
- he notes the Staden narrative in Gottfried/Merian 1655 with eight copperplates;
- he then reports the recent Kassel rediscovery of `bey vier und dreissig Holzschnitt` and attributes their commissioning to Staden `sonder Zweifel`;
- he explains his own intervention as rescuing them `von den Untergang` and adorning his renewed narrative with the old wood figures.

Evidence consequence:

`WINCKELMANN KNEW OLD BLOCK STOCK EXISTED`
+
`WINCKELMANN ATTRIBUTED ~34 BLOCKS TO STADEN`
+
`WINCKELMANN DID NOT ESTABLISH A PRINTED STADEN + WOODFIGURE EXEMPLAR`

therefore

`P.136 ≠ DIRECT 1557 SAME-MATRIX CONCORDANCE`.

It remains first-rate evidence for how Winckelmann understood, narrated, and legitimized the recovered stock.

## 2. P.168 scene-level mapping

The IA OCR anchors printed p.167 in the first-voyage section at the Portuguese settlement / siege narrative around Fernambuco, Marin and Garasu. The following image-heavy zone falls exactly where the text narrates the expedition by two boats to obtain provisions during the siege. ## 5. Bibliographic-tradition clue: why the 34-block claim propagated so easily

Later English-language Staden editions repeat a strong version of the rediscovery story. Malcolm Letts's introduction, for example, says that Winckelmann, while working in the Kassel archives, discovered thirty-four of the original wood blocks and used them again.

That later formulation is stronger than Winckelmann's own evidentiary posture on p.136 because it suppresses the `sonder Zweifel` attribution step and presents the thirty-four as already identified originals.

This creates a testable genealogy:

`WINCKELMANN 1664: recovered ~34 blocks + Staden attribution ('sonder Zweifel')`
→ `later bibliographic/editorial tradition: 34 original Staden blocks`
→ `modern catalogue shorthand: 32 Staden-section illustrations printed from original 1557 blocks + 2 provenance exceptions`.

This genealogy is a hypothesis about transmission of bibliographic language, not yet a demonstrated source chain. Priority D remains to trace JCB's exact wording through JCB pre-1675 catalogue, Moraes, Wegner and Fouquet.

Control:

- Malcolm Letts ed./trans., *Hans Staden: The True History of His Captivity, 1557* (Hakluyt Society), introduction, modern scan: https://web.as.uky.edu/history/faculty/myrup/his564/Staden%20Text%20%28complete%29.pdf

## 7. Repository-level conceptual payoff

The 1664 case is now more useful than a simple survival anecdote.

Winckelmann did not merely inherit images. He **made a provenance claim about rediscovered material stock**, used that claim to authorize a new textual/visual compilation, and explicitly framed his intervention as rescuing old visual matter from disappearance. The modern corpus then reveals that the stock normalized by that claim was not provenance-homogeneous.

That gives the project a compact historical mechanism:

`MATERIAL REDISCOVERY`
→ `PROVENANCE ATTRIBUTION`
→ `RESCUE / RE-PUBLICATION PROGRAMME`
→ `RECONTEXTUALIZED IMAGE ORDER`
→ `LATER BIBLIOGRAPHIC HARDENING OF ATTRIBUTION`
→ `MODERN MATRIX-LEVEL REOPENING`.

This is exactly why `frontispiece / image programme / composition / matrix / state / impression / host publication` must remain separate analytical layers.
