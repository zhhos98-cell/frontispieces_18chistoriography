## 2. Controlled preprint production sequence, 1686–1697

The current controlled sequence is:

### 1686 — formal revision commission

Thomas Fuchs's archival reconstruction controls a multi-month six-member censorship/revision commission:

- three Marburg professors;
- three Gießen professors;
- fundamental revision of the Hessian Chronicle;
- dynastic/governmental and epistemic/source-critical stakes.

### 1687 — surviving rule/note apparatus

Universitätsbibliothek Marburg `Ms. 309`, 180 leaves, is catalogued as:

`Fragmente derjenigen Regeln und sich darauf gründende Noten ...`

prescribed/made by Kassel and Darmstadt commissioners appointed to revise the Winckelmann Chronicle.

This is the strongest surviving item-level indication of:

`review rule / criterion`
→ `note grounded on rule`
→ `revision operation`.

Do not yet equate the unnamed `Ms. 309` commissioners with the six 1686 Marburg/Gießen censors at the personnel level.

### 1690 — unpublished material circulates outside the authorial codex

Leibniz working records control:

- excerpts from Winkelmann's unpublished `De dynastiis` manuscript;
- Hessian Chronicle excerpts routed with `Geographica`.

This proves a preprint excerpt/reservoir ecology rather than a closed manuscript waiting passively for print.

### c.1691 — six-part project already exists before the 1697 title

Leibniz's autograph note `Ms 12, 713h Bl. 93`, ID 5924, records among Winkelmann's promised publications:

`chrono<stemmato-polemografiam principum> partibus sex`.

Thus six-part project architecture and the chrono/stemmato/polemo/principum conception predate the 1697 print.

This does **not** yet prove that the later title formula `glaubwürdigsten Documenten und Scribenten` existed by 1691.

### 1692 — continued assisted production

## 3. Sixth-part and estate/manuscript transmission, 1697–1754

### A. 1697 is five published parts, not a six-part edition

This remains the edition-level control.

Market copies containing later attachments must not change this.

### B. 1701 resolves the physical architecture

Leibniz asks Nathanael von Staff about the late Winkelmann's Chronicle and says:

`j’ai vû même un Tome imprimé`.

Important correction: a current web translation renders this as `an unpublished volume`, which reverses the French. The primary wording is **printed tome**.

Staff's contemporaneous reply resolves the referent:

- `premier tome` = the printed object;
- `6me livre ou le second Tome` = the missing sixth book / second tome;
- the second tome had **never been seen in Kassel**;
- heirs claimed it was complete;
- they demanded a substantial sum to hand it over;
- the Kassel court was dissatisfied with the first tome and Staff feared the whole matter would be buried in oblivion.

This closes a major ambiguity:

`SIX PARTS`
≠ `SIX PHYSICAL TOMES`.

The material architecture was approximately:

`Parts I–V = first tome`
+
`Part VI = second tome`.

### C. 1702 — Leibniz has only a partial witness

Leibniz says he has `quelque commencement de la chronique`, brought to Hannover by Winkelmann himself in summer 1697, and intends to compare it with Staff's complete printed copy.

He also reports that Winkelmann's son had come to Hannover with `de si hautes prétensions` that Leibniz expected people to neglect the matter.

Do not yet classify `quelque commencement` as manuscript or print without direct object control.

### D. 1704 — Anton Günther as manuscript custodian

Gerhard von Mastricht tells Leibniz that `Winkelmannum filium` possesses `varia Manuscripta` of his father and proposes extracting what is hidden with him.

Critical editors identify the son as **Anton Günther Winkelmann**.

Controlled relation:

`HEIR-SIDE MANUSCRIPT CUSTODY / ACCESS FRICTION = PRIMARY LETTER CONTROLLED`.

It is not yet proved that the specific Hessian sixth-part manuscript was among these `varia Manuscripta`.

### E. Anton Günther 1742 death candidate is explicitly unresolved

A 1742 Westphalian church record names an Anton Günter Winckelmann as a Vikar in Lüdinghausen. This must **not** be automatically identified with Johann Just's jurist son / 1704 manuscript custodian.

The repository contains a dedicated identity guardrail.

### F. 1754 is a reconstructed completion state

The posthumous sixth part appears at Kassel in 1754 as:

`Johann Just Winkelmanns Sechster Theil ...`

with stable project/author identity and documentary-source language.

Modern scholarship following Fuchs reports:

- the sixth part was largely complete when Winkelmann died;
- by 1754 substantial portions of the original manuscript were no longer recoverable;
- missing material was replaced/supplied using texts by Hanau archivist **Johann Adam Bernhard**.

Historical ADB attribution gives a more specific boundary:

`Fortsetzung (von S. 377 an)` = Bernhard.

This p.377 boundary is a **historical attribution boundary**, not yet direct manuscript comparison.

Do not infer that pp.1–376 are a homogeneous untouched authorial block.

## 4. Market-copy / dealer-catalogue guardrail

The sixth-part line exposed an important copy-history problem.

One market copy is described as:

`10 Bl., 600 S., 184 S.`

and collapsed by Bassenge into a 1697 `6 Teile in 1 Band` description.

`4 leaves / 8 unnumbered pages + 490 + 67 pages`.

Therefore:

`dealer title / lot description`

≠ `bound-copy contents`.

The undated 184-page attachment remains unresolved:

## 6. Visual production branch: 1664 `Americanische Neue Welt Beschreibung`

This branch is now directly relevant to the repository's central plate/matrix problem.

### A. Bulk 32-block same-matrix claim was deliberately downgraded

JCB/Open Library catalogues 32 Staden-section images as impressions from original 1557 Marburg blocks.

However, Franz Obermeier's reception/iconography scholarship states only that **some / a portion** of the original Staden block stock survived into Winckelmann's 1664 production.

Therefore the repository now records:

`AT LEAST SOME ORIGINAL-MATRIX REUSE = STRONGLY SUPPORTED`

but:

`32-FOLD same_matrix_as 1557 = NOT PROMOTED`.

Every image requires pairwise matrix control through dimensions, lettering, defects, wear, frames and linework.

This downgrade is already committed and supersedes earlier bulk wording.

### B. Dual-source architecture

Obermeier reconstructs a much more important production structure:

- Winckelmann follows Gottfried/Merian in **text and image arrangement**;
- according to this reconstruction, Winckelmann did not know the original Staden edition;
- some original Staden matrices were nevertheless physically available;
- surviving old blocks were inserted at arbitrary/new locations in the 1664 host.

Therefore:

`TEXTUAL / ICONOGRAPHIC MODEL = Gottfried/Merian-mediated`

while:

`PHYSICAL MATRIX SOURCE = at least partly Staden 1557 stock`.

This yields one of the strongest repository formulations from the round:

`MATRIX AUTHENTICITY ≠ CONTEXTUAL AUTHENTICITY`.

An impression can come from an original sixteenth-century matrix while its placement, narrative relation and evidentiary function are seventeenth-century recompositions.

### C. p.168 / p.179 anomaly

JCB catalogue-level notes say names present in corresponding earlier images are absent in the 1664 images at pp.168 and 179.

Current safe status:

`NAME DIFFERENCE = CATALOGUE CONTROLLED`.

Still unresolved:

- exact 1557 pair;
- whether the physical matrix is the same;
- if same, whether difference comes from cutting, plugging, masking, selective inking, damage or another mechanism.

Do **not** call these proven altered states of the same block until pairwise matrix identity is re-established.

### D. pp.18 / 74 provenance exception

Two 1664 images are reported as not deriving from the original Staden image stock even though Winckelmann's p.136 provenance claim folds all 34 images into a Staden-origin narrative.

This is a direct programme/provenance problem:

`AUTHORIAL / BIBLIOGRAPHIC ORIGIN CLAIM`
≠ `ACTUAL IMAGE/MATRIX ORIGIN`.

Their source publications/matrices remain to be identified.

### E. separate threshold apparatus

The 1664 host also contains a separate folded illustrated title/threshold engraving with Columbus scenes and navigation/geography iconography.

This must remain distinct from the Staden-derived text-image ecology:

`threshold plate production`
≠ `reused/derived text-block stock`.

Designer/engraver/signature remain open.

### F. next primary wording target

Obermeier says Winckelmann lacked the Staden princeps `nach eigenen Angaben`.

The next chat should recover Winckelmann's own wording, especially around the block rediscovery and p.136 provenance statement.

## 8. 1684 Oldenburg Horn: object access, surrogate error, and long reuse

This late-round branch is already synchronized and should be preserved in the next chat.

### 1684 — direct object access as epistemic criterion

In *Des Oldenburgischen Wunder-Horns Ursprung ...*, Winckelmann uses material/visual features of the Oldenburg Horn to criticize Happel's source proximity. Scholarly transcription and object history indicate that the object was available to Winckelmann for weeks during drawing preparation.

Happel's response acknowledges Winckelmann's superior in-loco access.

Thus:

`DIRECT MATERIAL ACCESS`
becomes an explicit criterion for judging historical knowledge.

### Surrogate fidelity problem

Modern object comparison reports that Winckelmann's engraved representation includes an Oldenburg heraldic element/bars absent from the surviving Horn.

Therefore:

`OBJECT ACCESS ≠ SURROGATE FIDELITY`.

The cause remains open:

- engraver/designer intervention;
- inherited visual convention;
- restoration/change to object;
- interpretive supplementation;
- other production process.

Direct 1684 plate inspection is still required.

### 1696 reuse/derivation

`Museum Regium` Tab. XVI contains the Horn and is reported as apparently derived from the 1684 visual model.

Direct plate comparison is needed before `same design / copied from` is promoted.

### 1807–1808 literary threshold reuse

Correspondence around *Des Knaben Wunderhorn* indicates that `den Winkelmann` was supplied because it contained the best image; modern scholarship identifies the 1684 representation as the model for the 1808 threshold/title image.

Thus one antiquarian object surrogate changes function across more than a century:

`antiquarian object evidence`
→ `cabinet/catalogue image`
→ `literary threshold apparatus`.

## 12. Main conceptual gains now available for article-level use

The round supports several formulations stronger than the earlier generic `reuse` vocabulary.

### A. Matrix authenticity versus contextual authenticity

An original matrix can survive while the original publication context, sequence and evidentiary function are lost.

`MATRIX AUTHENTICITY ≠ CONTEXTUAL AUTHENTICITY`.

### B. Access to reproduction technology versus access to the original publication

Winckelmann can apparently gain access to surviving old image matrices while following a later Gottfried/Merian organization of text and image.

`ACCESS TO ORIGINAL MATRIX STOCK ≠ ACCESS TO ORIGINAL BIBLIOGRAPHIC CONTEXT`.

### C. Material access versus surrogate fidelity

The Horn case shows:

`DIRECT OBJECT ACCESS ≠ ACCURATE PRINTED SURROGATE`.

### D. Project identity versus material/textual state

The sixth-part history gives:

`project identity`
≠ `publication state`
≠ `physical tome structure`
≠ `heir-held manuscript reservoir`
≠ `surviving source reservoir`
≠ `posthumous reconstructed edition`
≠ `bound-copy composition`.

### E. Historical coherence as coordination

Across the textual and visual branches, the strongest larger claim remains:

> Historical coherence was produced by coordinating materially distributed, differently authorized, repeatedly revised and sometimes partially lost witnesses. Printed titles, visual programmes, stable authorship and apparently coherent historical narratives are outcomes of that coordination, not transparent reflections of already-stable texts, disciplines or evidence sets.
