# Evidence and truth sweep — 21 August 2026

This note records a third analytical pass. It does **not** claim a direct intellectual genealogy from Cicero to Cuper to Schlegel to Eccard to Köhler or to the `Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica.` frontispiece. Instead it separates demonstrable lexical and operational precedents from hypotheses of transmission.

The emerging question is whether the frontispiece belongs to a wider early-modern redistribution of testimonial authority: a language classically predicated of *Historia* becomes available to coins, seals, genealogies and heraldry when they are treated as material or auxiliary witnesses capable of checking historical claims.

## 1. Classical baseline: Historia as witness

Cicero, *De oratore* II.36, supplies the familiar formulation:

`Historia vero testis temporum, lux veritatis ...`

The important grammatical difference is simple. Cicero's *Historia* is singular and bears the testimonial predicates. The target frontispiece reads:

`Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica. Testes temporum et veritatis.`

Here testimonial authority is pluralised and distributed across History, Genealogy and Heraldry. Lexical resemblance is strong; direct borrowing by the designer/editor has not yet been demonstrated.

Primary-text anchor:
https://gottwein.de/Lat/CicDeOrat/de_orat02la.php

## 2. 1691: coins inherit Ciceronian testimonial language

A much closer operational precedent occurs in Gisbert Cuper's correspondence with Otto Sperling. In a letter of 30 October 1691, printed in Giovanni Poleni's 1737 collection, Cuper proposes arranging the history of kings from coins and describes those coins as:

`certissimi testes temporum, clarissima lux veritatis, & rerum gestarum monumenta aeterna`

This is a striking transfer. The Ciceronian language of witness/time/truth is no longer attached only to abstract History; it is attached to material numismatic evidence.

Heidelberg digital transcription/OCR of the early printed correspondence:
https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/poleni1737bd4/0033/text_ocr

This does not establish a route to Nürnberg, but it demonstrates that the rhetorical move existed in late-seventeenth-century antiquarian-numismatic practice.

## 3. c.1696–1697: historical truth demonstrated from coins

Christian Schlegel's manuscript project bears an unusually explicit programmatic title:

`Historiae Saxonicae Antiquioris Veritas ex Nummis Demonstrata`

Martin Mulsow's reconstruction shows that this was not simply a collection of coin descriptions. It was designed as a history of older Saxony demonstrated or illuminated through numismatic evidence, with coins compared against other monuments such as seals and with a substantial engraved apparatus.

Modern notices differ slightly on dating: the article title gives 1697, while a University of Erfurt research notice gives 1696. The corpus therefore preserves `c.1696–1697` until the dating argument and manuscript are fully collated.

https://nasg.publia.org/nasg/article/view/32

### Material-history significance

The Schlegel project is also unusually relevant to this repository's material model. The manuscript and its intended copperplate apparatus became separated. The plates moved with a collection into Weimar and were later reused/published in a different textual setting by Salomon Franck in 1723. The same graphic apparatus could therefore acquire a documentary life distinct from the manuscript for which it had been prepared.

This is not evidence for reuse of the Clio plate. It is a strong earlier control for the corpus principle that plate history and host-publication history must be recorded separately.

## 4. 1721–1722: Eccard builds a comparison and surrogate infrastructure

Johann Georg Eccard's correspondence with Mathurin Veyssière de La Croze provides the strongest immediate pre-Weigel operational parallel located so far.

On 10 July 1721 Eccard describes collecting specimens of scripts and impressions of seals, comparing the latter with coins and the former with manuscripts whose dates are secure, and visiting archives. The procedure is explicitly comparative and cross-medium.

In February 1722, while working on Brunswick-Lüneburg genealogy, he says that coins will not merely ornament genealogy but `imo et firmare` it: strengthen or confirm it.

A public notice reproduced in the same correspondence asks for historical monuments, including seals, coins and tombstones, to be accurately drawn and engraved. When originals cannot be sent, Eccard accepts drawings and material surrogates or `ectypes` made in isinglass, tin foil, wax, gypsum, lead or paper.

CAMENA transcription of the *Thesaurus Epistolicus Lacrozianus*:
https://mateo.uni-mannheim.de/cera/lacroze1/bd1/Lacroze_epistolae_1.html

The resulting operational chain can be described without imposing later terminology:

`object → transportable surrogate → comparison with dated/identified controls → engraving → genealogical confirmation`

This occurs immediately before the 1723 launch of the Weigel calendar series. A direct Eccard → Köhler or Eccard → Clio textual route has **not** been located in the current sweep.

## 5. 1722 printed comparator: Eccard's genealogical history

Eccard's *Historia genealogica principum Saxoniae superioris* (Leipzig, 1722) is advertised in bibliographic records as demonstrated from trustworthy and in substantial part previously unknown monuments. Catalogue descriptions also report copperplates and genealogical tables.

This printed work should be inspected plate by plate against the methods described in the correspondence. Until then, it remains a strong bibliographic comparator rather than a fully inspected visual witness.

## 6. 1724–1726: the Clio inscription as redistribution of testimonial authority

Current witness evidence brackets the entry of the target design between:

- 1724: a different annual-event/mnemonic frontispiece, `Gedenckwürdigkeiten des 1722ten Jahres`;
- 1725: a title copper is now documented in a historical antiquarian catalogue, but its identity is still unresolved;
- 1726: surviving auction imagery shows the target `Historia Genealogia Heraldica` allegory.

The working hypothesis is therefore narrower than an influence claim:

> The frontispiece participates in a broader **rhetorical-operational redistribution of evidentiary authority**, in which the authority once predicated of History can be attached to auxiliary disciplines and material witnesses that establish, compare or verify historical propositions.

This hypothesis predicts evidence that can be tested: earlier exact uses of the plural formula, direct citations in Köhler or the Weigel circle, signature/lettering evidence, and contemporary explanations of the image.

The current web sweep has **not** located an exact pre-1726 occurrence of the full formula `Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica. Testes temporum et veritatis`. Absence from the present search is not evidence of uniqueness.

## 7. 1743: a documented Köhler bridge to Tentzel

The searchable contents of Köhler's *Historische Münz-Belustigung* contain an explicit Wilhelm Ernst Tentzel item in volume 15 (1743), pp. 97–102: a 1700 commemorative medal of the Saxon historian and numismatist.

Contents index:
https://www.coingallery.de/zeitschriften/koehler-belustigung.htm

A modern study of Tentzel cites this Köhler notice among the contemporary/near-contemporary biographical sources.

This is a real documentary bridge from Köhler back into the Gotha-Saxony antiquarian-numismatic world. It does **not** prove that Köhler read Schlegel's unpublished manuscript.

The same full-series index search currently yields no `Eccard` / `Eckhart` hit. That negative result is bounded by the index and OCR environment and should not be generalized to absence from all of Köhler's writings.

## 8. 1749–1751: heraldry explicitly discovers a historical truth

Köhler's 1749 title makes the methodological claim explicit:

`Gezeigter und bestärckter Nutz der Wappenkenntnüß zur Entdeckung einer historischen Wahrheit`

The case is a 1518 silver votive ship at Altötting. Bibliographic and scholarly reconstruction indicates that Köhler published two folded copperplates representing the object and used twelve coats of arms and initials to identify the associated companions and distinguish among three contemporary Welf dukes named Heinrich.

The procedure is therefore unusually close to the operational implications of the Clio frontispiece:

`material object → engraved representation → heraldic comparison → historical identification → claim to historical truth`

The interpretation did not end the matter. A printed *Anzeige* in 1751 revisited the question and is attributed in catalogue records to Georg Christian Gebauer. Historical identification through heraldic evidence was therefore itself subject to printed checking and controversy.

The detailed page-level reconstruction of Köhler's 1749 argument still needs direct inspection of the digitised copy.

## 9. Gatterer and the historiographical comparator

André de Melo Araújo's 2025 article, `Evidentiary Authority as a System`, reconstructs Gatterer's 1755 *Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum* as an information system in which verbal and visual information, material evidence, artistic work and scholarly coordination interact. It is the closest modern historiographical comparator currently located.

Article:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12599130/

The corpus should not simply import that model backward. Its contribution can instead be empirical: test how far comparable operations are already visible in Cuper, Schlegel, Eccard, the 1717–26 calendar competition, Köhler's weekly numismatics and the Müller–Tyroff production network before Gatterer's mature system.

## 10. What is established, and what is not

Established at current evidence level:

1. Ciceronian witness/time/truth language exists as the classical rhetorical baseline.
2. Cuper in 1691 explicitly transfers closely related witness/time/truth language to coins.
3. Schlegel c.1696–97 programmatically frames Saxon historical truth as demonstrated from coins and constructs a copperplate apparatus whose later material history separates from the manuscript.
4. Eccard in 1721–22 explicitly compares scripts, seals, coins and dated manuscripts, uses archives, solicits portable surrogates, and says coins can confirm genealogy.
5. Köhler explicitly engages Tentzel in 1743.
6. Köhler in 1749 explicitly describes heraldic knowledge as useful for discovering a historical truth and builds the demonstration around a material object and engraved representation.

Not established:

- a direct citation chain from Cuper/Schlegel/Eccard to the Clio designer or Köhler;
- direct knowledge by Köhler of Schlegel's unpublished manuscript;
- uniqueness or first use of the full Clio formula in 1725/26;
- the identity of the 1725 title copper;
- survival of the target Clio design into the 1767–68 Seyfart phase.
