# Eccard → Döderlein: printed plate reuse and revision of attribution, 1725–1729

## Status

**Direct printed-primary control for Döderlein 1729; Eccard 1725 carrier identity and plate count independently controlled.**

This pass upgrades the 1725 Nürnberg Eccard comparator from `same-city object-centred antiquarian practice` to a demonstrable case in which Eccard's numbered printed apparatus enters a later scholar's act of numismatic reassessment.

## 1. Eccard 1725 carrier

Johann Georg von Eckhart, *Erklärung eines alten Kleinodien-Kästleins, aus dem Ebnerischen Cabinete zu Nürnberg... dadurch auch einiger massen die Braunschweig-Lüneburgische Historie, nebst einigen alten dahin gehörigen Müntzen, erläutert wird* (Nürnberg: Peter Conrad Monath, 1725).

Institutional controls:

- Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, `4 Bavar. 601`, VD18 `14444186`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10332488-3`;
- BSB/Asc copy, `4 Asc. 954#Beibd.1`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10625348-2`;

- Künker catalogue 301 independently collates the Eccard item as `6 unpag., 56 S., 3 Tfn.`.

The title itself states the evidentiary programme: an old jewel/casket from the Ebner cabinet is used to elucidate Brunswick-Lüneburg history together with related old coins.

## 2. Döderlein 1729: a prior attribution is changed after consulting Eccard

Johann Alexander Döderlein, *Commentatio Historica de Numis Germaniae Mediae* (Nürnberg, 1729), p.119, discusses a bilateral coin showing a prince with sword and lily-bearing sceptre and, on the reverse, crossed keys.

Döderlein says that the keys initially made him strongly inclined to assign the coin to Regensburg and to identify the ruler as Emperor Frederick II. He then explicitly records a change of judgment:

`Invitus tamen mutavi sententiam, cum forte fortuna in doctissimas Ill. Eccardi schedas, quarum rubrica: Erklärung eines alten Kleinodien Kastleins ... inciderem.`

The operative point is not merely the citation. The encounter with Eccard's printed study causes Döderlein to abandon a prior attribution.

He immediately specifies the visual-textual locator:

- Eccard, p.45;
- **Tab. III, Fig. XXVI**;
- the figure `luculentissime exprimit` the relevant coin.

He then quotes Eccard's description identifying the prince as Heinrich, Duke of Saxony and Count Palatine of the Rhine.

Direct digital primary carrier for Döderlein: e-rara PDF, p.119 in the printed pagination.

## 3. Evidentiary sequence

This yields a first-order sequence:

`physical/known coin`
→ `formal attributes: keys + ruler + sword + lily sceptre`
→ `provisional Regensburg / Frederick II attribution`
→ `encounter with Eccard 1725 printed study`
→ `specific numbered surrogate: Tab. III Fig. XXVI + p.45 text`
→ `prior attribution explicitly changed`.

The plate is therefore not decorative illustration and not merely an aide-mémoire. It is a **portable comparison object capable of altering a later numismatic judgment**.

## 4. Why this matters for the frontispiece project

The case gives a concrete Nürnberg control for the larger distinction between image presence and image function.

A printed image can be:

1. a surrogate for a material object;
2. given a stable plate/figure address;
3. cited independently in another book;
4. reinserted into a new comparison;
5. help force revision of an attribution.

This sharpens the project's vocabulary. The relevant infrastructure is not simply `objects were illustrated`; it is:

`object → reproducible numbered visual witness → remote citation → new comparison → revised identification`.

That operation is directly documented in Nürnberg between Eccard 1725 and Döderlein 1729, in the same print environment in which the Weigel calendar frontispiece transition and Köhler's numismatic serial work are occurring.

## 5. Boundary

Do **not** infer:

- that Döderlein's use of Eccard caused Köhler's method;
- that the Eccard plates were produced by Müller, Tyroff, Weigel or any member of their production network;
- that this plate-use relation explains the Clio frontispiece iconography;
- that `Fig. XXVI` by itself supplies the entire revised attribution independently of Eccard's surrounding text.

The safe claim is narrower and stronger: **Döderlein explicitly says that consulting Eccard's study changed his numismatic judgment, and he anchors the new comparison to Eccard p.45 / Tab. III Fig. XXVI.**
