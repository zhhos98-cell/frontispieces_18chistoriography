# Annual history modules, live dynastic updates and publisher-advertising infrastructure — 1724–1747

## Why this matters

The Weigel/Köhler *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender* should not be treated as a stable heraldic reference book with a changing date printed on the title page. Its annuality was structurally active.

Three distinct forms of updating are now visible:

1. **live dynastic/genealogical updating** — current events and `neueste Nachrichten` revise the Stammtafeln, with late information patched during printing;
2. **rotating historical modules** — a different territorial, imperial or political history is integrated into the annual host;
3. **publisher-stock updating** — recurrent Weigel catalogues advertise a changing publishing inventory and are incrementally enlarged.

The same annual object therefore maintains several different temporal datasets at once. Together they change how the Clio frontispiece should be interpreted. A reusable threshold image repeatedly introduced a materially updated package to a recurrent readership.

## 1. Live dynastic updating: the reference layer itself is versioned

Direct ULB Düsseldorf inspection now provides a primary control that was not available when this note was first drafted.

The 1728 `Geneigter Leser!` says that the calendar appears with renewed necessary `Verbesserung` and **`alljährigem Vermehrung`**. Its 108 Stammtafeln had been reviewed anew and enlarged according to the `neuesten Nachrichten`, so that readers could use them with `zuverlässiger Gewißheit` in daily newspaper reading.

The same volume closes with a separate page headed:

`Unter währenden Druck eingelauffene Zusätze und Verbesserungen.`

It records marriages, births and deaths that arrived after the relevant tables were already materially fixed and points readers back to the affected locations.

The 1733 issue repeats the architecture. Its preface defines the Wappenkalender's original purpose as `alljährliche Vermehrung und Verbesserung der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldic`, identifies a Rome-based `guter Freund` who supplied information about Italian princely houses, and says the tables were changed/enlarged according to the `neuesten und sichersten Nachrichten`. The volume again closes with `Unter währenden Druck geschehene Veränderungen`.

The operational sequence is therefore directly controlled:

`external reports / correspondents`
→ `annual table review and revision`
→ `printing begins`
→ `events and information continue changing`
→ `terminal delta page`
→ `next annual cycle`.

This layer is distinct from the rotating history module. The former updates **the current state of the represented political-genealogical world**; the latter replaces a selected historical chronology within the host.

## 2. Rotating historical modules in the 1720s–early 1730s

Secure title-level witnesses currently include:

- **1724** — `mit der kurz-verfasten Boehmischen Historie fast auf alle Tage`;
- **1725** — `mit der kurz-verfasten Oesterreichischen Historie fast auf alle Tage`;
- **1727** — `mit der kurtz-verfasten Frantzösischen Historie fast auf alle Tage`;
- **1728** — `mit der kurtz-verfasten Teutschen Reichs-Historie fast auf alle Tage`;
- **1729** — `mit einer kurtz-verfasten Friedens-Historie fast auf alle Tage`;
- **1732** — `mit der kurtz-verfasten Dänischen Historie fast auf alle Tage`.

The series therefore rotates geographically/politically differentiated historical matter through the same annual genealogy-heraldry infrastructure.

The still-unfilled 1723, 1726, 1730 and 1731 subtitles remain bounded bibliography tasks, not blockers for the structural point.

## 3. 1724 is now a richer negative control

The complete 1724 copy described by Antiquariat Lenzen combines:

- the Bohemian historical module;
- a double-page frontispiece `Gedenckwürdigkeiten des 1722ten Jahres`;
- twelve monthly engravings;
- eighty engraved armorial plates;
- an engraved colour-explanation plate;
- one hundred Roman-numbered genealogical tables.

This means the pre-Clio state was already a dense visual-historical apparatus. The transition to `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica / Testes temporum et veritatis` cannot be described as a move from decorative calendar imagery to scholarly visuality. It is a change in the **kind of threshold image** attached to an already complex serial evidence/reference package.

## 4. 1725 continues the rotating-history architecture

A physical-copy seller record gives the 1725 subtitle as the Austrian history `fast auf alle Tage`.

An eighteenth-century library catalogue independently lists:

`Geschichts- Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender auf das Jahr 725. nebst der Oesterreichischen Historie, Nürnberg, mit vielen Kupff.`

This contemporary/near-contemporary catalogue witness matters because it confirms that the 1725 volume was perceived bibliographically as a copper-rich historical calendar, even though the identity of its title copper remains unresolved.

## 5. 1729: “peace history” shows that the rotating unit is not simply a national history series

The 1729 issue is described as carrying a `Friedens-Historie fast auf alle Tage`.

The historical module can therefore be national/territorial (Bohemian, Austrian, French, Danish), imperial (German Reich), or thematic/political (peace). The serial architecture is best understood as a reusable calendar/genealogy/heraldry frame into which a different historical chronology could be inserted annually.

## 6. The calendar as a publisher-advertising carrier

Marie-Kristin Hauke's study of early-modern German book advertising identifies the Weigel Wappenkalender as an unusually persistent publisher-advertising vehicle.

Her broader formulation says that in the Nürnberg Wappenkalender published by Christoph Weigel's widow, a publisher list appeared in **nearly every issue between 1722 and 1747**, and was updated from time to time.

Her Erlangen control set preserves issues from:

`1732, 1734, 1737, 1739, 1742, 1743, 1745, 1747`.

Each contains a **two-leaf publisher list**. Hauke judges the lists to have been kept as standing type (`Stehsatz`) and only occasionally enlarged by a line. She therefore regards the same practice in missing intervening issues as a reasonable inference.

This matches physical-copy descriptions already in the corpus: many Wappenkalender collations explicitly end with `2 Bll. Verlagsanzeigen`.

This is a second versioning regime inside the same object. The dynastic tables are revised because **the represented world changes**; the publisher list is revised because **the publisher's own stock changes**.

`changing courts / houses`
→ `genealogical update`

while

`changing publishing inventory`
→ `catalogue update`.

The annual is therefore simultaneously a reference snapshot and a commercial inventory surface.

## 7. Three temporal layers coexist in one annual host

The architecture can now be modelled more precisely.

### A. Fast current-information layer

Births, marriages, deaths and political-personnel changes can occur while the issue is already in press. These create terminal `Zusätze / Verbesserungen / Veränderungen`.

### B. Annual editorial layer

Stammtafeln are reviewed against the newest reports; historical modules rotate; portraits and related current material can change from year to year.

### C. Publisher-stock layer

A standing two-leaf catalogue is retained and incrementally enlarged as the firm's inventory changes.

These layers update at different speeds and for different reasons. The physical book synchronises them only imperfectly at the annual publication event.

A useful abstraction is:

`social time`
+
`editorial annual time`
+
`publisher inventory time`
→
`one materially composite annual snapshot`.

This is stronger than the earlier formulation of annuality as simple `content refresh`. It is a **synchronisation problem across heterogeneous information streams**.

## 8. Comparison with a competing Leipzig format

Gottlieb Schumann's *Jährliches genealogisches Hand-Buch* (Leipzig: Gleditsch, 1729) provides a direct contemporary control for the market ecology. Its title explicitly promises `neueste Nachrichten`, and its opening preface describes the new handbook as appearing with `vielen Vermehrungen und andern Zusätzen` and promises annual continuation.

This proves that annual current-genealogy updating was not unique to the Weigel/Köhler product. What remains unusually visible in the Nürnberg witness is the combination of:

- heraldic image infrastructure;
- genealogical tables;
- rotating historical modules;
- explicit newspaper-reading use;
- recurrent in-press terminal delta pages;
- recurrent publisher-stock catalogues.

## 9. Analytical consequence for the Clio frontispiece

The annual package can now be modelled as:

`calendar rhythm`
+ `current rulers / portraits`
+ `versioned genealogical tables`
+ `armorial plates + colour decoder`
+ `rotating historical chronology`
+ `terminal live-event patches`
+ `publisher's changing backlist advertisement`
+ `reusable threshold/frontispiece`.

The frontispiece therefore participates in **serial recognition and recurrent market entry**, not just iconographic framing.

This strengthens a publication-regime reading of the Clio plate:

- it labels a recurring composite apparatus as `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica`;
- the reference state behind that threshold changes annually and can change even during printing;
- the historical module changes independently;
- the same annual carrier also advertises a changing Weigel publication stock;
- the image is therefore embedded in a publishing ecology of repeated sale, updating, patching and cross-promotion.

The relevant contrast with the older Eimmart Wappenbuch frontispiece becomes sharper:

- Eimmart threshold → cumulative/inherited armorial book lineage;
- Clio threshold → annually synchronised history-genealogy-heraldry-information package, later transferable into handbook form.
