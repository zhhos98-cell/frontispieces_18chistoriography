# Köhler 1734 and the lost colour-explanation layer — 1747 Rinck catalogue control

## Status

A near-contemporary bibliographical synopsis now gives a much sharper account of what Johann David Köhler discussed in the preface to the 1734 Weigel armorial. Adam Friedrich Glafey's catalogue of Eucharius Gottlieb Rinck's library, *Bibliotheca Rinckiana* (Leipzig, 1747), entry 1993, does not merely list the Weigel Wappenbuch. It reports a specific complaint from Köhler's preface concerning the **German explanation of heraldic colours**.

The 1747 catalogue is directly controlled through the Zentralbibliothek Zürich / e-rara full-text export for DOI `10.3931/e-rara-30605`, plain-text lines around the printed pp. 270–271. It is therefore a **direct primary control of the 1747 synopsis**, but still a secondary witness to Köhler's 1734 wording. Quotation-level claims about Köhler himself still require direct inspection of his preface.

## The 1747 synopsis

Entry 1993 identifies:

*Das große und vollständige Weigelische Wappen-Buch, in 6 Theilen mit einer Vorrede Joh. Dav. Köhlers. Nürnberg 1734.*

Glafey then says that in the preface Köhler regrets (`dolet`) that the **German explanation of the colours occurring in the shields** had been neglected in later editions after Siebmacher's death. The catalogue adds a striking second point: the same explanatory layer is also missing from the newest Weigel edition prepared with Köhler's work.

The e-rara OCR reads, with obvious typographical/OCR noise:

`In praefat. hujus utilissimi operis ... Köhlerus dolet, quod explicatio germanica colorum, in scutis obvenientium ... in sequentibus, post mortem auctoris ... repetitis, neglecta sit: Nihilo secius tamen eadem in hac quoque novissima editione ... Köhleri opera adornata, desideratur.`

The safe substantive reading is:

> **Köhler treated the disappearance of a German colour-explanation apparatus from the post-Siebmacher edition lineage as a loss, while the 1734 Weigel edition itself still failed to restore that explanatory layer.**

This is more informative than a generic history of tincture codes because it makes **paratextual explanation itself a versioned component of the armorial apparatus**.

## A bibliographical ambiguity that must remain explicit

The 1747 OCR appears to say that Siebmacher's `prima editio` was produced in `1609` and contained `226` leaves/plates. Those two data points do not sit comfortably together with the standard bibliography: the first major Siebmacher part is normally dated **1605** and associated with roughly **226 engraved plates**, while the **1609** second part contains a substantially smaller plate series.

Therefore do not silently normalise the 1747 sentence. At least three possibilities remain:

1. the printed catalogue itself gives `1605` and OCR has misread the final digit;
2. Glafey/Rinck gives `1609` but conflates the date with the 226-plate first part;
3. the word `prima editio` refers to a particular state/part differently than modern bibliography assumes.

Until the printed 1747 page image is checked, cite the exact problematic data only as **OCR-ambiguous**. The structural claim about the lost German colour explanation is secure because it does not depend on resolving the year.

## Why this changes the 1605/1609 → 1680 → 1734 → 1772 sequence

The previous working genealogy focused mainly on graphic coding:

`verbal colour description`
→ `letters embedded on plates`
→ `Spener's dot/line hatching`
→ `1772 criticism of letter coding`.

The 1747 synopsis of Köhler 1734 shows that this is too thin. A more accurate model needs at least two independently variable channels:

### A. Graphic tincture encoding

- letters / initials;
- dots and directed lines;
- possibly pigment in a particular copy.

### B. Explanatory apparatus

- separate German colour declaration;
- prose blazon / explanation;
- colour key;
- absence of explanation despite an encoded plate.

Köhler's complaint concerns the **loss of channel B across an edition lineage**. The matrix or its visual code might persist while the explanatory component disappears.

This makes the technical problem one of **redundancy and recoverability** rather than simply which code is more advanced.

## Relation to Spener 1680

Spener's direct primary preface gives the best comparison. He explains that his plates use dots and differently directed lines instead of the German habit of colour initials, but adds that when figures are too small for those graphic marks to distinguish tinctures adequately, readers must return to the printed explanation.

The relation is structural:

`graphic channel has resolution limits`
→ `textual explanation supplies redundancy / fallback`.

The 1747 synopsis suggests that Köhler, half a century later, was also historically attentive to the disappearance of such an explanatory layer from the Siebmacher publication lineage.

Do **not** yet write that Köhler derived this concern from Spener. No direct Köhler → Spener citation has been controlled in this connection. The safe claim is that both actors treat visual tincture coding and textual explanation as separable components of a usable heraldic information system.

## Relation to the Wappenkalender colour-key plate

The annual Weigel/Köhler Wappenkalender ecology repeatedly includes a separate engraved `Erklärung der Farben` / colour-key plate. Copy descriptions now control this feature in multiple years, including the already recorded 1725 physical-copy description, a 1729 copy lacking its expected colour-explanation plate, and later 1730s–1750s witnesses with such a plate present.

This raises a much stronger question than `when was hatching introduced?`:

> **Did the annual calendar solve the explanatory loss Köhler identified in the large armorial by externalising tincture decoding into a stable, reusable colour-key plate?**

That hypothesis is testable at object level. It requires comparing the literal content of the Wappenkalender colour-key copper against the coding actually used on the armorial plates, not merely noting that both exist.

If the key explains the same dot/line conventions used by the individual arms, the calendar has an explicit architecture:

`one reusable legend`
+
`many encoded armorial plates`
→
`reduced need to repeat prose colour descriptions beside every shield`.

This would be a highly efficient information design, but it remains a **testable inference**, not yet a closed result.

## The 1705 'first hatching' claim should be downgraded

Several modern secondary/online sources repeat that the 1705 Helmers edition was novel because it used hatching instead of the earlier colour letters. Current direct controls do not justify treating this as an edition-wide clean break.

Reasons:

1. accessible modern facsimiles explicitly mix **1701 and 1705** material;
2. the publication lineage reused inherited copperplates, so new coding could coexist with older unchanged matrices;
3. copy descriptions and later annuals show a separate colour-explanation plate, meaning encoded shields and external legend could operate together;
4. Köhler's historically salient complaint, as transmitted in 1747, concerns the **loss of explanatory text**, not simply the invention date of hatching.

Therefore retain:

`1705 hatching novelty = secondary claim requiring plate-specific verification`.

The highest-value test is not one generic 1705 page. It is the **same named arm / same plate lineage across earlier and later states**, asking whether letters were erased, hatching was added to the same matrix, or the image was re-engraved.

## Revised information model

The armorial now needs at least four separately versioned layers:

1. **iconographic content** — shield, charges, crest, supporters etc.;
2. **tincture encoding** — letters, hatching, colour/pigment;
3. **explanatory apparatus** — prose colour declaration, blazon, colour-key plate, legend;
4. **copy finishing** — hand colouring, partial colouring, later owner intervention.

A change in any one layer does not license a claim about the others.

This gives a stronger version of the repository's core identity rule:

`same composition / same matrix`
≠
`same encoded information`
≠
`same explanatory apparatus`
≠
`same finished copy-state`.

## Evidence guards

1. *Bibliotheca Rinckiana* (1747) is close in time to Köhler's 1734 preface and directly reports his complaint, but it remains a bibliographical synopsis, not the 1734 page itself.
2. The e-rara OCR around the early Siebmacher date is internally suspicious (`1609` + `226`); do not silently correct it in quotations.
3. `desideratur` in the 1747 synopsis indicates that the German colour explanation is also lacking in the 1734 edition. It does not mean the edition lacked all means of communicating tinctures.
4. The annual `Erklärung der Farben` plate may be a solution to the same information problem, but functional equivalence must be demonstrated by comparing its legend to actual armorial coding.
5. Do not equate disappearance of prose with disappearance of information; graphic coding or colour finishing may preserve some tincture information.
6. Do not promote the modern `1705 first hatching` statement above secondary status until a dated, copy-specific plate is inspected.
