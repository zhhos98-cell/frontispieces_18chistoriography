# Thuanus as a source operation in the Wappenkalender — 1733 to the 1740s

## Finding

The recurring wording `Geschichts-Calender aus dem Thuano` in the Weigel Wappenkalender refers to **Jacques-Auguste de Thou (1553–1617), Latin author-name Thuanus**, not to an unidentified chronicle or a modern bookseller's guess.

This matters because it exposes a concrete textual operation inside the annual visual-genealogical apparatus: de Thou's continuous historical narrative was being reformatted into a **monthly/day-organised historical calendar** and physically paired with ruler imagery.

## 1. Direct material witness

A detached copper portrait of the Bavarian elector/emperor Karl VII, dated by the dealer to about 1742 and identified as coming from this Wappenkalender ecology, preserves on its verso the printed heading:

> `Aprilis. Geschichts-Calender aus dem Thuano`

The object description is especially useful because the verso wording survives on a loose plate. It therefore demonstrates the physical pairing:

`recto: engraved ruler portrait`
→ `verso: monthly historical-calendar typography from Thuano`.

This is not merely a seller's paraphrase of the book's contents.

## 2. 1733 complete-copy control

A complete 1733 Wappenkalender seller description reports:

- title and preface;
- twelve rulers/princes;
- each associated with one month's historical calendar `aus dem Thuano`;
- then the larger genealogical/heraldic apparatus.

On its own this wording was catalogue-level. The detached 1740s plate now supplies independent physical evidence that `Geschichts-Calender aus dem Thuano` is genuine book wording and that it occurs on the versos of ruler engravings.

## 3. Identity of `Thuano`

Authority records close the name identity.

Kalliope's authority record for **Jacques-Auguste de Thou (1553–1617)** includes among the controlled variant forms:

- `Thuanus, Jacques Auguste`;
- `Thuanus, Jacobus Augustus`;
- `Thuano, Jacopo Augusto`;
- `Thuanus`.

Early-modern German bibliographical prose likewise uses `Thuano` when referring to the famous historian de Thou.

The secure normalization is therefore:

`Thuano` = `Jacques-Auguste de Thou / Jacobus Augustus Thuanus`.

The source family is his *Historiarum sui temporis* / history of his own times. The present evidence establishes author/source identity; exact edition and page-level extraction for individual calendar entries remain to be reconstructed.

## 4. Historical operation

The Wappenkalender is not simply citing de Thou as an authority in a note. It is materially **reformatting history**.

The recoverable operation is:

`continuous learned historical narrative (Thuanus)`
→ `selection of dated events`
→ `month/day calendar sequence`
→ `typography on verso of ruler portrait`
→ `annual serial package with genealogy + heraldry + portraits + calendar`.

This is a stronger formulation than saying that the calendar contains a historical section. Historical prose is being decomposed and recomposed to fit a recurring chronological interface.

## 5. Relation to the frontispiece problem

This source operation makes the `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica` frontispiece more concrete.

Behind the allegorical threshold, the publication actually performs several distinct transformations:

- historical narrative → dated calendar entries;
- dynastic relations → genealogical tables;
- political identity/status → armorial plates;
- current rulers → serial portraits;
- all of these → annually updated and reordered package.

The frontispiece can therefore be read as labeling a **composite evidentiary/reference architecture**, not merely personifying three abstract disciplines.

This does not prove that Müller or Tyroff consciously designed the image in response to Thuanus. Keep iconographic intention separate from the demonstrated source operation.

## 6. Chronological scope presently controlled

- **1733** — complete-copy description explicitly reports twelve monthly historical calendars from `Thuano`.
- **c.1742** — detached ruler plate preserves direct verso wording `Aprilis. Geschichts-Calender aus dem Thuano`.

Thus the Thuanus-based monthly module persists beyond a single issue and belongs to the continuing serial architecture.

Earlier and later limits remain to be tested. Do not project the Thuanus source backwards to 1723–32 without direct evidence: those years include explicitly different annual historical modules (Bohemian, Austrian, French, Reich, peace, Danish, etc.).

## 7. Next bounded tests

1. Inspect the 1733 digital/physical copy and transcribe one month's `Geschichts-Calender aus dem Thuano`.
2. Match 2–3 dated entries against a specific edition of de Thou's *Historiarum sui temporis* to determine whether the compiler translates, abridges, rearranges, or copies an intermediary chronology.
3. Locate additional detached Wappenkalender portrait leaves with verso month headings to reconstruct the twelve-portrait/month sequence.
4. Determine the first and last issue that explicitly uses the `aus dem Thuano` module.
5. Keep source extraction, ruler portrait sequence, and Clio frontispiece genealogy as related but separately evidenced layers.
