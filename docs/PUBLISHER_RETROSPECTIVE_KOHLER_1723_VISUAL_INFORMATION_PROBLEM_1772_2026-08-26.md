# Publisher retrospective on Köhler 1723 as a visual-information solution — 1772 control, 26 August 2026

## Status and source layer

The opening `Vorbericht` to UB Tübingen `Fe 18-1`, *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch*, is a publisher-side retrospective written roughly half a century after the opening of the Köhler Wappencalender sequence. The Tübingen catalogue dates the digital carrier to 1771, while the scanned/OCR title page reads 1772; for the claims below, identify the source as the **1772 title-state `Vorbericht` in the Tübingen Fe 18-1 carrier**, while keeping catalogue metadata and literal title-page dating distinct.

Carrier: UB Tübingen OpenDigi `Fe 18-1`, DOI `10.20345/digitue.20819`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bsz:21-dt-208196`, `Vorbericht` 2r–3v.

This is **not** a 1723 Köhler self-description. It is valuable as an internal publisher genealogy of what problem the Wappencalender was understood to have solved by the early 1770s.

## The older armorial format is criticized as an information architecture

The `Vorbericht` first discusses the large Siebmacher tradition and its continuations. It then identifies several specific deficiencies.

### 1. Excessive compression makes the figures insufficiently legible

Because the earlier project attempted to include very many noble arms, its initial format was too small. The preface says the figures could not be shown clearly enough at that scale.

The problem is therefore not merely aesthetic engraving quality. It is a relation between **corpus size, plate format and visual discrimination**:

`more items forced into finite graphic space`
→ `smaller figures`
→ `reduced legibility`.

### 2. Tincture notation is treated as an unstable encoding system

The preface says blazoning had not yet been as determinate as in its own present, and that later attempts to indicate tinctures with letters caused disorder and misunderstanding.

This identifies a second information problem:

`colour information unavailable in monochrome print`
→ `alphabetic surrogate encoding`
→ `ambiguity / misunderstanding`.

The issue is not colour as decoration but reliable transmission of heraldically meaningful distinctions.

### 3. Scale/corpus density prevents descriptive text

The large number of included arms left no room for description. The preface therefore connects the small-figure problem to a text-image adjacency problem: a very broad visual census can crowd out the explanatory prose needed to identify and interpret individual arms correctly.

`maximum visual coverage`
↔ `space for explicit description`.

This is structurally close to later Gattererian concerns with bringing proposition, image/table and proof into a usable relation: information has to be spatially organized so distinctions can actually be read and checked.

### 4. Arms change over time

The preface adds that many coats of arms undergo changes and augmentations `von Zeit zu Zeit`.

This turns a static reference-book problem into a temporal one. A heraldic corpus is not merely incomplete at first publication; its represented objects themselves mutate. A useful Wappenbuch therefore needs a mechanism for **updating living heraldic information**.

The four problems can be summarized as:

`legibility`
+
`encoding determinacy`
+
`descriptive capacity`
+
`temporal update`
→ `need for a different publication architecture`.

## Köhler 1723 is explicitly presented as a remedy

After listing those defects, the publisher preface says that Johann David Köhler in **1723** undertook to remedy these inconveniences, at least with respect to princely arms.

That retrospective claim matters because it gives the Wappencalender a functional genealogy. The 1723 project is remembered not simply as another genealogical calendar but as a response to concrete defects in an older armorial information format.

Safe formulation:

> **By the 1772 publisher retrospective, Köhler's 1723 Wappencalender was understood as a technical response to the legibility, coding, descriptive and updating limits of earlier large armorial compilations.**

Do not promote this to Köhler's own 1723 intention until the opening preface of the 1723 annual or another contemporary statement is controlled.

## Relation to the 1772 cumulative Wappenbuch programme

The same `Vorbericht` then explains why even the long Wappencalender tradition no longer satisfied current demand and why the publisher undertook a larger cumulative Wappenbuch. This gives a striking serial genealogy of successive technical solutions:

`large early armorial compilation`
→ problems of compression, tincture coding, missing description, temporal change
→ `Köhler Wappencalender from 1723`
→ annual updating + larger/clearer/described princely armorial apparatus
→ continuing incompleteness and demand for wider current coverage
→ `multi-volume cumulative Wappenbuch issued in annual instalments`.

The publication history is therefore narrated as repeated re-engineering of an information system rather than a simple succession of titles.

## Analytical consequence

This source strengthens a project-wide distinction between **coverage** and **evidentiary usability**. A representation can contain more objects while making each object harder to discriminate; an encoding system can compress colour into letters while introducing ambiguity; annual publication can improve currency while fragmenting a corpus; cumulative numbering can restore long-range completeness while retaining annual delivery.

The Wappencalender/Wappenbuch lineage can thus be read as a sequence of trade-offs among:

- visual resolution;
- corpus breadth;
- text-image adjacency;
- coding reliability;
- temporal currency;
- cumulative completeness.

This is more precise than treating heraldic illustration as ornament or the annual calendar merely as a genre container.

## Evidence guards

1. The account is retrospective to 1723 and should not be quoted as Köhler's own programme without a contemporary Köhler source.
2. The literal scanned title page in the Tübingen carrier reads 1772 although catalogue metadata gives 1771; keep title-state year and catalogue year separate.
3. The preface's criticism of letter-coded tinctures is a historical actor judgment, not a universal claim that all such systems failed.
4. `Abzuhelfen` establishes that the writer interpreted Köhler 1723 as a remedy, but does not by itself prove which particular copperplates or layouts first implemented each remedy.
5. Plate-level reuse, dimensions and same-matrix continuity remain separate empirical questions.
