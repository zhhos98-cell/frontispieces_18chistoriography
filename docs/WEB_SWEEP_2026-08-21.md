# Web sweep — 21 August 2026

This note records the first systematic web sweep beyond the correspondence-derived seed. It is a research log, not a final bibliography. Claims are intentionally separated by evidence type.

## 1. Series extent is larger than the initial calendar/handbook pair

Authoritative serial catalogues establish the Weigel *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender* at Nürnberg for 1723–1757, with ZDB recording `1723 - 35.1757 nachgewiesen` and the successor title *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik*. HEIDI aggregates digital coverage at BSB/MDZ, ULB Düsseldorf and Rostock and supplies VD18 90286901 / ZDB 2749226-6 / K10plus 775920789.

The successor chain currently visible online is:

- **1723–1757** — *Der durchlauchtigen Welt ... Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender*, Weigel constellation.
- **1759–1763** — *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik*, associated with Johann Christoph Gatterer and Raspe; DDB/BSB records survive for 1759, 1761, 1762 and 1763.
- **1764–1766** — *Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender ... oder jährliches Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik*, Raspe/Gatterer; DDB/BSB records survive for 1764 and 1766.
- **1767** — Seyfart continuation, *Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender auf das Jahr 1767 ...*, with a surviving Klimo Library copy (PTE I.VII.7) and BSB/DDB trace.
- **1768 onward** — Seyfart-associated *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie* / material later reorganised as *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch*. DDB exposes 1768, 1769, 1770, 1771 and 1773 as related serial objects. Secondary heraldic bibliography extends Seyfart's work into the 1770s and warns that individual bound copies can differ in contents.

## 2. The Clio plate is demonstrably serial, but probably not original to the first issues

The sweep produced evidence for two distinct early frontispiece regimes.

### 1724: event-memory frontispiece

An antiquarian description of the 1724 issue calls the frontispiece a double-sided engraving **`Gedenckwürdigkeiten des 1722ten Jahres`**. A Polish auction catalogue independently describes the frontispiece as representations of the most important events of 1722. This should be treated as evidence that at least one early issue used an annual historical-memory image rather than the later `Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica.` allegory.

Status: **reported from copy descriptions; primary scan inspection pending**.

### By 1726: target `Historia Genealogia Heraldica` design is present

Auction imagery for a 1726 copy visibly includes the familiar allegorical frontispiece: winged History, Time examining a genealogical sheet, putti, heraldic material and the `Historia Genealogia Heraldica` title. This is currently the earliest web-located positive witness for the target design.

Status: **image-level web witness; institutional copy/scan still required for plate-state work**.

### 1735–1738: frontispiece is structurally stable, identity increasingly explicit

- 1735: Venator & Hanstein records one engraved frontispiece plus medal, tincture plate and 93 armorial engravings. Identity not stated.
- 1736: a Narrenschiff record labels the item `Historia Genealocica Heraldica` and records an engraved frontispiece. This is a seller description, so the exact transcription/plate identity still requires inspection.
- ca. 1737: a detached engraving survives on the antiquarian market under the full title `Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica. Testes temporum et veritatis`, 198 × 122 mm. It retains traces of red ink reportedly from the book to which it had been attached. This is useful evidence for **detachment / afterlife as a loose print** as well as for dimensions.
- 1738: several copy descriptions record an allegorical engraved frontispiece. A Dorotheum copy is explicitly described as **lacking the frontispiece**, demonstrating why edition-level and copy-level absence must be separated.

### 1744, 1759, 1764: explicit target title persists across publishing phases

- **1744**: a ZVAB/antiquarian copy explicitly records `1 gest. Frontispiz (= 'Historia Genealogia Heraldica')`.
- **1759**: an auction description of Gatterer's *Handbuch* explicitly transcribes the pretitular copperplate `HISTORIA GENEALOGIA HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORVM ET VERITATIS`.
- **1764**: an Invaluable copy of Gatterer's *Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender* explicitly calls the engraved frontispiece `Historia Genealogia Heraldica: Testes Temporum Et Veritatis`.

This already demonstrates survival of the design across at least three bibliographic phases: Weigel calendar → Gatterer handbook → Gatterer/Raspe continued calendar.

## 3. Immediate research problem: date the visual regime change

The strongest new question is therefore not simply “how often was the Clio plate reprinted?” It is:

> **When and why did the series replace an annual-event/memory frontispiece with the generalised Historia–Genealogia–Heraldica allegory?**

Current bracket from web evidence: **after the 1724 event-memory frontispiece and by the 1726 target-design witness**. The 1725 issue is therefore a priority object.

The transition may be analytically important because it potentially marks a shift from an image keyed to the previous year's memorable events to a reusable threshold image that abstracts the evidentiary operations of history, genealogy and heraldry themselves.

This is a hypothesis. It should only be promoted after direct inspection of 1723–1726 copies.

## 4. Copy-level evidence is already complicating a simple edition history

The web sweep surfaced material evidence that should be preserved as corpus data rather than relegated to bibliography:

- a 1738 Dorotheum copy lacks its frontispiece and flyleaves;
- a detached ca. 1737 frontispiece circulates independently on the antiquarian market;
- a 1751 copy is described with 114 armorial engravings and an engraved frontispiece;
- early owners annotated genealogical tables in several antiquarian copies;
- a nineteenth-/twentieth-century collection catalogue describes armorial sheets cut from a 1740 calendar and pasted into manuscript `Wapen Sammlung` volumes;
- loose armorial leaves from ca. 1725 circulate today as independent prints, with the genealogical table still printed on the verso.

These are strong reasons to retain `copy`, `detached_plate`, `rebound_composite`, and `cut_and_reused` as distinct material states.

## 5. Numbering anomaly: preserve, do not repair

ZDB/DNB give the series as `1723 - 35.1757`. The normal sequence implies 1738 = 16, 1744 = 22, 1751 = 29, all of which are independently confirmed in catalogue descriptions.

However, a current Kiefer listing describes the **1755** issue as `zum vier und dreysigstenmal` (34th), while another listing gives **1756** as 34th; secondary heraldic bibliography claims no 1755 issue after Köhler's death, whereas ZDB/DNB and other holdings evidence point to a 1755 object. This discrepancy is not normalised away. It is entered as a numbering/cataloguing problem requiring title-page inspection.

## 6. Extension beyond 1766: Seyfart changes the object logic

Secondary but unusually specific historiographical evidence reports that under Johann Friedrich Seyfart the annual publication intentionally supplied **different arms each year**, with the plates/text separately numbered so that the run could accumulate into a general armorial. A 2024 editorial warning further notes that surviving volumes can differ in contents and binding configuration.

This makes the 1767+ continuation directly relevant to the corpus even if the Clio frontispiece eventually disappears: the serial publication itself becomes a cumulative, rearrangeable visual apparatus.

A Russian scholarly study located in the sweep is especially promising because it explicitly distinguishes **the same heraldic design re-engraved onto a new plate** and tracks reuse of that new plate into 1772. This is exactly the distinction encoded by this repository's `same_plate_as` / `variant_of` / `derived_from` policy and should be followed up bibliographically.

## 7. Cross-domain controls becoming more important

The GNM seed already supplied several controls. The web sweep strengthens the case for Johann David Köhler's **Historische Münz-Belustigung** (weekly from 1729) as a parallel apparatus: rare coins are engraved, serially issued, described, and historically explained. This sits unusually close to the coin/medal component of the Wappenkalender and to the later Gatterer discourse of testing historical truth.

Priority controls remain:

1. `Gedächtnuß-hülffliche Bilder-Lust` (1696/1697) and `Sculptura Historiarum Et Temporum Memoratrix` (1698): visual memory / chronology before the calendar series.
2. Köhler's *Historische Münz-Belustigung* (from 1729): serial numismatic evidence under the same editor-network.
3. Oetter, rare coin tract (1748): Weigel-heirs imprint + Martin Tyroff attribution.
4. Rösel, *Historia Naturalis Ranarum Nostratium* (1758): Tyroff engraving in natural history.
5. *Die Grösse und Mannigfaltigkeit in den Reichen der Natur und Sitten* (1766): cross-domain serial engraving under the Tyroff/Weigel afterlife.

The GNM list itself records the 1696/1697 mnemonic-history prints and 1698 *Sculptura* as Weigel-linked objects, the 1748 Oetter coin tract with Martin Tyroff as illustrator, and the 1758 frog natural history with M. Tyroff as engraver.

## 8. Attribution warning

The current research draft records Gabriel Müller as designer and Martin Tyroff as engraver for the Clio series. This web sweep found repeated support for the **Martin Tyroff association** in image/catalogue metadata, but did **not** locate an independent web source corroborating Gabriel Müller. Some antiquarian metadata instead attributes the detached print to Johann Christoph Weigel, despite dating it after his death; this is internally problematic and illustrates the danger of collapsing designer, engraver, publisher and issuer.

Therefore:

- do not delete the Müller attribution from the seed;
- keep it `provisional` and explicitly mark **external corroboration not yet found**;
- do not promote antiquarian Weigel attribution to authority evidence;
- inspect signatures on high-resolution institutional scans before resolving production roles.
