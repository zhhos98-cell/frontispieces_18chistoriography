# Calendar host year vs imprint-year search contamination, 1754–1755 — 26 August 2026

## Problem

Image and dealer search results for eighteenth-century annual calendars frequently index a copy by its printing/imprint year rather than the calendar year printed on the title page. This creates false year assignments in frontispiece work.

A concrete case is the Jasper52 / Lot-Art photographed copy whose title page explicitly reads the Wappenkalender **for 1755**. The market record gives Nürnberg, Bieling für Weigel, `[1754]`, because the annual was printed/published in the preceding year. Image search therefore surfaces this **1755 host** under queries for `1754 Wappenkalender frontispiece`.

The photographed opening is Type C, but it is evidence for the **1755 calendar host**, not for the 1754 annual.

## Required year hierarchy

For this corpus distinguish:

1. **host/calendar year** — the year named on the annual's title page (`auf das Jahr ... 1755`);
2. **imprint/printing year** — often the preceding civil year, supplied explicitly or inferred/catalogued in brackets (`[1754]`);
3. **catalogue/index year** — the date used by a dealer, auction platform, metadata provider or search engine;
4. **sale/digitisation date** — modern event metadata with no evidentiary bearing on annual identity.

For composition chronology, the default key is **host/calendar year** unless a research question explicitly concerns printing chronology.

## Consequences

- Do not promote a search result to a 1754 frontispiece witness merely because the indexed publication year is 1754.
- Require the title page, explicit annual wording, or an unambiguous institutional host identifier before assigning composition evidence to an annual year.
- Keep the known Jasper52/Lot-Art Type C image assigned to 1755.
- The genuine 1754 frontispiece remains unresolved at direct-image level despite an institutional BSB carrier and the NUK Ljubljana physical-copy control.

## Related catalogue anomaly

This year-layer problem compounds the separate Kiefer ordinal-transcription anomaly: current dealer metadata can simultaneously mis-handle the issue ordinal and the distinction between calendar year and printing year. See `docs/KIEFER_1755_ORDINAL_TRANSCRIPTION_ANOMALY_2026-08-26.md`.

## Guardrail

In every market-image record store calendar host year and imprint year separately when both are known. Never infer host year from the modern search result label alone.
