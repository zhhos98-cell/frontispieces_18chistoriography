# Winkelmann 1695: Haes's `Mystere` of delay, Landgrave Karl's return, and court-dependent closure

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE CONTEMPORARY PRODUCTION-BOTTLENECK CHECKPOINT — HAES DIRECTLY LINKS THE CHRONICLE'S PROTRACTED DELAY TO AN UNEXPLAINED `MYSTERE`, HIS OWN INTENDED INTERVENTION, AND THE RETURN OF LANDGRAVE KARL; THE UNDERLYING BOTTLENECK REMAINS UNIDENTIFIED

## Result

The Haes–Leibniz correspondence contains a second, later and operationally stronger contemporary statement about the production of Johann Just Winkelmann's Hessian Chronicle.

On **26 August / 5 September 1695**, Johann Sebastian Haes wrote from **Kassel** to Leibniz:

> `Touchant l'histoire de Mr Winckelman il y a une espece de Mystere dans sa lenteur, selon toutes les apparences, et Je tacheray de faire en sorte, au retour de S. A. S. que la chose trouve sa fin si tot qu'il sera possible.`

The Akademie-Ausgabe editors identify `l'histoire` as Winkelmann's *Gründliche und warhafte Beschreibung der Fürstenthümer Hessen und Hersfeld*, whose first five parts appeared in 1697.

They also identify `S. A. S.` as **Landgrave Karl of Hesse-Kassel**, then away as commander of Hessian forces during the fighting for the citadel of Namur in late August–early September 1695.

Source:

- Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, *Sämtliche Schriften und Briefe*, Reihe III, Bd. 6, no. 159, Johann Sebastian Haes an Leibniz, Kassel, 26 Aug./5 Sept. 1695, printed pp. 490–491; manuscript `LBr. 350 Bl. 80–81`.

Direct digital edition:

https://bobson.ludost.net/gwvl/www.gwlb.de/Leibniz/Leibnizarchiv/Veroeffentlichungen/III6B.pdf

The current browser extracted the critical text, document metadata, manuscript shelfmark, and editorial notes directly from the PDF. The page-image screenshot endpoint returned a cache error, so no page-image claim is made beyond the critical-edition transcription.

Safe translation:

> As for Mr Winckelmann's history, there appears to be a kind of mystery in its slowness; I shall try, when His Serene Highness returns, to arrange matters so that the affair reaches its end as soon as possible.

This is the strongest direct contemporary evidence yet recovered for a **court-dependent production bottleneck close to publication**.

---

## 1. What the letter directly controls

The sentence contains four distinct operational facts.

### A. Delay is itself an observed problem

Haes does not merely say the work is unfinished. He names its `lenteur` — its slowness/protracted pace — as something requiring explanation.

### B. The cause is opaque even to an insider

`une espece de Mystere`

indicates that Haes perceived some hidden, unclear, or difficult-to-account-for element behind the delay.

This is a report of **opacity**, not a disclosure of the cause.

### C. Haes intends to intervene

`Je tacheray de faire en sorte`

places Haes himself in an intended mediating/coordinating role. Unlike the 1692 indefinite `on assiste`, the 1695 statement gives us a named actor claiming that he will try to bring about a resolution.

### D. Resolution is tied to the Landgrave's return

`au retour de S. A. S.`

makes Landgrave Karl's physical/political availability a condition of Haes's intended intervention.

The desired outcome is explicitly closure:

`que la chose trouve sa fin si tot qu'il sera possible`.

Safe operational sequence:

`PROTRACTED DELAY`
→ `CAUSE PERCEIVED AS OPAQUE / MYSTERIOUS`
→ `WAIT FOR LANDGRAVE KARL'S RETURN`
→ `HAES MEDIATION / INTERVENTION`
→ `ATTEMPT TO BRING THE MATTER TO AN END`.

## 2. This is not yet an explanation of the `Mystere`

The word `Mystere` is analytically tempting because the repository already controls decades of:

- bilateral Hessian court review;
- 1686 six-member censorship/revision commission;
- 1687 prescribed rules and rule-grounded notes;
- confessional and dynastic stakes;
- criticism of method/source practice;
- continued source supply;
- printing finance and distribution under Kassel leadership.

But Haes does not specify which of these caused the 1695 delay.

Do **not** promote any of the following without further evidence:

- `Mystere = censorship dispute`;
- `Mystere = Landgrave Karl personally withheld permission`;
- `Mystere = financial blockage`;
- `Mystere = missing sources`;
- `Mystere = unfinished sixth part`;
- `Mystere = Winkelmann's own incapacity or reluctance`.

Current status:

`PRODUCTION BOTTLENECK = CONTROLLED`

`BOTTLENECK CAUSE = UNRESOLVED`.

## 3. Relation to Haes's 1692 report

Three years earlier, on 13/23 October 1692, Haes had written:

`On assiste encore Mr Winckelman pour la perfection de son ouvrage ...`

and expressed the hope that Winkelmann would be able to finish it himself.

The two letters create a substantial longitudinal control:

### 1692

`continued plural/indefinite assistance`
+
`authorial completion still open`.

### 1695

`slowness now explicitly problematized`
+
`cause perceived as mysterious/opaque`
+
`Haes intends active mediation`
+
`resolution awaits Landgrave Karl's return`.

Therefore the 1692 sentence is **not an isolated status report**. Haes remained attentive to the Chronicle's production problem for at least three years and, by 1695, described himself as prepared to intervene.

Safe longer sequence:

`1692 ASSISTED PERFECTION`
→ `1695 COURT-DEPENDENT CLOSURE ATTEMPT`
→ `1697 FIVE-PART PRINT STABILIZATION`.

## 4. Haes's institutional position sharpens the evidence

Johann Sebastian Haes/Haas (1641–1697) served the Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel as cabinet secretary, archivist, and librarian.

In the opening portion of the same 1695 letter he explains that his long silence toward Pierre Morel was caused partly by `diverses occupations tres speculatives pour S. A. S. mon Maître` — various highly intellectual/speculative occupations for his princely master — and discusses communicating medal impressions to Morel.

The letter therefore places the Winkelmann remark inside Haes's normal activity as a courtly knowledge intermediary.

His planned intervention is institutionally plausible without requiring us to invent a formal office for him in the Chronicle project.

Evidence status:

`HAES AS COURT KNOWLEDGE INTERMEDIARY = STRONGLY CONTROLLED`.

`HAES AS FORMAL CHRONICLE CENSOR/EDITOR = NOT ESTABLISHED`.

---

## 5. Landgrave Karl becomes a contemporaneously visible production node

Later scholarship already controls Karl's role in financing the Chronicle's printing and Kassel's increasing leadership in the project.

The 1695 letter adds a contemporary witness that production closure itself was expected to be addressed after Karl's return.

This lets the repository move from a later reconstructed relation:

`LANDGRAVE / COURT PATRONAGE → PRINTING FINANCE`

to a contemporaneously witnessed process relation:

`LANDGRAVE AVAILABILITY`
→ `HAES'S PLANNED INTERVENTION`
→ `ATTEMPTED PROJECT CLOSURE`.

Still, the exact decision Karl needed to make is unresolved.

Do not collapse:

`court-dependent closure`
into
`personal princely authorship/editorial control`.

## 6. Revised preprint production chronology

The controlled sequence is now unusually dense:

### 1686

six-person Marburg/Gießen commission, multi-month fundamental revision.

### 1687

Marburg `Ms. 309`, 180 leaves of prescribed rules and notes grounded on them.

### 1690

Leibniz excerpts from unpublished Winkelmann material; Hessian Chronicle excerpts routed with `Geographica`.

### 1691

Leibniz records the six-part `chrono-stemmato-polemographia principum` project among Winkelmann's still-promised publications.

### 1692

Haes reports that people are **still assisting** Winkelmann toward the `perfection` of the work and hopes he can finish it himself.

### 1695

Haes calls the work's slowness a kind of `Mystere` and intends, after Landgrave Karl returns, to bring the matter to an end as soon as possible.

### 1697

five of six projected parts finally stabilize in print; the title publicly claims the most credible documents and writers.

Current model:

`FORMAL REVISION`
→ `RULE-GOVERNED NOTES`
↔ `DISTRIBUTED MANUSCRIPT / EXCERPT CIRCULATION`
→ `ASSISTED PERFECTION`
→ `COURT-DEPENDENT CLOSURE ATTEMPT`
→ `TEMPORARY FIVE-PART PRINT STABILIZATION`.

This is now a directly evidenced production history rather than a reconstructed blank interval between censorship and print.

## 7. Article-level analytical payoff

The Chronicle's long delay should no longer be treated merely as failed publication or authorial procrastination.

The surviving evidence shows repeated **coordination problems** among:

- authorial work;
- expert revision;
- documentary/source infrastructures;
- court authority;
- archival/bibliographic intermediaries;
- publication closure.

A stronger proposition becomes available:

> In this case, historical publication was not simply the terminal conversion of a finished manuscript into print. “Finishing” the work was itself a socially and institutionally distributed operation, and contemporaries could perceive the reason for delay as opaque even from inside the courtly information network.

This is especially relevant to the frontispiece repository because it supplies a textual-production analogue for the distinction between stable public objects and distributed material histories.

### B. Identify the non-surviving Leibniz letters around this exchange

The critical edition notes that no.159 answered a lost Leibniz letter from August 1695 and was itself answered by another lost Leibniz letter to which his Papin letter of 9 September was attached.

Thus Leibniz may have asked explicitly about the Chronicle's delay.

Search indirect references in later correspondence for the content of those lost letters.

### C. Compare with the January 1696 Winkelmann–Leibniz letter

The Akademie correspondence chronology contains a surviving letter from Winkelmann to Leibniz dated **19 January 1696**, from Bremen.

This is now a priority primary witness because it falls only four months after Haes's intended intervention and less than two years before publication.

Test whether Winkelmann discusses:

- Chronicle progress;
- court constraints;
- source supply;
- printing;
- health/ability to complete;
- sixth part;
