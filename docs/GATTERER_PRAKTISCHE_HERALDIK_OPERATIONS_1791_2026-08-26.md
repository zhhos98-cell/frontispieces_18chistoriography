# Gatterer 1791: practical heraldry as four operations

## Status

The structure of Johann Christoph Gatterer's *Praktische Heraldik* (Nürnberg, 1791) can now be controlled closely enough that the existing manual task should be reduced to a **one-page primary wording check**, not an exploratory read.

Established at bibliographic/searchable-text level:

## 2. Four-operation taxonomy

A heraldic reference page explicitly introduces the quotation as Gatterer's 1791 summary of the practical work of a heraldist and transcribes:

> `Alle praktische Arbeit des Heraldikers lassen sich unter vier Klassen bringen: Er jedes gegebene Wappen Blasonieren, Historisieren und Kritisieren, und dann neue Wappen erfinden oder Aufreissen können.`

The reference page itself contains a date typo in the attribution label (`1771`) even while introducing the statement as 1791. The repository chronology should therefore continue to anchor the taxonomy in *Praktische Heraldik* **1791**, not in the 1771 pedagogical preface.

Analytically the operations are:

`given coat of arms`
→ `Blasonieren` (describe according to heraldic grammar)
→ `Historisieren` (reconstruct historical trajectory)
→ `Kritisieren` (judge authenticity/correctness)

and, on the productive side:

`new coat of arms`
→ `Erfinden / Aufreissen` (design/draw).

This division matters because it separates description, diachronic reconstruction, criticism and graphic production as different learned acts.

## 3. `Historisieren` as historical trajectory

A later instructional text in the Gattererian tradition defines the operation very sharply:

> `Historisiren heißt bei der Wappenkunde, die Geschichte eines Wappens, seinen Ursprung und die Veränderungen erklären, die es erlitten.`

The same passage says the heraldist uses historical sources to prove, for example, when the imperial eagle became double-headed and when particular figures entered royal arms. This is reception evidence, not a direct quotation from the 1791 page, but it fixes the operational meaning around which the later literature understood Gatterer's category.

The safe formulation for the project is therefore:

> In 1791 Gatterer explicitly formalized practical heraldry into separate operations including `Historisieren`; contemporary/direct-primary wording of its exact definition still needs the opening-page scan, while later reception consistently understands the operation as reconstructing a coat of arms' origin and transformations through historical evidence.
