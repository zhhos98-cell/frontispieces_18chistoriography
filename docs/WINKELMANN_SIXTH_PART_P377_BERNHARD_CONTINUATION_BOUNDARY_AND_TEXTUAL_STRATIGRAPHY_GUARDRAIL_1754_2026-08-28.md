# Winkelmann sixth part, 1754: p.377 Bernhard continuation boundary and textual-stratigraphy guardrail

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE ATTRIBUTION-BOUNDARY CHECKPOINT — ADB EXPLICITLY ATTRIBUTES THE CONTINUATION FROM P.377 OF THE PRINTED SIXTH PART TO JOHANN ADAM BERNHARD; MODERN FUCHS/RABELER RECONSTRUCTION CONTROLS BROADER MANUSCRIPT LOSS/REPLACEMENT. THE TWO CLAIMS ARE COMPATIBLE BUT MUST NOT BE COLLAPSED INTO A SIMPLE `P.1–376 = PURE WINKELMANN / P.377–490 = PURE BERNHARD` MODEL WITHOUT DIRECT TEXTUAL CONTROL.

## Result

A historical biographical source gives a specific internal attribution boundary inside the 1754 sixth part.

The *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* entry for Johann Just Winckelmann states:

`Der letzte (sechste) Theil ... ist aber nur zum Theil von W. verfaßt worden — die Fortsetzung (von S. 377 an) rührt von dem hanauischen Archivar Bernhard her`.

Source:

- Ludwig Curtze, `Winckelmann, Johann Just`, *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* 43 (1898), via Deutsche Biographie: https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd104280883.html

This supplies an explicit historical attribution:

`1754 PRINT P.377ff = BERNHARD CONTINUATION`.

A much later reconstruction by Peter Rabeler, explicitly citing Thomas Fuchs, states at a different evidentiary level that Winkelmann's sixth part was largely complete at his death, but that by 1754 a large part of the original manuscript was no longer findable and had to be replaced by texts of Johann Adam Bernhard.

Source:

- Peter Rabeler, `Darstellungen und Wahrnehmungen einer Residenzstadt in Texten und Bildern`, p.472, citing Fuchs 2002, pp.20–21, 305.

The two controls are compatible but not identical:

`ADB = explicit printed-page continuation attribution from p.377`

`FUCHS/RABELER = source-state account of manuscript loss and Bernhard replacement`.

Do not use one as a substitute for the other.

---

## 1. What p.377 does and does not establish

Promote:

`P.377ff BERNHARD CONTINUATION ATTRIBUTION = CONTROLLED HISTORICAL SCHOLARLY CLAIM`.

Do not yet promote:

- every word on pp.377–490 is newly composed by Bernhard;
- pp.1–376 are entirely Winkelmann's untouched authorial text;
- p.377 is the physical point at which a new printer's manuscript begins;
- p.377 corresponds to the moment where the lost authorial manuscript ran out;
- Bernhard had no editorial intervention before p.377;
- Bernhard's continuation contains no inherited Winkelmann notes, excerpts, headings or structure.

The ADB wording `Fortsetzung` is a strong attribution label, not a codicological description of printer's copy.

---

## 2. Modern manuscript-loss reconstruction changes how the boundary should be read

Rabeler/Fuchs controls that:

- the sixth part was `weitgehend abgeschlossen` at Winkelmann's death;
- a large part of the original manuscript was unavailable by 1754;
- missing material was replaced through Bernhard texts.

Therefore p.377 should initially be modeled as an **editorial attribution boundary inside a reconstructed print**, not automatically as the boundary between two pristine authorial manuscripts.

Possible structures include:

### Model A — clean continuation

Winkelmann text survives through p.376; Bernhard independently continues from p.377.

### Model B — edited first zone + Bernhard continuation

Pp.1–376 preserve substantially Winkelmann material but were normalized/supplemented by later editors; p.377 onward becomes principally Bernhard continuation.

### Model C — composite throughout, dominant-author shift at p.377

Earlier pages combine surviving Winkelmann material and editorial replacement; from p.377 Bernhard becomes the principal textual author.

Current evidence does not distinguish these models.

That does not prove every sentence on those pages is Winkelmann-authored, but it proves that the 1754 reconstructed state preserves pre-1697 textual matter in its early zone.

Thus a first stratigraphic model becomes possible:

`pp.10–96 selected passages = PREPRINT CONTINUITY CONTROLLED`

`pp.97–376 = ATTRIBUTION / RECONSTRUCTION MIX UNRESOLVED`

`p.377ff = BERNHARD CONTINUATION ATTRIBUTED BY ADB`.

## 4. Bernhard's historiographical persona makes the boundary analytically substantive

The ADB entry for Johann Adam Bernhard characterizes him as a Hessian historian who sought to remove chronicle fables and court-genealogical inventions and instead ground territorial history in better source writings and documents.

It also explicitly says that he completed Winckelmann's *Hessische Chronik bis auf L. Philipp den Großmüthigen*, whose sixth part appeared in Kassel in 1754.

Source:

- Karl Bernhardi, `Bernhard, Johann Adam`, *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie* 2 (1875), p.458, via Deutsche Biographie: https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz4021.html

Bernhard had entered the Hanau archive after Hanau-Münzenberg passed to Hesse-Kassel in 1736 and worked there until his death in 1771.

This means the p.377 boundary potentially marks not merely a change of hand but a change in **archival and historiographical production environment**:

`WINKELMANN LATE-SEVENTEENTH-CENTURY PROJECT`
→ `BERNHARD MID-EIGHTEENTH-CENTURY HANAU ARCHIVAL HISTORIOGRAPHY`.

Direct textual comparison is required before converting this contextual difference into claims about style or method on particular pages.

## 5. The same public project identity spans the attribution boundary

The printed object remains titled:

`Johann Just Winkelmanns Sechster Theil ...`

and retains the evidentiary formula:

`Aus glaubwürdigen Documenten und Scribenten ordentlich verfasset`.

Thus p.377 is internal to a book whose public title does not advertise a switch of principal textual author.

Current relation:

`PUBLIC AUTHORSHIP / PROJECT IDENTITY = CONTINUOUS`

while

`TEXTUAL ATTRIBUTION = DISCONTINUOUS`.

This is precisely the kind of layered identity problem central to the repository.

Then compare:

`p.376 ending`

A visible structural break at p.377 would materially strengthen the ADB attribution; absence of a break would suggest a smoother editorial integration.

## 7. Textual-stratigraphy promotion rules

Use four statuses:

### `preprint_continuity_controlled`
A 1754 passage can be aligned with a pre-1699 Winkelmann/Leibniz witness.

### `historically_attributed_winkelmann_zone`
Falls before p.377 under the ADB model but lacks direct early witness.

### `historically_attributed_bernhard_continuation`
Falls from p.377 onward under the ADB attribution but lacks manuscript-level Bernhard control.

### `direct_bernhard_text_controlled`
Requires a Bernhard manuscript/draft/excerpt directly aligned with the 1754 passage.

Do not jump from the third to the fourth status without manuscript evidence.
