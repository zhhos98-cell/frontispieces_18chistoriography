# Winkelmann 1692: Haes on continued assistance toward the `perfection` of the Hessian Chronicle

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE CONTEMPORARY PREPRINT-PRODUCTION CHECKPOINT — A KASSEL COURT/ARCHIVE INSIDER DIRECTLY REPORTS IN OCTOBER 1692 THAT WINKELMANN WAS STILL BEING ASSISTED TOWARD THE PERFECTION OF THE WORK LATER PRINTED IN 1697; ACTORS, OPERATIONS, AND RELATION TO THE 1686 COMMISSION REMAIN UNRESOLVED

## Result

A contemporary letter now directly controls that the production of Johann Just Winkelmann's Hessian Chronicle remained an **assisted and unfinished process in late 1692**, five years before the first print stabilization of 1697.

On **13/23 October 1692**, Johann Sebastian Haes wrote from **Kassel** to Leibniz:

> `On assiste encore Mr Winckelman pour la perfection de son ouvrage et J'espere que Dieu luy fera la grace de l'achever luy même.`

The Akademie-Ausgabe editors explicitly identify `son ouvrage` as Winkelmann's later:

*Gründliche und warhafte Beschreibung der Fürstenthümer Hessen und Hersfeld*,

whose first five parts appeared only in 1697.

Source:

- Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, *Sämtliche Schriften und Briefe*, Reihe III, Bd. 5, no. 112, Johann Sebastian Haes an Leibniz, Kassel, 13/23 Oct. 1692, printed pp. 412–415, especially p. 414; manuscript source `LBr. 350 Bl. 19–20`.

Direct digital edition:

https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2441/III5B.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2

The critical edition's page-image endpoint could not be rendered by the current browser cache, but the PDF's machine-readable critical text, document metadata, manuscript transmission note, line-level passage, and editorial identification were directly retrieved in the current sweep.

Current safe translation:

> People are still assisting Mr Winckelmann toward the perfection/completion of his work, and I hope God grants him the grace to finish it himself.

The indefinite French `on` is important. Haes does **not** identify who is assisting, what form the assistance takes, or whether it is identical with the formal 1686 censorship commission.

Therefore:

`CONTINUED ASSISTANCE IN 1692 = CONTROLLED`

but

`SAME SIX 1686 COMMISSIONERS = NOT ESTABLISHED`.

## 1. Haes is an unusually well-positioned witness

Haes is not merely an external correspondent passing literary gossip.

Independent biographical/editorial controls identify Johann Sebastian Haes/Haas (1641–1697) as a **cabinet secretary, archivist, and librarian to the Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel**. He served as an embassy secretary at Nijmegen in 1678 and maintained a substantial correspondence with Leibniz.

Kalliope catalogues the surviving Haes–Leibniz correspondence as:

`LBr. 350`

Kassel/Hannover, 30 July 1691–20 August 1696, **109 leaves**, mainly French.

A September 1692 letter in the same critical-edition volume places Haes directly in the Kassel court environment and records that he had himself worked on numismatic questions for `S. A. S. Monseigr Le Landgrave`.

Thus his October statement about assistance to Winkelmann should be treated as a **contemporary report from within the Kassel court/archive knowledge infrastructure**.

This still does not make Haes one of the censors or assistants. His evidentiary role is currently:

`INFORMED CONTEMPORARY COURT/ARCHIVE WITNESS`.

Sources:

- Kalliope, `Briefwechsel Johann Sebastian Haes mit Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz`, `LBr. 350`.
- Huygens, *Oeuvres complètes*, editorial note identifying Haes as Kassel cabinet secretary, archivist, and librarian.
- Leibniz Akademie-Ausgabe III,5, no. 105, Haes an Leibniz, Kassel, 15/25 Sept. 1692.

## 2. The verb phrase matters: `assiste encore ... pour la perfection`

The report is operationally stronger than a statement that the book was merely still unpublished.

It contains two separate temporal/process markers:

- `encore` — assistance was **still** continuing;
- `pour la perfection de son ouvrage` — that assistance was directed toward bringing the work to a more complete/perfected state.

The next clause:

`J'espere ... qu'il ... l'achever luy même`

shows that the work remained unfinished enough for Winkelmann's own ability to complete it to be a live concern.

Safe production-state inference:

`WORK NOT YET STABILIZED`
+
`EXTERNAL/PLURAL ASSISTANCE STILL ACTIVE`
+
`AUTHORIAL COMPLETION STILL OPEN`
→ `1692 ONGOING PREPRINT PRODUCTION`.

Do not translate `perfection` too narrowly as stylistic polishing. In this context the word can carry completion/improvement without specifying whether the operation concerned source supply, correction, censorship, editorial work, copying, documentary verification, financing, or some combination.

## 3. Relation to the 1686–87 revision regime

The repository already controls:

### 1686

Thomas Fuchs's reconstruction of a six-member censorship/revision commission:

- three Marburg professors;
- three Gießen professors;
- several months of work;
- fundamental revision of the Chronicle;
- dynastic and epistemic/source-critical stakes.

### 1687

Marburg `Ms. 309`, 180 leaves:

`Fragmente derjenigen Regeln und sich darauf gründende Noten ...`

made/prescribed by Kassel and Darmstadt commissioners appointed to revise the Winckelmann Chronicle.

### 1692

Haes:

`On assiste encore Mr Winckelman pour la perfection de son ouvrage`.

Chronologically this supports:

`1686 FORMAL COMMISSIONED REVISION`
→ `1687 RULES + RULE-GROUNDED NOTES WITNESS`
→ `1692 ASSISTED PRODUCTION STILL CONTINUING`.

What remains unsafe is a personnel or institutional identity equation:

`1686 six-member commission`
=
`1687 Ms.309 commissioners`
=
`1692 unnamed assistants`.

The evidence currently supports **continuity of an assisted/revised production regime**, not uninterrupted identity of the actors or administrative body.

## 4. 1690–92 fills the gap between censorship event and print

The Leibniz working archive supplies two additional intervening controls.

### June 1690 — unpublished Winkelmann material is circulating into Leibniz's working files

Leibniz-Katalog records:

- `Ms 23, 47(1) Bl.124` / ID 5959: an excerpt by Leibniz from Winkelmann's unpublished manuscript `De dynastiis`;
- `Ms 23, 47(1)` / ID 7139: `Winkelman ex excerpt. Heßischer Chronick. liegen bey Geograf.`

The latter is an operational cross-reference locating excerpts from the Hessian Chronicle with `Geographica`.

Thus by 1690 the still-unprinted Winkelmann source world was already partially mobile outside the authorial codex.

### c. April 1691 — the six-part project is still prospective

Leibniz's autograph note `Ms 12, 713h Bl. 93`, ID 5924, records among works Winkelmann still promised to publish:

`Ejusdem chrono<stemmato-polemografiam principum> partibus sex`.

This controls the six-part project architecture before the 1697 title page.

### October 1692 — assistance continues

Haes now directly reports the continuing assistance toward perfection/completion.

The strongest current sequence is therefore:

`1686 commissioned multi-month revision`
→ `1687 rule-governed revision witness`
→ `1690 manuscript/excerpt circulation and problem-oriented routing`
→ `1691 six-part project still listed among promised publications`
→ `1692 continuing assistance toward perfection/completion`
→ `1697 five-part temporary print stabilization`.

This sequence does not require a model of a closed printer's manuscript waiting passively for publication.

## 5. The adjacent Philip the Magnanimous passage is a guardrail, not a merger

Immediately after the Winkelmann sentence, Haes writes that what Leibniz had said about a work on the history of **Philip the Magnanimous** was presently very secret.

The critical editors identify this second project as a planned history by **F. A. Flemmer**, known to Leibniz through his correspondence with Paullini; that work did not appear.

Therefore:

`WINKELMANN CHRONICLE`
≠ `SECRET PHILIP THE MAGNANIMOUS PROJECT`.

The juxtaposition is nevertheless contextually useful. Haes is reporting from Kassel on multiple Hessian historical-writing projects in the same letter, one still being assisted toward completion and another currently secret.

Safe interpretation:

`HESSIAN COURT HISTORIOGRAPHICAL PROJECT ECOLOGY = VISIBLE`.

Do not use adjacency alone to infer shared personnel, archive packets, or political purpose.

## 6. Relation to the distributed revision model

The earlier repository model was:

`whole written source / authorial manuscript`
→ `excerpt reservoir`
→ `problem-file routing`
↔ `court/archive source supply`
↔ `joint princely review / censorship`
↔ `authorial revision workspace`

The Haes letter adds a contemporaneous process-status layer:

`ASSISTED PERFECTION / COMPLETION`.

A refined model is:

`formal review and prescribed criteria`
→ `rule-grounded revision notes`
↔ `distributed manuscript/excerpt circulation`
↔ `continued assistance / source or editorial support`
↔ `authorial completion`
→ `temporary print stabilization`.

This is historically stronger than describing the years 1687–97 as a blank delay between censorship and printing.

## 7. Relation to the 1697 public evidentiary persona

The 1697 title later claims the work was composed:

`Aus den glaubwürdigsten Documenten und Scribenten in Sechs Theilen verfasset`

while explicitly publishing only five parts for the present.

The chronology is now:

`1686–87 external adjudication/revision`
→ `1690 distributed access to unpublished materials`
→ `1691 six-part project still prospective`
→ `1692 continued assistance toward perfection/completion`
→ `1697 public claim to credible documents/writers + five-part publication`.

This makes the title's stabilized authorial singularity especially interesting: a book publicly attributed to Winkelmann and presented through source credibility emerged from a production history that contemporaries themselves describe in plural/assisted terms.

Safe proposition:

> The 1697 authorial and evidentiary self-presentation was the public face of a work whose preprint production remained materially distributed and socially assisted into the 1690s.

Still unsafe:

> Haes's 1692 `assiste` directly refers to the same source-critical corrections that generated the phrase `glaubwürdigsten Documenten und Scribenten`.

That requires item-level textual concordance.
