# Weidler 2026 lot 8009: a ca.1773 Clio-bearing handbook candidate

## Result

Auktionshaus Weidler's July 2026 catalogue records, inside mixed lot 8009, one eighteenth-century object as:

> `1 Wappenbuch "Historia Genealogia Heraldica/Testes Temporum et Veritatis" wohl um 1773 mit zahlreichen Wappendarstellungen und Stammtafeln`

This is a high-information late Clio occurrence, but the auction description should **not** yet be normalized as a 1773 Seyfart *Wappenbuch* witness. The wording strongly suggests that the cataloguer used the engraved frontispiece inscription as the book's identifying title. The same year 1773 contains at least two distinct Raspe/Seyfart bibliographic objects, and only one of them naturally matches the auction's emphasis on `Stammtafeln`.

The current best identification is therefore:

> **Weidler lot 8009 is a catalogue-verified physical book containing or headed by the Clio inscription and judged by the auction house to date from ca.1773. Its probable bibliographic host is Seyfart's 1773 *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie*, but that host identification remains a research inference until an opening leaf/title page is inspected.**

If confirmed, this would extend positive Clio persistence from the explicit 1764 control into the Seyfart genealogy-handbook line around 1773.

Safe use:

- approximate date ca.1773: **verified_catalogue as seller dating**, not exact bibliographic date;

## 2. Two different Raspe/Seyfart objects exist in 1773

### A. Cumulative Wappenbuch, volume 3

The Bayerische Staatsbibliothek controls the third volume of *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch*:

- Nürnberg: Raspe, 1773;
- BSB object / URN: `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10428783-2`;
- this volume belongs to the continuously numbered cumulative armorial sequence.

Bibliographic reconstructions place the third-volume armorial run at approximately Nos. 195–390, centered on Reichsgrafen arms. Its defining architecture is therefore a numbered armorial sequence.

### B. Seyfart's *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie*, 1773

A separate digital object is indexed for:

*Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie, welches aller jetzigen europäischen Potentaten und der geistlichen und weltlichen Fürsten Stammtafeln nebst einem Verzeichniße der regierenden Grafen des Heil. Röm. Reichs und des Cardinals-Collegii enthält*, by Johann Friedrich Seyfart, Nürnberg: Gabriel Nicolaus Raspe.

1773 BSB digital witness:

- URN: `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10428164-6`.

The series title itself foregrounds **Stammtafeln**, exactly the material category highlighted in Weidler's lot description. An open 1768 institutional copy of the same Seyfart series is explicitly catalogued with `Frontisp.` by ULB Halle, confirming that this host family could carry a frontispiece before 1773.

## 3. Why the handbook identification is currently stronger

The Weidler description combines three features:

1. the Clio inscription as identifying wording;
2. many armorial representations;
3. **Stammtafeln**.

That combination maps more naturally onto the genealogy-handbook line than onto the 1773 cumulative Wappenbuch volume alone. The latter certainly contains extensive armorial imagery, but its controlled bibliographic identity is the continuously numbered Wappenbuch volume. The Seyfart *Handbuch*, by contrast, explicitly organizes current European dynasties through genealogical tables while remaining inside the same Raspe heraldic publishing ecology.

There is also a plausible cataloguing mechanism: an imperfect, rebound or untitled copy may present the engraved Clio leaf first, encouraging a market cataloguer to name the book from the frontispiece rather than from a missing or less visually salient title page.

This remains an inference. Do not merge the Weidler object into BSB `bsb10428164` without copy-level control.

Before this lead, the strongest explicit late target controls were:

Weidler lot 8009 adds a different kind of late evidence:

`target inscription explicitly named`
+
`ca.1773 seller dating`
+
`armorial representations + Stammtafeln`

If the opening leaves of the 1773 Seyfart *Handbuch* confirm Clio, the target design can be traced into the genealogy-handbook line approximately **47 years after the earliest currently visible 1726 witness**. That would also help explain the otherwise unidentified repeated frontispieces in the 1767–1776 Wappenbuch ecology, though it would still not prove that every repeated frontispiece is the same matrix.

## 6. Evidence guard

- Do not treat `wohl um 1773` as exact publication dating.
- Do not treat the auction word `Wappenbuch` as a literal title transcription.
- Do not infer same-matrix identity from the target inscription alone.
- Keep the 1773 cumulative Wappenbuch volume and the 1773 genealogy handbook as separate hosts unless a surviving copy physically joins them.
- If BSB `bsb10428164` does not contain Clio, the Weidler object remains valuable as a ca.1773 market witness but requires a different bibliographic identification.
