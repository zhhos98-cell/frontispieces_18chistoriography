# Winckelmann Hessian Chronicle — planned visual apparatus source-boundary closure

Date: 2026-08-30  
Status: **PUBLIC/SCHOLARLY CLAIM CONTROLLED; PRIMARY PUBLICATION-HISTORY DOSSIER IDENTIFIED BUT REPORTED LOST; SURVIVING MANUSCRIPT ROUTE BOUNDED. GENERIC WEB RESEARCH CLOSED.**

## Result

The old open task was to move beyond the later statement that Winckelmann's Hessian Chronicle was originally planned with `Karten, Abbildungen u.s.w.` and to recover the production-stage evidence behind the loss of that apparatus.

The current source situation can now be closed more precisely.

1. The historical claim itself is explicit in the *Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie*: as Darmstadt court participation cooled, the project abandoned further provision with `Karten, Abbildungen u. s. w., wie sie anfangs im Plane des Verfassers lag`.
2. The best known manuscript witness to the publication history is not merely an unspecified archival possibility. Landesbibliothek Oldenburg `Cim I 204` is the author's working Latin Chronicle codex, 553 leaves, with numerous inserted/pasted materials and a terminal dossier of princely-letter extracts concerning the Chronicle.
3. Crucially, the HAB/LB Oldenburg manuscript description states that a formerly bound publication-history dossier, **`Wunder- und leßwürdige Relation wegen Verlaufs und verhinderter Publication der Hessischen Chronik de Anno 1648 bis 1696`**, was transferred in 1941 to the Hessisches Staatsarchiv Darmstadt and **could not be found there after the war**.
4. The surviving codex still preserves **fols.543r–552v, `Extracten aus den Hochfürstl. Schreiben die hessische Chronic betreffend`**. These leaves are the strongest bounded surviving primary route into court-level production history.

Thus the failure to recover a public primary sentence naming a particular map/engraving budget is now a **source-survival boundary**, not an invitation to continue generic searching.

## 2. Cim I 204 radically narrows the primary route

Herzog August Bibliothek's manuscript database describes Landesbibliothek Oldenburg `Cim I 204` as:

`Iohannis Justi Wynkelmanni Hassiacarum rerum libri`

- paper;
- 1 flyleaf + 553 leaves;
- c.1647–1699;
- authorial corrections/additions;
- multiple inserted leaves between 120v–121r, 161–162, 180–181, 182–183, 221–222, 234–235 and 304–305;
- pasted material at 264r and 273r;
- duplicate fourth book with differing authorial improvements/additions;
- terminal material at **543r–552v**: `Extracten aus den Hochfürstl. Schreiben die hessische Chronic betreffend`.

Official description:

`https://diglib.hab.de/?catalog=Bley&db=mss&id=ol-lbo-cim-i-204&list=ms`

The manuscript therefore directly preserves the sort of authorial/court communication layer relevant to planned apparatus, even though the public catalogue description does not itself mention `Karten`, `Abbildungen`, `Kupfer` or `Landkarten` within those extract leaves.

### Promotion rule

Only direct inspection of 543r–552v or a diplomatic edition should establish whether one of the princely letters discusses:

- maps/images;
- drafts/designs;
- engravers/cartographers;
- costs;
- court authorization;
- cancellation or reduction of visual apparatus.

## 3. The lost publication-history dossier is a real archival ceiling

The most important new control is the manuscript-history note:

> an originally attached **`Wunder- und leßwürdige Relation wegen Verlaufs und verhinderter Publication der Hessischen Chronik de Anno 1648 bis 1696`** was transferred in 1941 to the Hessisches Staatsarchiv Darmstadt, but was no longer locatable there after the war.

This matters methodologically. The repository had previously treated `court correspondence / budgets / printer-engraver correspondence / authorial dossier` as an open discovery field. It can now distinguish:

`KNOWN FORMER PUBLICATION-HISTORY DOSSIER`

→ `TRANSFER TO DARMSTADT 1941`

→ `REPORTED POSTWAR LOSS / NON-RECOVERY`.

A missing source with a known archival history is not equivalent to an unsearched source universe.

The correct next-state category is:

`PRIMARY DOSSIER LOST / SURVIVING EXTRACT ROUTE AVAILABLE`.

## 4. Relation to Fuchs

Thomas Fuchs, *Traditionsstiftung und Erinnerungspolitik* (2002), remains the principal modern reconstruction of Winckelmann's production/revision history. The Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek/DNB record exposes bibliographic metadata and table of contents but not the full monograph. Later scholarship repeatedly routes the detailed Chronicle revision history back to Fuchs, especially the large Winckelmann section.

This is a finite library upgrade, not a web-discovery frontier:

`consult Fuchs relevant pages`
+
`inspect Cim I 204, especially 543r–552v`
→ possible primary/scholarly strengthening.

No additional broad query for `Winckelmann Karten Abbildungen` is justified.

## 5. Current causal claim ceiling

Safe article-level formulation:

> Later historical reconstruction reports that Winckelmann's Hessian Chronicle initially envisaged maps and images, and that this visual programme was abandoned as Darmstadt court participation receded. The surviving authorial codex confirms a heavily revised, materially layered production process and preserves extracts from princely correspondence about the Chronicle; the fuller 1648–1696 publication-history dossier once attached to that codex was transferred to Darmstadt in 1941 and is reported lost after the war.

Do not state:

- which maps/images were planned;
- which artists were commissioned;
- that a specific budget was cut;
- that censorship directly caused image cancellation;
- that Darmstadt alone would have paid for all visual production;

without direct evidence.
