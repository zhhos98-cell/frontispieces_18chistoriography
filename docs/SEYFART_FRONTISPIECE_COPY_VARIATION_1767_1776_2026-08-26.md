# Seyfart Wappenbuch frontispiece copy variation, 1767–1776 — 26 August 2026

## Status

**Copy-level frontispiece variability: CLOSED. Image identity of the surviving frontispiece(s): OPEN.**

The earlier working shorthand `three repeated frontispieces across the 1767–1776 Seyfart Wappenbuch` is too edition-like. Multiple independently described sets show that frontispiece presence and placement vary by surviving copy/binding configuration.

## 1. Venator & Hanstein: three repeated frontispieces

Venator & Hanstein, catalogue 136, lot 239, describes a four-volume set of Johann Friedrich Seyfart, *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch* (Nürnberg: Raspe, 1767–1776), with:

- `3 wh. gest. Front.` = three repeated engraved frontispieces;
- 567 of 571 engraved armorial plates;
- volume I additionally carrying the title *Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender auf das Jahr 1767 oder jährliches Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik*.

This remains the strongest evidence that at least one multi-volume configuration carried a repeated frontispiece design.

## 2. Stockholm: only one unnumbered frontispiece recorded

Stockholms Auktionsverk, lot 672359, describes another four-volume set, 1767–1775, with four titles bearing vignettes and 738 armorial plates. Its detailed collation records:

- Volume I: plates 1–178 **plus one unnumbered frontispiece**;
- Volume II: plates 1–194;
- Volume III: plates 195–390;
- Volume IV: plates 391 onward.

No repeated frontispieces are recorded for volumes II–IV in this copy.

Stable source: `https://stockholmsauktionsverk.com/arkiv/online/672359`.

## 3. Müller & Gräff: the extra unnumbered copper is a colour key, not a frontispiece

A surviving 1767–1771 two-volume configuration described by Müller & Gräff contains 372 armorial engravings and `einem separaten Kupfer`.

The catalogue explicitly identifies that separate plate as:

`die Erklärung der Wappenfarben (Tingierung)`.

It is therefore not evidence for a frontispiece. This is an important negative control against turning every unnumbered/separate copper into a threshold image.

Inventory: ZVAB/AbeBooks `30636777998`.

## 4. Independent 1771 volume II: title copper present

Wissenschaftliches Antiquariat Th. Haker describes a standalone 1771 second volume as:

- `(4), 194 Bl.`;
- **`Mit Titelkupfer`**;
- 193 engraved armorial plates.

Inventory `71332` / ZVAB-AbeBooks `31262200903`.

Thus a frontispiece/title copper can occur in a volume-II configuration even though other multi-volume sets do not describe one there.

## 5. Result

The correct material model is:

`Seyfart annual issues / part issues`
→ `parts accumulated into Wappenbuch volumes`
→ `different surviving binding/copy configurations`
→ `frontispiece may be retained, repeated, omitted, lost or newly positioned`.

Accordingly:

- `3 repeated frontispieces` is a **copy-level fact for the Venator set**, not a universal edition statement;
- `one unnumbered frontispiece in volume I` is a **copy-level fact for the Stockholm set**;
- a `Titelkupfer` in the standalone 1771 volume II is another **copy-level fact**;
- an unnumbered extra copper must be functionally identified before being counted as a frontispiece.

## 6. Analytical consequence

The late Seyfart material independently confirms the repository's central material rule:

**host title / nominal volume ≠ stable plate assemblage.**

This is not only a problem of damaged copies. The annual-to-cumulative publication mechanism itself encouraged reassembly. The 1771 preface already states that Wappencalender arms were issued successively and accumulated into the larger Wappenbuch. Frontispiece variability is therefore structurally compatible with the publication regime.

## 7. Still open: image identity

No inspected image in this pass proves that any of the surviving Seyfart frontispieces is the Müller–Tyroff `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica / Testes temporum et veritatis` design.

Do not write:

- `Seyfart retained the Clio frontispiece`;
- `the three repeated frontispieces were Clio`;
- `each Wappenbuch volume originally had the same frontispiece`.

The remaining bounded task is image-level identification of:

1. the Stockholm volume-I unnumbered frontispiece;
2. the Haker 1771 volume-II `Titelkupfer`;
3. at least one of the repeated Venator frontispieces.

If two or more prove visually identical, then test whether that design is Clio and whether repeated impressions are from the same copperplate/state.
