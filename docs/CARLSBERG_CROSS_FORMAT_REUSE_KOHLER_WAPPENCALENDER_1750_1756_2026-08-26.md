# Carlsberg cross-format reuse: Köhler continuation and the 1756 Wappenkalender — 26 August 2026

## Status

**A title- and subject-level identity is directly controlled between the posthumous Köhler `Münz-Belustigung` material and the 1756 Weigel Wappenkalender: both center on the same medal of Landgrave Karl of Hessen and the Carlsberg. The `Münz-Belustigung` text is directly readable; the calendar is independently collated with a dedicated coin copperplate and the same historical-explanation title. Full page-by-page textual/plate identity remains to be checked. Direction of reuse is intentionally unresolved because the nominal weekly date and actual print/publication dates diverge.**

---

## I. The same Carlsberg medal appears across two publication rhythms

The posthumous continuation of Johann David Köhler's `Wöchentliche Historische Münz-Belustigung` contains:

`49. Stück den 3. Decemb. 1750`

with the title:

`Eine fürtreffliche Schaumünze Landgraf CARLS zu Hessen, auf den von demselben erbaueten Carlsberg.`

The article states that the medal's image appears on the accompanying leaf and then proceeds through:

1. `Beschreibung` of the medal;
2. `Historische Erklärung`;
3. description of Landgrave Karl, the Carlsberg and its architectural/waterworks setting.

The reverse is described as showing the mountain, buildings and cascades, with Time, Minerva and Hercules as part of the medal's visual programme.

The **1756** annual `Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender` carries in its title the same distinctive content module:

`Nebst historischer Erklärung einer fürtrefflichen Schaumünze Landgraf Carls zu Hessen, auf den ... erbaueten ... Carlsberg.`

Physical-copy collation of the 1756 calendar records:

- engraved frontispiece;
- one full-page colour-explanation copper;
- **one full-page coin copper**;
- 108 full-page armorial coppers;
- genealogical tables and accompanying historical material.

This is therefore not merely the recurrence of a subject. The annual calendar advertises the same specific medal/explanation module that exists as no. 49 of the Köhler numismatic sequence.

---

## II. Johann Tobias Köhler sits immediately behind the numismatic version

The surviving full-text carrier explicitly introduces the continuation after Johann David Köhler's death:

`nach des seeligen Verfassers Tode ... von dessen Sohne M. Johann Tobias Köhler fortgesetzten ... Historischen Münz-Belustigung`.

The Carlsberg item belongs inside this continued sequence.

This matters because Gierl's bibliographic attribution of Johann Christoph Gatterer's contributions begins immediately after it:

- no. 49 / pp. 385–392 — Carlsberg medal;
- no. 50 / pp. 393–400 — Gatterer contribution;
- no. 51 / pp. 401–408 — Gatterer contribution;
- no. 52 / pp. 409–416 — Gatterer contribution;
- supplement sheets 1–3 / pp. 417–444 — Gatterer contributions.

The terminal part of the nominal 1750 year therefore has a tightly packed collaborative structure:

`Johann Tobias Köhler continuation`
→ `Carlsberg no.49`
→ `Gatterer nos.50–52 + supplements`
→ `posthumous closure of Johann David Köhler's series`.

Do not infer from this sequence that Johann Tobias alone authored every word of no. 49 without checking the issue's own authorship markers. The direct point is that the item occurs inside the section explicitly framed as the son's continuation, while Gatterer's separately attributed contributions begin with no. 50.

---

## III. Do not assume a simple weekly → annual direction

The dates are materially awkward and therefore analytically useful.

The `Münz-Belustigung` retains a **nominal weekly date of 3 December 1750**, but the terminal material was delayed and the final volume was only brought to publication after Johann David Köhler's death, in **1756**.

The 1756 Wappenkalender, by contrast, is described in a detailed physical-copy catalogue as:

`Nürnberg, Bieling für Weigel (1755)`

for calendar year 1756.

Therefore the safe model is **cross-format reuse from a shared production reservoir**, not a directional claim that a published weekly article was later copied into the annual calendar.

Possible material sequences include:

1. manuscript/plate prepared for the delayed numismatic series → annual calendar deployment → later bound/final publication of the serial;
2. earlier proof/impression or privately circulating numismatic unit → calendar reuse → formal serial closure;
3. parallel setting/reuse of the same text and/or plate from common Weigel-heirs production files.

The present evidence does not distinguish among these.

---

## IV. Why this is a high-value control for the repository

This is a concrete example of the project's central rule:

`text unit ≠ plate unit ≠ publication rhythm ≠ bound host`.

The same historical-numismatic module can sit inside:

- a nominally weekly numismatic serial;
- an annual genealogy/heraldry calendar;
- potentially the same publisher-heirs/workshop ecology.

The module itself contains both **engraved object representation** and **historical explanation**. It is therefore a better comparator for the Clio frontispiece ecology than generic evidence of title continuity.

Operationally:

`medal / coin image`
→ `description`
→ `historical explanation`
→ `weekly serial slot`
AND/OR
→ `annual calendar historical module`.

This shows that historical explanation could be extracted/redeployed across publication rhythms without requiring the entire host publication to migrate with it.

---

## V. Relation to the Gatterer transition

The new cross-format control sharpens the 1755–1759 succession:

- Johann David Köhler dies in 1755;
- Johann Tobias Köhler participates in the posthumous continuation/closure of the numismatic series;
- the 1756 Wappenkalender under Weigel heirs advertises the same Carlsberg medal module as no.49 of that continuation;
- Gatterer supplies the immediately following numismatic pieces nos.50–52 and supplements 1–3, plus the Köhler biographical preface;
- in 1759 Gatterer takes over the genealogy/heraldry calendar ecology as `Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik`, still under the literal Weigel-heirs imprint and still carrying the Clio threshold plate.

This is increasingly a **publication-workflow succession** rather than an abstract sequence of scholarly influences.

---

## VI. Bounded next control

The decisive test is to compare the 1756 calendar's Carlsberg section against `Münz-Belustigung` no.49, pp.385–392:

Record separately:

- wording of section title;
- wording/order of `Beschreibung` and `Historische Erklärung`;
- exact medal image / engraving;
- engraver signature and dimensions;
- line breaks and typographic setting;
- omissions/additions in annual version;
- whether the calendar explicitly identifies Johann Tobias Köhler or only initials;
- whether the annual coin copper is the same plate/impression, a re-engraving, or a new copy.

Until this comparison, use `same_content_module / same_medal_subject` rather than `same_text` or `same_copperplate`.
