# Eimmart Wappenbuch frontispiece lineage, 1696–1734 — 26 August 2026

This pass separates the older Siebmacher–Fürst–Helmers–Weigel Wappenbuch frontispiece lineage from the later Müller–Tyroff Clio frontispiece used in the Weigel Wappenkalender.

## 1. 1696: Eimmart frontispiece is already present

A complete-copy description of the 1696 Nürnberg *Erneuert- und vermehrtes Wappen-Buch* (Paul Fürst widow and heirs) records:

- parts I–V plus supplement to part V in one volume;
- **engraved frontispiece by Eimmart** (`gest. Frontispiz von Eimmart`);
- 1,168 armorial engravings on 593 plates;
- folio format, approximately 33 × 20.6 cm.

The catalogue identifies this as the seventh edition of the armorial and notes that parts I–II derive from Siebmacher and III–V from Paul Fürst.

This establishes a secure terminus ante quem: an **Eimmart-designed/engraved frontispiece belonged to the cumulative Wappenbuch ecology by 1696**, decades before the Weigel calendar's Müller–Tyroff Clio design.

## 2. 1701/1703 Helmers: Eimmart remains attached to the renewed Wappenbuch

Universitäts- und Landesbibliothek Sachsen-Anhalt's complete digital record for the Helmers *Erneuert- und Vermehrtes Wappen-Buch* gives:

- title-page date 1701, intermediate-title imprint Nürnberg, Johann Rudolf Helmers, 1703;
- editor/publisher: Johann Rudolf Helmers;
- associated name: **Georg Christoph Eimmart**;
- engraved frontispiece (`Frontisp. (Kupferst.)`);
- folio format;
- open complete digital carrier, ULB Halle shelfmark AB 99068, DOI `10.25673/66636`, URN `urn:nbn:de:gbv:3:1-708622`.

The catalogue-level metadata strongly supports continuation of the Eimmart visual apparatus into the Helmers renewal. Image-level comparison is still required before calling the 1696 and 1701/03 frontispieces the same copperplate.

## 3. 1734 Weigel: Eimmart frontispiece survives the transfer into the elder Weigel house

A complete 1734 Weigel copy in the South Bohemian Research Library / Zlatá Koruna historical collection records:

- *Das grosse und Vollständige anfangs Siebmacherische, hernacher Fürstische und Helmerische, nun aber Weigelische Wappen-Buch*;
- Nürnberg: **Christoph Weigel the Elder's widow**; printer Lorenz Bieling;
- Köhler's new preface;
- engraved frontispiece explicitly signed **`G. C. Eimmart del. et sculp.`**;
- title page separately contains a copper-engraved device with motto `CRESCIT EVNDO`.

The frontispiece and the `CRESCIT EVNDO` title vignette must not be conflated: the catalogue records them as separate graphic elements.

Because Georg Christoph Eimmart died in 1705, the signed 1734 frontispiece cannot have been newly designed or engraved by him for the Weigel edition. The Weigel edition is therefore reusing an **older Eimmart plate/design tradition** inherited through the preceding Wappenbuch genealogy.

## 4. What is now established

A minimum visual genealogy can be written securely as:

`Fürst widow/heirs Wappenbuch, 1696 — Eimmart frontispiece`
→ `Helmers renewed Wappenbuch, 1701/1703 — Eimmart associated + engraved frontispiece`
→ `Weigel Wappenbuch, 1734 — frontispiece signed G. C. Eimmart del. et sculp.`

Evidence supports **frontispiece-lineage continuity** across publisher/editor regimes.

It does **not yet establish**:

- identical copperplate across all three witnesses;
- identical state/impression;
- whether the Eimmart plate first entered the Wappenbuch in 1695, 1696, or an earlier Fürst issue;
- whether the 1701/03 carrier retains the same exact signature/location as the 1734 plate.

## 5. Contrast with the Clio calendar lineage

This is the most important analytical consequence.

The 1734 cumulative Wappenbuch did **not** simply adopt the Müller–Tyroff `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica / Testes temporum et veritatis` frontispiece as a universal Weigel heraldic emblem. Instead it retained an older Eimmart threshold image from the inherited Wappenbuch tradition.

The Weigel ecology therefore contains at least two distinct frontispiece genealogies:

### Cumulative Wappenbuch line

`Siebmacher/Fürst/Helmers inherited plate tradition → Eimmart frontispiece → Weigel 1734`

### Annual Wappenkalender line

`1724 event-memory frontispiece → 1725 unresolved title copper → 1726 Müller–Tyroff Clio visible → later calendar/handbook recurrence`

This makes the frontispiece less like a publisher logo and more like a **publication-regime-specific threshold apparatus**. Different serial/cumulative forms can inherit different visual genealogies even when they share publisher kinship, armorial plate stocks and genealogical subject matter.
