# Eimmart Wappenbuch frontispiece lineage, 1696–1734 — 26 August 2026

This pass separates the older Siebmacher–Fürst–Helmers–Weigel Wappenbuch frontispiece lineage from the later Müller–Tyroff Clio frontispiece used in the Weigel Wappenkalender.

## 1. 1696: Eimmart frontispiece is directly attributed in two independent copy descriptions

Two independent complete/near-complete copy descriptions now attribute the 1696 Nürnberg *Erneuert- und vermehrtes Wappen-Buch* frontispiece to **Georg Christoph Eimmart**.

### Ketterer, auction 379, lot 400

Ketterer's detailed collation records:

- Nürnberg, Paul Fürst widow and heirs, 1696;
- six parts in one volume;
- `Mit gest. Frontispiz`;
- Part I begins `Gest. Frontispiz (von G. C. Einmart)`;
- 1,188 armorial engravings on 616 surviving of 622 expected plates in this copy.

This is important because it is not merely an associated-name catalogue field: the lot collation explicitly assigns the frontispiece itself to Eimmart.

Stable source: `https://www.kettererkunst.de/kunst/kd/details.php?anummer=379&detail=1&obnr=411003856`.

### Venator & Hanstein, lot 896

A separate 1696 copy likewise records `Mit gest. Frontispiz von Eimmart` and 1,168 armorial engravings on 593 plates.

The independent agreement closes the basic attribution at strong catalogue/copy-description level.

**Conclusion:** an Eimmart frontispiece belonged to the cumulative Wappenbuch ecology by 1696, decades before the Weigel calendar's Müller–Tyroff Clio design.

## 2. 1701–04 Helmers: direct copy collation again attributes the frontispiece to Eimmart

The Helmers stage is now stronger than an associated-name inference.

### Ketterer, auction 294, lot 292

A complete coloured copy of the renewed Wappenbuch, Nürnberg, Johann Rudolf Helmers, 1701–04, is collated as:

- engraved frontispiece;
- one text copper;
- 1,200 coloured armorial engravings on 623 plates;
- Part I begins `Gest. Frontispiz (von G. C. Einmart)`.

Ketterer identifies this as the second Helmers edition / ninth edition overall and notes that, relative to the 1699 issue, it adds a general register.

Stable source: `https://www.kettererkunst.de/kunst/kd/details.php?anummer=294&obnr=100500191`.

This directly establishes that an Eimmart-attributed frontispiece is still attached to the Helmers Wappenbuch in 1701–04.

### ULB Halle digital carrier

Universitäts- und Landesbibliothek Sachsen-Anhalt's record for AB 99068 independently gives:

- title-page date 1701 and intermediate-title imprint Nürnberg, Johann Rudolf Helmers, 1703;
- associated name Georg Christoph Eimmart;
- engraved frontispiece (`Frontisp. (Kupferst.)`);
- DOI `10.25673/66636`;
- URN `urn:nbn:de:gbv:3:1-708622`.

The Ketterer copy description therefore upgrades what was previously only an inference from the Halle metadata: **the Helmers frontispiece itself is explicitly attributed to Eimmart in a surviving-copy collation**.

## 3. 1734 Weigel: Eimmart frontispiece survives the transfer into the elder Weigel house

A complete 1734 Weigel copy in the South Bohemian Research Library / Zlatá Koruna historical collection records:

- *Das grosse und Vollständige anfangs Siebmacherische, hernacher Fürstische und Helmerische, nun aber Weigelische Wappen-Buch*;
- Nürnberg: `Christoph Weigels des ältern seel. Wittwe`; printer Lorenz Bieling;
- publisher authority resolved as **Magdalena Esther Weigel**;
- Köhler's new preface;
- engraved frontispiece explicitly signed **`G. C. Eimmart del. et sculp.`**;
- title page separately contains a copper-engraved device with motto `CRESCIT EVNDO`.

The frontispiece and `CRESCIT EVNDO` title vignette are distinct graphic elements and must not be conflated.

Stable catalogue: `https://katalog.cbvk.cz/arl-cbvk/cs/detail-cbvk_us_cat-0551003-Das-grosse-und-Vollstandige-anfangs-Siebmacherische-hernacher-Furstische-und-Helmerische-nun-aber/`.

Because Georg Christoph Eimmart died in 1705, the signed 1734 frontispiece cannot have been newly designed or engraved by him for the Weigel edition. The Weigel edition is therefore reusing an **older Eimmart plate/design tradition** inherited through the preceding Wappenbuch genealogy.

## 4. The threshold regime changes before 1696

Historical copy catalogues show an important structural shift in the older Wappenbuch series.

The mid-seventeenth-century Fürst production circulated as multi-part books with multiple engraved title/frontispiece leaves; a 1655–67 group, for example, survives with separate engraved titles for individual parts. By the Fürst-widow/heirs 1690s cumulative configuration, copy descriptions instead report **one Eimmart frontispiece before Part I** followed by the armorial plate sequence.

This means the most useful image question is no longer simply `1696 Eimmart versus 1734 Eimmart`. It is:

`multi-part engraved-title regime`
→ `single Eimmart threshold before the cumulative Wappenbuch`
→ `Helmers reissue/expansion with Eimmart threshold retained`
→ `Weigel 1734 cumulative recoding with Eimmart threshold retained`.

The exact first year/state of the single Eimmart threshold still needs image/copy-level collation; do not assign it to 1695 rather than 1696 solely from mixed bibliographic series descriptions.

## 5. What is now established

The minimum visual genealogy can now be written more strongly as:

`Fürst widow/heirs Wappenbuch, 1696 — frontispiece explicitly attributed to G. C. Eimmart`
→ `Helmers Wappenbuch, 1701–04 — frontispiece explicitly attributed to G. C. Eimmart`
→ `Weigel Wappenbuch, 1734 — frontispiece signed G. C. Eimmart del. et sculp.`

Evidence supports **frontispiece-lineage continuity across publisher/editor regimes**.

It still does **not establish**:

- identical copperplate across all three witnesses;
- identical state/impression;
- exact first entry date of the Eimmart frontispiece;
- whether the 1696 and 1701–04 plates carry the signature in exactly the same wording/location as 1734;
- whether intermediate reworking/retouching occurred.

Those require actual image comparison, especially scratches, plate boundaries, lettering and signature placement.

## 6. Contrast with the Clio calendar lineage

The 1734 cumulative Wappenbuch did **not** simply adopt the Müller–Tyroff `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica / Testes temporum et veritatis` frontispiece as a universal Weigel heraldic emblem. Instead it retained an older Eimmart threshold image from the inherited Wappenbuch tradition.

The Weigel ecology therefore contains at least two distinct frontispiece genealogies:

### Cumulative Wappenbuch line

`Siebmacher/Fürst inherited engraved-title ecology → Eimmart threshold → Helmers → Weigel 1734`

### Annual Wappenkalender line

`1724 event-memory frontispiece → 1725 unresolved title copper → 1726 Müller–Tyroff Clio visible → later calendar/handbook recurrence`

This makes the frontispiece less like a publisher logo and more like a **publication-regime-specific threshold apparatus**. Different serial/cumulative forms can inherit different visual genealogies even when they share publisher kinship, armorial plate stocks and genealogical subject matter.

## 7. Facsimile warning

The widely circulated 1975 Battenberg/Neubecker facsimile and derivative online plate images often label themselves as material from the `9. Ausgabe 1701` and `10. Ausgabe 1705` together. Those images are useful for identifying heraldic plate content but **must not be treated as a copy-level witness for either historical edition** unless their exact source plate is independently resolved.

For frontispiece identity and plate-state arguments, use surviving historical copies/digital carriers rather than a modern combined facsimile.
