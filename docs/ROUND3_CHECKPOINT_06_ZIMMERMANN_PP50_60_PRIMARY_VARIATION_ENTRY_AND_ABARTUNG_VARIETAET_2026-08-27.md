## pp.50–53 — preparation before human variation

The pages immediately before the `Zweite Abtheilung` are not yet a catalogue of human races. Zimmermann is establishing the unusual breadth and flexibility of human constitution through diet, bodily endurance and geographical range.

At p.53 he summarizes the preparation:

> `So viel haben wir nun aus allem diesem: der Mensch hält außerordentliche, ja fast unglaubliche Veränderungen von Klima und Nahrung aus.`

The point is methodological. Before asking how far human forms can diverge, Zimmermann first establishes that the human organism can survive extraordinary alteration of climate and nourishment.

### Primary-direct consequence

Human variation is introduced only after a **plasticity / endurance demonstration**.

The later racial argument should therefore not be reconstructed as taxonomy first and causation second. In the architecture of the chapter, causal capacity is established before the main variation catalogue begins.

## p.54 — the explicit causal question

Zimmermann characterizes the human `Maschine` as `biegsam, eindrucksfähig`, then asks what all the previously described changes do to it and how far it can be diverted from its `Originalgestalt`.

He formulates the central question in explicitly generative terms:

> `Konnte eine und eben dieselbe Menschengattung, durch diese Veränderungen, in alle die Gestalten und Ausartungen übergehen, die wir anjezo unter dem Menschengeschlechte antreffen?`

### Primary-direct consequence

This sentence is an early first-volume formulation of the same problem later answered more concretely in pp.100–115.

The explanandum is present diversity (`Gestalten und Ausartungen`); the proposed causal route is transformation of one and the same `Menschengattung` through environmental and historical changes.

Again the grammar is interrogative. It sets the problem rather than yet proving the historical route.

## pp.54–58 — Germany as a diachronic near-field test

Zimmermann then deliberately begins with Germany rather than immediately with distant peoples. He asks whether Germany's climate, food, bodily life and inhabitants have changed over roughly two thousand years since Tacitus and Caesar.

The sequence compares ancient descriptions of German bodily robustness and size with contemporary Germans, considering altered climate, diet, drink, sexual practice, labour, bathing, luxury, sedentary life and intellectual work as possible long-duration causes.

This near-field example is structurally important because it prevents the causal programme from operating only on exoticized distant populations. Zimmermann attempts to demonstrate bodily transformability using Europeans themselves before extending the mechanism to the `Varietäten des Menschengeschlechts`.

## pp.58–59 — accumulated causes and sustained action

The end of p.58 and opening of p.59 gather a long list of possible causes of smaller/weaker modern bodies: milder climate, altered nourishment, early or excessive sexual activity, warm/alcoholic drinks, loss of strengthening cold baths, luxury-induced cares, sedentary life, and exhausting intellectual labour.

Zimmermann then states:

> `Sie verwandeln durch anhaltende Wirkung den Kolossen in einen ohnmächtigen Zwerg, und dies beweist hier die Erfahrung unleugbar.`

### Primary-direct consequence

The operative form is **sustained action over time** (`durch anhaltende Wirkung`). The Germany comparison is used as an experiential warrant for the more ambitious causal inquiry that follows.

This does not prove that every later racial difference arose from the same causes; it supplies a local model of bodily transformation and a warrant for asking the larger question with less hesitation.

## p.59 — decisive entry into `Abartungen oder Varietäten`

Zimmermann marks the transition explicitly:

> `Nach dieser Vorbereitung gehe ich nunmehr schon mit weniger Furchtsamkeit zu den Abartungen oder Varietäten des Menschengeschlechts.`

This sentence resolves the Round 3 vocabulary question at the entrance to the variation catalogue.

### Primary-direct vocabulary result

`Abartungen` and `Varietäten` are here linked by **`oder`**, not arranged in a formal rank hierarchy.

At this locus the safest actor-level reading is therefore:

- coordinate / near-equivalent labels for the large variations of humankind;
- not evidence for a `Stamm > Race > Varietät > Spielart` ladder of the kind later made explicit by Meiners;
- part of a mobile explanatory vocabulary rather than a stabilized taxonomic rank system.

The passage does not prove perfect synonymy in every usage throughout the work. It does prove that Zimmermann himself can introduce the object of inquiry as `Abartungen oder Varietäten` without defining them as distinct ranks.

## p.59–60 — `möglicher Weise`: causal possibility remains bounded

Zimmermann immediately narrows the evidential entitlement of the programme. He says he will not catalogue every facial formation or small difference, and then writes that if he shows the greatest human varieties as

> `durch Klima und Nahrung möglicher Weise hervorgebracht`

then a reasonable reader may take the lesser `Abartungen` as even more easily producible by the same causes.

The sentence completes on p.60:

> `leichter durch eben diese Ursachen entstanden denken.`

He then begins the substantive catalogue:

> `Ich fange mit der Figur des Menschen zuerst an.`

### Primary-direct consequence

The modal phrase `möglicher Weise` is crucial.

Zimmermann is not claiming that he has reconstructed the actual historical cause of every human variety. The immediate aim is to establish **causal possibility / sufficient transformability**:

`if large varieties can possibly be produced by climate + nourishment, smaller variations are easier to explain by the same causal family.`

This is exactly continuous with:

- p.101: one common `Stamm` is shown as possible, while actual history cannot be known `mathematisch gewiß`;
- p.105: isolation/endogamy race formation is possible but not asserted as the actual origin of a major historical race;
- pp.114–115: the migration/origin reconstruction is explicitly called an `Einfall`.

The epistemic posture is therefore stable across widely separated parts of the volume.

## Revised architecture of Zimmermann's first-volume human-variation argument

The primary pages now support the following chapter architecture:

1. **pp.31–34 — environmental range / intensity**
   - what degrees of heat and cold can humans endure?
   - climate is measurable and enters the causal problem-space.

2. **pp.50–53 — constitutional plasticity / endurance preparation**
   - humans tolerate extraordinary changes of `Klima und Nahrung`.

3. **pp.54–59 — near-field transformation warrant**
   - one `Menschengattung` is asked to be capable of passing into many `Gestalten und Ausartungen`;
   - Germany supplies a diachronic European test of long-term bodily transformation.

4. **p.59–60 — explicit entry into the variation catalogue**
   - `Abartungen oder Varietäten` are introduced coordinately;
   - large varieties are to be shown as `möglicher Weise` produced by climate and nourishment.

5. **pp.100–108 — generative race mechanisms and colour gradation**
   - common-stock possibility;
   - isolation/endogamy and inheritance can make a `Race`;
   - colour appears as `Nüancen` / `Stufenfolge` and hypothetical rate of blackening;
   - migration and mixture complicate present classification.

6. **pp.109–111 — classificatory jurisdiction**
   - climate is explicitly recommended as a principle in the `Zerfällung in verschiedene Racen`;
   - Blumenbach's `Hauptracen` provide an explicit comparator.

7. **pp.114–115 — conjectural historical synthesis**
   - one Asian homeland / population growth / family migration / climatic transformation;
   - reconstruction explicitly downgraded to an `Einfall`.

This is not a flat climate-determinist scheme. It is a layered causal and classificatory architecture in which possibility, historical reconstruction, visible difference, reproductive history and taxonomic division remain distinguishable operations.

## Round 3 Question 3 — taxonomic vocabulary: final answer

The primary pages do not support a stable Zimmermann hierarchy equivalent to Meiners's later explicit ladder.

Instead:

- `Abartung oder Varietät` are explicitly coordinated at p.59;
- `Menschengattung`, `Art`, `Stamm`, `Race`, `Hauptrace`, `Nation`, and `Familie` operate at different explanatory moments without a consistently defined rank relation;
- a `Race` can be generated historically through non-mixture / endogamy and inheritance;
- several `Menschengattungen` can be derivable from one `Stamm` / same `Art`;
- `Hauptracen` appear in the classificatory comparison with Blumenbach.

The mobility is therefore substantive, not merely careless terminology: it corresponds to Zimmermann's emphasis on formation, transition, migration and mixture.

## Zimmermann ↔ Meiners boundary: now sufficiently primary-controlled

### Shared causal space

Both can operate with climate, bodily difference, human grouping and variation. Meiners does not introduce those operations into an otherwise non-taxonomic Zimmermannian world.

### Zimmermann 1778

- establishes bodily/environmental plasticity before classifying major variation;
- introduces `Abartung` and `Varietät` coordinately rather than as fixed ranks;
- uses causal possibility as a major inferential form (`möglicher Weise`, `haben entspringen können`);
- allows human races to arise historically through climate, inheritance, reproductive isolation and long duration;
- treats migration and mixture as processes that destabilize clean classification;
- treats colour as descriptive, gradable and causally transformable but not as a self-sufficient essence;
- recommends climate as a more natural classificatory principle than political continents.

### Meiners 1785/1790/1793 — fixed Round 2 control

- explicitly articulates a `Stamm / Race / Varietät / Spielart` hierarchy;
- explicitly refuses colour sole/principal-sign authority;
- increasingly regulates inference from bodily evidence, including anti-single-part / isolated-bone rules and provenance restrictions;
- retains climate but more sharply polices the evidential and rank structure used to stabilize human distinctions.

### Final Round 3 boundary formulation

> Zimmermann 1778 constructs a generative historical natural history in which large human differences can be made causally conceivable through climate, nourishment, duration, inheritance, isolation, migration and mixture, while his classificatory vocabulary remains deliberately mobile. Meiners later works inside much of the same causal field but increases the explicit regulation of taxonomic rank and admissible bodily evidence.

This is a stronger boundary than `Zimmermann = climate; Meiners = race`, and it is now supported by direct inspection of the primary page clusters on both sides.
