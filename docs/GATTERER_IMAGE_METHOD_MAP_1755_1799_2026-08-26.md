# Gatterer image-to-method map, 1755–1799 — 26 August 2026

## Scope

This file implements the corrected project direction: **Gatterer first, image to method**. It does not treat all images around Gatterer as one iconographic series. Instead it distinguishes visual forms by the historical operation they perform and the methodological problem they address.

The current evidence supports at least four distinct visual regimes.

---

## I. 1755 `Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum`: reproduced artifacts as testimony

### Visual operation

The large plates in Gatterer's 1755 genealogy reproduce family artifacts, charters, seals, inscriptions, armorial and other material evidence in order to support claims about antiquity, lineage and status.

A recent source-production reconstruction by André de Melo Araújo (2025) shows that the printed plates emerged through a collaborative chain involving family documents, handwritten copies, drawings, editorial correction and engraving. Some engravings were not copied directly from surviving originals but through intermediate manuscript/drawing states.

### Methodological tension

Gatterer states in the 1755 project that historical truth requires authoritative testimony and evidence. Yet the printed medium could abbreviate, resize and rearrange documentary artifacts. For surviving charters compared by Araújo, line breaks and widths differ from the originals.

This matters because the older Gatterer later criticized Gottfried Bessel's `Chronicon Gotwicense` (1732) precisely for adapting engraved charters to book format in ways that gave a false impression of their original overall appearance.

### Analytical use

The 1755 plates should therefore not be read simply as an early version of Gatterer's mature diplomatics. They expose an earlier mixed evidentiary economy:

`artifact / family archive`
→ `copy / drawing / editorial manuscript`
→ `engraved surrogate`
→ `genealogical testimony + social authority`

Later diplomatic standards tighten the required equivalence between artifact and representation.

### Source controls

- Johann Christoph Gatterer, `Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum` (Nürnberg, 1755).
- André de Melo Araújo, “Evidentiary Authority as a System: Johann Christoph Gatterer and the Collective Making of Historical Knowledge in the Eighteenth Century,” *Berichte zur Wissenschaftsgeschichte* (2025), DOI 10.1002/bewi.2145.
- Araújo, “Epistemologia paratextual…” (2025), on the 1755 preface, missing seals and erroneous images.

## II. 1759–1760s `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica / Testes temporum et veritatis`: disciplinary threshold image

### Visual operation

The inherited Müller–Tyroff Clio/frontispiece composition places `Historia`, `Genealogia` and `Heraldica` at the threshold of the handbook and surrounds historical work with material/graphic witnesses: genealogical tables, arms, coins, maps/papers and other learned objects.

The plate predates Gatterer's handbook use. It is therefore **not safely interpretable as a graphic manifesto designed by Gatterer**.

### Methodological importance under Gatterer

Once retained as the pretitular/frontispiece image of Gatterer's `Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik`, however, the inherited image acquires a new host-publication relation. The key question is not “what did Gatterer mean when he designed it?” but:

> What does an inherited visual equation of history, genealogy, heraldry and witnesses do when placed before a Gatterer handbook whose methodological programme increasingly systematizes the historical auxiliary sciences?

### Guard

Keep distinct:

`design genealogy`
≠ `Gatterer's intentional authorship`
≠ `new evidentiary function in a Gatterer host publication`.

The ongoing 1755 Tyroff plate-state test remains important only insofar as it clarifies what Gatterer inherited and in what physical state.

## III. 1766–1773 synchronistic tables: visual ordering against the successiveness of narrative

### Visual operation

This is the most directly Gattererian visual response to a problem in historical representation.

In `Vom historischen Plan` (1767), Gatterer defines the historian's work after collection and selection as the construction of a `Plan`: materials must be positioned so that the reader can understand why each piece stands where it does. The highest pragmatic ideal is the `Nexus rerum universalis`, the universal connection of events through causes and effects.

The problem is representational as well as conceptual. Universal history must coordinate distant and simultaneous events, while verbal narrative unfolds successively. Modern scholarship on Gatterer's tables has therefore described a tension between the successiveness of language and the simultaneity made possible by visual/tabular form.

Gatterer's relevant sequence is:

- `Synopsis historiae universalis sex tabulis comprehensa` (1766; enlarged 1769);
- `Vom historischen Plan` (1767);
- `Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie zur Erläuterung seiner synchronistischen Tabellen` (1771), including folded tables;
- second `Abriß der Universalhistorie` (1773), with chronological/historical tables.

### Methodological operation

The synchronistic table is not merely a mnemonic chronology. It can be modelled as an attempted solution to:

`successive narration`
versus
`simultaneous / spatially distributed history`.

It places historical units on paper according to temporal coexistence and relative sequence so that relations otherwise dispersed through prose can be grasped synoptically.

Later table-makers explicitly described the aim as arranging events spatially on paper as they stood beside or followed one another in time, making chronology and synchronism sensible to the eye.

### Analytical consequence

This is likely the strongest bridge from **image/layout** to Gatterer's theory of historical composition:

`collection → selection → plan → arrangement → causal/synchronous relation → historical whole`.

The table participates in the `Plan`; it is not downstream decoration.

### Source controls

- Gatterer, `Vom historischen Plan, und der darauf sich gründenden Zusammenfügung der Erzählungen`, *Allgemeine historische Bibliothek* 1 (1767), 15–89, esp. discussion of plan/arrangement and pp. 84–85 on `Nexus rerum universalis`.
- Gatterer, `Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie…` (Göttingen, 1771), complete BSB/MDZ digital object `bsb10716342`, 1370 scans, including ten folded leaves.
- Secondary control: scholarship on the “primacy of the optical” and the relation between Gatterer's synchronistic tables and the successiveness of language.

## IV. 1770–1799 Quedlinburg gravestone / diplomatics: visual form as an expert test and reproduction problem

### The 1770 controversy

A gravestone discovered at Quedlinburg was publicly proposed as the tomb of Henry the Fowler. Newspaper publicity framed it as a major antiquarian discovery; a counter-response dismissed both the identification and, potentially, its historical importance.

Gatterer was asked for an expert opinion. He deliberately framed his response as a `diplomatisches Gutachten` / `diplomatisches Responsum`, transferring procedures from diplomatics to a monumental inscription.

### Evidentiary sequence

The reconstructed procedure is:

1. **graphics / script form** — the letter forms are classified as neo-Gothic (`Genus Thulemarium`), ruling out a tenth-century royal tomb;
2. **semiotics / heraldic comparison** — comparison with engravings in Erath's `Codex diplomaticus Quedlinburgensis` links the arms to the Hoym family;
3. **iconography** — the figure is read as a knight rather than a king;
4. conclusion — the stone belongs to Friedrich von Hoym, not Henry the Fowler.

This is a strong case in which material/visual traits can overturn transmitted narrative identification.

### Reproduction failure

The first printed version in *Allgemeine historische Bibliothek* 15 (1770) introduced a crucial problem: the Halle printer represented distinct neo-Gothic letter forms with inappropriate or identical metal types. The graphic differences on which Gatterer's dating argument depended were therefore visually distorted or lost in print.

In his later `Praktische Diplomatik` (1799), Gatterer explicitly states that the earlier print contained serious errors and that the neo-Gothic letters decisive for dating had been badly deformed. The improved republication therefore required corrected type forms.

### Methodological consequence

The case yields a compact principle:

> **An evidentiary operation can be methodologically sound yet become unverifiable when its visual distinctions are not preserved through reproduction.**

The historian must therefore control not only source criticism but the media chain through which source distinctions reach readers.

### Source controls

- Gatterer, `Diplomatisches Responsum ... Heinrichs des Finklers Grabmal`, *Allgemeine historische Bibliothek* 15 (1770), 3–30, with acts and drawings.
- Gatterer, `Praktische Diplomatik` (1799), republication/correction of the responsum.
- André de Melo Araújo, “Transmediating Historical Artifacts…” (2022), detailed reconstruction of the gravestone images and their 1770, 1783 and 1799 reproductions.
- Kasper Risbjerg Eskildsen, *Modern Historiography in the Making* / related preprint discussion of the Quedlinburg case.

## V. A fifth visual method to keep separate: `Linnaeismus graphicus`

Gatterer's diplomatics and palaeography also use visual taxonomy. His `Linnaeismus graphicus` classifies scripts through classes, orders, series, genera and species, extending the visual-comparative logic of diplomatic tables beyond individual Latin examples toward geographically, nationally and chronologically ordered forms of writing.

This should not be collapsed into synchronistic world-history tables:

- synchronistic tables = ordering **events and peoples in time/space**;
- graphic/palaeographic tables = classifying **formal traits of historical scripts/documents**;
- genealogical plates = reproducing **particular artifacts as testimony**;
- Clio frontispiece = representing **disciplines/witnesses at the threshold of a publication**.

The shared issue is visual organization, not identical function.

## VI. Methodological disputes now most relevant to the image corpus

### 1. Antiquarian accumulation vs historical synthesis

Gatterer's programme does not reject source collection; it makes collection prior to a further task: selection, ordering and synthesis. His 1767 journal programme explicitly responds to a perceived lack of good German historical works and aims at improvement of historical knowledge.

The visual question is therefore: when does a table, plate or frontispiece remain a collection-display, and when does it become part of an explanatory historical `Plan`?

### 2. Successive narrative vs synchronous overview

This is the dispute most directly embodied in the synchronistic tables. Prose cannot easily show simultaneous distant processes. Tabular disposition can.

### 3. Testimony/text vs material and graphic traits

The Quedlinburg case shows professional visual/material analysis overruling transmitted identification and contemporary reportage.

### 4. Original artifact vs reproduction

The later critique of Bessel and the republication of the Quedlinburg responsum show that copied evidence is not neutral. Format, type, line breaks, reduction and engraving can change what can be inferred.

### 5. Gatterer vs Schlözer: what belongs inside Universalgeschichte

Both pursued systematic universal history, but their emphases diverged. Gatterer's system remained strongly built from chronology, charters, numismatics, heraldry, maps and auxiliary sciences; Schlözer increasingly argued for a universal history that could omit criticism, chronology, king-lists and antiquarian minutiae from the narrative itself while incorporating political, commercial, religious, artistic, learned and statistical history.

Their 1773 public quarrel also had an institutional/economic dimension: competition for the Universalgeschichte lecture audience. Do not reduce the methodological difference to personal rivalry, but do not detach it from the Göttingen teaching market either.

### 6. Historical standpoint / selection

Gatterer's 1768 `Abhandlung vom Standort und Gesichtspunct des Geschichtschreibers oder der teutsche Livius` belongs in the context of Chladenius's earlier theory of the historical viewpoint. The historian's selection changes with standpoint and temporal distance without, for Gatterer, simply dissolving historical truth into arbitrary subjectivity.

This matters for images/tables because any visual overview is itself a technology of selection: what enters the field, what receives a column, what becomes simultaneous, and what is omitted are methodological decisions.

## VII. Revised article architecture hypothesis

A stronger Gatterer-first architecture is now visible:

### A. Threshold
**Clio frontispiece:** inherited image; disciplines and witnesses made co-present before reading begins.

### B. Testimony
**1755 genealogical plates:** artifacts converted into visible authority, but through a reproduction chain capable of deformation.

### C. Plan
**1766–73 synchronistic tables:** visual layout used to solve the problem of assembling selected events into a historical whole beyond successive prose.

### D. Test
**1770 Quedlinburg / later diplomatics:** formal visual traits become an expert procedure that can overturn textual tradition; reproduction itself becomes an object of criticism.

This sequence is not yet a claim of linear intellectual development. It is a controlled comparison of four visual operations inside Gatterer's changing historical practice.
