# Gatterer's handbook from refreshed reference apparatus to embedded teaching tool, 1759–1766

## Result

The title-state sequence of Gatterer's *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik* shows more than ordinary reissue language. Between 1759 and 1766 the object is progressively redefined along two independent axes:

1. **current-reference maintenance** — fresh genealogical tables, arms, corrections, and eventually restored annual publication;
2. **methodological instruction** — an explicit `Abriß der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde`, culminating in a separately articulated student-oriented treatise with eight explanatory copperplates.

The sequence is therefore not simply:

`calendar → handbook → calendar again`.

A more precise material/intellectual sequence is:

`updated political-genealogical reference apparatus`
→ `expanded and repeatedly reworked reference corpus`
→ `reference corpus + compressed methodological instruction`
→ `annual handbook + explicit heraldic teaching apparatus`.

This matters because it shows the same publishing object being asked to do two jobs at once: identify current persons/houses and train readers how to read the visual-heraldic system itself.

## I. 1759: refreshed reference apparatus

The 1759 title reads:

*Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik ... Aufs neue ausgefertigt von M. Johann Christoph Gatterer*.

The object contains the familiar paired architecture of armorial engravings and genealogical tables. Surviving copy descriptions also report a terminal register/corrections layer (`Register und Verbesserungen` / equivalent collation language), showing that the format change away from an explicitly annual `Wappencalender` did not automatically eliminate correction apparatus.

Evidence level for the title and contents: direct bibliographic/digital-object control. Evidence level for the exact internal wording of the terminal corrections in 1759 remains copy-collation/catalogue rather than direct page transcription.

Safe formulation:

> The 1759 Handbuch is presented as a newly prepared current-reference work rather than as a static reprint of the old calendar.

## II. 1761: the same refresh language remains

The 1761 edition again appears as *Aufs neue ausgefertigt* by Gatterer.

The point is modest but useful: `Handbuch` did not mean a one-time frozen reference object. The work remained revisable across successive issues even before the word `jährlich` returned to the title.

Do not infer a fixed biennial publication rule merely from surviving years. The secure statement is that multiple revised Handbuch states circulated between 1759 and 1763.

## III. 1762: the rhetoric shifts from refresh to reconstruction and expansion

The 1762 title escalates its claim:

`Ganz neu ausgearbeitet und mit vielen Stammtafeln und Wappen vermehret`.

This is materially more ambitious than `aufs neue ausgefertigt`.

The work now advertises:

- comprehensive reworking (`ganz neu ausgearbeitet`);
- enlargement of the genealogical table corpus;
- enlargement of the armorial image corpus.

The object is therefore not merely refreshed at the level of current names/dates. Its representational infrastructure itself is expanded.

A useful distinction is:

`data refresh`
≠
`corpus expansion / architectural reworking`.

The title-state evidence shows Gatterer's Handbuch moving through both operations.

## IV. 1763: methodological instruction enters the advertised architecture

The 1763 title adds a further element:

`ganz neu ausgearbeitet und mit vielen Stammtafeln und Wappen wie auch einem Abrisse der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde vermehrt`.

This is a structural change in the function of the book.

The Handbuch already supplies:

- current genealogical identification;
- heraldic images;
- descriptions / reference lookup.

Now it also promises an **abstract of heraldry itself**.

The relation becomes:

`reference examples`
+
`genealogical tables`
+
`armorial corpus`
+
`explicit rules/concepts for reading heraldry`.

This moves the book toward a self-explanatory instructional environment. The reader is not only given answers to `which house/person/arms?`; the reader is increasingly given a method for decoding and classifying the represented system.

## V. 1764: annual time is explicitly reattached to the Handbuch

The 1764 title fuses the previously distinct genre labels:

*Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender auf das Jahr 1764 oder jährliches Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik*.

It also advertises:

- the genealogical tables and arms of current European rulers;
- a `richtige Beschreibung der Wappen`;
- an `Abriß der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde`.

This confirms that methodological instruction survives the re-annualization of the object.

The new configuration is therefore:

`annual refresh cycle`
+
`current-reference corpus`
+
`correct armorial description`
+
`compressed heraldic method`.

Annualization and didactic abstraction are not alternatives. They coexist inside one publication.

## VI. 1766: the method component becomes an explicit student tool with eight copperplates

By 1766 the publication environment directly names Gatterer's *Abriß der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde* as designed:

`zum Nutzen der studirenden Jugend`

and `mit acht Kupfertafeln erläutert`.

The *Abriß* exists as a separately identifiable teaching work, but contemporary descriptions of the 1766 *Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender / jährliches Handbuch* state that Gatterer's *Abriß* and its eight explanatory plates were **added to the calendar/handbook**.

This gives a striking compositional relation:

`annual current reference book`
+
`portable textbook of heraldic method`
+
`eight explanatory visual plates`.

The old calendar ecology had long included a central colour key, armorial images, descriptions and genealogical tables. Gatterer's intervention adds a higher-order explanatory layer: the object can now teach the rules governing the kinds of signs that its own reference section contains.

A useful analytical phrase is **self-interpreting reference apparatus**, provided it is marked as analytical vocabulary rather than actor language.

## VII. Two axes should be kept separate

The 1759–66 development is easier to understand if two variables are not collapsed.

### A. Temporal maintenance

- `aufs neue ausgefertigt`;
- `ganz neu ausgearbeitet`;
- enlarged tables and arms;
- eventual explicit `jährliches Handbuch`.

This axis concerns keeping the represented political-genealogical world current and sufficiently complete.

### B. Reader competence / method

- `Abriß der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde`;
- correct descriptions of arms;
- eight explanatory plates;
- explicit utility for `studirenden Jugend`.

This axis concerns teaching the reader how the representational system works.

The same publication increasingly combines both:

`update the world represented`
+
`train the reader who must interpret it`.

## VIII. Relation to the earlier colour-key system

The older Wappenkalender already contained a serially recurring `Erklärung der Farben` copperplate and, in directly controlled 1744 material, a three-layer decoding architecture:

`hatching in armorial image`
→ `central colour legend`
→ `prose description/blazon`.

Gatterer's 1763–66 methodological insertion works at a different level. The colour key decodes one symbolic subsystem; the *Abriß* abstracts the rules of heraldry more generally.

A safe hierarchy is:

`local graphic decoder` — colour key

`object-specific verbal decoder` — armorial description

`general methodological decoder` — Abriß der Heraldik.

This provides a stronger account of information architecture than treating the added treatise as merely supplementary text.

## IX. Relation to current-news use

The earlier Wappenkalender explicitly advertised genealogical tables for `täglichen Zeitungs-Lesen`, and the related *Bequemes ... Hand-Buch* placed `Zeitungs-Lesen` in its title.

The Gatterer sequence does not abandon that current-reference inheritance. Its titles continue to emphasise `neueste Genealogie`, `jetzige europäische Potentaten`, refreshed tables and current ruling houses while adding heraldic method.

The publication thus connects three reader operations:

1. identify current actors/houses encountered in news;
2. read and distinguish their genealogical/heraldic representations;
3. learn the rules that make those representations intelligible.

The object is simultaneously a political lookup instrument and a pedagogical apparatus for historical auxiliary knowledge.

## X. Relation to the 1767–1772 cumulative turn

The next Seyfart/Raspe phase changes the architecture again. The 1771 publisher *Vorbericht* explicitly says the old `genealogisch-heraldische Handbuch oder Wappencalender` supplied the basis for a larger Wappenbuch, whose armorial material would be gathered progressively and delivered in annual instalments.

The full sequence can therefore now be represented with two dimensions:

### Temporal / distribution architecture

`annual Wappenkalender`
→ `revised Handbuch states`
→ `jährliches Handbuch / Wappen-Calender`
→ `annual instalments toward cumulative Wappenbuch`.

### Interpretive architecture

`colour legend + descriptions`
→ `expanded armorial/genealogical reference corpus`
→ `embedded Abriß der Heraldik`
→ `student-oriented methodological treatise + explanatory plates`.

The project should resist reducing this to a history of titles. It is a reconfiguration of update frequency, corpus breadth, visual decoding and reader training.

## Evidence guards

1. The 1759 terminal `Verbesserungen` are currently controlled at surviving-copy collation level; their exact internal cross-reference mechanics still require direct page inspection.
2. `Aufs neue ausgefertigt` and `ganz neu ausgearbeitet` are title-state claims and should not be automatically converted into quantified amounts of revision without copy comparison.
3. The 1763 *Abriß* insertion establishes advertised methodological content; exact boundaries between the embedded version and the 1766 separately articulated treatise require text-level comparison.
4. The 1766 claim that the *Abriß* and eight plates were appended to the calendar/handbook is bibliographically well attested, but copy-specific binding configurations may vary.
5. Do not equate `zum Nutzen der studirenden Jugend` with use only by university students; it is actor language for pedagogical purpose, not a complete readership census.
6. Re-annualization in 1764 should be treated as a publication-architecture change. Its precise commercial cause remains open unless a contemporary publisher statement is found.
