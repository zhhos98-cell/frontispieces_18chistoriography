## Evidence basis

User-supplied full-page images of printed pp.100, 101, 105, 106 and 107 were visually inspected, together with the already controlled pp.108–111 cluster from checkpoint 04. These pages now permit a substantially tighter reconstruction of what Zimmermann means by `Race`, `Menschengattung`, `Stamm`, `Art`, `Abartung`, mixture and climatic gradation.

The main result is that Zimmermann's race vocabulary is not merely terminologically loose. The looseness corresponds to a deliberately historical and generative model in which human forms can arise, persist, mix, grade into one another, and be reorganised under different classificatory principles.

## p.100 — a Race can be generated by inherited family peculiarity plus non-mixture

Zimmermann argues that a visible inherited peculiarity in a European family could, if reproduction remained unmixed, itself generate a race:

> `Denn es ist höchst wahrscheinlich, daß solche europäische Eltern, wenn sie stets unvermischet blieben, gleichfalls eine Race mit eingedrückten Nasen hervorbringen würden ...`

The point is polemical. Philosophers who later encountered such a lineage might speculate about it just as they speculate about `Negerarten`.

### Primary-direct consequence

`Race` is here **historically producible**. It need not denote an original branch fixed at creation. A persistent inherited family character under reproductive separation is enough, in principle, to yield what observers would call a race.

This sharply limits any attempt to read Zimmermann's `Race` as a rigid ontological rank.

## p.101 — common Stamm is a possible derivation, not mathematical certainty

Zimmermann summarizes the preceding argument:

> `... daß alle Menschengattungen von einem und eben demselben Stamm entsprungen sind, oder wenigstens haben entspringen können.`

The modal qualification is essential: `entsprungen sind, oder wenigstens haben entspringen können`.

He then states the epistemic limit of the climatic argument:

> `Mir ist es hinreichend, einigermaßen gezeigt zu haben, was die Wirkung des Klima vermag: es ist freilich wieder eine andere Frage, ob sie sich wirklich so geäußert habe? Dies wird man nie mathematisch gewiß sagen können ...`

He therefore allows readers to retain several `Stammväter` if they wish, while insisting that a single-stock derivation is at least a possible, simple and unforced route.

### Primary-direct consequence

Zimmermann is not presenting monogenesis as demonstrated fact. His procedure is closer to **possibility proof / causal sufficiency**:

1. remove the apparent impossibility of deriving very different human forms from one stock;
2. show that climate and related causes can in principle produce the required variation;
3. refuse `mathematisch gewiß` historical certainty about whether the mechanism actually operated exactly so.

This epistemic posture directly anticipates the later p.114–115 self-description of the migration reconstruction as an `Einfall`.

### Taxonomic consequence

`Menschengattungen` and `Stamm` are not functioning here as a fixed Linnaean-style hierarchy. Multiple `Menschengattungen` may be derivable from one `Stamm`.

The following paragraph also speaks of deriving all humans `von eben derselben Art`, reinforcing that `Art`, `Gattung` and `Stamm` operate at overlapping but non-identical explanatory levels rather than in a clean rank sequence.

## p.105 — isolation and endogamy can produce a new Menschenrace

Zimmermann constructs a thought experiment in which a peculiar family becomes separated from other humans and settles an uninhabited region or island. Forced to marry within the family, it would eventually produce:

> `... ohnfehlbar eine wunderbare Menschenrace ...`

whose external appearance might differ from Europeans even more than the Negro does.

He imagines later philosophers treating the newly discovered population either as a local environmental degeneration or as proof of multiple original ancestors. Zimmermann then asks whether both parties would not have erred.

He explicitly bounds the claim:

> `Ich bin gar nicht gewiß, daß auf diese Art wirklich einige wichtige Abartungen in unserm Geschlechte entstanden sind; aber die Entstehung sich so möglich zu denken, wird mir niemand gänzlich läugnen können ...`

The discussion then invokes hereditary disease and asks how long inherited traits might persist if affected individuals reproduced only with one another, especially where the `Erdstrich` favoured the condition.

### Primary-direct consequence

Zimmermann has at least two distinct race-generating mechanisms in play:

1. **climatic/environmental transformation over time**;
2. **reproductive isolation + endogamy + inheritance**, potentially reinforced by locality/climate.

The existence of the second mechanism is crucial. Climate is powerful, but it is not the only mechanism by which a `Menschenrace` can emerge.

## p.106 — colour is explicitly continuous: `Nüancen`, `Stufenfolge`, `nach und nach`

Zimmermann returns to the forms lying between Negro and white:

> `Um nun wieder zu den Nüancen, welche zwischen dem Neger und Weißen in der Mitte liegen, zu kommen, so giebt es hier eine große Verschiedenheit, oder vielmehr Stufenfolge.`

He then moves through blond and brunette whites, brownish and brown populations, Hottentots and Moors, writing that nature passes `nach und nach` toward the Negro.

The page also links American differences to climate and then introduces the attempts of philosophical naturalists to bring human differences into `Klassen` or `Hauptracen`.

### Primary-direct consequence: colour intensity

Round 3 Question 4 can now be extended beyond environmental temperature.

Zimmermann does use explicit **gradation language for visible human colour/form**:

- `Nüancen`;
- `Stufenfolge`;
- `nach und nach`;
- intermediate colour terms between white and black.

Thus the 1778 text contains two different intensive regimes:

1. pp.31–34: degrees and thermometric intensity of climate;
2. p.106: shades / stages / gradual transitions of bodily colour and appearance.

These must remain analytically distinct, but Zimmermann places them inside one causal-geographical argument.

## p.107 — classification by continents fails; migration and mixture destabilize racial division

Zimmermann first criticizes Linné's human taxonomy. He rejects the supposed second human `Art` and states that the pathological `Kakerlack` is only a sick individual of the Negro race, not a separate species.

Linné's division of the true human into four `Racen` by the four parts of the world is then rejected as impossible because politically divided continents do not correspond to natural/climatic boundaries:

> `... daß es ganz und gar unmöglich ist, die Menschengattungen nach der bloß politisch getheilten Erde zu theilen.`

The reason given is physical: parts of Asia have the same climate as parts of Europe, and nature has not itself separated those world regions.

Zimmermann then introduces a second complication:

> `Zweitens wird die Zerfällung des Menschengeschlechts noch durch eine wichtige Nebenursache schwer, nemlich durch die Völkerwanderung, und die daher entstandenen Vermischungen.`

He adduces migrations in Asia, Siberia, Africa and potentially America.

### Primary-direct consequence

Race classification is difficult for Zimmermann because present populations are **historically mixed distributions**, not simply local expressions of fixed continental partitions.

This provides the necessary context for p.109. His recommendation to attend more closely to climate when dividing humanity into races is not a naïve mapping of latitude to phenotype. It is an attempt to replace political-continent classification with a physical-causal principle while explicitly acknowledging that migration and mixture obscure the signal.

## p.108 — mixture introduces temporal lag; blackness becomes a hypothetical rate variable

The already controlled p.108 now reads differently in light of pp.100–107.

Zimmermann says migration and mixture naturally produce such a mixture of human form that only a long sequence of years can adapt newcomers to climate or produce the form climate would otherwise have generated.

He then proposes, explicitly as a conjectural calculation, asking how much time would be required to make a Negro from a white person in a given climate, and:

> `... um wie viel die Schwärze mit der Zeit etwa in jedem Jahrhunderte zunähme ...`

### Primary-direct consequence: temporally indexed intensity

This is stronger than a simple statement that skin colour varies continuously.

Zimmermann imagines **blackness itself as a quantity changing through time**. The relevant operator is not merely shade but rate:

`degree of blackness × centuries of climatic exposure`.

The passage is hypothetical rather than empirical. It should therefore be recorded as a proposed inferential programme, not as a measured law.

Still, it gives the project an unusually explicit eighteenth-century instance of bodily difference represented as a temporally graded process.

## Integrated mechanism map after pp.100–108

The first volume now supports the following actor-level model:

### A. Common-stock possibility

`one Stamm / same Art → multiple Menschengattungen possible`

with explicit epistemic modesty rather than demonstration.

### B. Climatic transformation

`migration into different climates → time of exposure → gradual bodily change / colour gradation`

with climate measurable as physical intensity and bodily colour representable as `Nüancen` / `Stufenfolge`.

### C. Reproductive isolation

`family peculiarity → non-mixture / endogamy → inherited persistence → possible new Race`

with locality capable of reinforcing inheritance.

### D. Historical mixture

`Völkerwanderung → Vermischung → mixed present forms → classification becomes difficult`

so present geography cannot be read as an uncomplicated map of original races.

### E. Classification

`visible differences → attempts at Klassen / Hauptracen → political-continent schemes rejected → climate preferred as a more natural classificatory principle`

but climate is only one organizing principle inside a historically mixed world.

## What `Race` now means operationally in Zimmermann 1778

A rigid lexical definition is still unavailable, but the primary pages establish a strong operational profile.

A `Race` can be:

- produced historically rather than given at origin;
- sustained through non-mixture and reproduction;
- altered by climate over long duration;
- mixed through migration;
- grouped into larger `Hauptracen` by naturalists;
- misclassified if political geography is substituted for physical-natural relations.

This makes `Race` a **generative and historical population category**, not a stable modern biological taxon and not a simple synonym for skin colour.

## Revision to the colour-jurisdiction result

Checkpoint 04 correctly established that climate enters the classificatory act. The present pages refine the earlier bounded statement that colour is merely a visible variable mediated by climate.

Colour has at least four jurisdictions in Zimmermann:

1. **description** — populations are distinguished by visible colour;
2. **gradation** — colour is arranged as `Nüancen` / `Stufenfolge`;
3. **causal outcome** — climate can generate or deepen colour over time;
4. **classification input** — visible differences motivate race schemes, but colour alone does not determine the natural division.

The key point is therefore not that Zimmermann excludes colour from taxonomy. He **subordinates its taxonomic meaning to a causal-historical model** involving climate, duration, inheritance, migration and mixture.

## Zimmermann ↔ Meiners boundary after checkpoint 05

The contrast can now be stated much more precisely.

### Zimmermann 1778

- uses a mobile vocabulary: `Art`, `Menschengattung`, `Stamm`, `Race`, `Hauptrace`, `Abartung`, `Nation`, `Familie`;
- demonstrates the *possibility* of deriving large human differences from one stock while refusing mathematical certainty about actual history;
- permits races to emerge through reproductive isolation and inherited family peculiarities;
- represents colour as a continuum and, hypothetically, as a time-dependent rate of change;
- treats migration and mixture as major obstacles to clean classification;
- prefers climate to political continents as a natural organizing principle.

### Meiners 1785/1790/1793 — fixed Round 2 control

- retains climatic causation but articulates a more explicit `Stamm / Race / Varietät / Spielart` rank structure;
- explicitly denies colour sole/principal evidential authority;
- introduces stronger rules against inference from isolated bodily parts and stronger specimen-provenance controls;
- later selectively incorporates Kantian terminology while retaining his own hierarchy.

### Strong current boundary formulation

> Zimmermann supplies a generative historical model of human variation in which races can arise, grade, migrate and mix; Meiners does not invent that causal space, but increases the regulation of taxonomic rank and of the evidence licensed to stabilize distinctions within it.

This is now primary-supported rather than a secondary-literature hypothesis.

## Consequence for the broader frontispiece / historiography project

The sequence is structurally important because it shows that late-eighteenth-century human classification could operate through a layered inferential architecture:

`measured environment → gradual visible difference → historical reproduction and migration → classificatory grouping`.

The layers are connected but not collapsed. A visible shade is not automatically a race; a race does not automatically imply a separate origin; current geography does not automatically reveal natural history; and a causal possibility is not automatically historical certainty.

That separation of inferential levels is now one of the strongest controls in the repository.
