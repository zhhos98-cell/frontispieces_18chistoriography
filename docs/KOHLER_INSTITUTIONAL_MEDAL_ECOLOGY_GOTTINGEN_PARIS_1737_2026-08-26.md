## II. One 1737 annual volume contains a cluster of Göttingen institutional medals

The structured volume index locates four unusually concentrated entries concerning the foundation/inauguration of Göttingen's new university:

### p. 233–240 — 1734 foundation medal

Reverse inscription:

`IN PVBLICA COMMODA - ACADEMIA GEORGIA AVGVSTA GOTTINGAE FVNTATA MDCCXXXIIII`.

### p. 297–304 — 1737 inauguration medal

The medal explicitly records the inauguration of the Georgia Augusta at Göttingen on 17 September 1737.

### p. 305–312 — second inauguration medal

Reverse includes:

`MVSARVM IVNGIT AMORES - ACADEMIA GEORGIA AVGVSTA GOETINGAE INAVGVRATA ...`.

### p. 313–320 — third inauguration medal

Reverse includes:

`VT CAPIANT FRVCTVS - ACADEMIA GEORGIA AVGVSTA GOETTINGAE INAVGVRATA ...`.

The point is not simply that university medals exist. Köhler gives each object the normal serial treatment of an eight-page unit: engraved medal, description, and historical explanation.

Safe operation:

`institutional event`
→ `medal object`
→ `engraved serial surrogate`
→ `inscription/iconography`
→ `eight-page historical explanation`.

The newly founded university becomes a historical object through numismatic serialization almost immediately after its creation.

## III. p. 361–368: the Paris inscription-medal academy enters the same serial field

Later in the same 1737 volume Köhler devotes another eight-page article to the 1663 medal commemorating Louis XIV's foundation of the royal academy of inscriptions and medals.

The structured index records:

`Frankreich: Gedenk-Münze 1663 auf die von Kg. Ludwig XIV. gestiftete Königl. Academie der Aufschriften und Gedenk-Münzen`.

The reverse legend is especially important:

`RERVM GESTARVM FIDES`

with the institutional exergue/legend:

`ACADEMIA REG INSCRPT ET NVMISM INSTIT MDCLXIII`.

Thus the medal makes a proposition about the institution's historical function at the level of its own inscription: inscriptions and medals are associated with the `fides` of deeds/events.

Do not translate this directly into Köhler's own theoretical wording until the eight printed pages are inspected. What is secure is that Köhler selected, engraved, described and historically explained a medal whose institutional self-representation explicitly joined an academy of inscriptions/medals to `RERUM GESTARUM FIDES`.

## IV. Specialist scholarship confirms that this was a full historical explanation, not a catalogue mention

Torsten Fried and Mark Hengerer's study of Louis XIV's medals in the Holy Roman Empire reconstructs Köhler's treatment in detail.

They identify the 1737 `RERUM GESTARUM FIDES` piece, pp. 361–368, among a select group of Louis XIV medals that Köhler reproduced graphically and **explained in detail through the history of the event represented**, normally in an eight-page text.

They further show that when Köhler returned in 1742 to the full 318-medal Louis XIV series, he explicitly referred readers back to this 1737 Académie article (p. 362).

Therefore the 1737 item had a continuing navigational life inside Köhler's serial:

`1737 detailed academy-medal article`
→ `1742 systematic Louis XIV medal catalogue cross-reference`.

This is important for the repository's concern with reusable printed reference objects: an earlier weekly article becomes a node inside a later systematic series discussion.

## VII. The Paris medal's own iconographic programme is independently significant

Modern study of the 1663 academy medal identifies an antiquarian programme around `RERVM GESTARVM FIDES`: the academy is represented through historical monuments, medals and inscription-making as media for faithful remembrance of royal deeds.

This makes the medal a strong comparator for the project's wider language of `testes temporum et veritatis`, but the relation should presently be phrased as a **rhetorical-operational convergence around materialized historical fidelity**, not as a direct source for the Clio inscription.

The Latin formulas differ and the transmission path has not been established.
