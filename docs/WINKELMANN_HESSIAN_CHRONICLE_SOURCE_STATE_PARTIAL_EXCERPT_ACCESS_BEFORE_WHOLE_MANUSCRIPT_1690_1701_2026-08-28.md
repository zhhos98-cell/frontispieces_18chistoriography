# Winkelmann's Hessian Chronicle source state: partial excerpt access before whole-manuscript access, c.1690–1701

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE SOURCE-STATE RESOLUTION — PRE-1697 MANUSCRIPT/WRITTEN HESSIAN CHRONICLE DIRECTLY CONTROLLED; LEIBNIZ HAD EXCERPT-LEVEL ACCESS BY c.1690 BUT IN 1691 STILL WISHED TO READ THE WRITTEN CHRONICLE ITSELF; WHOLE-MANUSCRIPT TRANSMISSION TO LEIBNIZ NOT ESTABLISHED

## Result

The apparent chronological contradiction in the Winkelmann material can now be resolved without forcing the 1694 Leibniz notes into the 1697 printed edition.

The controlled sequence is:

`c.1690: Leibniz already possesses / knows Winkelmann Hessian-Chronicle excerpts, stored with Geographica`

→

`c.1691: Leibniz explicitly wishes to read Winkelmann's "geschriebene heßische Chronick selbst" if permission can be obtained, especially if printing will be delayed`

→

`c.1691: Winkelmann himself refers to "meiner lezten und teutschen heßischen Chronik" as an already composed work containing extinct Hessian counties/lordships`

→

`1694?: Leibniz working notes use material that the modern editors can identify against passages in the later 1697 print`

→

`1697: Parts I–V of the printed Beschreibung der Fürstenthümer Hessen und Hersfeld appear in Bremen`

→

`1701: Leibniz says he has seen one printed tome of the Hessian Chronicle and asks whether the work will appear further/fully`.

The most economical historical model is therefore:

> **Leibniz had partial, excerpt-level access to Winkelmann's written Hessian Chronicle before he had access to the whole manuscript and years before the 1697 print.**

This solves the source-state problem behind the 1690/1694 Geographica-linked notes.

Do **not** describe them as Leibniz reading the 1697 print before publication.

Do **not** yet claim that Winkelmann personally sent Leibniz the entire Hessian Chronicle manuscript.

Working label:

`PREPRINT MANUSCRIPT SOURCE → PARTIAL EXCERPT RESERVOIR → PROBLEM-FILE RETRIEVAL`.

## 1. c.1690: explicit Hessian-Chronicle excerpts already exist

Leibniz-Katalog ID **7139**, June 1690, filed under `Insignia`, states:

`Winkelman ex excerpt. Heßischer Chronick. liegen bey Geograf.`

The note concerns the two walking lions of the Counts of Diez and compares them with Brunswick insignia.

This establishes three points directly:

1. Leibniz had access to material described as excerpts from a Winkelmann Hessian Chronicle;
2. those excerpts were not kept with the individual `Insignia` note;
3. they were stored/retrieved through a `Geographica` location.

Source:

https://leibniz-katalog.bbaw.de/de/extended-search?ordnungsvermerk_normiert=insignia&view=list

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH CROSS-REFERENCE CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL LEIBNIZ-KATALOG / AKADEMIEAUSGABE`.

This is earlier than the 1697 print and even earlier than the intensified surviving Winkelmann–Leibniz correspondence beginning in 1693.

## 2. c.1691: Leibniz distinguishes excerpts from the written Chronicle itself

The decisive control is Akademieausgabe manuscript edition `Ms XII 713h`, N.324, dated around April 1691.

The piece is a Leibniz note / assessment concerning Winkelmann's historical work, especially the `Erneuertes Denckmahl`.

Leibniz criticizes Winkelmann's specimen for relying largely on known printed authorities and for not yet showing enough use of:

- `fontes`;
- `diplomata`;
- `scripta inedita`.

He says he would much rather have Winkelmann communicate or at least indicate:

`allerhand chronica Manuscripta inedita, diplomata und dergleichen`.

He then adds a particularly important wish: it would be useful if one could read Winkelmann's:

`geschriebene heßische Chronick selbst`

if permission were granted, especially:

`da es sich mit dem druck noch lange verweilsen solte`.

Official edition:

https://leibniz-potsdam.bbaw.de/fileadmin/Webdateien/Dateien/Ms_12_713h.pdf

N.324, pp.177–178 in the edition PDF.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY LEIBNIZ AUTOGRAPH TEXT CONTROLLED THROUGH AKADEMIEAUSGABE`.

### Consequence

By c.1691 Leibniz knew a **written Hessian Chronicle** existed and regarded full reading of the written work as distinct from whatever excerpts or derivative information he already possessed.

This is the key source-state distinction:

`excerpt access ≠ whole-manuscript access`.

---

## 3. The wording implies that the Chronicle was written but not yet printed

The same N.324 combines two explicit states:

- `geschriebene heßische Chronick`;
- printing might still be delayed for a long time.

Thus the work exists in a **written/pre-print state** around 1691.

This is not an inference from the 1697 publication date alone.

It is Leibniz's own contemporary wording.

The safe bibliographic sequence is:

`written Hessian Chronicle by 1691 or earlier`
→ `print delayed`
→ `printed Parts I–V in 1697`.

---

## 4. Winkelmann himself confirms an already composed German Hessian Chronicle

The immediately following Akademie item, N.325, is Leibniz's excerpt from Winkelmann's manuscript `De dynastiis Brunsvicensibus`, around April 1691.

The heading explicitly identifies Winkelmann's words from the manuscript preface:

`Verba domini Winkelmann in praef. Mso. de dynastiis Brunsv.`

Winkelmann explains that he has long collected hundreds of extinct counts and lords across Lower Saxony, Westphalia and Hesse and says that the 28 extinct counties/lordships reverted to the Hessian principality were already inserted in:

`meiner lezten und teutschen heßischen Chronik`.

Official edition:

https://leibniz-potsdam.bbaw.de/fileadmin/Webdateien/Dateien/Ms_12_713h.pdf

N.325, pp.178–179.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY WINKELMANN MANUSCRIPT WORDING PRESERVED IN LEIBNIZ AUTOGRAPH EXCERPT`.

### Consequence

The `geschriebene heßische Chronick` in Leibniz's N.324 is not a vague projected title.

Winkelmann's own contemporary manuscript language treats a recent German Hessian Chronicle as an already developed textual work containing substantial dynastic material.

## 5. N.324 also controls an actual manuscript/specimen transmission channel

N.324 begins from:

`Herrn Winckelmanni uberschickten Specimine des denckmahls der Familien ...`

Leibniz had therefore received a **specimen sent by Winkelmann** from another related historical/genealogical project.

The note assesses its source base and compares it with Letzner and other authorities.

This proves a direct document-transmission practice between Winkelmann's work and Leibniz's desk by c.1691.

It still does not prove that the entire Hessian Chronicle manuscript was sent.

The distinction must remain:

`Winkelmann → Leibniz specimen transmission = DIRECTLY CONTROLLED`.

`Winkelmann → Leibniz whole Hessian Chronicle manuscript = NOT CONTROLLED`.

But the transmission channel makes excerpt-level access historically unsurprising.

## 9. 1701: Leibniz later distinguishes the printed state too

In a letter of September 1701 to Nathanael von Staff, Leibniz writes that the late Winkelmann had composed a German Hessian Chronicle and says:

`dont j'ai vû même un Tome imprimé`.

He asks whether there is hope that the work will appear.

The critical edition identifies the work as Winkelmann's *Gründliche Beschreibung der Fürstenthümer Hessen und Hersfeld*, Parts I–V, Bremen 1697; Part VI appeared only in 1754.

Official critical-edition PDF:

https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2438/I20B.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2

`PRIMARY DIRECT CORRESPONDENCE TEXT CONTROLLED`.

This later statement adds a third source state:

`written Chronicle`
→ `excerpt access`
→ `printed tome(s)`.

Leibniz himself experienced these as distinguishable forms.

## 14. Strongest article-level proposition

> **Leibniz's access to Winkelmann's Hessian Chronicle was layered. By c.1690 he could refer to excerpts from the Chronicle stored with Geographica, yet around 1691 he still wished to read the “written Hessian Chronicle itself” if permission could be obtained, especially because printing might be delayed. Winkelmann's own manuscript preface simultaneously refers to his recent German Hessian Chronicle as an already composed work. The Geographica material is therefore best reconstructed as a partial pre-print excerpt reservoir rather than evidence that Leibniz possessed the whole manuscript or somehow read the 1697 edition in advance.**

This gives the repository its clearest current example of how a whole source, excerpt reservoir and problem files could remain materially distinct while participating in one historical argument.
