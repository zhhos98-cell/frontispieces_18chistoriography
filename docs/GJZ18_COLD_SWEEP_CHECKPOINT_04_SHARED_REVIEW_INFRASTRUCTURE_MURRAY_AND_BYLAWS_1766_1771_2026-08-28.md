# GJZ18 cold sweep checkpoint 04: shared review infrastructure, Murray identification pressure, and the 1766 by-laws

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE STRUCTURAL CORRECTION — REVIEW INFRASTRUCTURE CONTROLLED; MURRAY IDENTITY STRONG CANDIDATE ONLY; ARCHIVAL BY-LAWS LOCUS UPGRADED

## Result

The 1767 conflict between Johann Christoph Gatterer's Historical Institute and the Göttingen Society of Sciences must **not** be modeled as two cleanly separated institutional camps with separate publication organs.

A stronger reconstruction is now available:

`jurisdictional conflict`
+
`shared review infrastructure`
+
`overlapping personnel`
+
`continued publication inside Society channels`.

The official learned journal of the Göttingen Society, the *Göttingische Anzeigen von gelehrten Sachen* (GGA), was under the Society's supervision and was edited by **Johann David Michaelis from 1753 until October 1770**. Yet **Gatterer himself remained a contributor to the GGA during the very period in which Michaelis, Kästner and a Prof. Murray opposed the Historical Institute's jurisdictional programme**.

Modern attribution work also assigns several anonymous GGA reviews of Gatterer's own publications to Gatterer himself, including the 1767 notice of his *Synopsis historiae universalis*.

Thus the institutional quarrel was **not expulsion from the Society's communication infrastructure**. It was a dispute over what institutional form could claim autonomous scientific jurisdiction while the actors continued to inhabit overlapping Göttingen review, university and Society systems.

Working relation label:

`DIRECT INSTITUTIONAL POLEMIC WITH SHARED PUBLICATION INFRASTRUCTURE`.

## 1. The GGA was the Society's official organ under Michaelis

The IdRZ/GJZ18 periodical documentation identifies the *Göttingische Anzeigen von gelehrten Sachen* as:

- published under the supervision of the **Königliche Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften**;
- the official organ of the Göttingen Society of Sciences;
- edited by **Johann David Michaelis from March 1753 to October 1770**;
- succeeded in editorial direction by Christian Gottlob Heyne from October 1770.

This matters because Michaelis is also the actor who, as director of the Society, delivered the decisive institutional objection in the 15 April 1767 Senate discussion reconstructed by Martin Gierl.

Therefore:

`Michaelis as Society director / jurisdictional opponent`
+
`Michaelis as editor of the Society review organ`

are simultaneous roles.

Evidence status:

`PERIODICAL-INSTITUTIONAL METADATA CONTROLLED`.

This roster is structurally important.

Actors who later appear as rivals, opponents, successors or intermediary figures in the historiographical controversy map were simultaneously producing reviews inside the same Society-controlled medium.

A simple camp model therefore fails:

`Society journal = Michaelis/Kästner side`

versus

`Institute journal = Gatterer side`.

A better model is:

`shared Göttingen review infrastructure`
→ `multiple partially overlapping jurisdictions`
→ `institutional conflict over autonomy and disciplinary scope`
→ `competition also expressed through new specialist journals`.

Evidence status:

## 3. Gatterer's *Synopsis historiae universalis* was reviewed in the Society organ in 1767

Martin Gierl's source bibliography records an anonymous review of:

`Johann Christoph Gatterer, Synopsis historiae universalis, sex tabulis` (Göttingen, 1766)

in:

`Göttingische Anzeigen von gelehrten Sachen`, no. 13 (1767), pp. 98–104.

This is especially relevant because the *Synopsis* was explicitly offered to Gatterer's Historical Academy and materially embodied the synchronistic/table architecture around which his programme of universal history was being reorganized.

The *Synopsis* therefore entered the Society's official review channel immediately before the April 1767 jurisdictional confrontation.

## 4. Modern attribution places the *Synopsis* review under Gatterer himself

Gierl's *Fundamenta Historica* bibliography places the anonymous 1767 GGA review of the *Synopsis* inside the bibliography of **Gatterer's own writings**:

`[–] (1767i): o. T. [Rezension zu ders.: Synopsis historiae universalis, sex tabulis. Göttingen 1766] in: Göttingische Anzeigen von gelehrten Sachen 13, S. 98–104.`

The same bibliography similarly places anonymous GGA notices of Gatterer's:

- *Handbuch der Universalhistorie* (1765; GGA no. 74, pp. 593–598), and
- *Elementa artis diplomaticae universalis* (1765; GGA no. 47, pp. 377–384)

under Gatterer.

This is consistent with the documented GGA practice that contributors frequently reviewed their own publications and with the existence of manuscript reviewer keys later used by Schimpf/Fambach to reconstruct authorship.

The safe formulation is:

> **Modern reviewer attribution assigns the anonymous GGA notice of Gatterer's 1766 *Synopsis* to Gatterer himself.**

Do not describe it as an external Society-side judgment unless the reviewer attribution is overturned by primary reviewer-key control.

This is a major structural correction. The Society organ could serve at once as:

- a corporate publication of the institution resisting Gatterer's Academy/Institute jurisdiction;
- a venue in which Gatterer promoted or evaluated his own scholarly production;
- a shared review machine used by Michaelis, Kästner, Gatterer, Murray, Schlözer and others.

Evidence status:

## 5. Consequence: the conflict was jurisdictional, not communicative exclusion

Checkpoint 03 established the 1767 objection:

`Historical Institute should remain within history proper`
versus
`Gatterer's attempt to coordinate broader empirical / natural-historical resources`.

The GGA evidence now sharpens its meaning.

Michaelis and the Society were not simply trying to silence Gatterer or remove him from Göttingen's learned communication network. Gatterer could still publish in the Society organ and, on modern attribution, review his own works there.

The institutional issue was more precise:

`who may constitute an academy-like scientific umbrella?`

`which body may claim autonomous research jurisdiction and finance?`

`how far may a historical institute draw natural history and other sciences into its programme?`

This fits Gierl's reconstruction better than an intellectual-faction narrative.

Article-level payoff:

> **The boundary-policing of history occurred inside a densely shared publication ecology. The same medium that represented the Society's corporate learned authority also carried Gatterer's own historical programme. The 1767 conflict therefore concerned jurisdiction over the organization of knowledge rather than a clean division between mutually exclusive scholarly publics.**

## 6. `Prof. Murray`: Johann Philipp Murray is now the strongest candidate, but not yet normalized

Checkpoint 03 deliberately retained Gierl's `Prof. Murray` without a first name.

The new actor control changes the probability landscape.

### Johann Philipp Murray

The Niedersächsische Personen authority record identifies **Johann Philipp Murray (1726–1776)** as:

- historian;
- university teacher at Göttingen;
- extraordinary professor from 1755 and ordinary professor from 1762;
- **secretary of the Göttingen Academy/Society from 1762 to 1770**;
- ordinary member from 1770;
- later director in 1771–72 and 1774–75.

This places him institutionally at the center of the Society precisely in April 1767.

### Johann Andreas Murray

Johann Andreas Murray (1740–1791) was also a Göttingen professor in the period, but his field was medicine/botany/natural science. He was extraordinary professor from 1764 and ordinary professor from 1769; later he became a member of the Göttingen Academy.

Both brothers appear in the reconstructed 1767 GGA reviewer roster.

## 7. The by-laws locus is now much tighter: UAG `Kur 7540`, 23 Dec. 1766

A separate archival locus can now be added to the institutional evidence map:

`Gesetze des königl. Instituts der historischen Wissenschaften zu Göttingen`

- date: **23 December 1766**;
- archive: Göttingen University Archive;
- shelfmark: **`Kur 7540`**.

Peter N. Miller's *History and Its Objects: Antiquarianism and Material Culture since 1500* reproduces photographs of the **first and last pages** of these by-laws and cites the archival document directly.

Miller's reconstruction states that the institute:

- began with eight members, two in the natural sciences;
- organized translations, source collections and editions of medieval German historians;
- possessed diplomatic, numismatic and geographical study collections;
- held weekly meetings and lectures;
- issued diplomas and occupied an ambiguous space between academy and university faculty;
- was envisioned by Gatterer as the center of a wider network of historical institutes.

This materially reinforces the jurisdictional reading from checkpoint 03.

The institutional programme was not merely a printed manifesto. It had a formal by-law architecture, and the by-laws survive in the same archival complex as the later Society/Institute dispute.

Evidence status:

`ARCHIVAL LOCUS + SECONDARY-DIRECT DESCRIPTION + REPRODUCED DOCUMENT PHOTOGRAPHS CONTROLLED`.

The repository has **not** directly inspected/transcribed the document images. Keep it below `PRIMARY-DIRECT`.

## 13. Current strongest article-level proposition

> **The 1767 boundary conflict did not divide Göttingen into separate scholarly publics. Gatterer, Michaelis, Kästner and the Murray brothers continued to inhabit a shared Society-controlled review infrastructure while the Historical Institute built a competing specialist institutional and journal apparatus. The dispute was therefore about jurisdiction over the organization of historical knowledge — academy status, autonomy, disciplinary reach and the coordination of auxiliary and natural-historical evidence — inside an overlapping learned ecology.**

This strengthens the Clio argument.

The inherited `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS` constitution should not be treated simply as an emblem moving from one camp to another. Under Gatterer, its logic of coordinated historical witnesses was institutionalized inside a field whose communication channels were shared even when its jurisdiction was contested.
