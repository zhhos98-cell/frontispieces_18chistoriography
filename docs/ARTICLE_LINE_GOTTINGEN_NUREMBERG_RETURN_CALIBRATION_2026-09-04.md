## 0. Core correction

The article is **not** a second-order argument about how historical knowledge, historical relations, or historical method were “made” by material operations.

Its core remains first-order:

> **colour in books, prints, tables, plates, and copies — how it was encoded, omitted, added, transmitted, decoded, reused, and discussed — and how those concrete book-historical practices intersected with the Göttingen historiographical disputes of the 1760s–1770s.**

The article should make one retrospective turn and one return:

`Göttingen historiographical dispute`
→ `rapid retrospective into Nürnberg colour / engraving / heraldic book practice`
→ `return to Göttingen and reread colour, tables, and presentation in the dispute`.

No perfect causal genealogy from Nürnberg to Göttingen is required. Direct bridges should be used where secure; elsewhere the connection may remain historically proximate and suggestive.

## 1. Governing question

Keep the question concrete:

> **What did colour actually do in eighteenth-century historical books, and why did its use, absence, encoding, or application matter when Göttingen historians argued about facts, tables, synopsis, presentation, and method?**

Alternative compact formulation:

> **Where did colour sit in the material life of historical books from Nürnberg to Göttingen?**

Colour is the subject, not merely a probe for a more abstract epistemology.

## 2. Node I — Göttingen opening, c. 1766–1775

Open with the historiographical dispute because it gives colour and tables an intellectual edge before the article turns backward.

### Schlözer

Use the 1772 `Facta färben` passage as a concrete verbal problem around colouring, not as a general theory of epistemic jurisdiction. Pair it with:

- `Zusammenstellung`;
- `gemahlte chronologische Tabellen` (1773);
- `Illumination der Tabelle` (1775);
- pedagogy, synchronism, overview, and memory.

### Opening function

Do not conclude that “arrangement became method.” Simply establish that, in Göttingen, historians were arguing about factual correctness, presentation, tables, synopsis, teaching, and method at the same moment when colour was literally present in some historical tables and verbally contested in discussions of historical writing.

Then turn backward: what did colour look like as a material/book-historical practice before this Göttingen moment?

## 3. Node II — Nürnberg retrospective, c. 1723–1759, with selective earlier controls

This is the book-history centre of the article.

### A. Colour without pigment

Use the heraldic tincture tradition to show that chromatic information could survive in monochrome books.

Relevant sequence:

`verbal colour declaration`
→ `letter coding`
→ `hatching / dot-line coding`
→ `colour key / legend`
→ `prose blazon`
→ possible `hand colouring` at copy level.

Selective earlier controls:
- Siebmacher;
- Spener.

Do not turn this into a long seventeenth-century genealogy; use it to establish the technical problem of transmitting colour in black-and-white print.

### B. Wappenkalender colour-key architecture

The strongest Nürnberg object remains the Weigel/Köhler *Wappenkalender*.

Use the directly controlled 1744 configuration:

- `Erklärung der Farben`;
- armorial plates using hatch conventions;
- prose blazon naming tinctures;
- genealogical tables in the same publication environment.

This is first-order book history: where colour information sits in a book, how a reader recovers it, and what happens when the decoder leaf is missing.

Core file:
- `docs/WAPPENKALENDER_COLOUR_KEY_REDUNDANT_INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE_1728_1757_2026-08-26.md`

### C. Hand colouring and copy states

Preserve the distinction:

`edition contains a colour-capable design`
≠ `every copy is coloured`
≠ `pigment belongs to the matrix`
≠ `later owner / colourist intervention`.

The article should use coloured, uncoloured, and mixed surviving copies as book-historical evidence, especially for Gatterer's *Synopsis* and any Wappenkalender witnesses with secure copy-level colour information.

### D. Annual updating and colour within a serial book ecology

The 1728 and 1733 annuals matter because they show the same books being continuously revised, corrected, and reissued. This supplies the publication context in which colour keys, armorial plates, genealogical tables, and textual updates coexist.

Keep this concrete:
- annual revision;
- newest reports;
- corrections received during printing;
- terminal additions;
- limited page space;
- usefulness for newspaper reading.

Do not promote this into a general theory of information systems.

### E. 1772 publisher retrospective

Use the 1772 `Vorbericht` because it gives an eighteenth-century book-trade diagnosis of colour and print problems:

- small figures;
- letter-coded tinctures causing disorder/misunderstanding;
- lack of descriptive space;
- changing coats of arms;
- Köhler 1723 retrospectively framed as a remedy.

This is a book-historical actor account of why a publication format changed.

## 4. Hinge — direct Nürnberg / Gatterer connection

The article does not need perfect proof of intellectual transfer. It needs enough direct contact to make the retrospective historically grounded.

### 1755

Gatterer's *Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum* is produced in Nürnberg with engravers from the same broader print environment.

### 1756

Gatterer contributes six numismatic pieces plus the Köhler biographical preface to the posthumous closure of Köhler's *Wöchentliche Historische Münz-Belustigung*.

### 1759

Gatterer's *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik* remains in the Weigel-heirs publication ecology and re-hosts inherited engraved material.

This is enough to justify the article's return to Göttingen.

Core files:
- `docs/GATTERER_KOHLER_WEIGEL_EDITORIAL_BRIDGE_1750_1759_2026-08-26.md`
- `docs/TYROFF_GATTERER_TRANSITION_1753_1762_2026-08-26.md`
- `docs/KOHLER_GATTERER_FOURFOLD_SUCCESSION_1755_1759_2026-08-26.md`

Do **not** claim:
- Nürnberg caused Gatterer's historical method;
- heraldic colour coding generated synchronistic historical colour;
- Gatterer consciously theorised the whole print tradition as one method.

The bridge may remain partial.

## 5. Node III — return to Göttingen

Return to the same dispute, but now with concrete book-historical questions in hand.

Ask:

- when Schlözer writes of `Facta färben`, what kinds of literal colouring practices were already familiar in historical/genealogical books?
- when he later accepts `gemahlte chronologische Tabellen` or `Illumination der Tabelle`, what exactly is being coloured: facts, relations, periods, or a teaching surface?
- in Gatterer's *Synopsis*, what survives in uncoloured copies and what is added by pigment?
- how stable was colour across copies?
- was colour printed, encoded, hand-applied, optional, or reconstructed by a reader?
- how do coloured tables sit alongside monochrome engravings, legends, prose explanations, and other historical book apparatus?

The return should therefore stay on book-historical ground.

The article can still discuss `Plan`, `Evidenz`, `Übersicht`, `Zusammenstellung`, `Synchronismus`, and method, but these are the historical context in which colour practices acquire significance — not an invitation to replace the article with a theory of knowledge production.

## 7. Working article movement

The article should feel simple:

1. **Göttingen:** colour appears inside a live dispute about historical writing, tables, teaching, and method.
2. **Nürnberg:** step backward into actual colour-book practices — monochrome encoding, colour keys, hand colouring, serial publication, copy variation, plate reuse.
3. **Göttingen again:** return and read the same dispute with those material practices now visible.

One fold is enough.

## 8. Claim discipline

Prefer first-order claims:

- this copy is coloured / uncoloured;
- this plate uses hatching;
- this legend decodes tinctures;
- this prose blazon duplicates chromatic information;
- this annual revised its tables during production;
- this publisher criticised letter-coded colour;
- this Gatterer book re-used inherited engraved material;
- this Göttingen text discusses coloured facts / painted tables / illuminated tables.

Only after those facts are secure should the article make modest interpretive claims about historical method.

## 9. Current concise formulation

> **This is a history of colour in historical books, moving from Göttingen's disputes over historical writing and tables back into the Nürnberg print practices that encoded, omitted, reconstructed, and applied colour, and then returning to Göttingen to ask what those material practices make visible in the dispute.**

Short form:

> **colour-book history first; historiographical interpretation second.**
