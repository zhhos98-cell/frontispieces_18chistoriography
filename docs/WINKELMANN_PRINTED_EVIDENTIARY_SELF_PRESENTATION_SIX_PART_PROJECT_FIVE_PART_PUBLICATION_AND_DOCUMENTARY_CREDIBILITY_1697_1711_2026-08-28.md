# Winkelmann 1697–1711: printed evidentiary self-presentation, six-part project identity, and five-part publication state

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE PRINT/PARATEXT CHECKPOINT — 1697 TITLE, FIVE-PART FIRST PUBLICATION, 1711 REPRINT, AND DOCUMENTARY-CREDIBILITY CLAIM CONTROLLED; FRONT-MATTER EXPLANATION OF DELAY/REVISION NOT YET DIRECTLY RECOVERED

## Result

The printed *Gründliche und Warhafte Beschreibung der Fürstenthümer Hessen und Hersfeld* exposes a sharp distinction between **project identity** and **actual publication state**.

The 1697 title page publicly presents the work as:

`Aus den glaubwürdigsten Documenten und Scribenten in Sechs Theilen verfasset`

but immediately adds:

`Aber vors erste werden Fünf Theile publiciret.`

Thus the first print state itself declares:

`six-part total project`
≠
`five-part currently publishable object`.

The 1711 reprint retains the six-part formulation and the documentary-credibility claim, but the explicit 1697 qualification that only five parts are being published "for now" disappears from the title. The contents nevertheless still comprise only five substantive parts, followed by `Anmerk- Erinnerungen und Erläuterungen der vorhergegangenen Theilen` rather than the missing sixth part.

The sixth part appeared only posthumously in 1754.

This print history matters because the archival production history has already established decades of revision, joint princely review/censorship, disputes over historical credibility and missing source references, and continued source supply into the publication window.

The printed title therefore constitutes a strong **public evidentiary self-presentation**:

`glaubwürdigsten Documenten und Scribenten`

while the object simultaneously acknowledges incompletion.

Safe interpretation:

> The printed work publicly claims documentary and authorial credibility after a production history in which credibility, source use, and publication permission had been contested.

Do **not** yet claim that the title formula was written as a direct response to a particular censorial objection. A pre-1697 version history of the title wording has not yet been recovered.

Working labels:

`PRINTED EVIDENTIARY SELF-PRESENTATION`

`PROJECT IDENTITY ≠ PUBLICATION STATE`

`TEMPORARY TEXTUAL STABILIZATION`.

## 1. 1697: the title combines evidentiary authority and explicit incompletion

The first edition was printed at Bremen by Herman Brauer in 1697.

Bibliographic controls include:

- Bayerische Staatsbibliothek `2 Germ.sp. 164-1`;
- VD17 `23:319408P`;
- Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek / BSB digital object;
- contemporary/modern copy descriptions recording 10 preliminary leaves and 600 pages.

The long title ends with the evidentiary claim:

`Aus den glaubwürdigsten Documenten und Scribenten in Sechs Theilen verfasset`.

The 1697 title further states:

`Aber vors erste werden Fünf Theile publiciret.`

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY PRINT TITLE / BIBLIOGRAPHIC OBJECT CONTROLLED`.

### Consequence

The object refuses a simple equation:

`planned work = printed work`.

Instead:

`six-part project identity`
→ `five-part first publication state`.

The incompletion is not only a modern bibliographical reconstruction. It is publicly announced on the first-edition title page.

---

## 2. The phrase `glaubwürdigsten Documenten und Scribenten` is a public epistemic claim

The title does not merely say that the work was compiled from sources.

It qualifies those sources as the:

`glaubwürdigsten`

— the most credible/trustworthy documents and writers.

The printed authorial persona therefore claims authority through a hierarchy of source credibility:

`historical narrative`
← `Documenta + Scribenten`
← `selection by credibility`.

This is especially important in light of the independently controlled production history, where later archival scholarship identifies objections concerning:

- Winckelmann's working method;
- insufficient historical credibility;
- absent or inadequate source references.

The safe relation is:

`ARCHIVAL CONTEST OVER CREDIBILITY/SOURCE PRACTICE`

alongside

`PRINTED PUBLIC CLAIM TO CREDIBLE DOCUMENTA/SCRIBENTEN`.

Current evidence does not establish a direct textual response chain between a specific censorial memorandum and the title formulation.

---

## 3. 1711: project identity survives while the explicit incompletion formula disappears

A 1711 Bremen reprint by Herman Brauer retains the title formula:

`Aus den glaubwürdigsten Documenten und Scribenten in Sechs Theilen verfasset`.

Google Books, Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek, HAB and other bibliographic controls identify this 1711 state.

However, the title as currently controlled no longer includes the 1697 sentence:

`Aber vors erste werden Fünf Theile publiciret.`

The internal contents still do not supply the sixth substantive part. Instead the five parts are followed by:

`Anmerk- Erinnerungen und Erläuterungen der vorhergegangenen Theilen`.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY / BIBLIOGRAPHIC PRINT-STATE COMPARISON CONTROLLED`.

### Consequence

Between 1697 and 1711:

`project identity = six parts`

remains stable,

while

`explicit public statement of current incompletion`

changes.

This is a paratextual-state difference even where the substantive five-part publication structure remains.

---

## 4. 1754: the sixth part confirms the long persistence of project identity

The sixth part appeared posthumously in 1754.

Its title continues documentary-source language, describing the work as composed from credible documents and writers.

This confirms that the six-part project identity outlived Winckelmann and could be completed editorially decades after the first publication.

Guardrail:

The 1754 wording belongs to a later editorial/production context and must not be automatically retrojected as exact proof of Winckelmann's own 1650s–1690s formulation.

---

## 5. Print state and manuscript state must remain separate

The Oldenburg authorial manuscript `Cim I 204` already shows:

- multiple textual states;
- inserted leaves;
- authorial additions;
- two differently corrected versions of Book IV;
- a production dossier of princely-letter extracts.

The 1697 print, by contrast, creates a publicly stabilized object:

- one long authoritative title;
- an explicit credibility claim;
- a six-part project identity;
- five parts actually printed.

Thus:

`AUTHORIAL MANUSCRIPT VERSION ECOLOGY`
≠
`PUBLIC PRINT STATE`.

The printed title compresses a distributed revision history into a single claim of documentary credibility.

## 6. Relation to the distributed revision regime

The preceding production-history checkpoint reconstructed:

`court/archive source supply`
↔ `authorial manuscript apparatus`
↔ `joint court review/censorship`
↔ `authorial defence + revision`
→ `temporary print state`.

The present print evidence adds the public-facing layer:

`temporary print state`
→ `evidentiary self-presentation`.

The title can therefore be read as part of the work's epistemic public identity without treating it as a transparent report of the entire production process.

A useful distinction is:

`production credibility negotiation`
≠
`printed credibility claim`.

The historical problem is precisely their relation.

## 7. Relation to the repository's wider frontispiece problem

The repository has repeatedly distinguished:

`programme identity`
≠ `composition identity`
≠ `plate identity`
≠ `impression/copy identity`.

The Winckelmann print history supplies an analogous textual distinction:

`project identity`
≠ `manuscript state`
≠ `reviewed/revised state`
≠ `publication state`
≠ `later completion state`.

This is not merely methodological symmetry.

It shows historically that apparently stable statements of historical knowledge can be produced from unstable, distributed, and institutionally negotiated materials.

Working longer sequence:

`distributed source/revision infrastructure`
→ `temporary textual stabilization`
→ `public evidentiary self-presentation`.

This remains a production-history comparator, not a demonstrated Winckelmann → Weigel/Köhler influence chain.

## 8. Evidence boundary: front matter beyond the title remains open

The 1697 first edition contains approximately **10 unnumbered preliminary leaves** before the 600-page main text.

Current public OCR/search access has not yet yielded a controlled transcription of those leaves beyond the title page.

Open questions:

- dedication / dedicatory recipients;
- author/printer preface;
- explanation for the delayed publication;
- explanation for publishing only five parts;
- discussion of documents, archives, reviewers or critics;
- privilege/censorship statements;
- tables or errata in the preliminary gathering.

Do not infer their contents from later scholarship.
