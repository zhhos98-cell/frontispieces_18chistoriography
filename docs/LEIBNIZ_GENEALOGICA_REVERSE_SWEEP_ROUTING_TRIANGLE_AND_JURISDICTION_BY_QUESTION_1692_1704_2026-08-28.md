## Result

The reverse sweep of `Genealogica` completes a three-sided archival model.

Current official Leibniz-Katalog query scales are:

- `Insignia`: **100** catalogue hits;
- `Genealogica`: **79** catalogue hits;
- `Geographica`: **53** catalogue hits.

These numbers are catalogue results, not counts of independent research episodes and should not be compared quantitatively without deduplication and witness normalization.

Qualitatively, however, the three result sets now expose a common principle.

The same evidentiary media can be routed into different files according to the historical question:

`seal / sigillum`
→ `Insignia` when testing armorial chronology or status;
→ `Genealogica` when testing dynastic succession or family identity;

`diploma`
→ `Geographica` when fixing the extent of a pagus or territorial name;
→ `Genealogica` when fixing descent, investiture or succession;

`topographical material`
→ `Genealogica` when locating burials or dynastic monuments;

`archive consultation`
→ `Genealogica` when establishing certainty of descent.

The emerging model is therefore:

> **jurisdiction by question, not jurisdiction by medium.**

This is stronger than a simple claim that Leibniz's categories overlapped.

The working files behave as **problem-oriented jurisdictions** over shared witnesses.

## 2. d'Hozier 1692: archive criticism is filed as `Genealogica`

Leibniz-Katalog ID **65372**, `Ms 23, 250 Bl. 118`, is Leibniz's autograph extraction from Charles-René d'Hozier's letter of 14 August 1692.

Filed:

`Genealogica ("Genealog. varia")`.

The note criticizes modern genealogists for:

- insufficient precaution;
- false and badly constructed genealogies;
- confusing fathers with sons;
- accepting what authors merely wrote or imagined;
- failing to inspect abbey archives.

The methodological endpoint is certainty: many great-family genealogies can only be fixed securely around 1200 because of the available evidence.

The original d'Hozier letter itself is catalogued under the `Origines Estenses` project complex; Leibniz then extracts its methodological content into `Genealogica`.

Working movement:

`incoming project correspondence / Origines Estenses`
→ `methodological extraction`
→ `Genealogica`.

This is direct evidence of **reclassification by use**.

## 3. Genealogy can contain `insigne` + `sigillum` without being filed under `Insignia`

One of the strongest records is Leibniz-Katalog ID **65333**, `Ms 23, 211 Bl. 9`, filed:

`Genealogica Brunsvicensia ("Geneal. Br.")`.

The note begins with historical/dynastic material around Brunswick dukes and feudal grants.

Inside the same genealogical record Leibniz notes:

- that the brothers von Itter have a lion `pro insigni`;
- that Duke Otto uses a smaller seal;
- that the seal bears a lion's head and an identifying legend.

Thus:

`genealogical / dynastic problem`
+
`insigne`
+
`sigillum`

remains filed under:

`Genealogica Brunsvicensia`.

This is a decisive control against an object-essentialist reading of the categories.

A lion device or seal does not automatically belong to `Insignia`.

Its destination depends on what it is being used to establish.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH EXCERPT CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL CATALOGUE / AKADEMIEAUSGABE LOCATOR`.

## 5. Genealogical `inquirenda` can be routed through Topographia

## 9. The key theoretical distinction: medium vs question

The strongest repository rule emerging from this material is:

`evidentiary medium identity ≠ classificatory jurisdiction`.

Examples:

### Seal

`seal` + question about arms/status chronology
→ `Insignia`.

`seal` + question about family succession/identity
→ `Genealogica`.

### Diploma

`diploma` + question about pagus/territorial extent
→ `Geographica`.

`diploma` + question about descent/investiture/succession
→ `Genealogica`.

### Topography

`topographical source` + question about burial/place of dynasty
→ `Genealogica inquirenda`.

### Winkelmann source environment

same source author/material neighborhood
→ `Insignia`
→ `Geographica`
→ `Linguae | Geographica`.

Therefore the categories are best modeled as **jurisdictions over operations**.

## 14. Strongest article-level proposition

> **Leibniz's Genealogica, Insignia and Geographica did not divide the historical record by evidentiary medium. They functioned as problem-oriented jurisdictions over shared witnesses. A seal can be filed under Genealogica when it establishes dynastic identity, while seals and arms populate Insignia when their chronology is at issue; diplomas enter Geographica when they establish territorial extent and Genealogica when they establish succession. Cross-file instructions and inquirenda make this mobility explicit. The later compression of Genealogica and Heraldica beside Geographia can therefore be situated against an archival routing triangle in which jurisdiction followed the question asked of the evidence.**
