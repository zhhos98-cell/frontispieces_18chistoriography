# Raspe publisher gift: cumulative *Wappenbuch* as a three-volume unit by 1775

## Result

A near-contemporary library catalogue supplies strong independent evidence that the first three volumes of Seyfart/Raspe's *Der Durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch* circulated as a deliberately cumulative multi-volume object by 1775, and that this configuration could come directly from the publisher rather than being a later collector's reconstruction.

The decisive catalogue entry reads:

> `3323 Der Durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch. Nürnberg. 1—3. Band. gr. 8. 1773. GPappe. (Geschenk von Herrn Raspe)`

This is recorded in the *Verzeichniß der Martinischen Bibliothek*, compiled/published in 1775.

The key phrase is **`Geschenk von Herrn Raspe`**.

Safe conclusion:

> **By 1775, Raspe himself was associated with the transfer of a bound/collected first-three-volume Wappenbuch configuration dated through 1773. The cumulative three-volume object therefore cannot be explained solely as a much later rebinding of annual material.**

## Source

Universitätsbibliothek Tübingen, OpenDigi:

- *Verzeichniß der Martinischen Bibliothek* / Friedrich Heinrich Wilhelm Martini, [1775];
- shelfmark `Ke XXIV 71`;
- catalogue item 3323 in section `A. Weltgeschichte`, printed p.137 in the digitised catalogue;
- OpenDigi search/transcription preserves the literal entry and links the page image.

The catalogue was produced only about two years after the 1773 third volume, making it much closer to the publishing event than Christian Samuel Theodor Bernd's 1830 retrospective history.

## What it adds to the serial-to-cumulative reconstruction

Bernd 1830 explains a sequence:

`annual repeated armorial stock`
→ `subscriber resistance to repurchasing the same plates`
→ `different arms in successive years`
→ `continuous numbering + special titles`
→ `general Wappenbuch`.

The Martini catalogue does not repeat that explanation. Its evidentiary value is different and complementary. It controls the **resulting object in circulation**:

- title: *Der Durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch*;
- extent: volumes 1–3;
- date: 1773, corresponding to completion through the third volume;
- binding: `GPappe` (grey/paper boards in catalogue shorthand);
- provenance/acquisition: gift from Raspe.

Thus the repository now has both:

1. a later specialist explanation of why the serial armorial corpus became cumulative;
2. a near-contemporary acquisition record showing the cumulative configuration moving from the publisher into a library/collector context.

## Why `Geschenk von Herrn Raspe` matters

The provenance phrase sharply reduces one ambiguity in surviving copies. A three-volume 1767–1773 Wappenbuch set could in principle be:

- an originally publisher-conceived cumulative unit;
- annual/part material later assembled by a binder or collector;
- a hybrid of publisher-supplied special titles and later recombination.

The 1775 gift record establishes that at least one first-three-volume configuration had a direct Raspe provenance. It does not prove that its exact binding was executed in Raspe's shop, but it makes publisher-mediated cumulative distribution demonstrable.

This supports a stronger formulation than `later readers could rebind the serial as a Wappenbuch`:

> **Raspe could distribute the accumulated material under its Wappenbuch identity as a multi-volume set during the active life of the series.**

## Relation to the 1773 Clio candidate

This control also clarifies why the 1773 Weidler Clio-bearing book must remain separated bibliographically from the cumulative third volume until its title leaf is seen.

In 1773 the ecology contains at least:

- the third volume completing a first-three-volume cumulative Wappenbuch set, directly evidenced as such by the 1775 Raspe gift;
- Seyfart's separate 1773 *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie*, dominated by Stammtafeln;
- the Weidler 2026 market object described from the Clio inscription and said to contain both arms and Stammtafeln.

The Raspe gift makes the cumulative Wappenbuch host more concrete, but it does not erase the structural difference between the two 1773 publication lines. M025 therefore remains the correct discriminating check.

## Evidence status

- literal 1775 catalogue entry: **verified_primary / near-contemporary printed catalogue**;
- first three Wappenbuch volumes treated as a cumulative object by 1775: **verified_primary**;
- Raspe as donor/source of that set: **verified_primary**, literal `Geschenk von Herrn Raspe`;
- exact original binding sequence, supplied title leaves and frontispiece identity in the gifted set: **unresolved** without inspecting the surviving Martini-library copy or its acquisition/binding evidence.

## Consequence

The strongest current model is now:

`annual delivery / dual-title parts`
→ `continuous armorial numbering`
→ `special Wappenbuch title structure`
→ `publisher-mediated cumulative distribution by 1775`
→ `later copy-level rebinding, loss and divergent frontispiece retention`.

The cumulative Wappenbuch is therefore an eighteenth-century publishing object, not merely a retrospective bibliographic abstraction.
