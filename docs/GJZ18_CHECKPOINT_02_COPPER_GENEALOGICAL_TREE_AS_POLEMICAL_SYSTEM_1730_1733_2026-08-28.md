# GJZ18 checkpoint 02 — copper genealogical tree as a polemical historical system, 1730–1733

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **HIGH-VALUE UPGRADE / TARGET IDENTITY UNRESOLVED**

## 1. Correction to the first GJZ18 sweep

The 1730–1733 Carolino-Boica genealogy controversy is stronger than the first discovery-map formulation suggested. It is not merely a controversy conducted through genealogical publications. The title of Ignaz Franz Xaver von Wilhelm's 1730 *Vindiciae Arboris Genealogicae Augustae Gentis Carolino-Boicae* explicitly says that it attacks the **system of a genealogical author embodied in a genealogical tree engraved on copper a few years earlier**.

Controlled title wording:

`Contra Sistema Authoris Geneographi, Qui In Arbore Genealogica Ante Paucos Annos Cupro Incisa Serenissimorum Bojorum Principum Fundatorem Ponit Luitpoldum, Et Abrupta Penitus Arbore, Negat Carolum Magnum ...`

The title continues by describing Wilhelm's counter-argument as drawing on chronicles, documents, authors, and reasons derived from the combination of acts and times.

Evidence grade: `PRIMARY-BIBLIOGRAPHIC-TITLE-DIRECT`, controlled by Google Books/Google Play and DDB/BSB metadata for the 1730 edition (BSB `bsb10983081`, VD18 `14771616`).

## 2. What this licenses

The target of the polemic is not merely a verbal proposition about Wittelsbach descent. A prior `arbore genealogica ... cupro incisa` materially carries a historical system in which:

- Luitpold is positioned as founder of the Bavarian princely line;
- the tree is broken/cut so that Charlemagne is denied as an ancestor.

Therefore a graphic genealogy can function here as a **historical proposition with contestable inferential content**.

Safe operational chain:

`copper-engraved genealogical tree`
→ `graphic placement / truncation`
→ `historical descent system`
→ `printed challenge (Vindiciae)`
→ `chronicles + documents + authors + temporal combination used as counter-warrant`.

This is unusually close to the Clio project's object scale because the disputed historical claim is materially encoded in an engraved graphic object rather than only in prose.

## 3. The controversy then becomes explicitly methodological

The subsequent *Palignesi Monita Genealogica* / Spannagel controversy is not only a dynastic counterclaim. Bibliographic title language describes the *Monita* as explaining the `principum genealogias probandi ratio` — the method/rule for proving princes' genealogies — while weighing documents adduced in the *Vindiciae* and revisiting rules for the proper use of negative argument (`argumentum negativum`).

Evidence grade: `PRIMARY-BIBLIOGRAPHIC-TITLE-DIRECT / GJZ18-METADATA-DIRECT`; exact interior methodological formulations remain `PRIMARY-TEXT-PENDING`.

The sequence can therefore be sharpened:

`engraved tree as historical system`
→ `challenge to the system`
→ `documentary/chronological counter-proof`
→ `argument about probandi ratio`
→ `rules governing documentary weight and negative inference`
→ `reply / satire / renewed Vindiciae`.

## 4. Why this matters for the later Clio problem

This supplies a pre-Gatterer control for a central proposition of the project:

> A printed graphic configuration can be more than illustration or mnemonic display; its arrangement can itself assert a historical relation whose legitimacy becomes the object of public methodological controversy.

This does **not** establish:

- that the anonymous/unnamed earlier genealogical author was Köhler;
- that the target tree was printed in a Weigel Wappenkalender;
- that the Clio frontispiece was designed in response to this controversy;
- that Gatterer knew or inherited the precise argument;
- that `genealogical tree → Clio` is a direct design genealogy.

All such relations remain `UNPROVED`.

What is now secure is the **functional prehistory**:

`Genealogia as engraved arrangement`
can already become
`Genealogia as contestable historical proof`.

This makes the later Clio formula `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS` historically denser: at least one of the disciplines named beside Historia belonged to a print culture in which graphic arrangement and the rules of proof could be publicly disputed.

## 5. Highest-priority unresolved identity

The phrase `Authoris Geneographi` still leaves the original target author/tree unidentified in the present pass.

The decisive next question is:

> Which copper-engraved genealogical tree, produced only a few years before 1730, placed Luitpold at the beginning of the Bavarian princely line and cut off Charlemagne?

Until that object is identified, do not connect the controversy directly to Köhler, Weigel, Tyroff, the Wappenkalender, or the Clio plate.

Search targets:
- pre-1730 Wittelsbach / Carolino-Boica copper genealogies;
- wording around `Luitpold`, `Carolus Magnus`, `Stammvater`, `Arbor genealogica`;
- named/pseudonymous genealogists attacked by Wilhelm;
- catalogues recording a separate engraved genealogical sheet rather than a book;
- internal addresses (`Ad Authorem`) in the 1730 *Vindiciae* that may disclose the target.

## 7. Current strongest formulation

> **By 1730 a copper-engraved genealogical tree could be named as the bearer of a `systema` of dynastic history, challenged because the graphic genealogy began with Luitpold and cut off Charlemagne; the ensuing printed controversy then moved from the correctness of that tree to documentary proof and the `probandi ratio` of princely genealogy. This is a direct prehistory for treating graphical historical arrangement as an object of methodological dispute, while any direct link to the later Clio plate remains unproved.**
