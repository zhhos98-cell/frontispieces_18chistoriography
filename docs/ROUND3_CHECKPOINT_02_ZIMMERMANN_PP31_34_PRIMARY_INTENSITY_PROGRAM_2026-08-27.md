## p.31 — programme, climatic degree, and competing causal hypotheses

The human section opens by stressing the unusually broad geographical and climatic range of the human body. Zimmermann immediately formulates the inquiry in intensive terms:

> `Was kann der Mensch für Klimate, für Grade der Hitze und Kälte ertragen?`

This establishes `Grad` at the outset as an operator for **environmental intensity**, specifically degrees of heat and cold.

The same programme then asks what climate, nourishment and analogous `Nebenursachen` do to human beings, whether they are sufficient to produce the presently observable `Ausartungen`, or whether nature originally had to create several individuals, each for its own climate.

### Evidence status

`PRIMARY-DIRECT — PROGRAMMATIC / INTERROGATIVE`

This page licenses the following claims:

1. climate is not merely a geographical backdrop; it enters Zimmermann's explicit causal problem-space;
2. nourishment is coupled with climate among possible secondary causes;
3. present human `Ausartungen` are posed as effects requiring explanation;
4. a secondary-cause account is explicitly contrasted with a multiple-original-creation-by-climate alternative;
5. environmental intensity is articulated as `Grade der Hitze und Kälte`.

It does **not** yet license the conclusion that Zimmermann has answered the causal question in favour of climate, because p.31 is explicitly setting up the investigation.

## p.32 — original-human problem and geographical distribution

Zimmermann asks where the human species' proper homeland was and what the `Originalmensch` looked like, contrasting bodily/geographical types such as `Patagone oder Eskimo, Neger oder Georgier`.

He then turns to the present `Wohnplätze des Menschengeschlechts`, whose boundaries are effectively the whole known earth. The passage moves through Greenlanders/Eskimos at very high northern latitudes, peoples under the equator, Tierra del Fuego, and other southern limits.

`Grad` also appears here in a different jurisdiction, as a latitude measure (`Grade nördlicher Breite`). This should be kept analytically separate from p.31's degrees of heat/cold.

### Evidence status

`PRIMARY-DIRECT — DISTRIBUTIONAL / PROBLEM-FRAMING`

The sequence matters: distributional description and the original-human question are established before the later causal reconstruction.

## p.33 — thermometric intensification

Zimmermann states that the hottest zone is inhabited and that humans can reproduce under extreme heat and cold. He then explicitly asks the reader to consider `thermometrisch` the extraordinary `Verschiedenheiten der Hitze und Kälte` that humankind can and does endure in its general distribution across the earth.

### Evidence status

`PRIMARY-DIRECT — MEASUREMENT REGIME`

This is stronger than a generic climate reference. The climatic variable is treated as measurable intensity, not merely as a named regional category.

## p.34 — numerical/observational implementation

The following page continues the cold-limit discussion through reports of thermometer readings and extreme northern observations, including material associated with Pallas. It therefore operationalises the thermometric programme of p.33 with quantitative observational evidence.

### Evidence status

`PRIMARY-DIRECT — MEASUREMENT IMPLEMENTATION`

## Interim answer to Round 3 Question 4: intensity / degree

**Yes, decisively, but with a bounded jurisdiction.** Zimmermann 1778 uses `Grad`, extreme (`äußerste`) heat/cold, `thermometrisch`, and measured temperature differences as intensive operators in the human-environment argument.

The primary pages presently establish intensity in the **environmental causal variable**. They do not yet establish degree/shade operators for skin colour, nor do they establish graded essence among human taxa.

Canonical formulation for later comparison:

> Zimmermann's opening anthropogeographical programme already quantifies climatic difference before it settles the taxonomic or causal status of visible human difference.

This distinction should be retained when comparing him with Meiners. The relevant contrast is not merely whether climate appears, but what kinds of inference are licensed from measured environmental difference to bodily/group difference.

## Additional Round 3 consequence

The pp.31–34 sequence sharpens the architecture of the work:

1. establish human ubiquity across extreme environments;
2. measure the climatic range thermometrically;
3. pose climate/nourishment as possible `Nebenursachen` of `Ausartungen`;
4. oppose such an account to multiple original creations adapted to separate climates;
5. only later adjudicate origin, migration and transformation.

This means pp.114–115 should be read as a later answer to a problem deliberately posed at the opening of `Der Mensch`, not as an isolated climatic speculation.

## Inferential guardrails

- `Grade der Hitze und Kälte` does not equal graded skin colour.
- `Ausartungen` on p.31 is an explanandum in an interrogative research programme, not yet a settled taxonomic category.
- geographic names/types in the `Originalmensch` question do not prove a stable `Race` hierarchy.
- thermometric climate measurement does not by itself establish heredity or permanence.
- do not collapse `Grad` of latitude, `Grad` of temperature, and any later degree of bodily difference into one semantic function.
