# Gatterer 1766: heraldry taught through its sources, not only its vocabulary

## Result

Gatterer's 1766 *Abriß der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde* is more epistemically ambitious than a compact blazon manual. Its table of contents places a section titled **`Quellen der Wappenkunde`** immediately after the opening `Erklärung und Eintheilung der Wappen` and before the long technical sequence on divisions, figures, tinctures, terminology, combinations, helmets and other formal features.

The visible contents sequence includes:

- `Erklärung und Eintheilung der Wappen` — p. 1;
- `Quellen der Wappenkunde` — p. 7;
- sections on shield divisions / line forms;
- technical terms used in description;
- combination of arms;
- helmet ornaments;
- speaking arms.

The pedagogical order is therefore significant:

`what a coat of arms is`
→ `where knowledge of coats of arms comes from`
→ `how heraldic forms are analysed and described`.

The book does not train visual classification while leaving source authority implicit. Source theory is part of the elementary architecture.

## Relation to the Wappenkalender/Handbuch ecology

By 1763 the Gatterer Handbuch advertised an embedded `Abriß der Heraldik`; by 1766 the methodological component was articulated as a student-oriented treatise `zum Nutzen der studirenden Jugend` and explained with eight copperplates.

This means that the same publication ecology increasingly joined:

- current genealogical tables;
- armorial representations;
- verbal descriptions;
- general heraldic rules;
- explicit discussion of the sources of heraldic knowledge.

A reader using the calendar/handbook was therefore potentially trained not only to decode symbols but also to ask what kinds of evidence warrant heraldic knowledge.

## Longer source-critical lineage

This source section can be placed beside earlier primary controls without claiming a direct textual chain where none has been shown.

### Fürst, 1657

Fürst describes a live information network using originals, court mediation, copied models, correspondents, fair-catalogue submissions, reader corrections and specialist contributors. His production notice separates incoming information from the engraved plate that eventually represents it.

### Spener, 1680

Spener explicitly distinguishes copied/received family information, independent checking, doubtful reports and source accountability; he explains how he departs from Siebmacher/Fürst where other evidence shows them wrong.

### Köhler, 1734

Köhler's reported five-copy collation of Helmers witnesses uses exact comparison to diagnose divergent copy configurations, corrections, substitutions and additions.

### Gatterer, 1766

`Quellen der Wappenkunde` appears as a teachable methodological category near the beginning of an elementary heraldic manual.

The safe project-level claim is therefore:

> By the mid-eighteenth century, questions that earlier publishers and compilers had handled operationally through originals, submissions, comparison and correction could themselves be abstracted into a teachable problem of the `sources of heraldry`.

This is a shift from **source practice** to **source theory**, without requiring a simple line of influence from Fürst or Spener to Gatterer.

## Why this matters for visual evidence

The section title blocks a common modern simplification. Heraldry in Gatterer's pedagogical scheme is not reducible to a grammar of lines, tinctures and figures.

The epistemic sequence is closer to:

`source / witness`
→ `heraldic knowledge claim`
→ `graphic representation`
→ `technical description/classification`.

That sequence aligns with the repository's broader evidence discipline, where image identity, plate identity, witness occurrence and copy state are kept distinct.

The historical vocabulary is different, but the structural concern is recognisable: a visual representation becomes historical knowledge only through controlled relations to sources and descriptions.

## Relation to Gatterer's later four-operation taxonomy

The repository already controls Gatterer's later *Praktische Heraldik* (1791), where practical heraldic work is divided into operations including `Blasonieren`, `Historisieren`, `Kritisieren`, and `Erfinden oder Aufreissen`.

The 1766 contents provide an earlier methodological anchor. Before the later explicit taxonomy of operations, Gatterer was already teaching `Quellen der Wappenkunde` as a foundational topic.

A plausible developmental relation is:

`1766 source foundations + formal description`
→ `1791 differentiated practical operations including historical and critical work`.

Treat this as a chronology of Gatterer's published pedagogy, not yet as proof of a single unchanged conceptual scheme.

## Evidence guards

1. The present control is table-of-contents level; the exact 1766 prose defining the `Quellen der Wappenkunde` section still requires direct textual inspection before its categories are paraphrased in detail.
2. Do not assume that Gatterer's `Quellen` maps one-to-one onto modern source criticism.
3. Do not infer direct Fürst→Spener→Köhler→Gatterer influence from structural resemblance alone.
4. The importance here is the curricular placement: source questions are explicitly elementary enough to appear before most formal heraldic technique.

## Primary control

Johann Christoph Gatterer, *Abriß der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde zum Nutzen der studirenden Jugend entworfen und mit 8 Kupfertafeln erläutert* (Nürnberg: Raspe, 1766), 68 pp. Google Books exposes the contents sequence directly, including `Erklärung und Eintheilung der Wappen`, `Quellen der Wappenkunde`, and the later technical sections.
