# Leibniz routing verbs and archival locatives: `huc`, `bey`, `inter`, `ad`, and a spatial grammar of historical work, c.1690–1698

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE SYNTHESIS — MULTIPLE PRIMARY-CATALOGUE-CONTROLLED ROUTING FORMULAS NOW RECOVERED; CONNECTION TO FORMAL `HUC` IS STRUCTURAL/GRAMMATICAL, NOT A DEMONSTRATED LEXICAL DERIVATION

## Result

The archive-routing model can now be grounded in Leibniz's own working language.

Across his preparatory materials for historical work, Leibniz repeatedly uses **locative and directive formulations** to manage knowledge:

- `liegen bey ...` — material lies with another file;
- `so ich gelegt bey ...` — material I placed with another dossier;
- `vide inter ...` — look among another set of excerpts;
- `inspiciantur ...` — inspect another source/file;
- `gehören ... zu inquirendis` — these matters belong among things still to be investigated;
- `ist zu inquirendis gelegt` — the problem has been placed among inquiry items;
- `ad ... pertinentia` — matters pertaining to a target domain;
- compound ordering marks separated by `|` — one note can inhabit multiple jurisdictions.

The later formal library taxonomy uses another explicitly locative term:

`Geographia. Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.`

`Huc` means approximately `to this place / hither`.

The point is **not** that the word `huc` was copied from a filing instruction.

The stronger and safer interpretation is structural:

> Leibniz organized both working papers and formal knowledge through a spatial grammar of placement, redirection, adjacency and jurisdiction.

This creates a three-level sequence:

`archival location / routing`
→ `taxonomic location / huc`
→ `visual-spatial coordination of disciplines and witnesses`.

The 1726 frontispiece can therefore be approached not merely as an allegory of disciplines, but as a visual stabilization of relations that earlier existed as operations of **where evidence belongs for a given question**.

## 1. `liegen bey Geograf.` — Insignia routes to Geographica

Leibniz-Katalog ID **7139**, June 1690, is filed under:

`Insignia`.

It concerns the two lions of the Counts of Diez and refers to Winkelmann/Hessian excerpts, ending:

`liegen bey Geograf.`

The note is therefore simultaneously:

- semantically an `Insignia` problem;
- materially/intellectually redirected to material lying with `Geographica`.

Source:

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH ROUTING FORMULA CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL CATALOGUE / AKADEMIEAUSGABE LOCATOR`.

## 2. The formula repeats: `so liegen bey Geogr.`

ID **14091**, dated 1694, belongs to a different working category:

`Antiquissimi habitatores`.

It concerns large stone columns near Miltenberg and Zwingenberg and directs the reader/researcher to Winkelmann's Hessian excerpts:

`so liegen bey Geogr.`

Source:

https://leibniz-katalog.bbaw.de/de/extended-search?ordnungsvermerk_normiert=Antiquissimi+habitatores&view=list

This is a crucial repeat control.

The earlier ID 7139 is **not an isolated phrase**.

At least two different problem files now explicitly route Winkelmann/Hessian material to a Geographica location:

`Insignia`
→ `Winkelmann excerpts lie with Geographica`;

`Antiquissimi habitatores`
→ `Winkelmann excerpts lie with Geographica`.

This supports a stronger model:

`Geographica`
can function as a **source reservoir / destination packet** called upon by other historical problems.

Do not yet infer one physically intact surviving Winkelmann dossier until manuscript reconstruction confirms it.

## 3. `gehören noch zu inquirendis` — a file can contain future research

ID **2803**, June 1690, filed under `Insignia`, says that matters pertaining to seals and insignia from Hofrat Lüdecke:

`gehören noch zu inquirendis`.

This is a directive classification of **future work**.

The note does not say merely what a fact is.

It says where an unresolved lead belongs in the workflow.

Working sequence:

`ad sigilla et insignia pertinentia`
→ `belongs to inquirenda`
→ future investigation.

Thus the archive includes an explicit task-management layer.

---

## 4. `vide inter ...` + `ist zu inquirendis gelegt` — Genealogica routes via Topographia

ID **65334**, filed under `Genealogica Brunsvicensia`, asks where several princes were buried.

The note tells the researcher to look among excerpts from Bünting and `Topographia`, and says the matter:

`ist zu inquirendis gelegt`.

This combines two operations:

1. **cross-source/file lookup**: `vide inter ... Topographia`;
2. **workflow placement**: placed among `inquirenda`.

The genealogical problem is therefore solved through topographical material and an inquiry queue.

This is another direct control for jurisdiction by question.

## 5. `so ich gelegt bey Orig. Est.` — project-dossier placement is explicit

A record under the ordering mark `Azo Marchio`, ID **65960**, dated after 5 March 1693, refers to Du Chesne and d'Hozier evidence and tells the reader:

`Vide Epistolam Hozierii so ich gelegt bey Orig. Est.`

The instruction is unusually transparent:

`see Hozier's letter`
→ `which I placed with Origines Estenses`.

Source:

https://leibniz-katalog.bbaw.de/de/extended-search?ordnungsvermerk_normiert=Azo+Marchio&view=list

This is explicit first-person evidence that Leibniz consciously **placed** an item inside a project-specific documentary context.

The archive therefore contains not only later cataloguers' reconstructed categories but Leibniz's own statements of placement.

## 6. `liegt bey Estensibus generalibus` — Genealogica points to a project packet

Within the `Genealogica` corpus, ID **65345** records a genealogical table/compendium and says that Leibniz's own compendium:

`liegt bey Estensibus generalibus`.

This is the same grammar as the Geographica routing cases:

`this item belongs to / lies with that dossier`.

It supports a model in which thematic categories and project complexes are connected through explicit locative instructions.

## 7. Compound ordering marks: one item can occupy more than one jurisdiction

The archive also expresses mobility directly through composite `Ordnungsvermerke`.

Examples already controlled include:

- `Linguae | Geographica`;
- `Witikindea | Geographica`;
- `Geographica | Slavi`;
- `Origines Estenses | Genealogica`;
- `Insignia | Sigilla`;
- `Sigilla et insignia`.

The vertical bar is a modern normalized catalogue representation, while the diplomatic forms differ.

Still, the underlying records show that Leibniz could mark one note with multiple intellectual destinations or overlapping rubrics.

This is a direct challenge to any tree-like reconstruction that assumes mutually exclusive categories.

## 8. The formal `huc`: taxonomy is also locative

The posthumous print tradition of the `Idea Leibnitiana bibliothecae ordinandae contractior` controls the compressed phrase:

`Historia civilis.`

`Universalis.`

`Geographia. Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.`

The exact N.72 autograph wording/layout still requires direct IV.6 inspection.

Nevertheless, in the controlled print formula `huc` has genuine local directional force:

`hither / to this place belong Genealogica and Heraldica`.

At macro level, Geographia remains inside `Historia civilis`; IV.6's later systematic context explicitly places Geography, Genealogy and Heraldry inside `HISTORIA`.

The correct model remains dual:

`Historia`
→ `Geographia`
→ `huc Genealogica & Heraldica`.

## 9. Archival `bey` and taxonomic `huc`: structural relation, not word genealogy

The archival formulas and the formal taxonomy share a spatial grammar:

### archive

`liegen bey Geograf.`

`so liegen bey Geogr.`

`so ich gelegt bey Orig. Est.`

`liegt bey Estensibus generalibus`.

### taxonomy

`Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.`

This is analytically striking, but the evidentiary rule must remain strict.

Do **not** claim:

`Leibniz used huc in the contractior because he used bey in his filing notes`.

No such lexical derivation has been demonstrated.

Safe interpretation:

`shared locative organizational grammar`.

The same scholar repeatedly describes knowledge organization through **where material is placed and where one should go to retrieve it**.

## 10. From archival space to taxonomic space

The working archive has at least four spatial operations:

### Placement

`gelegt bey` / `liegt bey`.

### Redirection

`vide inter` / `inspiciantur`.

### Assignment

`ad ... pertinentia` / `gehören zu`.

### Deferred investigation

`inquirendis`.

Formal taxonomy then compresses relationships through terms such as:

`huc`.

This suggests a general research proposition:

> **Classification is not only a logical ordering of concepts; it is an abstraction of retrieval and placement operations.**

For this repository, that proposition is currently strongly supported as a structural interpretation of Leibniz's working practice.

## 11. From taxonomic space to visual space

The later frontispiece spatializes a knowledge constitution:

`HISTORIA`

`GENEALOGIA`

`HERALDICA`

`TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`

are not listed merely in prose but arranged in an engraved scene among books, arms, genealogical media and historical witnesses.

The longer trajectory can therefore be formulated:

`archive: where does this evidence lie?`
→ `taxonomy: huc — where does this field belong?`
→ `image: where are disciplines and witnesses placed in relation to Historia?`.

This is not a transmission chain.

It is a **change of representational register**:

`retrieval space`
→ `classification space`
→ `visual space`.

This may become one of the repo's strongest conceptual bridges between mundane research practice and allegorical imagery.

## 12. Why this matters for `TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`

The earlier analysis established that seals, diplomas, coins, inscriptions and manuscripts function as historical witnesses.

The routing evidence adds another layer.

A witness is not valuable only because of what it materially is.

Its evidentiary force depends on:

- the question asked;
- the comparison set in which it is placed;
- the file/dossier through which it is retrieved;
- the other witnesses beside which it is read.

Thus `testis` is relational.

The historical witness becomes meaningful inside a **configured evidentiary neighborhood**.

This has direct relevance to an engraved composition that visually assembles different witnesses around Historia.

## 14. Strongest article-level proposition

> **Leibniz's historical papers expose a spatial grammar of research. Materials “lie with” Geographica or Estensia; letters are explicitly “placed with” project dossiers; unresolved questions are placed among inquirenda; notes direct the researcher to look among other excerpts. His formal library taxonomy likewise uses the locative `huc` to assign Genealogica and Heraldica. Without claiming a lexical derivation, the archive and taxonomy share an organizational grammar in which knowledge is constituted through placement and retrieval. The later frontispiece changes register once more, translating relations of archival and taxonomic location into visual space around Historia.**
