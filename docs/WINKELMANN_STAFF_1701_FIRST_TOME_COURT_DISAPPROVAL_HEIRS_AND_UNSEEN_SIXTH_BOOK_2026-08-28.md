# Winkelmann 1701: printed first tome, court disapproval, heirs, and the unseen sixth book

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: DECISIVE POSTPRINT / PROJECT-COMPLETION CHECKPOINT — STAFF'S CONTEMPORARY REPLY RESOLVES LEIBNIZ'S SEPTEMBER 1701 QUERY: PARTS I–V CIRCULATED AS A PRINTED `PREMIER TOME`; THE SIXTH BOOK / SECOND TOME HAD NEVER BEEN SEEN IN KASSEL; WINKELMANN'S HEIRS CLAIMED IT WAS COMPLETE AND DEMANDED A SUBSTANTIAL SUM TO HAND IT OVER; COURT DISAPPROVAL OF THE FIRST TOME THREATENED THE REMAINDER WITH OBLIVION

## Result

Nathanael von Staff's reply to Leibniz of **24 November 1701** resolves the principal ambiguity in Leibniz's earlier question about Winkelmann's Hessian Chronicle.

Leibniz had written on 23 September 1701 that he had seen `un Tome imprimé` and asked whether the work might one day `paroitre`.

Staff answers from Kassel:

> `Pour La Cronique de Mr Winkelman le premier tome est imprimé dont on m’a donné un exemplaire: Si vous le voulez, Mr je vous en enverray un, cet ouvrage n’a pas trouvé toute l’approbation, on accuse l’autheur en partie d’indiscretion, en partie qu[’]i[l] n’a rien fait voir au premier tome qui fust assez solide, renvoyant le lecteur dans toutes les meilleures rubriques au 6me livre ou le second Tome, qui n’a jamais eté veu icy: il est vray qu’apres sa mort les Heretiers ont declaré que ce livre etoit achevé et ont demandé une somme assez grande pour le remettre. Mais la Cour etant disgousté du premier Tome il paroit que tout sera ensevely dans l’oubly.`

Source control:

- Nathanael von Staff to Leibniz, Kassel, 24 Nov. 1701;
- manuscript: `LBr. 888 Bl. 23–24`, four pages;
- provisional Akademie-Ausgabe numbering: no.356, pp.632–633;
- final-edition cross-reference from Leibniz no.284 identifies the reply as Staff no.354;
- Staff's surviving correspondence with Leibniz is catalogued in Kalliope under `LBr. 888`.

The screenshot route for the critical-edition PDF returned an internal rendering error in the current environment; the critical text, metadata, page location, manuscript shelfmark, and internal cross-reference were nevertheless directly recovered from the searchable Akademie-Ausgabe PDF text. No visual/codicological claim is made from the failed screenshot.

The reply supplies a substantially stronger model than the previous generic category `publication incompletion`:

`FIRST TOME PRINTED AND AVAILABLE IN KASSEL`
→ `COURT / READER DISAPPROVAL`
→ `SECOND TOME / SIXTH BOOK NOT SEEN IN KASSEL`
→ `HEIRS CLAIM COMPLETION`
→ `HEIRS DEMAND SUBSTANTIAL PAYMENT FOR TRANSFER`
→ `COURT UNWILLING TO PROCEED`
→ `RISK OF PROJECT BEING BURIED IN OBLIVION`.

This is a posthumous **reception + property/estate-transfer + court-patronage closure bottleneck**.

## 1. The `premier tome` is printed and Staff possesses a copy

Staff states directly:

`le premier tome est imprimé`

and adds:

`on m’a donné un exemplaire`.

He then offers Leibniz:

`Si vous le voulez ... je vous en enverray un`.

This controls three separate facts:

1. a physical first tome had been printed;
2. at least one copy had reached Staff in Kassel;
3. Staff had access to another copy or to a supply from which he could send one to Leibniz.

This contemporaneous evidence sits well with later archival scholarship reporting that Landgrave Karl financed the printing and that most of the edition went to Kassel for distribution.

However, the letter itself does not quantify stock, identify a warehouse, or specify whether Staff's offered copy came from princely stock, a bookseller, a printer, or his personal network.

Safe label:

`KASSEL-BASED PRINTED-COPY ACCESS / DISTRIBUTION NODE`.

---

## 2. The physical publication architecture is now much clearer

Staff calls the printed object:

`le premier tome`.

He then identifies:

`le 6me livre ou le second Tome`.

This is decisive for interpreting the project's material architecture.

The contemporaneous structure is:

`FIRST TOME`
= the already printed object containing Books/Parts I–V

while

`SECOND TOME`
= the sixth book / sixth part.

Thus:

`six-part intellectual/project architecture`
≠ `six physical volumes`.

Instead:

`Tome I = Parts I–V`

`Tome II = Part/Book VI`.

This explains why Leibniz's September 1701 singular `un Tome imprimé` is naturally compatible with the standard 1697 first-edition object.

It also directly undercuts the modern web annotation that Leibniz's `un Tome imprimé` was 'presumably volume 6'. Staff's contemporaneous answer says the sixth book / second tome:

`n’a jamais eté veu icy`.

Current safe conclusion:

> Leibniz's printed tome should not be identified with a printed sixth part. Staff's reply instead distinguishes the printed first tome from an unseen sixth book / second tome.

---

## 3. `n’a jamais eté veu icy`: the sixth book had not been seen in Kassel

Staff's wording is stronger than saying the sixth book was unpublished.

He says it:

`n’a jamais eté veu icy`

— had **never been seen here**, i.e. in his Kassel context.

This is a situated knowledge statement.

Promote:

`SIXTH BOOK / SECOND TOME NOT SEEN BY STAFF'S KASSEL ENVIRONMENT BY 24 NOV. 1701`.

Do not overpromote to:

- no manuscript existed anywhere;
- nobody outside Kassel had seen it;
- no proof sheets existed;
- the heirs' later claim of completion was false.

The phrase controls Kassel-side absence/visibility, not universal nonexistence.

---

## 4. Court reception of the first tome was materially consequential

Staff states that:

`cet ouvrage n’a pas trouvé toute l’approbation`.

He then gives two kinds of accusation against the author.

### A. `indiscretion`

`on accuse l’autheur en partie d’indiscretion`

The exact passages or political/confessional offences meant by `indiscretion` are not identified in the letter.

Possible relation to earlier dynastic/confessional review is analytically important but remains to be demonstrated passage by passage.

### B. insufficient `solidité`

Staff continues that the author had not shown in the first tome anything:

`qui fust assez solide`.

The criticism is then structurally explained: Winkelmann repeatedly sends the reader, in the most important/best headings or rubrics,

`au 6me livre ou le second Tome`.

Thus the first tome could be experienced as evidentially or substantively insufficient because key demonstrations were deferred into an absent component.

Safe relation:

`POSTPRINT COURT/READER EVALUATION`
= `indiscretion` + `insufficient solidity` + `deferred support/content in missing sixth book`.

This is strikingly compatible with the repository's earlier evidence for preprint disputes over historical method, source references, and acceptable dynastic representation, but identity of vocabulary or objections has not yet been established.

Do **not** claim:

`1686 censorial rule X = Staff's 1701 solidité criticism`

until Marburg `Ms.309` / Kassel `35[1.2` item-level text is inspected.

## 5. The heirs introduce a posthumous property/economic bottleneck

Staff says:

`apres sa mort les Heretiers ont declaré que ce livre etoit achevé`

and:

`ont demandé une somme assez grande pour le remettre`.

This controls a new posthumous stage.

After Winkelmann's death:

- the **heirs** claimed that the missing book was complete;
- transfer/delivery of it was conditioned on a **substantial sum**.

The French `remettre` can mean to hand over, deliver, restore, or place back into someone's hands. Until the underlying estate/court documents are found, the repository should avoid translating it as sale of copyright or sale of the manuscript in a narrowly modern legal sense.

Safe label:

`HEIR-CONTROLLED TRANSFER / DELIVERY CLAIM WITH SUBSTANTIAL PAYMENT DEMAND`.

This introduces a property/economic layer absent from the pre-1697 model:

`AUTHORIAL COMPLETION CLAIM`
→ `ESTATE / HEIR CONTROL`
→ `PRICE / COMPENSATION DEMAND`
→ `COURT DECISION WHETHER TO ACQUIRE / CONTINUE`.

---

## 6. Court dissatisfaction blocks posthumous project completion

Staff's final causal turn is explicit:

`Mais la Cour etant disgousté du premier Tome il paroit que tout sera ensevely dans l’oubly.`

The court's dissatisfaction with the first tome is presented as the reason the larger project now appears likely to be abandoned.

This is stronger than a generic statement that the sixth part was lost or delayed.

The 1701 closure problem is:

`MISSING SECOND TOME EXISTS AS HEIRS' COMPLETION CLAIM`
+
`SUBSTANTIAL TRANSFER COST`
+
`COURT DISAPPROVAL OF FIRST TOME`
→ `NO APPARENT INCENTIVE TO RECOVER / PUBLISH SECOND TOME`
→ `EXPECTED OBLIVION`.

Safe analytical label:

`COURT-DEPENDENT POSTHUMOUS PROJECT CLOSURE`.

This extends the 1695 Haes evidence. In 1695 Haes hoped, after Landgrave Karl's return, to bring the delayed affair to an end. By 1701, after Winkelmann's death and after first-tome printing, Staff describes a different closure crisis: postprint reception has reduced the court's willingness to pay for or recover the missing completion.

## 7. Leibniz's September 1701 question is now resolved more precisely

Before Staff's answer, Leibniz's sentence allowed several hypotheses:

`j’ai vû même un Tome imprimé`
+
`N’y a-t-il pas d’esperance, que cet ouvrage paroitra un jour?`

Possible explanations included restricted distribution, knowledge lag, a proof state, or incompleteness.

Staff's reply makes **project incompletion** the leading controlled explanation.

He distinguishes:

`premier tome = printed`

from

`6me livre / second tome = never seen here`.

Therefore Leibniz's question about whether the `ouvrage` would appear can now safely be read against the missing completion of the two-tome/six-part project.

Still preserve one nuance:

Staff also shows Kassel-based copy circulation rather than universal public distribution, so distribution history remains a separate research problem. The letter resolves the referential structure more strongly than it resolves every meaning of `paroitra`.

## 8. The 184-page market attachment becomes even more problematic

The repository currently controls a later composite/market copy with an undated 184-page attachment described as:

`Sechster Theil der Hessischen Chronik. Ohne Ort und Jahr. 184 Seiten (statt ?).`

Staff's 24 Nov. 1701 statement that the sixth book / second tome had `never been seen here` in Kassel does not prove that the 184-page object postdates 1701, because his evidence is geographically and socially situated.

But it raises the evidentiary threshold substantially for any claim that the 184-page object is a normally circulating pre-1701 sixth-part print.

Required before such a promotion:

- independent date/provenance before Nov.1701;
- typography/paper/collation demonstrating an early independent print state;
- or contemporary correspondence identifying such a printed sixth-part object.

Current hierarchy becomes:

`1701 STAFF: SECOND TOME / SIXTH BOOK UNSEEN IN KASSEL`

vs.

`LATER MARKET: UNDATED 184-PAGE ATTACHMENT CALLED SIXTH PART`.

The latter cannot override the former without direct object evidence.

## 9. Revised production-to-completion sequence

The controlled history now extends through a qualitatively different postprint phase:

`1686 formal commissioned revision`
→ `1687 rules + rule-grounded notes`
→ `1690 distributed excerpt circulation`
→ `1691 six-part project still prospective`
→ `1692 continued assistance toward perfection`
→ `1695 Haes identifies a mysterious delay and court-dependent closure attempt`
→ `1697 first tome / Parts I–V printed`
→ `1701 first tome circulates in Kassel but receives incomplete approval`
→ `1701 sixth book / second tome unseen in Kassel`
→ `heirs claim sixth book complete + demand substantial payment for transfer`
→ `court dissatisfaction reduces willingness to continue`
→ `anticipated oblivion`
→ `1711 reprint of first-tome content without substantive sixth part`
→ `1754 posthumous sixth-part publication`.

The transition from 1701 anticipated oblivion to 1754 recovery is now one of the highest-value unresolved questions in the entire Winkelmann line.

## 11. Repository-level payoff

The case now demands a still more granular distinction:

`project identity`
≠ `part/book identity`
≠ `physical tome identity`
≠ `printed-object state`
≠ `distribution state`
≠ `court reception state`
≠ `estate/property state`
≠ `project-completion state`
≠ `later editorial recovery state`.

The first five parts could be textually stabilized and physically printed while the work remained incomplete as a project, controversial as a court object, and economically blocked as an estate-controlled continuation.

The larger proposition is now sharper:

> Publication is not one event. A historical work can pass through textual stabilization, printing, court distribution, evaluative rejection, posthumous property negotiation, abandonment, and later recovery as analytically distinct states.

This is a particularly strong textual analogue for the frontispiece corpus, where composition, matrix, impression, host, copy, and later recombination likewise diverge.
