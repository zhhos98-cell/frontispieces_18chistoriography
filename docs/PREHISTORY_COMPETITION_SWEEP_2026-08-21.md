# Prehistory and competition sweep — 21 August 2026

This pass moves the corpus backwards before the 1723 Weigel calendar. The result changes the historical model: genealogy + heraldry + annual calendar + engraved armorial apparatus was already a functioning publishing technology before Köhler/Weigel, and a competing Augsburg series overlapped the first Weigel years.

## 1. 1717–1719: Ostertag's Genealogisch-Heraldischer Calender

A 1717 volume is available through Google Books/Google Play under Heinrich Jonas Ostertag. Its title already combines:

- births, marriages and deaths of European ruling houses;
- kinship relations and Stamm-Tafeln;
- engraved coats of arms for each high house;
- short explanations of those arms.

The 1718 issue is secured institutionally in the BSB/DDB as `Geneal. 21-1718/19`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10428021-4`.

An antiquarian copy description adds material detail: the 1718 volume has a **frontispiece engraved by Johann Matthias Steidlin**, twelve half-page monthly engravings, thirty-five engraved armorial plates and a folding genealogical table. The seller identifies this as the second of three issues under Ostertag's title.

This is important negative/precedent evidence. Engraved front matter, monthly imagery, armorial plates and genealogical tables predate the Weigel series. The Clio plate therefore cannot be explained simply as the arrival of visual apparatus into a previously textual calendar.

## 2. 1720–1726: Steidlin's Genealogisch-heraldischer Staats-Calender

Authoritative serial metadata establishes Johann Matthias Steidlin's Augsburg *Genealogisch-heraldischer Staats-Calender* as an annual serial for **1720–1726** (ISSN-L 2628-4863; VD18 90282876). KIT/Heidelberg list surviving/digitised years 1720, 1721, 1723 and 1726, with BSB external digitisation for later years.

The 1720 Heidelberg copy is directly digitised (`DOI 10.11588/diglit.48534`). Its digital table of contents separately identifies:

- `Frontispiz`;
- title page;
- calendar and monthly material;
- a large genealogy-heraldry section;
- register;
- `Bericht an den Buchbinder`;
- a continuation for newer princely houses;
- another register and another `Bericht an den Buchbinder`.

The binder instructions are analytically significant: the visual-tabular apparatus was not merely present but required prescribed physical ordering. Direct image-level identification of the 1720 frontispiece remains pending.

## 3. The crucial correction: 1723–1726 is overlap, not succession

The Steidlin serial did **not** simply end before the Weigel *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender* began in 1723. Serial records preserve a Steidlin 1723 issue and a terminal 1726 issue.

Therefore at least part of **1723–1726** should be modelled as a competitive/parallel publication ecology:

- Augsburg / Steidlin: *Genealogisch-heraldischer Staats-Calender*;
- Nürnberg / Weigel–Köhler constellation: *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender*.

This matters because the target Clio allegory becomes visible in the Weigel series by 1726, exactly while the older Steidlin series is still alive.

The research question can therefore be sharpened:

> Was `Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica. Testes temporum et veritatis` part of a Nürnberg strategy for reframing or differentiating an already-established genealogy-heraldry calendar technology?

This is a hypothesis about competition and epistemic positioning, not yet a demonstrated causal explanation.

## 4. Three frontispiece regimes now need comparison

The evidence now supports at least three different early front-matter states/contexts:

1. **1718 Ostertag/Steidlin** — engraved frontispiece by Johann Matthias Steidlin; image identity still to inspect.
2. **1724 Weigel** — frontispiece described as `Gedenckwürdigkeiten des 1722ten Jahres`, an annual-event/memory image.
3. **by 1726 Weigel** — reusable `Historia Genealogia Heraldica / Testes temporum et veritatis` allegory.

A fourth comparator is the **1720 Steidlin Staats-Calender** frontispiece, securely present in a direct Heidelberg digitisation but not yet iconographically identified.

The next plate-level task is therefore not only 1725. It is a four-point comparison of **1718 / 1720 / 1724 / 1726**, followed by the 1725 transition witness.

## 5. The pre-Gatterer handbook complicates the ecology further

The undated *Bequemes und zum Zeitungs-Lesen sehr dienliches genealogisches und heraldisches Hand-Buch* belongs in the same early-to-mid-century object ecology. Its title describes genealogy/heraldry as a reference technology for newspaper reading.

Copy-level collation now shows severe material variation:

- SB-17124: 106 armorial engravings + 107 genealogical tables; seller dates c.1725 by Pope Benedict XIII evidence.
- Honterus lot 295: one copper title/frontispiece + 81 full-page arms with genealogical tables on versos; auction date c.1740 and Köhler attribution remain catalogue-level.
- Babel lot 128: one copper title/frontispiece + 60 full-page armorial plates with genealogical tables on versos; incomplete.
- Hôtel des Ventes Genève 2013 lot 66: one frontispiece, 140 leaves, 105 arms and 107 numbered armorial descriptions.
- Darabanth 2026 lot 22539: original title/frontispiece and title page missing; a later owner inserted a **frontispiece from another work** followed by six leaves of two-colour calendar pages.
- Brandenburgisches Landeshauptarchiv: institutional catalogue dates a copy `[nach 1750]`.

The Darabanth evidence demonstrates at least one case of later recombination. Therefore divergent plate/leaf counts cannot be translated mechanically into editions. The project must first distinguish publication state from copy intervention.

## 6. Revised object ecology

The current corpus now has overlapping rhythms rather than a single evolutionary chain:

- 1717–1719? — Ostertag genealogy-heraldry calendar;
- 1720–1726 — Steidlin genealogy-heraldry state calendar;
- 1723–1757 — Weigel/Köhler historical-genealogical-heraldic calendar;
- undated, catalogue brackets from c.1725 to after 1750 — non-annual genealogy/heraldry handbook for newspaper reading;
- 1729–1750 — Köhler weekly numismatic serial;
- 1759–1766 — Gatterer handbook / continued calendar;
- 1767–1776 — Seyfart/Raspe cumulative Wappenbuch ecology.

The promising explanatory vocabulary is therefore **competition, extraction, recomposition, de-serialisation and re-serialisation**: the same kinds of armorial, genealogical and evidentiary material move between annual, weekly, handbook and cumulative formats.
