# Seyfart/Raspe frontispiece persistence and copy divergence, 1767–1776

## Result

The late Seyfart/Raspe phase can now be stated more precisely. The question is no longer simply whether a frontispiece survived after 1766. Surviving-set descriptions and one structured institutional digital witness show **copy/configuration divergence**:

- one four-volume 1767–1776 set is catalogued with **three repeated engraved frontispieces** (`3 wh. gest. Front.`);
- a separate 1767–1771 two-volume set is catalogued with **372 armorial coppers plus one separate copper**, identified as the tincture/colour-key plate, without a frontispiece counted in the collation;
- UB Tübingen's structured digital object for a 1771 first-volume witness likewise moves from binding/endpaper to colour-key plate, title page and *Vorbericht* without a frontispiece node;
- a separately catalogued 1771 second volume is explicitly described as having a **`Titelkupfer`** plus 193 armorial plates.

The strongest current conclusion is therefore:

> **threshold-image persistence is verified in the Seyfart/Wappenbuch phase, but it is copy- and binding-dependent. The identity of the repeated/title coppers as the Historia/Genealogia/Heraldica design remains unresolved.**

This is a materially better problem than a binary `frontispiece present / absent` census. It requires copy-level control of what was retained, repeated, omitted, or rebound when the old annual calendar apparatus was converted into the cumulative Wappenbuch.

## 1. Four-volume set: three repeated frontispieces

Venator & Hanstein, auction catalogue 136, lot 239, describes:

- Johann Friedrich Seyfart, *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch*;
- four volumes, Nürnberg, Raspe, 1767–1776;
- `Mit 3 wh. gest. Front. und 567 (von 571) gest. Wappentafeln`;
- volume I additionally retains the title leaf `Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender auf das Jahr 1767 oder jährliches Handbuch der neuesten ...`.

Stable carrier:

- https://www.venator-hanstein.de/assets/Pdfkataloge/Katalog_136.pdf
- lot 239, catalogue p.121.

`wh.` is read here in the ordinary antiquarian-catalogue sense `wiederholten`: three repeated engraved frontispieces. The description does **not** identify the image iconographically, so this is evidence for repetition/persistence, not yet for Historia/Genealogia/Heraldica identity.

The same lot is especially useful because it keeps two host architectures physically adjacent in the first volume: the `Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender 1767` title survives inside a set catalogued as the cumulative *Wappenbuch*.

## 2. Another 1767–1771 set: no frontispiece counted

A current Müller & Gräff / AbeBooks description of volumes 1–2 gives:

- 1767–1771, Nürnberg, Raspische Handlung;
- `Mit 372 Wappenkupfern und einem separaten Kupfer`;
- the separate plate is then identified as the **Erklärung der Wappenfarben (Tingierung)**;
- no frontispiece is counted in the collation.

Stable carrier:

- https://www.abebooks.co.uk/book-search/author/seyfart-johann-friedrich/
- listing: *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch ... Bd. 1–2 [von 4]*.

This should not be converted into a universal negative. Antiquarian collations can omit unnumbered leaves, and copies may have lost or never received a threshold plate. What it does establish is that the late-series frontispiece cannot be treated as mechanically present in every surviving set.

## 3. UB Tübingen 1771 first volume: structured institutional object without a frontispiece node

UB Tübingen OpenDigi provides a fully structured digital object for a 1771 first-volume witness, shelfmark `Fe 18-1`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bsz:21-dt-208196`, DOI `10.20345/digitue.20819`.

Its opening structural sequence is explicitly indexed as:

`Einband → Spiegel → Vorsatz → Erklärung der Farben → Titelblatt → 2r–3v Vorbericht → Register ...`

No frontispiece or `Titelkupfer` node occurs in the structured opening sequence. The digital object then proceeds directly into the 178 numbered armorial plates.

This is a stronger negative control than a bookseller's abbreviated collation because the institutional object exposes and labels its opening leaves image-by-image. It still must be framed as a **copy-level absence**, not an edition-level rule: the frontispiece may have been omitted at issue, lost before digitisation, removed during rebinding, or otherwise absent from this particular surviving witness.

The value is comparative. A separately catalogued 1771 volume below explicitly retains a `Titelkupfer`, while `Fe 18-1` does not expose one. The divergence therefore exists among surviving 1771 Wappenbuch witnesses/configurations, not merely between different decades.

The same Tübingen object also contains the publisher's contemporary *Vorbericht* explaining that earlier Wappencalender arms were taken as the basis of the cumulative Wappenbuch and that successive sections were to be issued annually with the Wappencalender; see `docs/SEYFART_PUBLISHER_PROGRAMME_ANNUAL_DELIVERY_TO_CUMULATIVE_WAPPENBUCH_1771_2026-08-26.md`.

## 4. 1771 volume: `Titelkupfer` survives in another copy

A separate 1771 second-volume copy, Raspische Handlung, is described by Wissenschaftliches Antiquariat Thomas Haker as:

- `(4), 194 Bl.`;
- `Mit Titelkupfer und 193 gestochenen Wappentafeln`.

Stable carrier:

- https://www.zvab.com/durchlauchtigen-Welt-vollst%C3%A4ndiges-Wappenbuch-welchem-Wappen/31262200903/bd
- same description mirrored on AbeBooks.

Again, the image identity is not supplied. The value is copy-level: a `Titelkupfer` demonstrably exists in at least one 1771 Wappenbuch witness even though the Tübingen first-volume witness and another multi-volume collation do not count/expose a frontispiece.

Google Play also exposes a free 138-page digital object for Seyfart's *Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender auf das Jahr ... 1767*, book ID `O5ZAAAAAcAAJ`.

A 2024 National Széchényi Library article reproduces the title page of a surviving Pécs/Klimo copy (`I.VII.7`) and records a Klimo ex libris dated by hand to 1769. The reproduced facing image is the Klimo ex libris, not evidence for the book's original frontispiece. This copy is therefore a useful provenance witness but does not close the plate identity question.

## 6. Consequence for the frontispiece chronology

The late endpoint should now be stated in two layers:

### Verified

- threshold/title copper apparatus persists somewhere in the Seyfart/Raspe Wappenbuch ecology after 1766;
- at least one 1767–1776 set contains three repeated engraved frontispieces;
- at least one 1771 volume contains a `Titelkupfer`;
- a structured institutional 1771 first-volume witness exposes no frontispiece in its opening sequence;
- the 1767 Wappenkalender and cumulative Wappenbuch architectures coexist in surviving bound configurations.

### Still unresolved

- whether any of those repeated/title coppers are the `HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS` design;
- whether three repeated frontispieces are three impressions from one matrix, repetitions of one design from different matrices, or bibliographically repeated but materially non-identical plates;
- whether absence from particular collations/digital witnesses reflects original issue, later loss, rebinding, or cataloguing practice.

This is exactly the repository's preferred distinction:

`design identity ≠ matrix identity ≠ witness occurrence ≠ copy completeness`.

## 7. Manual task is now narrower

Existing manual rows M006/M007 remain correctly targeted. User time is needed only for the **image identity/state** question:

- open the first image/frontispiece in `bsb11343160`, `bsb10481709` and/or related 1767–68 objects;
- test literal Historia/Genealogia/Heraldica inscriptions and composition;
- if similar, capture bottom signatures, frame, lettering, wear and dimensions;
- record whether the threshold plate belongs to the annual `Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender` title sequence, the cumulative Wappenbuch title sequence, or both in the same bound copy.

There is no longer a need to spend manual time establishing that late Seyfart/Raspe copies could contain frontispieces at all.
