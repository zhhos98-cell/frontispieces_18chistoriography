# Colour as method — second sweep: epistemic jurisdiction, 2026-08-27

## 1. The novelty boundary has tightened again

The first sweep established that several broad claims are unavailable:

- colour has cognitive work in Gatterer's `Synopsis` — prior art in André de Melo Araújo;
- colour charts, reference samples and eighteenth-century colour order — now a consolidated field, especially Giulia Simonini;
- scientific hand-colouring creates problems of standardisation, fidelity and copy variance — established by Kärin Nickelsen and related literature;
- Göttingen had an active colour-science context — established around Tobias Mayer, Erxleben and Lichtenberg.

The second sweep adds a more direct boundary.

Franz L. Fillafer's 2017 `A World Connecting? From the Unity of History to Global History` already places **Gatterer's coloured synchronistic table and Schlözer's synoptic/systematic problem in the same historiographical sequence**. Fillafer reproduces the coloured Gatterer table and reports a detailed secondary semantics:

- dominant empires — red;
- rival systems — shades of green;
- groups about to be subdued — yellow;
- conglomerates of tribal peoples — blue;
- groups disconnected from the main world-historical system — black.

Immediately afterward, he turns to Schlözer's reflections on the synoptic vision required to transform an aggregate of special histories into a system of world history.

Therefore the project must not claim novelty simply for connecting `Gatterer + colour + Schlözer + Universalgeschichte`.

## 2. Schlözer's colour vocabulary is itself already noticed

Schlözer's 1772 mosaic passage is not an unknown text. Modern historiography has repeatedly quoted or analysed:

- `farbichte Steinchen` as the units of mosaic history;
- the artist/historian's act of arrangement;
- `die Voltaires machen sie selbst, oder färben sie wenigstens`;
- the distinction between Kritik and Zusammenstellung;
- the transformation of Aggregat into System;
- the image/painting metaphor for historical wholes.

Recent scholarship also quotes Schlözer's demand that the historian answer **for every stroke** (`für jeden Strich`) in the historical picture before criticism, like a mapmaker.

Thus neither the mosaic metaphor nor the `Facta färben` phrase can carry novelty by themselves.

## 3. The remaining opening is structural

What the closest prior art located so far does not appear to formulate is the following question:

> **At what epistemic layer may colour legitimately enter historical knowledge?**

The distinction emerges from Schlözer's own sequence:

`Annalen / Denkmäler`
→ `Kritik`
→ `Facta`
→ `Zusammenstellung`
→ `System / Gemälde`.

Within that sequence:

- making or `colouring` the **Facta** is polemically suspect;
- arranging already recovered facts is explicitly the historian's work;
- the particular manner of arrangement constitutes the method of Universalgeschichte;
- every stroke in the resulting historical picture remains accountable to criticism.

This means that historical composition is legitimate only under continuing source accountability.

The issue is therefore not a simple opposition between colour and objectivity. It is a problem of **epistemic jurisdiction**.

## 4. Gatterer's corpus supplies several different locations for colour

### A. Arrangement / display

In the `Synopsis`, actual pigment is applied to a diagram whose line geometry already carries temporal and political relations. Colour can sharpen distinction, boundary and revolution at the level of historical arrangement/overview.

### B. Surrogate / encoding

In heraldry, semantic tinctures must travel through monochrome print. Letters, dots, hatchings, central legends and prose descriptions provide competing/redundant translation channels.

### C. Source-tested notation

A secondary transcription of Gatterer's `Abriß der Heraldik` p. 12 reports that he evaluates Rink's additional signs for `Naturfarbe` and `Eisenfarbe` against old `Wappenbriefe`. If the primary page confirms this, the choice of chromatic notation is itself judged through historical source practice.

### D. Classification

Secondary scholarship reconstructs Gatterer's 1775 Menschen- und Völkerkunde as beginning with human bodies according to shape and colour. Here colour becomes an observed/classificatory variable rather than an arbitrary diagram syntax or encoded tincture.

### E. Copy finishing

Hand-applied pigment remains a property of particular impressions/copies and may vary independently of the engraved matrix and line system.

These operations should remain analytically separate unless actor-level evidence links them.

## 5. A revised colour-method pipeline

The working pipeline is:

`source / object`
→ `critical authentication / evaluation`
→ `fact / proposition`
→ `classification / selection`
→ `arrangement / system`
→ `surrogate / representation / overview`
→ `reader inspection`
→ possible `copy finishing`.

Colour can occur at every stage, but with different legitimacy conditions.

### Source/object layer
Colour may be part of the object that must be transmitted or described.

### Fact/proposition layer
Colouring can metaphorically mean unauthorized alteration, rhetorical loading or invention. Schlözer's Voltaire aside belongs here.

### Classification layer
Colour can function as a discriminator that partitions bodies, objects or territories.

### Arrangement/system layer
Colour can be assigned to relations that are not themselves chromatic, making a historical structure more rapidly surveyable.

### Surrogate/encoding layer
A colour property can be translated into letter, hatch, word or legend. Accuracy and recoverability become the methodological tests.

### Reader/display layer
Colour can reduce visual search and comparison effort without necessarily changing the underlying factual propositions.

### Copy-finishing layer
Pigment can add perceptual information or completion but also introduce copy variance and a new error channel.

## 6. The strongest present proposition

The present project-specific proposition is:

> **The late-Enlightenment methodological problem of colour was not simply whether historical knowledge should be visual or coloured. It concerned the epistemic jurisdiction of chromatic intervention: whether colour belonged to the source, the fact, the code, the classifier, the arrangement, the display, or the finished copy, and what kind of warrant was required at each location.**

A shorter formulation:

> **The question is not whether history may be coloured, but where colour may enter without colouring the fact.**

This formulation is currently stronger than `colour as cognition`, `colour as historical visualization`, or `Gatterer versus Schlözer on colour`.

## 7. Why the heraldic branch matters more now

Heraldry supplies an unusually clean test because the same chromatic semantic information can appear in multiple carriers:

`tincture category`
→ `letter code`
→ `hatching`
→ `central monochrome legend`
→ `prose blazon`
→ optional `hand colouring`.

The 1772 publisher retrospective explicitly diagnoses letter notation as producing `Unordnung und Mißverstand`. The 1744 Wappenkalender directly demonstrates a redundant alternative architecture of hatching, centralized decoder and prose colour names.

This is a miniature laboratory for the larger methodological problem: chromatic information can be preserved while its **epistemic location and material carrier change**.

## 8. Why Gatterer's heraldic p. 12 is now a high-value primary target

The secondary quotation attributed to Gatterer is unusually significant because it reportedly says:

- Rink added signs for natural and iron colour to Colombière's hatchings;
- Gatterer regards the first as superfluous;
- he finds the second instructive because old German Wappenbriefe taught him that heralds used iron colour and white/silver terms in a particular way;
- he directs the reader toward Köhler's programme on the origin of hatchings.

If directly verified, this is not merely a description of signs. It is a methodological judgment in which a **graphic convention is accepted/rejected by comparison with historical source usage**.

That would be one of the cleanest actor-level instances of the colour-method problem.

## 9. Adjacent Göttingen classification context

There is another important but potentially expansive branch. Scholarship on Gatterer's geography and Göttingen ethnography reports that colour enters the classification of human bodies. This must be handled with strict conceptual and source controls.

The immediate question is not to project later racial taxonomies backward. It is narrower:

> what does `Farbe` mean as a classificatory variable in Gatterer's own geographical system, and what operations of comparison does it permit?

This can later be compared with the graphical classification strategies of Gatterer's `Linnaeismus graphicus`, where scripts are sorted by historically, nationally, geographically and chronologically discriminating formal features.

The common problem may be operationalized difference; the commonality is not yet an actor category.

## 10. Relation to the 1773 Gatterer–Schlözer dispute

The polemic itself is still not demonstrably a `colour controversy`.

The defensible relation is:

- the dispute concerns ownership/priority of Universalhistorie as a method of selection and arrangement;
- Schlözer explicitly defines method through `Zusammenstellung` and uses painting/mosaic/mapmaking vocabulary to delimit legitimate composition;
- Gatterer's programme materially delegates part of overview to tables, maps and sometimes pigment;
- colour is therefore a **high-resolution discriminator of two historical architectures**, even if neither polemicist explicitly says `we disagree about colour`.

The next primary check is to inspect the polemical texts for language of `Plan`, `Gemälde`, `Tafel`, `Karte`, `Strich`, `Farbe`, `Übersicht`, `Zusammenstellung` and `Methode` rather than assuming the link.

## 12. Stop rule

Do not widen this branch into a general `colour in Enlightenment` bibliography.

New literature should enter only if it bears directly on one of these distinctions:

1. colour of the source/object;
2. colour as encoded semantic information;
3. colour as fact alteration/rhetorical loading;
4. colour as classification;
5. colour as relational arrangement;
6. colour as inspectability aid;
7. colour as copy finishing;
8. rules governing translation between these layers.
