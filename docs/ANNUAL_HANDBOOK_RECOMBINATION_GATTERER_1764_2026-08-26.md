# `Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender ... oder jährliches Handbuch` — recombining two temporal formats in 1764

## Status

The 1764 Gatterer/Raspe title supplies a strong actor-level control for the temporal distinction reconstructed from the 1720s Weigel publication ecology.

The book is titled:

`Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender auf das Jahr 1764 oder jährliches Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik, worinnen aller jetzigen Potentaten Stammtafeln und Wappen mit einer richtigen Beschreibung der Wappen und einem Abrisse der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde enthalten sind.`

The formulation matters because it explicitly fuses two previously separable publication identities:

`Wappen-Calender`
+
`jährliches Handbuch`

into one object.

This is not merely a bibliographic synonym. It makes **annuality** itself a property of the handbook form.

## 1. Earlier ecology: annual calendar and undated handbook

By the mid-1720s the Weigel publishing environment contains at least two related architectures.

### Annual Wappenkalender

The calendar is host-year specific and repeatedly described as `neu vermehrt` / `verbessert`. Direct 1728 and 1733 prefaces show:

- annual review of genealogical tables;
- use of newest / most reliable reports;
- external correspondence;
- explicit usefulness for daily newspaper reading;
- terminal in-press additions or changes when events occur too late for normal reintegration.

Its temporal strength is currency; its weakness is a hard production deadline.

### Undated `Bequemes Hand-Buch`

The related Weigel-widow handbook removes the annual calendar host while preserving a dense genealogy/heraldry lookup apparatus. Its title also explicitly advertises usefulness for `Zeitungs-Lesen`.

Its temporal strength is reusable consultation; its weakness is that an undated reference configuration can become stale unless replaced, augmented or materially recomposed.

The two formats can therefore be described analytically as:

`annual current-state maintenance`
versus
`de-serialised reference persistence`.

## 2. 1759 changes title and editor, but retains the handbook identity

Gatterer's 1759 *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik* is formally recorded as the continuation of the earlier Weigel Wappenkalender. The change therefore already joins:

`serial succession`
+
`handbook title`.

At this stage, however, the title itself does not yet contain the especially explicit phrase `jährliches Handbuch`.

The 1759 object demonstrates that an annual serial lineage can survive a host-title change to `Handbuch` while publisher and plate infrastructures continue.

## 3. 1764 makes the hybrid explicit

The 1764 title reverses part of the 1759 shift by restoring `Wappen-Calender` while simultaneously retaining the handbook identity:

`Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender ... oder jährliches Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik`.

This is a highly compressed publication-theoretical statement.

It tells the reader that the object is at once:

- a continuation (`Fortgesetzter`);
- a calendar attached to a particular year (`auf das Jahr 1764`);
- a handbook (`Handbuch`);
- and a handbook whose reference function is itself annualized (`jährliches`).

The hybrid solves the tension between currency and reusable reference by making **the reference book recurrent**.

A useful formulation is:

`handbook lookup architecture`
+
`calendar replacement rhythm`
→
`annual handbook`.

## 4. Why `jährliches Handbuch` matters

The phrase should be taken seriously rather than normalized as a long title variant.

A handbook normally implies relatively stable consultation across time. An annual normally implies recurrent replacement. `jährliches Handbuch` therefore binds two temporal expectations that pull in different directions:

`stable navigability`
+
`scheduled obsolescence`.

This is exactly the problem exposed by the 1728/1733 evidence. Genealogical relations and political offices need a stable structure for lookup, while births, marriages, deaths, accessions and other changes continuously invalidate parts of that structure.

The 1764 hybrid can thus be read as a mature publishing response:

> maintain a recognisable handbook architecture, but regenerate its current political state every year.

Do not claim that this was the sole reason for the title without a contemporary preface explicitly saying so. The safe claim is that the title **materializes the same temporal problem at the level of publication identity**.

## 5. The object still combines identity, description and instruction

The 1764 title specifies that the annual handbook contains:

- Stammtafeln of all present rulers;
- coats of arms;
- a `richtige Beschreibung der Wappen`;
- an outline of heraldry / Wappenkunde.

This produces several nested layers:

`current rulers`
→ `genealogical positioning`
+
`armorial visual identity`
+
`correct verbal description`
+
`general decoding instruction`.

The same object therefore contains both **data** and **instructions for interpreting the data**.

This continues the earlier architecture in which armorial images could be supported by colour legends and prose descriptions, but now at a more explicitly pedagogical handbook level.

## 6. Annualization is not merely new content; it is version governance

The earlier Wappenkalender evidence shows that annual publication entails:

- deciding what current information enters the main state;
- handling late information through a delta layer;
- revising the next state from newer reports.

When the 1764 publication calls itself a `jährliches Handbuch`, annuality can therefore be treated analytically as a **governance rule for reference currency**.

The handbook is not simply republished often. Its claim to be about the `neueste Genealogie` depends on recurrent version replacement.

A bounded model is:

`stable schema / lookup design`
+
`annually refreshed political-genealogical contents`
→
`versioned reference system`.

This is stronger than saying merely that the work was a yearly genealogy book.

## 7. Relation to the frontispiece programme

The recurring `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica / Testes temporum et veritatis` frontispiece survives across changing host formats, including Gatterer's mid-century continuation ecology.

The 1764 hybrid title helps explain why such a threshold image could persist while the bibliographic form changed. The visual programme names relatively stable domains of evidence; the publication architecture beneath it can shift among:

- calendar;
- handbook;
- annual handbook;
- later cumulative Wappenbuch.

This suggests a useful distinction:

`stable disciplinary/evidentiary threshold`
versus
`variable temporal packaging of the underlying information`.

The frontispiece's recurrence is therefore compatible with substantial re-engineering of the host's update rhythm.

## 8. Relation to the 1771/1772 cumulative Wappenbuch programme

The later Seyfart/Raspe cumulative Wappenbuch again reorganizes the same informational field. The 1771/1772 publisher programme explains a move toward a multi-volume cumulative armorial delivered in instalments.

The broader temporal sequence can now be expressed as:

`annual calendar`
↔ `undated handbook`
→ `annual handbook`
→ `cumulative Wappenbuch issued serially`.

These are not simply successive titles. They are alternative ways to manage the competing demands of:

- currentness;
- stable lookup;
- cumulative completeness;
- visual/textual description;
- manageable production increments.

This is one of the clearest long-run information-architecture sequences currently visible in the repository.

## 9. Evidence guards

1. The 1764 title itself proves the explicit hybrid `Wappen-Calender` / `jährliches Handbuch`; it does not by itself explain authorial intention behind every word.
2. Do not claim a simple linear evolution from calendar to handbook to annual handbook. The formats overlap, recur and are materially recombined.
3. The c.1725 handbook is undated and survives in materially divergent copy states; use copy-specific dating.
4. `version governance`, `stable schema`, `scheduled obsolescence` and similar terms are modern analytical vocabulary.
5. The relation to newspaper reading is explicit for the earlier handbook title and 1728/1733 annual prefaces; this note does not claim that the 1764 title itself repeats `Zeitungs-Lesen` unless separately controlled.
