# Winckelmann 1671: Oldenburg copperplate, portrait, and map apparatus census

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE VISUAL-APPARATUS CHECKPOINT — OFFICIAL LB OLDENBURG DIGITAL OBJECT CONTROLS A LARGE COPPERPLATE PROGRAMME; INDIVIDUAL PLATE-LEVEL ATTRIBUTIONS AND SOURCE RELATIONS REMAIN TO BE ITEMIZED

## Result

The Landesbibliothek Oldenburg digital record for Johann Just Winckelmann's 1671 *Oldenburgische Friedens- und der benachbarten Oerter Kriegs-Handlungen* gives a precise apparatus-level collation:

- `Kupfert.` — engraved title;
- `24 Portr. (Kupferst.)` — 24 engraved portraits;
- `15 Ill. (Kupferst.)` — 15 further copperplate illustrations;
- `2 Kt. (Kupferst.)` — 2 engraved maps;
- additional engraved illustration notation in the catalogue;
- 8 folded leaves in the physical collation.

The same official record identifies participants including:

- Gerhart Muntinck;
- Johann Nutzhorn;
- Johann von Sandrart;
- `I. H. S.`;
- Henrich van Lennep;
- P. Philip;
- Count Anton Günther.

Source:

- Landesbibliothek Oldenburg digital object: https://digital.lb-oldenburg.de/ihd/content/titleinfo/189024
- structure/contents view: https://digital.lb-oldenburg.de/periodical/structure/190144

The long title presents the work as compiled from `Original-Archivalischen Acten` and simultaneously `Mit schönen kunstreichen Kupferstücken` and portraits. Thus documentary source rhetoric and graphic apparatus are programmatically coordinated in the publication itself.

---

## 1. Production regime differs sharply from 1664

The 1664 *Americanische Neue Welt Beschreibung* relies heavily on reactivated sixteenth-century woodblock stock.

The 1671 Oldenburg history instead presents a large copperplate infrastructure oriented toward:

- dynastic portraiture;
- territorial description;
- fortification / city views;
- maps;
- heraldic/genealogical representation;
- historical scenes or object images.

Working contrast:

`1664 = inherited matrix stock ecology`

versus

`1671 = commissioned / coordinated copperplate apparatus ecology`.

The shift is not simply woodcut → engraving as technique. It changes the social and evidentiary organization of image production:

`rediscovered printer stock`
→ `named artists/engravers + court-historical programme + archival narrative`.

---

## 2. Official structure index already exposes image-text embedding

The LB Oldenburg structure record explicitly indexes a visual item inside the first part / third chapter:

`Eigentliche Abbildung der Hochgräflichen Residenz Statt und Festung Oldenburg`.

This is valuable because the plate is not merely listed as paratext. It is structurally embedded in a chapter-level historical/geographical argument.

The corpus should therefore distinguish:

- threshold/title image;
- dynastic portrait plate;
- city/fortification image;
- map;
- heraldic plate;
- genealogical/table apparatus;
- historical/event illustration;
- author portrait.

The 15 `Ill.` should not be normalized into one generic illustration class.

---

## 3. Copy variation is already visible in the market record

Surviving market copies often lack substantial portions of the original apparatus.

One current Antiquariat Lenzen description reports a copy with:

- author portrait/frontispiece surviving;
- allegorical engraved title surviving;
- two armorial engravings / genealogical material;
- most of the copperplate plates and map missing.

Other market descriptions count 39 copperplate plates plus map(s), while institutional records differ slightly in portrait counts depending on whether certain threshold/author/armorial plates are classified separately.

This is precisely a copy-level corpus problem:

`EDITION APPARATUS`
≠ `SURVIVING COPY APPARATUS`.

Do not use one market copy to reconstruct the edition-level image census without institutional collation control.

## 4. Engraver/personnel controls

The Russian National Electronic Library catalogue independently notes:

- author portrait engraved by H. Lennep;
- portraits engraved by J. Sandrart, P. Philipp and others;
- illustrations/maps engraved by H. Lennep and others.

Source:

https://rusneb.ru/catalog/000199_000009_002785081/

LB Oldenburg separately identifies Henrich van Lennep as a Dutch/German copper engraver, mathematician and engineer, later resident in Kassel.

This raises a high-value network question:

`Winckelmann court historiography`
↔ `engraver-engineer/cartographic expertise`
↔ `portrait production`
↔ `territorial map/view production`.

The personnel list should be resolved plate by plate before any global role is assigned.

## 5. Current edition-level apparatus model

Provisional controlled classes:

### Threshold

- engraved title / allegorical threshold;
- author portrait/frontispiece may be catalogued separately in some copies/descriptions.

### Dynastic/personnel

- 24 portrait engravings in the LB Oldenburg collation;
- market/facsimile descriptions sometimes count 25 portraits depending on treatment of the author/frontispiece.

### Territorial / spatial

- 2 engraved maps;
- city/fortress views among the other illustrations;
- at least the Oldenburg residence/fortress image is structurally indexed in the digital contents.

### Other historical/material

- 15 further copperplate illustrations;
- armorial and genealogical material appears in copy descriptions;
- exact classification needs direct item-level inspection.

This means that simple totals such as `39 plates` can hide several materially and functionally different subprogrammes.

## 6. Relation to archival-source rhetoric

The title explicitly coordinates two claims:

`Aus ... Original-Archivalischen Acten / Documenten ... auch eigener Erfahrung`

and

`Mit schönen kunstreichen Kupferstücken / ... Conterfaiten ausgezieret`.

The work therefore publicly presents archival documentary evidence and visual representation as parts of one historical apparatus.

This is highly relevant to the repository's larger question:

> how did objects, documents, images, maps, portraits and their reproductions become coordinated historical witnesses?

The immediate task is to identify which visual classes merely decorate dynastic authority and which actively represent evidence, places, objects, events, or genealogical relations discussed in the text.

Classify one by one rather than accepting the catalogue's umbrella `Ill.`.

## 8. Author-level visual production sequence

The current Winckelmann visual-production sequence can now be stated with controlled asymmetry:

`1664 New World`
= old Staden woodblock matrices reactivated + mixed-provenance blocks + separate new threshold plate;

`1671 Oldenburg`
= large coordinated copperplate/portrait/map apparatus with named production participants;

`Hessian Chronicle`
= maps/illustrations reportedly planned, then dropped as Darmstadt court participation cooled.

Working analytical proposition:

> Winckelmann's historical books show that visual apparatus was not an invariant authorial preference. Its material form depended on access to old matrix stock, courtly commissioning networks, engraver/cartographic labor, and the continuing willingness of patrons to sustain the cost and politics of visual production.

Direct causal relations among particular plates and the abandoned Hessian designs remain open.
