# Schumann 1729 as a competing annual genealogy-update format — direct primary OCR control, 26 August 2026

## Status

The 1733 Weigel/Köhler Wappenkalender says that other books serving the same useful purpose had appeared and that scholars were working in several places toward the completeness of current genealogy and heraldry. A concrete contemporary competitor can now be identified and textually controlled: Gottlieb Schumann's *Jährliches genealogisches Hand-Buch* (Leipzig: Gleditsch), first issue **1729**.

Heidelberg University Library provides a complete digitisation with OCR. The title explicitly defines the work as containing the **`neuesten Nachrichten`** of the currently reigning European imperial, royal, electoral, princely and comital houses, together with cardinals, order members and cathedral/chapter personnel. The preface presents the work as a continuation/reworking of Johann Christoph Spiess's annually issued *Jetzt-herrschendes Europa / allerneueste Genealogie* and promises that the new Hand-Buch will likewise be continued **annually**.

Evidence level: `verified_primary` for the OCR-controlled title/preface substance, with diplomatic quotation still best checked against page images if exact typography matters.

Primary carrier: UB Heidelberg, DOI `10.11588/diglit.49474`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bsz:16-diglit-494744`, preface scans `0007–0008`.

## 1. `Neueste Nachrichten` is part of the product definition

The title is not merely `genealogisches Hand-Buch`. It advertises a current-information function:

`die neuesten Nachrichten von allen Häusern jetzt-regierender ...`

The work therefore defines its corpus by **present political status plus newest reports**, not by exhaustive dynastic antiquity.

This closely parallels the Weigel/Köhler language reconstructed for 1728 and 1733:

- `neuesten Nachrichten`;
- annual revision/augmentation;
- currently living houses/persons;
- practical use for interpreting current information.

The formats differ, but they occupy the same informational problem-space.

## 2. The preface frames genealogy as an annually maintained field

The 1729 preface begins by recalling Johann Christoph Spiess's *Jetzt-herrschendes Europa oder allerneueste Genealogie*, which had been issued from 1725 onward at the beginning of each year. Schumann praises Spiess for the `grossen Fleisse` and `Accuratesse` with which he had advanced the newest genealogical study.

After Spiess's death, Schumann says he was asked to undertake the work in expanded form. The new design would include not only continued newest genealogies but also high court/state personnel and ecclesiastical chapter personnel.

The operational relation is:

`annual predecessor series`
→ `editor dies`
→ `new editor/publisher reorganises and enlarges scope`
→ `current genealogy + administrative personnel`
→ `new annual continuation`.

This is structurally comparable to later Weigel/Köhler → Gatterer succession, although no production or personnel connection is implied.

## 3. `Vermehrungen` and `Zusätze` are explicit launch vocabulary

On the following preface page Schumann describes the 1729 work as a `neues Genealogisches Hand-Buch` appearing with **many augmentations and other additions** (`mit vielen Vermehrungen und andern Zusätzen`). He then promises to continue it `alljährlich`.

This matters because it gives an independent Leipzig control for the same publication logic visible in Nürnberg:

`current-state reference work`
+
`annual augmentation`
+
`new information/personnel categories`
→
`serially refreshed genealogical apparatus`.

Köhler's 1733 statement that other books were pursuing the same laudable end can therefore be grounded in a demonstrable nearby competitor rather than treated as a rhetorical commonplace.

## 4. Competition is over update architecture, not just title/genre

The comparison between Nürnberg and Leipzig is analytically useful because both products solve a moving-information problem but package it differently.

### Weigel/Köhler Wappenkalender

- genealogical tables;
- heraldic plates;
- colour-key apparatus;
- historical modules;
- explicit use for newspaper reading;
- annual revision;
- terminal in-press delta pages.

### Schumann/Gleditsch Hand-Buch

- prose/reference handbook architecture;
- broad current dynastic and officeholder coverage;
- title-level `neuesten Nachrichten` claim;
- continuity from an earlier annual current-genealogy series;
- explicit `Vermehrungen` and `Zusätze`;
- annual continuation promise.

The relevant competition is therefore not simply `calendar vs handbook`. It is competition between **different interfaces for maintaining and consulting current dynastic-political information**.

## 5. Implication for the 1733 Köhler preface

Köhler's 1733 language becomes much more concrete when placed against Schumann 1729.

Köhler says that since the Wappenkalender began, other books serving the same useful purpose had appeared and scholars were working everywhere toward the completeness of genealogy and heraldry. Schumann's 1729 launch sits squarely in that chronology and explicitly claims:

- newest genealogical information;
- extension of scope;
- augmentation/additions;
- annual renewal.

This does not prove Köhler was referring specifically to Schumann. Use the relation as:

`contemporary competitor matching Köhler's described update ecology`,

not

`identified target of Köhler's remark`.

## 6. A broader ecology of current-reference publishing

The comparison suggests a market and scholarly ecology in which current dynastic reference had to be repeatedly refreshed because its objects changed continuously.

A minimal common model is:

`political/dynastic world changes`
→ `newest reports collected`
→ `reference apparatus revised/augmented`
→ `annual issue`
→ `next cycle`.

The Nürnberg Wappenkalender adds a particularly visible in-press patch layer and a much denser image/table apparatus. The Leipzig Hand-Buch shows that annual updating and `neuesten Nachrichten` were not unique to that product.

This strengthens the project-wide interpretation of annuality as an **information-maintenance technology** rather than merely a calendar convention.

## 7. Evidence guards

1. The Heidelberg OCR is sufficient for substance but contains predictable Fraktur recognition errors; exact quotations should be checked against the scan.
2. Do not infer a direct Schumann↔Köhler correspondence or citation without separate evidence.
3. Köhler's 1733 plural reference to competing books should not be reduced to Schumann alone.
4. `neuesten Nachrichten` in Schumann's title does not identify the channels by which all information arrived.
5. The present pass has not yet shown a Schumann equivalent of the Weigel/Köhler terminal in-press correction page; annual update architecture is controlled, intra-press delta architecture is not.
