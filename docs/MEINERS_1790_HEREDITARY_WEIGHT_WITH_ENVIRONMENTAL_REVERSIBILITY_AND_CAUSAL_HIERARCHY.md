# Meiners 1790: hereditary weight with environmental reversibility and causal hierarchy

Date: 2026-08-27

## Status

This note refines the emerging Meiners–Kant comparison. The 1790 race/slavery essays do support increased weight on descent, hereditary transmission and supposedly inborn population characters. They do **not** support a simple model of absolute hereditary fixity.

The stronger reconstruction is a **weighted plural causal hierarchy** in which descent/blood can receive default priority while climate, transplantation, discipline (`Zucht`), life-way and mixture remain capable of producing change.

## 1. The key falsification: heredity is not an absolute closure condition

A page-specific transcription of `Von den Varietäten und Abarten der Neger`, p.639, gives Meiners explicitly considering two non-mixture mechanisms of intergenerational change:

- `Klima`;
- `Zucht`, including enforced work, exertion and removal from practices he stigmatizes as vices.

He then states that whichever of climate or discipline acts more strongly, transplantation into other regions and altered life-way can bring marked bodily and mental change **even without mixture with supposedly better blood**.

This is analytically decisive.

A model of the form:

`hereditary race essence → immutable phenotype / character`

is too strong for this passage.

The safer model is:

`inherited disposition + climate + discipline/life-way + transplantation + mixture → intergenerational bodily / mental outcome`.

## 2. Descent can still have default causal priority

Other exact-source controls on the same 1790 essay report Meiners giving greater explanatory weight to origin/parents than to the land/climate of birth. Recent scholarship paraphrases the contrast as:

`which people / parents one descends from > in which land / climate one is born`.

This is compatible with the current 1792 reconstruction if treated as **causal reprioritisation**, not causal exclusivity.

The important distinction is:

`highest default weight ≠ only active cause`.

Therefore the post-1788 Kantian move toward descent/hereditary persistence did not force Meiners into a single-cause model.

## 3. `Ueber die Natur der Afrikanischen Neger`: mixed causal grammar in the same year

The German transcription of the earlier 1790 essay supplies several compatible operations.

### Environmental degeneration remains active

Meiners uses a broad natural analogy in which plants/animals moved from favorable climate and soil into less favorable circumstances `ausarten`. He then applies the same grammar to Europeans moved outside Europe, claiming loss of part of their inherited (`angestammt`) physical and mental qualities.

Whatever the empirical and moral defects of this argument, its causal structure matters:

`inherited endowment × new environment → degeneration / modification`.

### Mixture is treated as transmissive

He also claims that mixture with persons of different descent, colour, formation and dispositions changes the child's bodily and mental characteristics.

Operationally:

`parental descent / blood → partial transmission across multiple trait domains`.

This is not a skin-colour-only model. Colour, bodily formation and mental/moral properties are bundled in his inheritance claims.

### Some characters are explicitly said to propagate in lineages

The same essay says that external and internal gifts, like natural defects, can `fortpflanzen` in certain `Geschlechtern`.

This supplies a hereditary-persistence grammar without making every outcome environmentally irreversible.

## 4. The political inference is coupled to, not identical with, heredity

The 1790 essay does something Blumenbach's taxonomic use of persistence does not do: it moves from alleged inherited or natural population differences to unequal rights and political treatment.

The argumentative chain is not simply:

`heredity → slavery`.

A more accurate reconstruction is:

`travel-report aggregation / bodily and behavioural claims`
`→ alleged inborn or persistent population differences`
`→ ranking of capacities and dispositions`
`→ doctrine that rights/duties should track capacities`
`→ defence of unequal political status / slavery`.

This is a **jurisdiction expansion** from natural-historical and anthropological claims into normative-political inference.

The p.639 reversibility passage does not neutralize that political hierarchy. It shows instead that Meiners could combine hierarchy with a theory of partial improvement under climate, discipline and transplantation.

## 5. Consequence: persistence operates as a weight, not a binary gate

The comparison with Blumenbach can now be sharpened.

### Blumenbach 1797

`visible difference → necessary inheritance test → Rasse / Spielart categorical jurisdiction`.

Persistence is used as a threshold for category membership.

### Meiners 1790

`descent / inherited disposition` receives high causal weight, but remains inside a field containing:
- climate;
- soil/place;
- transplantation;
- `Zucht` / discipline;
- life-way;
- mixture;
- education/contact.

Thus:

`persistence/descent → weighted causal prior`,

not:

`persistence/descent → exclusive causal gate`.

This is a better foundation for the later 1792 colour analysis.

## 6. Link to Meiners 1792 colour method

The 1792 colour reconstruction already requires:

`parental blood with highest default weight + climate/local environment + mixture + internal physiology + witness triangulation`.

The 1790 p.639 control makes that architecture historically less surprising. Two years earlier, Meiners was already prepared to hold together:

- inherited difference;
- environmental modification;
- intergenerational change;
- mixture;
- differential causal weight.

The 1792 colour essay should therefore be tested for **how it ranks causes**, not for a binary choice between heredity and climate.

Potential sequence:

`1788–91 active Kant engagement`
`→ 1790 stronger hereditary/descent weighting without environmental closure`
`→ 1792 colour essay: causal ranking under visible equifinality`
`→ 1793 Grundriß: further explicit Kant citation / stronger descent wording`.

This sequence remains chronological/operational unless direct textual evidence establishes a precise borrowing chain.

## 7. Important complication: reversibility is itself stratified

Meiners's p.639 claim implies that apparently inherited population characters can be changed by transplantation and altered life-way over generations. This creates a new falsification target:

> Does Meiners assign the same reversibility to colour, bodily formation, mental dispositions and moral character, or do these traits have different response times and persistence thresholds?

That question connects directly to the repo's `stratified phenotype` model.

Do not assume the answer from the p.639 passage alone. It may apply unevenly across trait domains.

## 8. Evidence hierarchy / stop rules

- `descent receives greater weight` does not mean `climate is causally excluded`.
- `hereditary` does not mean `immutable` in Meiners's 1790 usage.
- p.639 environmental reversibility should not be generalized automatically to every trait.
- `Zucht` in this argument is a historically situated coercive/social causal category, not modern genetics or modern developmental biology.
- exact p.639 wording remains page-specific transcription control until the primary page is directly inspected.
- the modern Black Central Europe transcription is useful for source structure and German wording, but should not be silently promoted to facsimile verification.
- Meiners's political conclusions are his own argumentative transformations; do not attribute them to Kant merely because Kantian hereditary concepts entered the same problem-space.

## 10. Strong current formulation

> Meiners's selective uptake of hereditary descent around 1790 did not produce a binary theory in which inherited race characters were causally closed against environment. In `Von den Varietäten und Abarten der Neger`, p.639, he explicitly allowed climate, discipline and altered life-way to produce intergenerational bodily and mental change even without mixture, while elsewhere giving descent and parental blood greater default weight than place or climate. Persistence therefore functions more accurately as a weighted causal prior than as an exclusive gate. This sharply distinguishes Meiners from Blumenbach's later categorical use of necessary inheritance and supplies a direct upstream control for the 1792 colour essay's plural, ranked causal field.
