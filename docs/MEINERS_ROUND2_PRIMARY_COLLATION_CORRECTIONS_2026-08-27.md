## 1. The anti-isolated-body-part quotation is not from *Grundriss* p.23

The 2023 specialist volume quotes Meiners against Blumenbach:

`dass man ganze Völkerschaften eben so wenig, als einzelne Menschen, nach der Bildung eines einzigen Theils des Cörpers ... in ganzen Nationen nicht nach einzelnen Knochen beurtheilen könne`

and its note cites `Meiners: Grundriß ... S. 23`.

Direct inspection of both local *Grundriss* carriers falsifies that locator:

- 1785 printed pp.23–24 concern the descent/geographical distribution of northern Asian, American, Eskimo/Greenland and related peoples, with bibliography and continuation of §§6–8;
- 1793 printed p.23 belongs to the methodological definition of `Geschichte der Menschheit` against Universalhistorie.

The quoted anti-body-part passage is directly recoverable instead in Meiners, *Beyträge zur Naturgeschichte* (1790), pp.63–64, in the public Blumenbach Online transcription. It is part of Meiners's published controversy with Blumenbach and continues into a provenance warning about Negro skulls.

Correction:

`anti-single-body-part / isolated-bone rule = 1790 Beyträge zur Naturgeschichte pp.63–64, primary-direct public transcription`

not

`1793 Grundriss p.23`.

Historiographical implication:

The broader chronology remains:

`1785 anti-single/principal-colour rule`
→ `1790 anti-single-body-part + specimen-provenance rule`
→ `1792 causal underdetermination / multi-trait triangulation`
→ `1793 revised taxonomy and retained anti-single-colour rule`.

The old `1785 p.23 ↔ 1793 p.23` formulation-continuity task should be retired as based on a modern locator error.

## 2. 1785 p.39 ↔ 1793 pp.84–85 is a real revision, but its exact operation is sharper than “climate replaced by descent”

### 1785 p.39 primary text

Within §14 on the `verschiedene Grad der körperlichen Stärke`, Meiners says the effects of climate on the human body are highly indeterminate and apparently contradictory. Food and climate do not produce uniform effects across countries and nations.

He then distinguishes hardness/conditioning from strength and writes, in OCR-normalised form:

`Abhärtung ist etwas ganz anders, als Stärke, indem die eine ohne die andere da seyn kann. Abhärtung ist allerdings in einem gewissen Grade eine Folge von Uebung und Lebens-Art; vielmehr ist sie aber eine Eigenthümlichkeit gewisser Stämme, Racen und Klimate.`

### 1793 pp.84–85 primary text

The surrounding climate/food argument is retained and expanded. The corresponding end of the paragraph now reads:

`Härte ist etwas ganz anders, als Stärke, die eine ohne die andere da seyn kann. Abhärtung ist allerdings in einem gewissen Grade eine Folge von Uebung und Lebensart; Härte hingegen ist eine Eigenthümlichkeit gewisser Stämme, und Racen.`

This is a precise lexical/causal reorganisation:

1785:

`Abhärtung = partly acquired by exercise/life-way + more fundamentally property of Stämme/Racen/Klimate`

1793:

`Abhärtung = acquired by exercise/life-way`

while

`Härte = property of Stämme/Racen`.

The word `Klimate` disappears from the inherited-property side at exactly this point.

Therefore the strongest safe formulation is:

`1793 separates acquired hardening (Abhärtung) from group-attributed hardness (Härte) and removes climate from the latter attribution.`

This is more specific than a generic `climate → descent` replacement and is compatible with the same paragraph continuing to treat climate as causally active but variable.

Status:

`1785 p.39 ↔ 1793 pp.84–85 = PRIMARY-DIRECT CLOSED`.

## 3. Intensity/degree observation

Both editions open §14 with an explicit intensive formulation:

`verschiedene Grad der körperlichen Stärke`.

The paragraph also uses `in gleichem Maße` and `in einem gewissen Grade` while separating strength, hardness and hardening.

This gives the CSMBR/Bigotti intensity comparator a concrete actor-language foothold:

`degree / measure / more-less distinctions are primary-text variables in Meiners, not merely analyst vocabulary`.

Keep this as a bounded comparator, not a genealogy claim.
