# Schertz 1699: an early Nürnberg comparator for describing a coat of arms historically

## Result

Carl Ferdinand von Schertz's *Mährisches Landes-Wapen Aus der Histori Und Herolds-Kunst Illuminiret* (Nürnberg, 1699) is a strong pre-Gatterer comparator for the later question of how a coat of arms can be described **historically** rather than only blazoned.

The title itself already joins two domains that Gatterer's 1767 methodological vocabulary later keeps analytically distinct: `Histori` and `Herolds-Kunst`. More importantly, the surviving long transcription shows that Schertz does not merely name the colours and figures of the Moravian eagle. He constructs an explanation of one territorial coat of arms through historical writers, legal-political change, older armorial usage, material/monumental witnesses and heraldic interpretation.

This should not be treated as a demonstrated source for Gatterer. Its significance is comparative and chronological: **a Nürnberg print of 1699 already makes a particular armorial object the centre of a historical-heraldic inquiry.**

## I. The object of inquiry is one specific territorial coat of arms

Schertz explicitly says that the current coat of arms of the Margraviate of Moravia is the target toward which he directs his description. He notes that Paprocký and Pešina had already written about it but judges their treatment too brief and in need of supplementation.

This is important methodologically. The work does not start from a general grammar of heraldry and then provide Moravia as an example. It starts from a **given armorial object** and mobilizes history and heraldic knowledge around it.

Operationally:

`given territorial arms`
→ `recover previous descriptions / historical accounts`
→ `identify deficiencies or errors`
→ `supplement with historical and heraldic reasoning`
→ `interpret the observed configuration`.

That structure is strikingly close to the later problem expressed in the phrase `ein gegebenes Wappen historisch zu beschreiben`, although no direct textual relation is claimed.

## II. Historical writers are compared rather than merely cited

Schertz invokes Pešina's *Mars Moravicus* positively while criticizing other historical writers for errors, tales and fables about Moravian history. He also says Stranský stumbles in describing the arms.

The evidentiary use of historical literature is therefore comparative:

- some authors supply usable historical reconstruction;
- some transmit errors or fables;
- some misdescribe the armorial object;
- the present author positions his own account as correction and supplementation.

This already exceeds simple antiquarian citation density. Historical authors are ranked by their usefulness for explaining a particular coat of arms.

## III. Historical change is used to explain heraldic form

Schertz's interpretation links features of the Moravian eagle to a narrative of the territory's political history, including the transformation from kingdom to margraviate and the relation of Moravia to larger imperial structures.

Whether every symbolic inference is historically persuasive is a separate question. What matters here is the operation: visible armorial features are treated as effects or memorial traces of historical change.

That is different from blazon.

`the shield contains X`
≠
`X is explained through a historical sequence`.

This distinction provides a prehistory for the later Gattererian boundary between `Blasonisten` and historical describers of arms.

## IV. Heraldic interpretation and historical explanation are interleaved

Schertz moves repeatedly between:

- formal features of the eagle and shield;
- heraldic doctrines concerning old/new arms, colours and figures;
- historical claims about Moravia;
- political/legal status;
- prior learned authorities.

The result is not yet a modern source-critical history of an armorial state. Symbolic interpretation remains prominent. But it is equally not reducible to a vocabulary lesson.

The text therefore belongs in an intermediate comparative category:

**historical-heraldic exposition centred on one armorial object.**

## V. Access to manuscript repositories is presented as a condition of better historical work

In the dedicatory/prefatory material Schertz laments that he did not gain access to major Moravian manuscript collections, including the Olomouc cathedral chapter library, the Hoffmann/Dietrichstein collection and the Velehrad monastic library. He says that with access to such rare Moravian manuscripts he could have produced a more fully worked study.

This is highly relevant to the repository's source-practice line:

`armorial explanation`
→ `depends on access to historical manuscripts`
→ `quality of account constrained by archive/library access`.

A later Czech book-history study independently highlights this passage and Schertz's concern for preserving manuscript monuments of the past.

The work therefore links the historical description of arms to an explicit manuscript-source problem before Gatterer's mid-eighteenth-century pedagogical source hierarchy.

## VI. Why this matters for the 1767 Gatterer problem

The 1767 Rudolphi control currently establishes a negative boundary:

`blazon`
≠ `historische Erläuterungen`
≠ `Historie der Wappen`.

Schertz 1699 supplies an earlier positive comparator for what a source-rich, history-centred treatment of a **particular** Wappen could look like in Nürnberg.

The comparison should be framed carefully:

### Rudolphi 1698 / Gatterer 1767 judgment

Rudolphi's *Heraldica Curiosa* is broad, technical and antiquarian, yet Gatterer later says it contains only a handful of historical elucidations and no history of the arms, placing him among the blazonists.

### Schertz 1699

Schertz narrows the object to one territorial Wappen and organizes historical authors, manuscript access, political change and heraldic interpretation around explaining that object.

This suggests a potentially important distinction for the later Gatterer sweep:

> **breadth of heraldic erudition is not the same as historical concentration on the armorial object.**

A general heraldic compendium can remain methodologically descriptive, while a short monograph on one coat of arms can perform a more recognizably historical operation.

## VII. Nürnberg continuity without an influence claim

Schertz's 1699 book was printed in Nürnberg by Christian Sigmund Froberg for Johann Zieger. Rudolphi's 1698 *Heraldica Curiosa* was likewise a Nürnberg publication environment. Decades later the Weigel/Köhler calendar, Gatterer's heraldic manuals and Raspe publishing ecology also operate through Nürnberg.

This geographical continuity is worth recording because the repository is already reconstructing Nürnberg as a dense material and methodological ecology of heraldry, genealogy, numismatics and historical illustration.

But use only the safe claim:

> Nürnberg repeatedly hosted printed experiments in joining heraldic form, historical knowledge and visual representation.

Do not infer a continuous workshop, reading chain or intellectual lineage from Schertz to Köhler or Gatterer without bibliographic or citation evidence.

## VIII. Relation to later historization

Hefner's 1863 definition of `Historisierung` is much tighter than Schertz's practice: it reconstructs the origin, development, augmentation and alteration of a given coat of arms up to the observed state.

Schertz's 1699 text should therefore not be retroactively classified as a mature historization in Hefner's sense. Its value lies precisely in the difference.

The longer sequence can now be tested as:

`1699 Schertz: one Wappen explained through history + heraldic art + learned authorities + manuscript desiderata`
→ `1766 Gatterer: sources of heraldry become an explicit curricular problem`
→ `1767 Gatterer environment: blazon distinguished from historical elucidation/history of arms`
→ `1791 practical heraldry: worked operations on armorial objects`
→ `1863 Hefner: historization codified as bounded temporal reconstruction of a given armorial state`.

This is a comparative chronology, not a demonstrated genealogy.
