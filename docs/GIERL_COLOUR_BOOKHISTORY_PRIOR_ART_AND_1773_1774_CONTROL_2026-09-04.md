# Martin Gierl control: prior art, locators, and the 1773→1774 heraldry branch

**Date:** 2026-09-04  
**Branch:** `article-line-goettingen-nuremberg-return`  
**Use:** source/locator and prior-art control for the HLQ colour/book-history article

## Working rule

Use Martin Gierl as a **primary-source locator, bibliographical control, and prior-art boundary**, not as the theoretical spine of the article. The article remains locked to `colour + book history first`. Gierl's large argument about Gatterer's programme as *präzisierte Wissenschaft* is relevant background, but it should not pull the article upward into a generic institutional or epistemological account.

The productive question is narrower: **where does Gierl already occupy the visual/colour claim, and where does his evidence expose a book-historical seam that the present corpus can inspect at finer resolution?**

## 1. Gierl 2012: exact prior-art boundary now controlled

Martin Gierl, *Geschichte als präzisierte Wissenschaft. Johann Christoph Gatterer und die Historiographie des 18. Jahrhunderts im ganzen Umfang* (Stuttgart-Bad Cannstatt: frommann-holzboog, 2012), 458 pp., 63 illustrations and one colour plate with two colour images.

The publisher's reading sample includes the full table of contents, figure list, bibliography and index. Relevant chapter locations are:

- p. 82: `Die Präzisierung von Herrschaft: Heraldik, Genealogie, Numismatik, Diplomatik, Statistik`
- p. 86: `Heraldik`
- p. 217: `Die Evidenz der Zeit: Historische Tafeln und Chronometrie`
- p. 283: `Die Synopse der Geschichte`
- p. 365: Göttingen school / Gatterer / Schlözer / Herder / historical institute

This confirms that Gierl already treats heraldry and historical tables inside his larger programme of precision/evidence. That framework is established historiographical ground for the present article.

### 1.1 Exact colour/hatching prior art

Gierl's figure list is decisive:

> `Abb. 17 Wappentopographie (Fig. 15) und Schwarz-Weiß-Darstellung der Tinkturen. Gatterer, J. Chr. 1773a, Tabelle I.`

The source is Gatterer's 1773 Göttingen/Gotha *Abriß der Heraldik*.

**Consequence:** the present article must not claim novelty for noticing that Gatterer represented heraldic tinctures in black and white. Gierl has already named and reproduced that operation explicitly.

Gierl also reproduces:

- fig. 18: systematic heraldic figures and combinations, Gatterer 1773, Tables III and VII;
- fig. 19: arms and genealogy of the Lorraine house, Gatterer 1762, Table 1;
- fig. 25: Köhler's *Genealogia familiae augustae Stauffensis* (1733), genealogical table plus `Probationes`;
- fig. 27: Gatterer on Sigismund III in Köhler's *Münzbelustigung*.

These are useful locators for the Nürnberg/Göttingen publication and information ecology, but they do not establish plate identity or plate-stock transfer.

### 1.2 Exact coloured-*Synopsis* prior art

Gierl's foldout captions state that both reproduced plates from Gatterer's 1769 *Synopsis historiae universalis* come from a **coloured exemplar in the library of the Francke Foundations, Halle**:

- `Falttafel 1`: *Tab. I. Durationem Populorum, Regnorum, Civitatum sistens.*
- `Falttafel 2`: *Tab. II. Durationem Populorum, Regnorum, Civitatum sistens.*

**Consequence:** a coloured *Synopsis* witness is already explicit prior art. The present article's distinct contribution is the **copy-state problem**: colour promised at edition level is not uniformly realized across surviving witnesses, and Tübingen `Fn 28.2` is especially strong because one relevant table is coloured and the other uncoloured inside the same surviving book.

The article therefore gains a clean distinction:

`Gierl: coloured exemplar as evidentiary/visual object`  
`present article: bibliographical variability of colour across and within copies`

## 2. The strongest new branch: 1773 Dieterich → 1774 Raspe

Gierl's fig. 17 fixes the 1773 first independent edition as a control point. The current article, however, uses the **1774 second edition** for the p. 12 discussion of `Naturfarbe`, `Eisenfarbe`, old `Wappenbriefe`, and the explicit reference to Köhler's 1736 programme on hachures by which metals and colours could be indicated `absque pigmentis`.

The bibliographical change is substantial enough to deserve direct comparison.

| Feature | 1773 | 1774 | Current status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title | *Abriß der Heraldik oder Wappenkunde* | same | secure |
| Place / publisher | Göttingen und Gotha, Johann Christian Dieterich | Nürnberg, Gabriel Nicolaus Raspe | secure |
| Extent | 11 unnumbered pp. + 115 pp. | [4] leaves + 128 pp. | secure |
| Copperplates | 8 leaves / 8 copper illustrations | 13 copper illustrations | secure |
| Edition statement | first independent issue in this form | `bey dieser zweyten Auflage ... mit fünf Kupfertafeln und doppelten Registern vermehrt` | secure |
| Tincture image | Gierl fig. 17 identifies Table I as black-and-white representation of tinctures | direct plate comparison pending | open |
| `Naturfarbe` / `Eisenfarbe` figs. 19–20 | exact location/state pending | p. 12 text controlled; Tab. II attribution currently controlled secondarily | open |
| Köhler 1736 programme reference | presence/wording pending | explicit on p. 12 | open |
| same copper matrix across editions? | unknown | unknown | **do not infer** |

### Questions to close

1. Does the 1773 text already contain the sentence directing the reader to the `köhlerische Programma`, or is that reference introduced/changed in 1774?
2. Are the `Naturfarbe` and `Eisenfarbe` signs already present in the 1773 eight-plate apparatus, or do they belong to material newly added/rearranged in the thirteen-plate 1774 edition?
3. Is 1773 Table I materially/design-wise the same tincture table as the corresponding 1774 plate? Distinguish `same_design_as` from `same_matrix_as`.
4. Which five plates are genuinely added in 1774? The title-page edition statement gives a precise enumeration problem rather than a generic claim of expansion.
5. Did the move from Dieterich (Göttingen/Gotha) to Raspe (Nürnberg) alter only the host/imprint, or also the visual apparatus and explanatory relation among text, key, and plates?

This is a first-order book-history problem and fits the current article much better than a larger claim about Gatterer's scientific method.

## 3. Gierl 2013: collection provenance, with a strict firewall

Gierl's 2013 Gatterer-Medaille address states that in the Nürnberg/Altdorf environment Johann David Köhler and Johann Heumann von Teutschenbrunn supported Gatterer and **left/transferred to him their `Urkunden- und Wappensammlungen`**. Gierl then places Gatterer's succession to Köhler at Göttingen in 1759.

This is valuable because it establishes a **collection-provenance lead** joining Gatterer to two older antiquarian/heraldic collections.

It is not evidence that:

- the Weigel/Köhler *Erklärung der Farben* copperplate passed to Gatterer;
- Weigel/Tyroff publisher-held plate stock entered Gatterer's personal collection;
- a specific printing matrix travelled from Nürnberg to Göttingen;
- the collection transfer caused Gatterer's later synchronistic colour practice.

Keep the levels separate:

`Urkunden-/Wappensammlung provenance ≠ publisher plate-stock provenance ≠ matrix identity ≠ methodological influence`.

The useful next move is to recover Gierl's own source/footnote for the two-donor collection statement and identify surviving catalogue or archival traces of those collections.

## 4. What Gierl changes in the novelty hierarchy

### Demote to prior art

- Gatterer used black-and-white graphic conventions to represent heraldic tinctures.
- Gatterer's *Synopsis* existed in coloured copies.
- Heraldry, genealogy, numismatics, diplomatics and tables formed part of Gatterer's broader programme of historical precision/evidence.
- Gatterer's work can be read institutionally through the Göttingen historical institute.

### Still strong and project-specific

- the recurring/separable *Erklärung der Farben* as a bibliographical module within the Weigel/Köhler serial;
- a black printed key carrying chromatic information without coloured pigment;
- copy loss of the key and survival of encoded armorial matter;
- the key's persistence/re-hosting into the 1771/72 cumulative *Wappenbuch*;
- annual updating and in-press corrections surrounding a relatively stable chromatic convention;
- the direct textual Köhler-1736 → Gatterer-heraldry reference;
- edition-level colour promises versus copy-level finishing in the *Synopsis*;
- the mixed Tübingen witness as an internal copy-state demonstration;
- the 1773 Dieterich → 1774 Raspe edition/plate-state comparison, if direct inspection closes it.

The article's novelty therefore remains where it should be: **not “Gatterer knew monochrome colour signs,” but the longer book history that made colour information portable, separable, re-hostable and copy-variable.**
