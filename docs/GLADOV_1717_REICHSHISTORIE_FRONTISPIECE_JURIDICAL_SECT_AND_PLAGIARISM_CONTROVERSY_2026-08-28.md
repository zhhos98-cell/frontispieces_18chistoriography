# Gladov 1717: Reichshistorie frontispiece, the later `juridical sect` problem, and a plagiarism controversy over lecture knowledge

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE PREHISTORY / FRONTISPIECE-CONTROVERSY BRIDGE — OBJECT/BIBLIOGRAPHY CONTROLLED; ICONOGRAPHY CONTROLLED THROUGH MODERN DIRECT ANALYSIS + MODERN REPRODUCTION; ORIGINAL PLATE NOT YET INSPECTED BY REPOSITORY

## Result

A previously uncoded frontispiece creates a strong prehistory for the later Gatterer controversy map.

Friedrich Gladov's posthumously published:

`Versuch Einer vollständigen und accuraten Reichs-Historie von Teutschland` (Leipzig/Halle: Felix du Serre, 1717)

contains an engraved frontispiece built around **Historia and Chronos** and a temporal division of the image between known past and uncertain future.

The work's title simultaneously defines Reichshistorie as:

- `pragmatisch` explanation of the principal changes in the Empire and their true causes;
- a `richtige Einleitung in ein gründliches Teutsches Staats-Recht`;
- an account proved throughout by `glaubwürdigen Zeugnissen alter und neuer Scribenten`.

This is important for the frontispiece project because the plate does not accompany an abstract philosophy of history. It stands before a work in the Halle Reichshistorie tradition that explicitly subordinates historical knowledge to training in German public law while also claiming pragmatic causal explanation and evidentiary control.

Fifty years later, in `Zufällige Gedanken über die teutsche Geschichte`, *Allgemeine historische Bibliothek* 2 (1767), pp. 23–34, esp. p. 29, Johann Christoph Gatterer retrospectively named the tradition associated with **Johann Peter Ludewig and Nicolaus Hieronymus Gundling** a large historical `juridical` sect and complained that imperial history had nearly become only an aid to German public law.

Gladov had heard both Ludewig and Gundling at Halle and belongs to the first student generation of that Reichshistorie programme.

This produces a controlled historical relation:

`Ludewig + Gundling Reichshistorie pedagogy`
→ `Gladov 1717 German-language Reichshistorie + frontispiece`
→ `history explicitly positioned as introduction to Staatsrecht`
→ `Gatterer 1767 retrospective critique of the Ludewig/Gundling juridical sect`.

**Do not convert this into a direct Gatterer → Gladov influence/attack claim.** No evidence has yet been found that Gatterer cited the 1717 frontispiece or Gladov when formulating the 1767 passage.

Working relation label:

`STRUCTURAL / PEDAGOGICAL PREHISTORY OF A LATER JURISDICTIONAL CRITIQUE`.

## 2. Why Gladov belongs specifically to the Ludewig/Gundling line

Daniel Fulda's direct study of the Halle Reichshistorie programme states that Gladov:

- heard **Johann Peter Ludewig** and **Nicolaus Hieronymus Gundling** at the University of Halle;
- himself taught at the Fridericiana in the early 1710s;
- belonged to the student generation of the founders of Reichshistorie.

The relevant foundational teaching texts were:

- Gundling, *Abriß Zu einer rechten Reichs-Historie* (Halle, 1707);
- Ludewig, *Entwurff der Reichs-Historie* (first edition 1707; later Wendisch-Halle edition 1710).

Gladov's 1717 book was advertised by its publisher as the first extended Reichshistorie in German, following earlier outlines/lectures and extended Latin works.

This connection matters because Gatterer's 1767 retrospective label does not name an amorphous legal tradition. It names Ludewig and Gundling as founders of the historical tendency he calls `juridical`.

Evidence status:

`SECONDARY-DIRECT PEDAGOGICAL LINEAGE CONTROLLED`.

## 3. Frontispiece iconography: Historia writes on Chronos

Fulda provides the first extended modern analysis of the 1717 frontispiece and reproduces it as his figure 1. A 2017 Halle University magazine also reproduces the plate and summarizes the composition.

The controlled iconographic description is:

- **Historia** sits centrally and writes history in a folio;
- the folio rests on the back of the serviceable time-god **Chronos**;
- Chronos has laid down his scythe;
- the scene is divided into two temporal fields;
- the **right side is bright** and coded as the known past through a grave, ruins and a pyramid;
- historically differentiated human figures include a foreground knight associated with the medieval past and an imperial figure further back associated with antiquity;
- the **left side is dark** and represents present/future uncertainty;
- a foreground figure in contemporary fashionable dress marks the present;
- an historically indeterminate figure positioned further into the background marks the future;
- a narrow bridge/footway over a stream and an ascent into mountainous terrain encode danger, uncertainty and movement forward;
- one airborne genius with trumpets projects historical fame toward posterity/present-future;
- another genius carries torches and lights the dangerous forward path.

The temporal architecture is therefore not:

`dark past → enlightened present`.

It is closer to:

`bright / knowable past`
→ `Historia as knowledge-work in the present`
→ `dark / unknown but traversable future`.

Evidence status:

`SECONDARY-DIRECT ICONOGRAPHIC ANALYSIS + MODERN REPRODUCTION CONTROLLED; ORIGINAL-PLATE INSPECTION PENDING`.

## 4. The subscriptio gives Historia two symmetric temporal tasks

Fulda transcribes the distich placed before the steps to Historia's throne:

`Et vivis praeferre facem et revocare sepultos / Historia ex tumulo, ceu tuba sacra potest.`

The important structure is the coordination of two historiographical operations:

1. carrying a torch **before the living**;
2. recalling the buried/dead **from the tomb**.

This makes the plate highly relevant to the present corpus because historical visualization is not restricted to recovery of past evidence. Historia is staged as an orientation technology for living actors moving into uncertain time.

Fulda interprets the second half of the comparison through the sacred trumpet of the Last Judgment and reads the plate as reallocating part of an older eschatological future orientation toward historical-pragmatic orientation.

Evidence status:

- inscription: `SECONDARY-DIRECT TRANSCRIPTION`; primary plate transcription still pending;
- eschatology/open-future interpretation: `MODERN SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION`, not corpus-level primary fact.

## 5. Relation to pragmatic history and political prudence

Fulda places the plate inside a Halle programme in which Reichs- and Staaten-Historie were designed for practical political orientation.

For Gundling, historical knowledge could support conjecture `de futuris`: past and present were combined to reason about what may come. Fulda stresses that the Gladov plate makes that programmatic future orientation visually explicit in a way the predominantly additive prose histories often did not.

For this repository the safe structural formulation is:

> **The 1717 frontispiece visualizes Historia as a practical temporal operator: knowledge of the past is staged as light/resources for action under the uncertainty of present and future.**

Do not convert this into a claim that the plate constitutes a developed philosophy of historical progress.

Fulda himself explicitly distinguishes the plate from later Geschichtsphilosophie and from Schillerian history-as-world-judgment.

## 6. Authorship / invention must remain unresolved

Gladov died in 1715, two years before the Reichshistorie appeared.

Fulda therefore explicitly states that whether the frontispiece derives from Gladov's own ideas **must remain open**.

He considers the plate a close fit with the historical conception of Halle Reichshistorie shortly after 1700, but this is not a designer attribution.

Current corpus fields should therefore be:

- `concept_author = unresolved`;
- `designer = unresolved`;
- `engraver = unresolved`;
- `Gladov_as_conceptor = unproved`;
- `publisher_role = potentially important; unresolved at image-invention level`.

Do not use `Gladov frontispiece` as shorthand to mean `frontispiece invented by Gladov`; it means frontispiece in the work published under Gladov's name.

## 7. A direct controversy erupts around ownership of the Reichshistorie knowledge

The 1717 volume immediately generated a controversy that is especially relevant to a project tracking frontispieces as part of scholarly infrastructures.

### 23 February 1718 — Hahn's public accusation

Simon Friedrich Hahn, then professor of history at Helmstedt and previously a student/teacher in Halle, published a short notice in:

`Neue Zeitungen von Gelehrten Sachen`, no. 16 (1718), p. 125.

According to Daniel Fulda's modern direct transcription/analysis, Hahn announced his own forthcoming Reichshistorie and complained that the work published under Gladov's name had at innumerable places been taken partly from Hahn's 1710–11 dictata and partly from discourses he had delivered in Halle on Ludewig's propositions, nearly word for word.

Evidence status:

`SECONDARY-DIRECT PRIMARY QUOTATION CONTROLLED; PRIMARY ISSUE CARRIER IDENTIFIED`.

Primary carriers:

- Google Play full view, *Neue Zeitungen von Gelehrten Sachen ... 1718, Erster Theil*, Google Books ID `_Tz5TBJPSSIC`;
- GDZ volume 1718 in series `PPN728151391`;
- SuUB Bremen periodical carrier, public-domain scan.

Primary page 125 remains to be inspected directly by repository.

### April 1718 — publisher-side counterattack

A seven-page anonymous reply appeared in the Halle:

`Vermischte Bibliothec oder Zulängliche Nachrichten und unpartheyische Gutachten ...` 1, no. 4 (1718), pp. 361–367,

under the title:

`Gedancken über Herrn Prof. Hahns Erklärung / als ob die Gladovische Reichs-Historie aus ihm abgeschrieben`.

Modern scholarship attributes it probably to **Johann Christoph Franck(e)**, one of the publishers connected to the Halle *Neue Buchhandlung*; individual authorship remains probabilistic.

The reply mocked Hahn, attacked his ownership claim and argued substantively that **Reichshistorie was a kind of shared resource in Halle** because both Gladov and Hahn had profited from the lectures/programmes of Ludewig and Gundling.

This is a major history-of-knowledge point:

`lecture / dictata / shared pedagogical programme`
→ `printed synthesis`
→ `priority / plagiarism accusation`
→ `publisher defense through claims of disciplinary common property`.

Evidence status:

`MODERN DIRECT ANALYSIS OF PRIMARY POLEMIC; AUTHORSHIP ATTRIBUTION PROVISIONAL`.

## 8. 1718–1719 escalation: critique and refutatio

The dispute escalated beyond periodical notices.

### Hildebrandt Heinrich Herbst, 1718

`Abgenöthigte Critiqve, über die, so wohl andern, als auch fürnemlich dem Herrn Prof. Hahn in vielen Stücken abgeborgte Gladovische Reichs-Historie` (Helmstedt: Hamm, 1718).

Modern scholarship describes this as an approximately 86-page detailed prosecution of the borrowing case and notes specific parallel evidence between Gladov and Hahn.

### `Der gerettete Herr Friedr. Gladov`, 1719

A roughly 168-page answer appeared in Halle in 1719:

`Der gerettete Herr Friedr. Gladov ... Gegen ... Simon Friedrich Hahns ... Beschuldigung eines unverantwortlichen Plagii ...`

Modern scholarship attributes it probably to **Johann Christoph Franck(e)**; some catalogue traditions give Johannes/Christoph Franck. Keep attribution controlled rather than silently normalized.

A Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek / SLUB Dresden digitization exists, and physical-copy descriptions record examples in which the 1719 defense is bound with the 1717 Reichshistorie.

This material afterlife is relevant:

`contested historical synthesis`
+
`frontispiece`
+
`later polemical defense`
→ sometimes survive inside one physical volume.

That is an unusually concrete case of **object + visual programme + controversy archive becoming one consultation unit**.

Evidence status:

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC / DIGITAL-CARRIER CONTROLLED`; exact copy-level binding relation is controlled for described market copies but requires institutional copy inspection before generalization.

## 9. The frontispiece and controversy should be analytically connected, but not causally conflated

No evidence currently shows that Hahn, Herbst or Franck discussed the frontispiece itself.

Therefore the safe relation is:

`frontispiece visualizes the practical constitution of Reichshistorie`
+
`book title defines history as pragmatic causal knowledge and introduction to Staatsrecht`
+
`the book becomes an object of controversy over ownership/derivation of that historical knowledge`.

Do **not** write:

`frontispiece was used as a polemical weapon in the Gladov–Hahn controversy`.

Instead:

> **The frontispiece belongs to the same published knowledge-object whose epistemic and pedagogical ownership was almost immediately contested.**

That distinction matches the existing corpus rule separating image function, host-publication function and controversy relation.

## 10. Direct connection to Gatterer's 1767 `juridical sect` passage

The primary locus has now been narrowed:

Johann Christoph Gatterer, `Zufällige Gedanken über die teutsche Geschichte`, *Allgemeine historische Bibliothek* 2 (1767), pp. 23–34, especially p. 29.

Kasper Risbjerg Eskildsen's direct citation identifies this article/page as the passage in which Gatterer:

- dates a major transition in modern German historical writing to Ludewig and Gundling;
- calls them founders of a large historical sect that could be called `juridical`;
- describes the danger that historical learning becomes subordinated to professional/legal `bread studies`;
- complains that imperial history is almost treated only as an auxiliary to German public law.

The Google Play full-view carrier for AHB vol. 2 (1767) has been identified; primary p. 29 transcription remains pending.

This changes the value of Gladov 1717.

Gladov's title literally advertises the book as:

`eine richtige Einleitung in ein gründliches Teutsches Staats-Recht`.

And Gladov had heard the two figures whom Gatterer later names as founders.

Therefore Gladov provides an unusually concrete **prehistory/control case for what Gatterer means by the jurisdictional subordination of Reichshistorie to Staatsrecht**.

Evidence status:

`STRUCTURAL CONNECTION STRONG; DIRECT RECEPTION/INFLUENCE UNPROVED`.

## 11. Why this matters for the Clio/frontispiece article architecture

The existing corpus had largely moved from:

`portable Clio constitution`
→ `genealogy / heraldry / material witnesses`
→ `Gatterer handbook and Historical Institute`
→ `institutional jurisdiction conflict`.

Gladov adds an earlier, different frontispiece ecology:

`Historia + Chronos`
→ `pragmatic Reichshistorie`
→ `Staatsrecht usefulness`
→ `future-oriented prudential function`
→ `controversy over copied / shared lecture knowledge`.

This means the article can now distinguish at least two early-eighteenth-century visual constitutions of Historia:

### Gladov 1717

`Historia / Chronos / temporal orientation / pragmatic-political usefulness`

### Weigel–Köhler lineage from 1726

`Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica / material and documentary witnesses / serial consultation`.

They need not be genealogically related to be analytically comparable.

A productive question is whether Gatterer's later programme recombined functions that had earlier been distributed across these separate visual/genre ecologies:

- temporal ordering and pragmatic utility;
- auxiliary disciplines;
- material witnesses;
- universal/synchronistic coordination;
- institutional jurisdiction.

This is a **comparison hypothesis**, not yet a transmission claim.

## 14. Current strongest article-level proposition

> **Before the inherited genealogy-heraldry Clio entered Gatterer's apparatus, a different Halle frontispiece had already made Historia into a practical temporal technology. Gladov's 1717 Reichshistorie places Historia on Chronos, divides known past from uncertain future, and accompanies a text explicitly advertised as a pragmatic introduction to German public law. The same volume immediately became the center of a dispute over whether Reichshistorie knowledge belonged to an individual lecturer, a compiler, or the shared Halle pedagogical field. When Gatterer in 1767 attacked the Ludewig–Gundling tradition as a `juridical sect`, he was therefore criticizing a historical jurisdiction whose earlier publication ecology had already been materially visualized and publicly contested.**

The last sentence must remain structural: no direct Gatterer reception of Gladov's plate has yet been demonstrated.
