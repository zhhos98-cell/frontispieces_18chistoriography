# Winkelmann Kassel censorship dossier `2° Ms. Hass. 35/1`: court revision and publication control, c.1647–1698

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE PRODUCTION / CENSORSHIP CHECKPOINT — EXACT KASSEL SHELFMARK CONTROLLED THROUGH FUCHS CITATION; LONG COURT-REVISION CHAIN CONTROLLED THROUGH MODERN SCHOLARSHIP; ITEM-LEVEL MODERN CATALOGUE DESCRIPTION AND EXACT CENSORED PASSAGES STILL PENDING

## Result

The production history of Johann Just Winkelmann's Hessian chronicle can now be separated into at least three materially and institutionally distinct layers:

1. **authorial manuscript apparatus** — Oldenburg `Cim I 204`, an autograph / authorially revised Latin chronicle witness with inserted leaves and multiple revision states;
2. **rolling court / archive source supply** — Darmstadt `HStAD D 4, 214/4`, including a 1697–98 request to transmit Landgrave Ernst Ludwig's `Lebenslauf` to the historiographer Winkelmann in Bremen `zur Abfassung einer Chronik`;
3. **court revision / censorship / publication control** — Kassel `2° Ms. Hass. 35/1`, explicitly cited by Thomas Fuchs as `Zensurakten` for Winkelmann's *Gründliche und wahrhafte Beschreibung der Fürstenthümer Hessen und Hersfeld*.

This substantially revises the production model.

The chronicle should not be reconstructed as:

`closed author manuscript → printer → book`.

The stronger model is:

`COURT COMMISSION + ARCHIVE ACCESS`
↕
`AUTHORIAL VERSIONED MANUSCRIPT APPARATUS`
↕
`GOVERNMENTAL REVISION / CENSORSHIP`
↕
`ROLLING SOURCE SUPPLY`
→ `PARTIAL PRINT STATE, 1697`

The evidentiary significance for the larger repository is considerable. Historical knowledge here was materially distributed before publication and was also **jurisdictionally governed**: courts supplied archives and biographies, reviewed the emerging work, and repeatedly intervened in its content.

This gives a political-institutional layer beneath the later taxonomic and visual coordination of historical evidence.

## 2. Fuchs's larger study makes censorship central, not incidental

Fuchs's monograph:

Thomas Fuchs, *Traditionsstiftung und Erinnerungspolitik. Geschichtsschreibung in Hessen in der Frühen Neuzeit*, Hessische Forschungen 40 (Kassel, 2002)

contains an extended treatment of the production history of Winkelmann's chronicle.

Later scholarship summarizes Fuchs pp. 246–305 as dealing in detail with:

- revisions (`Revisionen`);
- censorship (`Zensur`);
- the complicated prehistory of printing (`Vorgeschichte der Drucklegung`).

A separate modern study characterizes the process more sharply: the Hessian princes and their governments sought, through **multiple revisions**, to exert direct influence on the work's substantive/contentual shape.

Evidence status:

`STRONG SECONDARY-DIRECT SYNTHESIS OF ARCHIVAL RESEARCH`.

Current safe proposition:

> Winkelmann's Hessian chronicle was repeatedly subjected to governmental revision before publication; court participation affected the content, not merely the legal permission to print.

Do not yet reconstruct the exact passages altered, removed or demanded until the censorship dossier or Fuchs's detailed pages are directly inspected.

## 3. The chronicle was court-sponsored from the beginning

Modern reconstruction places Winkelmann in Landgrave Georg II of Hessen-Darmstadt's service by 1646 and the chronicle project by 1647.

The court did not simply wait for a completed text.

Officials, councillors and municipal authorities were instructed to assist Winkelmann and, importantly, to give him access to archival materials.

From 1652 Hessen-Kassel also became involved in the wider Hessian project.

This creates the first production layer:

`dynastic/court commission`
→ `authorized archival access`
→ `historical compilation`.

The later censorship therefore did not intervene from outside an autonomous scholarly project. The work was embedded in courtly documentary infrastructures from an early stage.

Evidence status:

`MODERN ARCHIVAL SCHOLARSHIP CONTROLLED`.

## 4. Oldenburg `Cim I 204`: authorial manuscript apparatus

The Herzog August Bibliothek manuscript description of Oldenburg `Cim I 204` controls an authentic Latin manuscript of Winkelmann's Hessian chronicle.

Important material features include:

- authorial supplementation and emendation;
- handwritten spine/title identifying `Winkelmanni rer. Hassiac. lib. I-V mss.`;
- numerous inserted / attached leaves;
- multiple textual states, including revision around Book IV;
- substantial divergence from the later German printed work.

Modern scholarship dates the first five Latin books approximately to summer 1655.

This witness demonstrates that long before the 1697 print the chronicle existed as a **versioned authorial apparatus**, not a single clean copy.

Evidence status:

`INSTITUTIONAL MANUSCRIPT DESCRIPTION + SCHOLARLY CODICOLOGICAL CONTROL`.

Working label:

`AUTHORIAL VERSIONED MANUSCRIPT LAYER`.

## 5. Darmstadt `HStAD D 4, 214/4`: rolling source supply reaches Bremen in the publication window

A Darmstadt archival record provides a different production mechanism.

The file includes a request by Passer that a biographical account (`Lebenslauf`) of Landgrave Ernst Ludwig be transmitted to the historiographer Winkelmann in Bremen for the composition of a chronicle.

The archival unit reaches into **1698**, overlapping the 1697 publication of Parts I–V.

This means source acquisition remained active in the publication window.

The production process was therefore not simply:

`manuscript complete`
→ `review`
→ `print`.

It also included:

`late / continuing biographical-documentary supply`
→ `Bremen historian`
→ `chronicle compilation / revision`.

Evidence status:

`OFFICIAL ARCHIVAL DESCRIPTION CONTROLLED`.

Working label:

`ROLLING COURT / ARCHIVE SOURCE-SUPPLY CHANNEL`.

## 6. Kassel `2° Ms. Hass. 35/1`: a third layer, governmental revision / censorship

The Fuchs citation now adds a distinct archive object:

`2° Ms. Hass. 35/1`

identified as `Zensurakten` connected to Winkelmann's chronicle.

This should not be merged into either:

- the author's manuscript witness;
- the source-supply archive.

Its analytical function is different:

`historical text / draft`
→ `court/government review`
→ `revision / censorship / publication control`.

The three archival loci therefore expose three different document-producing institutions:

| Layer | Archive / witness | Function |
|---|---|---|
| authorial composition | Oldenburg `Cim I 204` | drafting, insertion, revision, versioning |
| source acquisition | Darmstadt `HStAD D 4, 214/4` | official supply of biographical/historical material |
| review / jurisdiction | Kassel `2° Ms. Hass. 35/1` | censorship and governmental revision |

This tripartite structure is currently one of the strongest material-production controls in the repository.

## 7. Why `Zensur` must be understood broadly here

The modern synthesis of Fuchs's archival findings states that the Hessian princes and governments used repeated revisions to influence the **inhaltliche Gestaltung** of the work.

Therefore `Zensur` here should not initially be reduced to a final binary:

`permission / prohibition to print`.

The stronger working category is:

`PREPUBLICATION GOVERNMENTAL TEXTUAL JURISDICTION`.

That may include:

- review;
- correction;
- requested alteration;
- suppression;
- addition;
- dispute between author and patrons;
- negotiation between Hessen-Darmstadt and Hessen-Kassel;
- authorization or delay of printing.

Which of these operations are directly documented in `35/1` remains to be reconstructed from the dossier itself or Fuchs's detailed archival analysis.

## 8. Relation to the lost / unresolved publication-history narrative

The Oldenburg manuscript tradition separately records a work described as:

`Wunder- und leßwürdige Relation wegen Verlaufs und verhinderter Publication der Hessischen Chronik de Anno 1648 bis 1696`

whose present status is unresolved / lost in the current research chain.

The Kassel censorship dossier may preserve a documentary counterpart to the publication history narrated in that `Relation`.

This is an explicit research hypothesis, not yet a proven textual relation.

Potential reconstruction:

`author's retrospective / publication narrative`
↔
`court censorship and revision records`
↔
`source-supply and administrative correspondence`.

If the three can be aligned chronologically, the prehistory of the 1697 print could be reconstructed transaction by transaction rather than from the printed book backward.

## 9. Consequence for the Hessian-Chronicle excerpt reservoir used by Leibniz

Previous checkpoints established:

- c.1690 Leibniz already has / uses excerpts from Winkelmann's Hessian Chronicle stored `bey Geograf.`;
- c.1691 he nevertheless wishes to read the `geschriebene heßische Chronick selbst`;
- a c.1694(?) autograph collectanea cites source pagination (`p. 6`, `41`, `56`, `59`, `96` etc.);
- the later 1697 print preserves identifiable textual correspondences;
- the exact Geographica excerpt packet referred to by the 1690 note has not been recovered in the critical edition.

The censorship/source-supply findings now make a single fixed `pre-print manuscript` model even less adequate.

The source behind Leibniz's excerpts could have belonged to a **versioned, continuously revised textual ecology**.

Thus:

`stable pagination in Leibniz's source state`

does not imply

`final / closed printer's manuscript`.

The chronicle could retain pagination across revisions while still receiving new materials and undergoing governmental correction.

Current label:

`PAGINATED PREPRINT SOURCE STATE WITHIN A ROLLING / VERSIONED COMPILATION ECOLOGY`.

The repository has already reconstructed:

`source reservoir`
↔ `problem jurisdiction`
↔ `project dossier`

The Winkelmann production history now adds a prior institutional layer:

`COURT / ARCHIVE JURISDICTION`

This matters because the later visual coordination of `Historia`, `Genealogia`, `Heraldica` and historical witnesses should not be read as organizing neutral raw evidence.

The source material and historical narratives entering such systems could already be institutionally selected, supplied, revised and delayed.

A stronger long-form model is therefore:

`institutionally governed source access`
→ `versioned authorial compilation`
→ `governmental revision / censorship`
→ `distributed excerpts and problem files`
→ `taxonomic compression`
→ `visual / disciplinary coordination`.

No direct causal path from Winkelmann's censorship history to the 1726 frontispiece is claimed.

## Working proposition

> **Winkelmann's Hessian chronicle was not merely a historian's manuscript later subjected to a final censor. Its production was distributed across court commission, privileged archive access, a heavily revised authorial manuscript apparatus, continuing official source supply, and repeated governmental revision. Kassel `2° Ms. Hass. 35/1`, cited by Fuchs as the censorship dossier, supplies a specific archival locus for this jurisdictional layer. The 1697 print should therefore be treated as one output state within a long, institutionally governed and materially versioned historical-information system.**
