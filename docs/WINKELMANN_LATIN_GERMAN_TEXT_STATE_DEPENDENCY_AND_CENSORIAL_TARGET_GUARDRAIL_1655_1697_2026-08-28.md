# Winkelmann 1655–1697: Latin/German text-state dependency and the censorial-target guardrail

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE VERSION-HISTORY GUARDRAIL — OLDENBURG `Cim I 204` IS CONTROLLED AS AN AUTHORIAL VERSIONED WITNESS, BUT ITS EXACT RELATION TO THE GERMAN CENSORIAL TARGET AND 1697 PRINT REMAINS UNRESOLVED

## Result

The repository must now separate two questions that had previously sat too close together:

1. **what survives as an authorial manuscript witness?**
2. **what exact textual state did the Hessian censorship commissions review and revise?**

The modern Herzog August Bibliothek manuscript description of Landesbibliothek Oldenburg `Cim I 204` explicitly states that the dependency relation between the Latin and German versions of Winkelmann's Hessian Chronicle **has not been finally clarified**.

The catalogue records a real historiographical disagreement:

- Ludwig Wilhelm Christian von Halem (1802) treated the Latin version as the **original textual stage** and observed that it contains a surplus of material relative to the German print;
- Thomas Fuchs, at one point in his 2002 monograph, described it instead as a **planned Latin translation**.

The modern catalogue therefore does not resolve the stemma by choosing one of these positions. It preserves the problem.

This creates an essential guardrail for the censorship reconstruction:

`Oldenburg Cim I 204`

is securely:

`AUTHORIAL VERSIONED MANUSCRIPT WITNESS`

but is not yet securely:

`THE EXACT MANUSCRIPT / TEXT STATE REVIEWED BY THE 1686 COMMISSION`.

The latter relation requires item-level concordance with the Kassel censorship dossier, the princely-letter extracts in `Cim I 204`, and the German printed text.

## 1. What `Cim I 204` securely controls

The HAB description controls the following material facts:

- 553 leaves;
- an authorially supplemented and emended codex;
- inserted and pasted material;
- two copies/states of Book IV with different autograph corrections and additions;
- a substantial Latin chronicle in five books;
- fols. 543r–552v: `Extracten aus den Hochfürstl. Schreiben die hessische Chronic betreffend`;
- an originally attached `Wunder- und leßwürdige Relation wegen Verlaufs und verhinderter Publication der Hessischen Chronik de Anno 1648 bis 1696`, transferred to Darmstadt in 1941 and not located after the war.

The same description, following Fuchs p. 246, dates completion of the first five Latin books approximately to summer 1655.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY PHYSICAL / MODERN MANUSCRIPT-CATALOGUE CONTROL`.

Source:

- HAB manuscript database, Landesbibliothek Oldenburg `Cim I 204`:  
  `https://diglib.hab.de/?catalog=Bley&db=mss&hi=Thomas&id=ol-lbo-cim-i-204&list=ms`

## 2. The Latin/German direction of dependence is unresolved

The HAB catalogue states in substance that the dependency relation between the Latin and German textual versions appears not to have been definitively clarified.

It then records two different reconstructions.

### Halem 1802

Halem identified the Latin text as an **earlier/original textual stage**.

The modern catalogue further notes that the Latin version contains **more material than the German printed version**.

This creates a possible model:

`Latin authorial state`
→ `selection / translation / revision / compression`
→ `German print state`.

But the precise intermediate operations are not established by the catalogue alone.

### Fuchs 2002

The catalogue notes that Fuchs, p. 199, speaks of a **planned Latin translation**.

That would reverse the textual direction assumed by Halem.

The presence of this disagreement means the repository should not silently naturalize either stemma.

Evidence status:

`MODERN CATALOGUE-DIRECT REPORT OF SCHOLARLY STEMMATIC DISAGREEMENT`.

Working label:

`LATIN/GERMAN TEXT-STATE DEPENDENCY = UNRESOLVED`.

---

## 3. Why this matters for censorship

The repository now controls a major 1686 censorship event:

- several months;
- six censors;
- three Marburg professors and three Gießen professors;
- fundamental revision of the chronicle;
- dynastic interests plus a methodological clash over historical knowledge standards.

It would be tempting to align that event directly with `Cim I 204`.

That alignment is currently too strong.

The surviving Oldenburg codex demonstrates authorial versioning, but the following remain open:

- whether the 1686 commission reviewed Latin, German, or mixed textual materials;
- whether it reviewed a copy now lost rather than the surviving Oldenburg witness;
- whether specific corrections in `Cim I 204` respond to censorial demands;
- whether the duplicate Book IV states bracket a review episode;
- whether the princely-letter extracts at fols. 543r–552v correspond item-for-item to surviving Kassel review records;
- which transformations separate the reviewed state from the 1697 German print.

Therefore use:

`AUTHORIAL MANUSCRIPT APPARATUS ↔ CENSORIAL REVIEW REGIME`

not yet:

`Cim I 204 = CENSOR'S COPY`.

## 4. Halem 1802 is now a directly located digital target

Halem's article is directly identified in the UB Marburg digitisation of:

*Hessische Denkwürdigkeiten*, Theil 3 (Marburg, 1802):

`Nachricht von dem in der Herzogl. Bibliothek zu Oldenburg befindlichen Manuskripte der Winkelmannischen lateinischen Chronik von Hessen`.

The Marburg DFG Viewer places the start of Halem's contribution at viewer page 285; the next contribution begins at viewer page 308. This provides a bounded digital target for direct transcription/inspection.

Official digitisation:

- UB Marburg: `https://archiv.ub.uni-marburg.de/eb/2010/0263/`

Current access note:

- the article structure and page window are controlled through the DFG Viewer;
- direct page-image download through the automated research environment returned access errors;
- the article's detailed prose has therefore **not yet been used to assert the contents of the lost attached Relation**.

Evidence status:

`DIGITAL OBJECT + ARTICLE LOCATION CONTROLLED; DIRECT ARTICLE TRANSCRIPTION PENDING`.

This distinction matters because Halem may have seen the manuscript complex before later archival separation, but no claim should be made about what he says concerning the lost `Relation` until the pages themselves are inspected.

## 5. New comparison task: reconstruct the censorial target by textual deltas

Instead of asking only `what did the censors object to?`, the stronger version-history question is:

> **Which surviving or reconstructable textual state carries the changes produced by the review regime?**

The minimum comparison should use four nodes:

| node | witness | role |
|---|---|---|
| A | `Cim I 204` Latin books / internal version states | authorial manuscript apparatus |
| B | `Cim I 204`, fols. 543r–552v | author-side extracts from princely communications |
| C | Kassel `2° Ms. Hass. 35/1` | external review/censorship dossier |
| D | Bremen 1697 German print | public textual stabilization |

For every recoverable revision event, record:

`A before?`
→ `B instruction / objection?`
↔ `C external record?`
→ `A or lost intermediate response?`
→ `D printed outcome?`.

This is the route from a broad censorship history to a **textual revision stemma**.

---

## 6. Consequence for the repository's larger production model

The production history should now be written as:

`versioned authorial witnesses`
↔ `court / university review`
↔ `lost or unresolved intermediate text states`
→ `temporary German print stabilization`.

The phrase `versioned authorial manuscript apparatus` remains valid.

The phrase `the manuscript censored in 1686` should be reserved until the reviewed textual object is identified.

This is a useful methodological comparator for the frontispiece project itself. Just as:

`programme identity ≠ composition identity ≠ matrix identity ≠ impression identity`,

so here:

`work identity ≠ manuscript-witness identity ≠ censorial-target identity ≠ print-state identity`.

The parallel is methodological, not a historical influence claim.
