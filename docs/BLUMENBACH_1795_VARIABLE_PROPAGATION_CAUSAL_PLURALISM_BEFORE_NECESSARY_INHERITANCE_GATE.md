# Blumenbach 1795: variable propagation and causal pluralism before the necessary-inheritance gate

Date: 2026-08-27

## Status

This note replaces the weaker formulation that the 1795 third edition of *De generis humani varietate nativa* is merely an intermediate anthropological recasting whose relation to hereditary persistence remains unspecified.

The 1795 primary text now supports a stronger and more precise conclusion:

**Blumenbach publicly discusses intergenerational propagation, variable constancy of familial/national characters, and the possible conversion of artificial or accidental modifications into congenital form. What is still absent from the controlled 1795 passages is the later 1797 rule that necessary inheritance itself functions as the categorical threshold separating `Rasse` from `Spielart`.**

Thus the transition is not:

`no heredity → heredity`.

It is closer to:

`open propagation problem + variable constancy + plural causation → necessary inheritance promoted to categorical jurisdiction`.

Evidence status:
- 1795 primary Latin carrier directly identified in multiple public facsimiles, including the Moscow State University Zoological Museum PDF and Internet Archive/Wikimedia copy;
- search-indexed primary text exposes §§33, 39, 40 and exact printed page numbers;
- current web PDF renderer could not promote the object to an in-session screenshot despite direct PDF identification, so status is `primary_text_extracted_from_indexed_facsimile + independent primary-carrier control`, not `primary_screenshot_verified`.

## 1. §33: degeneration into varieties sits inside reproductive/formative continuity

At printed p.85, Blumenbach describes organic reproduction through the `nisus formativus` and states that plants and animals propagate their species into offspring, whether immediately or through successive stimuli and metamorphosis.

He then gives three principal ways in which the formative drive can deviate from its established direction and norm:

1. monstrous formation;
2. hybrid generation from reproductive mixture between different species;
3. `degeneratione in varietates` — degeneration into varieties properly so called.

Operational consequence:

`formative continuity / reproduction → possible deviation of formative drive → degeneration into varieties`.

Recent source-critical work by Simone De Angelis further reconstructs §33, pp.82–88, as a model in which persistent and prolonged stimuli acting through long series of generations gradually divert the formative drive and generate varieties. This recent reconstruction is useful as an interpretive control; the repo should continue to privilege the Latin primary wording for quotation-level claims.

## 2. §39, p.106: acquired-to-congenital conversion is an explicit problem

Section 39 asks whether mutilations and other artificial interventions can provide the occasion for native animal varieties.

The problem is stated in explicitly temporal and generational terms. Blumenbach asks whether deformations or mutilations inflicted accidentally or deliberately, especially when repeated through long series of generations, might in the course of time pass into something like a second nature, so that what was formerly produced by art becomes congenital conformation.

Operational reconstruction:

`artificial / accidental modification`
`→ repetition across long generations`
`→ possible temporal stabilization`
`→ possible connate conformation`.

This is not yet a rule that such conversion necessarily occurs. It is a deliberately open causal question.

## 3. Kant 1785 appears inside the 1795 propagation dispute

The note to §39 identifies Kant's 1785 *Berliner Monatsschrift* intervention as an example on the negative side of the dispute over inherited mutilations.

This matters for chronology and source use:

- Blumenbach is not merely carrying a generic idea of heredity in 1795;
- he is publicly staging a specific dispute about acquired/artificial modification, generational repetition and congenitalization;
- Kant is explicitly one of the interlocutors in that dispute.

Do not infer from this citation that Blumenbach simply accepts or rejects Kant's position wholesale.

## 4. §39, p.108: Blumenbach remains open, but uses variable familial propagation as the counter-pressure

The end of §39 is especially important.

Blumenbach says he has not yet made either the affirmative or negative position his own. He would willingly join the deniers if they could explain why peculiar conformations originally produced deliberately or accidentally could **never** be propagated to descendants, given that other `notae gentilitiae` arise from causes still unknown and are in fact propagated in families.

His examples include facial marks such as:
- nose;
- lips;
- eyebrows.

Crucially, their propagation is not described as absolutely necessary. He says such marks may persist through **more or fewer generations** and with **greater or lesser constancy**. He compares this to organic diseases and defects of speech/pronunciation that can likewise run in families.

This supplies a much more exact 1795 persistence model:

`familial / gentilitial mark → propagation across variable number of generations → greater / lesser constancy`.

The decisive phrase for modelling is therefore not `necessary inheritance`, but **variable constancy**.

## 5. §40, p.109: causal pluralism and organ-specific susceptibility are explicit methodological cautions

Section 40 immediately follows with cautions for investigating the causes of degeneration.

Blumenbach states that some causal relations are clear enough to connect effects to causes confidently. But other cases are complicated by:

- several causes acting together (`conspiratio`);
- causes acting in contrary directions (`contraria adversitas`);
- different and multiple susceptibilities or resistances of organic bodies/organs to degeneration (`pronitas` / `renisus`);
- different effects of causes depending on whether they act more immediately or mediately.

This is a direct actor-explicit causal architecture, not a modern reconstruction imposed on scattered examples.

Operationally:

`multiple causes × organ-specific susceptibility/resistance × immediate/mediate action → heterogeneous degenerative outcomes`.

This independently strengthens the repo's `stratified phenotype` and causal-under\-determination models.

## 6. §40 downstream examples: persistence itself can differ by trait and causal route

The continuation of §40 gives further indications that persistence is not treated as uniform across visible traits.

The indexed primary facsimile preserves Blumenbach's example that dark skin contracted in the torrid zone can persist under another climate more strongly than northern whiteness when transferred southward. He also assigns some persistent `gentilitial` skull forms and eye colours to more mediated causes that remain partly unknown.

Therefore the 1795 architecture is compatible with:

`trait → distinct causal route → distinct persistence behaviour`.

This is stronger than a generic statement that climate causes variation.

It implies a historical problem of **differential persistence among traits**, although the exact trait hierarchy still needs full page-by-page primary closure.

## 7. Hard revision to the 1795 intermediate-state model

The previous repository wording was:

`1795 = major anthropological recasting + named varietates + degeneration framework; persistence gate not yet controlled`.

Replace it with:

`1795 = public degeneration framework + explicit propagation problem + variable constancy + acquired-to-congenital conversion question + causal pluralism + organ/trait-specific susceptibility; no controlled necessary-inheritance category gate`.

The distinction is now:

`propagation exists ≠ propagation is necessary ≠ persistence determines category membership`.

Or in stronger modular form:

`causal propagation`
`≠ variable familial constancy`
`≠ necessary reproductive persistence`
`≠ categorical gate`.

## 8. Revised chronology, 1788–1797

### 1788 private working copy

`necessary inheritance / non-necessary inheritance → Race / Varietät` is privately recorded from Kant.

### 1790–1791 public natural history

Causal architecture of `Bildungstrieb → Ausartung / Degeneration` becomes more explicit while public human grouping remains `Spielart / Varietät`.

### 1795 third *De generis*

Hereditary/persistence questions are now public, but in a different operational form:

`propagation across generations + variable constancy + open acquired-to-congenital conversion + causal underdetermination`.

### 1797 fifth *Handbuch*

The public text finally turns reproductive persistence into a categorical threshold:

`unavoidably / necessarily inherited through generation → Rasse`
`versus`
`no such necessity → Spielart`.

The publication lag is therefore not simply silence followed by adoption. An intermediate **public propagation regime** appears before the categorical gate.

## 9. Comparison with Meiners 1790–1792

This new Blumenbach control makes the comparison with Meiners more exact.

### Shared problem-space

Both actors can operate with:
- descent / familial transmission;
- environmental or acquired modification;
- multiple causes;
- intergenerational change;
- variable persistence.

### Different inferential organization

Blumenbach 1795:

`causal uncertainty + variable propagation + organ-specific susceptibility → caution in assigning degeneration causes`.

Meiners 1790–1792:

`descent / blood given high default weight + environment / mixture / life-way remain active → weighted historical/evaluative inference`.

Blumenbach 1797 then adds a further move:

`necessary reproductive persistence → category jurisdiction`.

Thus the divergence cannot be reduced to one actor possessing heredity while the other lacks it. The relevant differences lie in **how persistence is quantified, how uncertainty is handled, and what inferential jurisdiction persistence is allowed to control**.

## 11. Evidence and source controls

Primary:
- Johann Friedrich Blumenbach, *De generis humani varietate nativa*, 3rd ed. (Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1795), especially §33 p.85, §39 pp.106–108, §40 pp.109ff.
- public facsimile carriers indexed through Moscow State University Zoological Museum and Internet Archive/Wikimedia.
- Blumenbach Online bibliography entry 00004 and the official physical-anthropology / Bildungstrieb guides.

Important source-use caution:
- Thomas Bendyshe's 1865 *Anthropological Treatises* does **not** provide a clean full translation of the 1795 third-edition body in the way it is sometimes cited. Bendyshe explicitly says he translated the 1775/1776 first-edition text as his base and attached selected later material; the 1795 table of contents, Banks letter, and collection are useful controls, but passages from the earlier translated body must not automatically be labelled `1795 primary translation`.
- Johann Gottfried Gruber's 1798 German translation follows the third edition and Blumenbach's reminders/additions, but Blumenbach Online notes contemporary criticism of its translation quality. Use it as a near-contemporary derivative witness, not a substitute for Latin when a terminological distinction matters.

## 12. Stop rules

- Do not describe heredity as absent from Blumenbach 1795.
- Do not turn Blumenbach's openness about inherited acquired modifications into an affirmative Lamarckian doctrine.
- Do not treat `propagari` as equivalent to necessary inheritance.
- Do not treat greater/lesser constancy as a binary categorical threshold.
- Do not backdate the 1797 `Rasse / Spielart` gate into 1795 merely because propagation is explicit.
- Do not assume all traits have equal persistence; §40 points in the opposite methodological direction.
- Do not use Bendyshe's first-edition translation body as if every passage represented the 1795 third edition.

## 13. Strong current formulation

> The 1795 third edition does not sit between an earlier world without heredity and the 1797 appearance of a hereditary race criterion. Blumenbach already makes intergenerational propagation a public problem. In §39 he asks whether artificial or accidental modifications repeated through long generations can become congenital, remains explicitly undecided, and points to familial and `gentilitial` characters that propagate through more or fewer generations with greater or lesser constancy. In §40 he further insists that degeneration is causally plural and organ-specific: causes may reinforce or oppose one another, while organic structures differ in susceptibility and resistance. The decisive change by 1797 is therefore not the arrival of heredity but the promotion of one stronger form of persistence — necessary reproductive inheritance — from a variable causal phenomenon into a categorical gate separating race from variety.
