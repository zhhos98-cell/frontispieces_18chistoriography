## Result

Jacob Friedrich Reimmann's genealogy work now forms a controlled three-stage sequence before the 1710 auxiliary-science bundle and the 1724 Zschackwitz source-critical apparatus:

`1702 genealogy itself becomes an object of historia literaria`
→ `1705 genealogy is subjected explicitly to sceptical method`
→ `1710 printed + manuscript / common + rare genealogical books are reorganized as a bibliographic corpus, with the scepticism essay incorporated`.

A separate correspondence control establishes that Reimmann's 1702 title entered **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz and Johann Georg Eckhart's review workflow immediately**. In a letter of 1 November 1701, Leibniz explicitly names `Reimanni de fatis Genealog. studii` among several works whose reviewing could be deferred until Leibniz was present.

This is stronger than same-field proximity but weaker than methodological adoption.

Working relation label:

`LEIBNIZ ↔ REIMMANN 1702 = DIRECT_RECEPTION / PLANNED_RECENSION_PIPELINE`.

Do **not** write that Leibniz derived his earlier library classification from Reimmann. Leibniz's controlled short classification is dated 1695–spring 1698 (?), several years earlier.

## 1. Reimmann 1702: genealogy becomes an object of *historia literaria*

Primary title:

`Jacobi Friderici Reimanni Historia Literaria de Fatis Studii Genealogicii apud Hebraeos, Graecos, Romanos & Germanos, in qua Scriptores harum gentium potissimi enumerantur & Totus Genealogiae Cursus ab orbe condito ad nostra usque tempora deducitur`.

Publication controls:

- Aschersleben / Quedlinburg;
- Gottlob Ernst Strunz;
- 1702;
- `[2] Bl., 120 S.`;
- VD18 `1462589X-001`;
- BSB digital object `bsb10208675`, 126 scans.

The title itself gives the operation:

1. genealogy is treated historically through its `fata` / fortunes;
2. Hebrew, Greek, Roman and German genealogical writers are enumerated;
3. the whole course of genealogy is traced from the creation of the world to Reimmann's present.

This is not merely a collection of pedigrees.

It is a **history and bibliography of genealogical knowledge**.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-TITLE / INSTITUTIONAL OBJECT METADATA CONTROLLED`.

Primary full-text inspection remains a queue item.

## 2. 1 November 1701: Leibniz places the Reimmann title in a review pipeline

The critical edition of Leibniz's correspondence controls a letter:

`Leibniz an Johann Georg Eckhart, Berlin, 1 November 1701`, no. 42.

In a passage discussing recent books and recension work, Leibniz says that the treatment of several titles may be deferred until he is present. The list includes:

- Canitz;
- Imhof's genealogical history;
- *Justice encouragée*;
- `Reimanni de fatis Genealog. studii`;
- Schilter on renunciations.

The editors identify the Reimmann item explicitly as:

`J. F. Reimmann, Historia literaria de fatis studii genealogici apud Hebraeos, Graecos, Romanos et Germanos, 1702`.

The key verb/context is recension, not acquisition.

Safe formulation:

> **By 1 November 1701, Reimmann's work carrying the 1702 imprint had entered Leibniz and Eckhart's planned review workflow.**

This suggests pre-imprint-year circulation or advance availability, but the repository should not infer the exact material form in Leibniz's hands from this sentence alone.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY-DIRECT CORRESPONDENCE TEXT IN CRITICAL EDITION`.

The web PDF page-image cache failed when a screenshot was requested, so visual/diplomatic page-image control remains pending. The critical-edition text itself is directly parsed and locates the passage on printed p.53.

## 3. What the Leibniz edge proves — and what it does not

It proves:

`Reimmann 1702 title`
→ known inside Leibniz/Eckhart communication
→ assigned to a recension/review queue by 1 Nov. 1701.

It does **not** yet prove:

- Leibniz actually published a review;
- Leibniz endorsed Reimmann;
- Reimmann influenced Leibniz's 1690s classification;
- Reimmann had read Leibniz's unpublished classification;
- a personal correspondence between Leibniz and Reimmann.

The chronology actually blocks one tempting influence claim:

`Leibniz short library schema = 1695–spring 1698 (?)`

precedes

`Reimmann genealogy historia literaria = 1702`.

Therefore Reimmann cannot be the source of the already-controlled short Leibniz classification unless an earlier Reimmann text is discovered.

The interesting relation is instead **later convergence/contact between two already developing classificatory programmes**.

## 5. This makes genealogical scepticism part of the Halle learned-periodical ecology

The first controlled publication context is significant.

Reimmann's genealogical scepticism did not initially appear as an isolated later handbook appendix. It appeared inside the Halle *Observationes Selectae*, a learned miscellany/review environment associated with the early Halle intellectual network.

Thus the sequence is:

`genealogical knowledge problem`
→ `periodical learned observation / sceptical intervention`
→ later incorporation into a dedicated genealogical bibliography.

This provides a useful prehistory for the repo's later review and controversy infrastructures.

Working status:

`HISTORIA-LITERARIA / REVIEW-ECOLOGY PREHISTORY`.

Do not assign the *Observationes* as a whole to a single author or turn its collaborative/mixed authorship into a unified "Halle school" without item-level control.

## 6. 1710: bibliography of common/rare, printed/manuscript, published/unpublished genealogical books

The later work is catalogued as:

`Jacobi Frid. Reimmanni Historiae literariae exotericae & acroamaticae particula sive De libris genealogicis vulgatioribus & rarioribus commentatio`.

Its title explicitly states that it reviews genealogical works that are:

- common and rare;
- printed and manuscript;
- published and unpublished;

and offers material toward assembling a:

`bibliotheca scriptorum genealogicorum`.

It then adds:

`Disquisitio historica de necessitate scepticismi in studio genealogico`.

BnF-derived catalogue control dates this assembled form to `[1710]` on the basis of the preface. Other library records expose inconsistent or misleading inferred dates for component parts; these must not override the preface-based 1710 control without direct copy collation.

## 8. Reimmann's sequence before the 1710 auxiliary-science bundle

The repo now has two distinct Reimmann 1710 operations, which should not be merged.

### A. Genealogical bibliography / scepticism

`genealogy as historically changing learned field`
→ `writers/books enumerated`
→ `printed + manuscript evidence`
→ `rare + common evidence`
→ `scepticism required`.

### B. Tomus V of *Versuch einer Einleitung in die Historiam Literariam*

`Historia Civilis`
→ `Chronologia / Geographia / Genealogia / Heraldica / Numismatica / Antiquaria` as `Neben-Wissenschafften`.

These are related but analytically different:

- one organizes **knowledge about genealogy**;
- the other organizes **genealogy as one auxiliary field around civil history**.

This double placement is highly consequential.

Genealogy is simultaneously:

`an object of historical-bibliographic criticism`

and

`an instrument/neighbor of civil history`.

## 9. Comparison with Zschackwitz 1724

Zschackwitz 1724 has now been primary-controlled as arguing that:

- genealogy depends on history, especially Reichshistorie;
- genealogies must be purged of fables;
- authors/tables can be ranked through error, certainty and trustworthiness;
- `Autores und fontes`, documents and older tables should be checked;
- Hahn is praised especially for necessary `allegata` and source-grounded testimonies.

The structural similarity to Reimmann 1702/1705/1710 is strong:

`genealogy as learned-history corpus`
+
`bibliographic/source discrimination`

## 10. Hübner 1729 is a stronger candidate for explicit bibliographic uptake

Johann Hübner the younger's *Bibliotheca genealogica* (Hamburg, 1729) presents itself as a systematically ordered catalogue of old and new genealogical books from all nations for users of political knowledge.

Modern histories of genealogical bibliography explicitly identify Reimmann's 1702 and 1710 works as predecessors of Hübner 1729, and one modern genealogical study states that Hübner followed Reimmann's path.

This is not yet enough for an actor-level `DIRECT_CITATION` edge.

Primary Hübner inspection is required to determine whether:

- Reimmann is cited in the text/index;
- Hübner names him as predecessor;
- classificatory structure is borrowed or independently reorganized.

Current status:

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC SUCCESSOR / DIRECT UPTAKE CANDIDATE`.

## 11. Consequence for the frontispiece prehistory

The pre-1726 sequence now includes a form of genealogy more epistemically demanding than a simple lineage-table tradition:

`Leibniz 1695–98 (?) library classification`
→ `Reimmann 1702 history/bibliography of genealogical study`
→ `Reimmann 1705 explicit genealogical scepticism`
→ `Reimmann 1710 genealogical bibliography + six-field civil-history classification`
→ `Gladov 1717 pragmatic Historia/Chronos`
→ `Zschackwitz 1724 genealogy/history/source criticism`
→ `1726 HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`.

This does **not** constitute one transmission chain.

It does establish that by the time the 1726 plate personifies Genealogia next to Historia, genealogy had already become:

- a bibliographically historicized field;
- an object of sceptical criticism;
- an auxiliary of civil history;
- a discipline whose books and manuscript witnesses could themselves be collected and compared.

That is a much richer baseline for interpreting the plate.
