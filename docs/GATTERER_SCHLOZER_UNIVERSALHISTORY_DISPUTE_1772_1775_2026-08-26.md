# Gatterer–Schlözer and Universalgeschichte, 1772–1775 — controlled dispute map

## Purpose

The 1773 Gatterer–Schlözer quarrel must not be reduced either to a purely intellectual controversy or to a petty competition for students. Both levels were active and became entangled.

This file separates:

1. institutional/teaching competition;
2. overlapping universal-historical programmes;
3. claims of intellectual priority/plagiarism;
4. substantive methodological differences that should be tested independently of the quarrel.

---

## I. Institutional competition is real and first-order relevant

Schlözer began teaching Universalgeschichte at Göttingen alongside Gatterer. Later biographical evidence records that Gatterer rejected a proposal that they alternate the lecture and publicly complained when Schlözer's course attracted substantially larger audiences. Since student fees mattered materially, this was also an economic competition.

Schlözer answered the public allegations through a `Species Facti`, appended to some copies of the second part of his `Vorstellung seiner Universal-Historie` (1773). Gatterer replied with the separate 48-page pamphlet:

`Antwort auf die Schlözersche Species Facti` (Göttingen: Dieterich, 1773).

Direct open copy: LMU München, MALTE, shelfmark `0014/W 8 H.lit. 2605`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:19-epub-11937-9`.

---

## II. The methodological proximity itself became contested property

Recent scholarship stresses that the programmes guiding Gatterer's and Schlözer's Universalgeschichte lectures/publications lay close enough that **both accused the other of plagiarism**.

The key first-order Schlözer locus is:

`Species Facti`, especially pp. 412–413 in the later Blanke reprint of the 1772/73 `Vorstellung` corpus.

This changes the interpretation of the quarrel. The dispute was not merely over who had the right to lecture on the same established subject. It also concerned the ownership and priority of a way of reorganising Universalgeschichte.

---

## III. What they shared

Both Göttingen projects reject universal history as a mere registry of facts and aim at a selective, pragmatic/philosophical synthesis.

Shared problem-field includes:

- selection from an already accumulated mass of historical facts;
- construction of a system/whole rather than simple annalistic succession;
- Polybian `pragmatic` history and causal connection;
- a universal-historical overview useful in teaching;
- the difficulty of coordinating ethnographic/diachronic and synchronistic relations.

Schlözer explicitly grounds the `Grund-Idee` of his plan in Polybius. Gatterer's 1767 `Vom historischen Plan` had already made pragmatic connection and the `Nexus rerum universalis` central.

Hence similarity by itself cannot be used as evidence of borrowing. The 1773 priority accusations require exact chronological/source reconstruction.

---

## IV. A useful contrast: Gatterer's construction vs Schlözer's didactic condensation

A current secondary synthesis draws a defensible working distinction.

### Gatterer

Gatterer's Universalgeschichte grows from a research architecture that remains closely integrated with the historical auxiliary sciences: chronology, geography, diplomatics, heraldry, numismatics, genealogy, statistics and source criticism. His tables, maps and compendia are part of the construction of the historical object itself.

### Schlözer

Schlözer never attempted world history primarily by carrying out new source research across the whole field. His `Vorstellung` was explicitly designed as a teaching guide: to save weeks of prolegomena, reduce students' note-taking and facilitate constant overview of the whole. His project transforms already available scholarship into a highly compressed didactic system.

This distinction must remain provisional and claim-specific. Schlözer was himself a critical historian and should not be caricatured as a mere popularizer.

---

## V. Schlözer's own visual/systematic problem is close to Gatterer's

Schlözer's mature programme demands that world history be read twice:

1. **ethnographically** — histories of peoples along their own lengths;
2. **synchronistically** — the world across its breadth.

His projected synchronistic system was never completed in full, but he deliberately sought overview, memorability and condensation through tables, maps and rounded dates.

This proximity is important for the Gatterer image project. Gatterer's colored and synchronistic tables should not be treated as evidence that only Gatterer recognized the problem of representing simultaneity. The stronger question is how Gatterer's specific graphical implementations differ from Schlözer's largely textual/didactic programme.

---

## VI. 1773 is therefore a collision of three economies

The quarrel can be modelled as the intersection of:

### 1. Economy of teaching
Who gets the Universalgeschichte audience, fees and curricular space?

### 2. Economy of method
Who can claim priority for the `Plan`, selection and synthesis of universal history?

### 3. Economy of media
What counts as an adequate instrument for keeping the whole before students/readers: prose outline, handbook, table, map, double ethnographic/synchronistic reading, or a combination?

The third economy is especially important for this project and should be reconstructed without assuming that the 1773 polemic itself explicitly centres visual media.

## VII. Immediate source work

1. Read Schlözer's `Species Facti`, pp. 401–416, especially 412–13, against Gatterer's 48-page `Antwort`.
2. Extract every claim about chronology of lectures/publications and intellectual priority.
3. Mark sentences that concern **content/method** separately from those concerning **professional conduct, audience and fees**.
4. Compare those priority claims against:
   - Gatterer, `Vom historischen Plan` (1767);
   - Gatterer, `Synopsis` (1766/69);
   - Gatterer, `Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie` (1771);
   - Schlözer, `Vorstellung seiner Universal-Historie` (1772/73).
5. Do not infer methodological difference from polemical self-description without checking the works themselves.

## Current judgment

The 1773 controversy belongs in the project, but as **context for contested historical architecture**, not as the automatic explanatory centre of the images. Its strongest present contribution is to show that arranging Universalgeschichte had become a recognizable and professionally valuable methodological asset.
