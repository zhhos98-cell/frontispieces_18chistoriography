# Gatterer: division of historical labour among plan, table, map and narrative — 26 August 2026

## Problem

A recurrent mistake in reading Gatterer's visual works would be to assume that tables and maps simply illustrate what prose history already says. His methodological texts and visual practice instead suggest a more differentiated allocation of historical operations among media.

This note records the strongest current formulation while keeping the distinction between direct statement and analytical reconstruction explicit.

## 1. Direct rule for pragmatic narrative

In `Vom historischen Plan, und der darauf sich gründenden Zusammenfügung der Erzählungen` (1767), Gatterer rejects several mechanical principles for arranging historical narration:

`Man darf also den Plan zur Erzählung der Begebenheiten nicht nach einer geographischen Ordnung, auch nicht nach einzelnen Jahren, und noch weniger nach gewissen Classen der Begebenheiten machen, sondern man ordnet nach Systemen; die Ursachen gehen voran, die Wirkungen folgen, und der Geschichtsschreiber, der so verfährt, ist pragmatisch.`

The rule is unusually clear. Finished pragmatic narration should **not** be organized simply:

- geographically;
- year by year;
- by classes/categories of events.

Instead it should be organized by **systems**, with causes preceding effects.

## 2. Yet Gatterer's visual instruments deliberately use the orders prose rejects

### Chronological/synchronistic tables

The `Synopsis` and later synchronistic tables visibly use temporal juxtaposition and segmented time. They allow events and political entities to be placed beside one another in common chronological fields.

### Classified matrices

At least one `Synopsis` table organizes material by classes such as state history, church history and `historia literaria`, subdivided by centuries.

### Maps

Historical maps necessarily organize material spatially/geographically; Gatterer's own map practice uses the spatial persistence of rivers, mountains, seas and lakes to compare changing historical divisions.

Thus:

`year / chronology`
`class / category`
`geography / space`

are not illegitimate historical orders in themselves. They are specifically inadequate as the governing order of **pragmatic prose narration**.

## 3. Working reconstruction: complementary rather than contradictory media

The strongest current analytical model is:

### Tables/maps externalize relations that need to remain simultaneously surveyable

They can preserve:
- simultaneity;
- temporal duration;
- spatial position;
- categorical coexistence;
- changing political relations;
- cross-temporal map comparison.

### Pragmatic prose performs causal/systemic recomposition

The writer selects from and reorders historically available material according to systems of causes and consequences.

This produces a possible division of labour:

`table / map = surveyability of coexistence, position and classification`
→ `historical Plan = selection and assignment of relevance`
→ `pragmatic narrative = causal/systemic sequence`.

## 4. Why this matters for the article

If this reconstruction holds, Gatterer's images are neither decorative illustration nor embryonic prose narratives. Their methodological value partly depends on their ability to use orders that prose, when written pragmatically, should not imitate.

This yields a sharper question than “How did Gatterer visualize history?”:

> **Which relations did Gatterer keep spatial, tabular or classificatory because turning them directly into narrative order would fragment the historical system?**

## 5. Relation to `Evidenz`

This division also prevents several forms of evidence from collapsing into one another:

- documentary plate → source warrant / `historische Demonstration`;
- table/map → overview, placement and comparison;
- pragmatic prose → causal relation and systemic synthesis;
- vivid narration → `ideale Gegenwart`, reader as `Zuschauer`.

A reader may move among these operations, but no single one substitutes for all the others.

## 6. Evidentiary status

### Directly stated by Gatterer

- pragmatic narration should not be ordered by geography, individual years or event classes;
- it should be ordered by systems, causes and effects;
- historical `Plan` governs the placement and joining of selected historical material.

### Directly visible in surviving Gatterer apparatus

- synchronistic chronological disposition;
- categorical columns/matrices;
- colored power-duration diagrams;
- historical-geographical comparison.

### Analytical reconstruction requiring further testing

- that Gatterer consciously theorized these media as an explicit division of labour;
- that he understood tables/maps as deliberately preserving orders forbidden to prose.

Until a direct statement is found, write the latter as an analytical consequence of juxtaposing his methodological rule and his media practice, not as actor-language.
