# 1747 Wappenkalender owner-augmentation witness — 26 August 2026

A historical antiquarian catalogue preserves an unusually strong copy-level witness for the active remaking of a Weigel Wappenkalender after publication.

## Source

Richard Bertling, *Genealogie und Heraldik, Geschichte*, Lager-Katalog Nr. 74 (Dresden, 1912), item 951.

The catalogue describes:

- *Geschichte-, Geschlechts- u. Wappen-Calender. Der Durchlauchtigen Welt, auf das Jahr 1747*;
- Nürnberg, Christoph Weigel;
- large octavo;
- `Mit Titelkpfr.`;
- many heraldic illustrations on **106 engraved plates**;
- parchment binding.

The catalogue then adds a copy-history note. A previous owner, identified by an inscription on the front flyleaf as **`A. W. von Arnstedt`** (reading preserved exactly as catalogued), had added **more than one hundred separate aristocratic coats of arms**, themselves eighteenth-century copper engravings, all named, cut out and **lightly attached to the backs of the existing plates**.

## Why this matters

This is not ordinary loss, rebinding or accidental incompleteness. The owner deliberately enlarged the book's visual comparison set.

The resulting material sequence is:

`published Wappenkalender with 106 engraved plates`
→ `owner collects >100 additional heraldic engravings`
→ `names the added arms`
→ `mounts them on the versos of the existing plates`
→ `one bound copy becomes a substantially enlarged heraldic reference assemblage`.

The copy therefore does not simply preserve an edition. It records a later act of evidentiary compilation.

This strengthens several corpus rules:

1. **copy history can change the available comparison set**;
2. `plate count in a surviving copy` is not automatically `publisher-issued plate count`;
3. later augmentation must be distinguished from publisher-level plate insertion and from missing leaves;
4. a Wappenkalender could function as an expandable armorial dossier rather than a closed annual object;
5. versos and interleaving/mounting surfaces are part of the historical use of the visual apparatus, not empty bibliographic space.

## Relation to other copy-variation controls

The 1747 witness complements three different mechanisms already present in the corpus:

- **production variation:** Köhler's reported 1734 statement that five Helmers copies did not agree in the constitution and number of arms because plates were altered, filled and supplemented;
- **later recombination:** the Darabanth *Bequemes Hand-Buch* copy whose original frontispiece/title were missing and which received a foreign frontispiece plus calendar leaves;
- **serial-to-cumulative recomposition:** the 1771 Seyfart preface stating that Wappencalender arms were used as the basis of the Wappenbuch and successive divisions issued annually with the calendar.

These are not the same process and should remain separately typed:

`publisher alters plate stock`
≠ `owner augments a bound copy`
≠ `later binder inserts foreign material`
≠ `publisher extracts/reissues apparatus across publication forms`.

## Evidence status

`verified_primary_catalogue_description` — the 1912 catalogue is a historical description of the then-physical copy. The present location of that augmented 1747 volume has not yet been identified, so the mounted additions have not been independently image-inspected.

Do not infer that other 1747 copies contained the added arms.
