# Gatterer: maps as historical operations, 1766–1775 — 26 August 2026

## Scope

This note treats maps inside the corrected Gatterer-first project. The question is not whether geography was a conventional auxiliary science, but **what historical work a map was licensed to perform** in Gatterer's Göttingen environment.

## 1. December 1766: charter → historical geography → map → demonstration

A Göttingen historical prize question announced in the *Göttingische Anzeigen von gelehrten Sachen* in December 1766 requested:

> an exact geographical description of the Saxon lands in the times of Henry the Proud and Henry the Lion, together with the division under the sons of the latter, **from reliable charters**, in **complete maps**, with their **demonstration**.

Literal task wording preserved in the contemporary notice:

`eine genaue Erdbeschreibung der Sächsischen Lande ... aus zuverlässigen Urkunden, in vollständigen Charten, mit ihrer Demonstration.`

The chain is methodologically explicit:

`reliable charters`
→ `historical-geographical reconstruction`
→ `complete maps`
→ `Demonstration`.

This is strong institutional evidence that the map could be an output of critical historical reconstruction and part of its proof, not merely a didactic illustration appended after the research was complete.

The date matters. The prize question appears in the same December 1766 Göttingen environment in which Gatterer's `Synopsis historiae universalis` and the institutional programme for historical study are being consolidated.

## 2. 1768: Gatterer's `Versuch über die Landkarten`

Gatterer's own map-method text is bibliographically fixed:

`Versuch über die Landkarten`, printed as the preface to volume 32 of the German continuation of the *Allgemeine Welthistorie* (Halle, 1768).

Later Gatterer bibliographies separately list this essay and distinguish it from his subsequent geographical prefaces.

The full preface still requires direct collation before detailed quotation. Do not reconstruct its argument from later summaries alone.

## 3. 1769: stable physical geography as a cross-temporal comparison grid

In Gatterer's review of d'Anville's abridged ancient geography, the methodological operation becomes concrete. To compare old and new geographical divisions, one should retain especially those natural boundaries that change least: the locations of major rivers and mountain chains, together with seas and lakes.

The point is not simply greater locational accuracy. Stable natural geography provides a comparison frame across changing historical partitions.

Operationally:

`relatively stable rivers / mountains / seas / lakes`
→ `anchor old political-geographical divisions`
→ `locate them on newer maps`
→ `project newer divisions onto older maps`
→ `compare historical spatial states`.

For ancient Germany, the Rhine, Vistula, Baltic, Danube and Alps supply such boundaries.

This is a genuinely historical use of cartography: the map makes **two different temporal states of spatial organization comparable in one operation**.

## 4. 1771: the operation enters synchronistic Universalgeschichte

Gatterer's `Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie` repeats the requirement that the historian learn to find old countries on modern maps and modern divisions on old maps in order to see correctly in ancient and modern history.

Map comparison therefore belongs inside the same universal-historical architecture as his synchronistic tables. Geography does not merely precede narrative as factual preliminaries; it supplies a controlled spatial relation among changing historical units.

## 5. 1775: geography itself becomes historical

In the `Abriss der Geographie` (1775), Gatterer insists that every event has both a time and a place, and uses longitude/latitude as a precise locational framework. His geographical programme aims at the true form of the earth and its inhabitants **in every age**.

This is stronger than the commonplace `geography + chronology = eyes of history`. Geography itself is temporally indexed:

`earth / inhabitants / boundaries at t1`
≠ `earth / inhabitants / boundaries at t2`.

The historian therefore requires operations for reconstructing and comparing spatial states through time.

## 6. Relation to the image-method corpus

Maps occupy a distinct register from the other Gatterer images:

- genealogical/document plates → reproduce particular artifacts as testimony;
- synchronistic tables → arrange duration, simultaneity, categories and systems;
- maps → reconstruct and compare spatial states across time;
- diplomatic graphic comparison → diagnose formal traits;
- pragmatic prose → narrate causal systems.

The commonality is not generic visuality. Each medium stabilizes a different relation that ordinary sequential prose handles poorly.

## 7. Current article-level hypothesis

The strongest current formulation is:

> Gatterer's visual apparatus redistributed historical operations across media. A map could preserve and compare changing spatial divisions by anchoring them to more stable physical features; a synchronistic table could make temporal coexistence and classification surveyable; pragmatic narrative could then reorganize selected material into systems of causes and effects.

This must be tested against the full 1768 `Versuch über die Landkarten` before being treated as final.
