# Martin Tyroff before Weigel-firm incorporation, c.1726–1729 — bounded control, 26 August 2026

## Status

**Enough evidence now exists to separate Tyroff's activity as an engraver from his later position inside the Weigel family firm. A precise signed 1727/28 plate has not been located in the present web pass, but independent institutional evidence places his engraving activity in a window beginning around 1726, while British Museum/DSI authority data place his work for / incorporation into the Weigel firm from 1729. Further trawling of the 852-page 1727 *Acta physico-medica* is low-information unless a signature index emerges.**

## 1. Independent engraving activity around 1726

Royal Collection Trust `RCIN 607241`, a portrait of George Frederick Charles, Margrave of Brandenburg-Bayreuth, is catalogued as:

- printmaker: Martin Tyroff;
- after Johann Andreas Brendel;
- engraving;
- date range: **c.1726–35**;
- plate approximately 14.8 × 7.7 cm.

This does not prove a literal 1726 execution date. It does establish an institutional object whose production window begins around the same time as the earliest currently located target Clio witness.

Austrian National Library-derived image aggregation describes the same print with the range `1726/1735`, consistent with the Royal Collection dating.

## 2. Firm integration belongs to 1729

British Museum authority records and DSI agree on the key structural date:

- Tyroff married Barbara Sibylla Weigel in **1729**;
- thereafter he became part of the Weigel family publishing/engraving infrastructure;
- DSI summarizes his relation as `Worked for Weigel 1729–58`.

Therefore any Tyroff engraving associated with the target composition in a 1726 host should be described, if confirmed, as **pre-firm / external collaboration or commission**, not as work executed after he already controlled the Weigel firm.

## 3. *Acta physico-medica* bounded check

DSI lists Tyroff among collaborators/illustrators associated with the Nürnberg *Acta physico-medica Academiae Caesareae Leopoldino-Carolinae Naturae Curiosorum* and gives the serial span `1727–54`.

Independent bibliographic control confirms volume 1 appeared at Nürnberg in 1727 and survives in full digital copies (BHL; Google Play, 820/852-page carriers).

The current bounded search did **not** locate an item-level 1727 or 1728 plate with a directly searchable `Tyroff fec./sc.` signature. The DSI date range may refer to the publication span rather than Tyroff's first contribution date.

Evidence rule:

- use the *Acta* as evidence that Tyroff belongs to this scientific-illustration production ecology;
- do not cite `1727` as the date of a specific Tyroff plate unless one is directly identified.

## 4. Consequence for the Clio problem

The current safe sequence is:

`Tyroff independently active as engraver by a window beginning c.1726`
→ `target Clio composition visible in a 1726 Wappenkalender host`
→ `Tyroff marries into / works for the Weigel firm from 1729`
→ `Köhler–Müller–Tyroff historical-image collaboration directly documented by 1733`
→ `1740 target frontispiece catalogued by BM as Tyroff after Gabriel Müller`.

This makes a pre-1729 Tyroff role in the target plate **production-historically plausible**, while the actual 1726 signature remains the decisive test (`M023`).

## 5. Stop rule

Do not continue page-by-page searching of the 1727 *Acta physico-medica* merely to obtain an earlier exact Tyroff date. Reopen only if:

- an engraver index appears;
- a specific plate is surfaced by another catalogue;
- or the 1726 Clio signature makes the pre-firm collaboration question decisive at item level.
