# Gatterer: surrogate–original pedagogy in Pütter, 1765–1788 — 26 August 2026

## Status

**Primary text controlled through the full OCR of Johann Stephan Pütter's Göttingen university history, with stable public carriers in e-rara and Google Books.** The evidence supports a stronger model than `originals were preferred to reproductions`: Gatterer's teaching repeatedly moves from printed/engraved surrogates to representative originals and then back to the interpretation of future surrogates. The operational result is a **surrogate–original feedback loop** in which acquaintance with material originals trains the reader to extract more reliable information from copies.

Primary carriers:

- Johann Stephan Pütter, *Versuch einer academischen Gelehrten-Geschichte von der Georg-Augustus-Universität zu Göttingen*, continuation through 1788, e-rara full-text and page-image carrier;
- Google Books full-view carrier `QXOxp3GD7w4C`.

The central wording belongs to Pütter's 1788 description of Gatterer's teaching practice.

## I. Heraldry: engraving → handled object → blazoning → historical/critical judgment

Pütter describes Gatterer's historical encyclopedia as an integrated course across the historical auxiliary sciences.

In heraldry the sequence is materially explicit:

1. students first learn the principal terms from engravings in the compendium;
2. coats of arms from the teacher's heraldic cabinet are then literally put `in die Hände` of the students;
3. students practice blazoning themselves;
4. the exercise proceeds to historical explanation and critical assessment.

The pedagogical object is therefore not simply an illustration of verbal rules. The engraving supplies an initial classificatory vocabulary; handling/inspection of material examples then trains recognition and judgment.

Operational sequence:

`engraved scheme`
→ `handled heraldic object / materially controlled example`
→ `student blazoning`
→ `historical explanation / criticism`.

---

## II. Diplomatics: the strongest primary formulation — copies become `gleichsam originalisirt`

Pütter's description of diplomatics is especially important.

The students first read **engravings of diplomas**. Then, for each type or class, a corresponding original is shown. Pütter explains why: much cannot be learned from engravings alone.

The decisive methodological consequence follows immediately. Once students have examined originals, the engraved, printed or handwritten copies they have already read or may encounter in future are, as Pütter puts it, **`gleichsam originalisirt`**.

The point should not be paraphrased as a simple hierarchy:

`original > reproduction`.

A more accurate sequence is:

`engraved diploma`
→ `representative original of the same type`
→ `material discrimination / calibrated perception`
→ `later engraved, printed, or handwritten copy`
→ `copy can be read as if materially thickened by prior experience of originals`.

This is a pedagogical feedback circuit. The original does not make the surrogate dispensable; it trains the scholar to read surrogates more competently when originals are unavailable.

For the article this is one of the strongest eighteenth-century formulations yet found for **trained surrogate reading**.

---

## III. Numismatics: engravings, originals, and casts are functionally combined

Pütter's account of numismatic teaching again refuses a strict original/copy binary. Principal types of money and medals are treated through **Kupferstiche and Originalien**, drawing from Gatterer's holdings, the university museum, and the historical institute.

The 1765 description of the institute's projected numismatic cabinet makes the functional criterion explicit: historical usefulness matters more than the precious-metal value of the specimen. The cabinet may therefore contain casts in tin or lead, while still seeking originals of important types.

Safe operational model:

`original coin / medal`
+
`engraved representation`
+
`cast surrogate`
→ `historical comparison and classification`.

A surrogate is acceptable when it preserves the feature required by the historical task.

---

## IV. Genealogy and universal history: Evidenz and synoptic materialization sit in the same teaching ecology

Pütter's 1788 teaching description also links genealogy to proof relations that should be `diplomatisch-gewiß und evident`. This aligns with Gatterer's 1769 formulation of genealogical Evidenz as the rapidly inspectable agreement between proposition and proof passage, but the two sources should remain distinct.

At the universal-historical scale, Pütter says revolutions are not merely narrated verbally. Gatterer's students encounter:

- genealogical country tables;
- painted synchronistic tables;
- specially illuminated planiglobes.

Thus the teaching system delegates different relations to different media:

`object/surrogate inspection → trained discrimination`

`genealogical proof arrangement → warrant inspectability`

`synchronistic tables / planiglobes → world-historical overview`.

This strengthens the repository's `media division of labour` model.

## V. 1765 → 1788: the cabinet grows from a mixed teaching collection into a large comparison apparatus

Pütter's 1765 account already describes Gatterer's cabinet as heterogeneous:

- principal seal types;
- monograms;
- chancery signs;
- chrismons;
- alphabets and scripts;
- writing instruments;
- original charters;
- engraved and drawn charter surrogates.

About twenty particularly useful old original charters were separately entrusted for teaching from the royal-electoral archive.

By 1788 Pütter describes a much larger practical apparatus:

- more than **500 original diplomas**;
- several thousand engraved diplomas;
- many thousands of seals represented as engravings, drawings, and originals;
- older archival originals supplied for teaching when useful.

The expansion is not simply quantitative. It creates a sufficiently dense comparative field in which types, variants, attachment practices, materials, and reproduction conventions can be learned through repeated juxtaposition.

---

## VI. Deliberately damaged seals: teaching the material conditions of evidence

One of Pütter's most revealing details is that some seals in the teaching apparatus were deliberately damaged or opened so that students could inspect features otherwise hidden:

- modes of attachment;
- impression technique;
- internal/substrate material;
- features relevant to detecting fraud.

This is not preservation in the modern museum sense. The teaching object is partially sacrificed to expose the material structure required for expert judgment.

The sequence is:

`historical object`
→ `controlled intervention / opening`
→ `hidden production features become visible`
→ `fraud/authenticity judgment becomes teachable`.

This belongs beside the repository's reproduction-error genealogy: material evidence can be altered in order to make another evidentiary property inspectable.

---

## VII. `Cabinet` is methodological infrastructure, not decorative collection

Pütter states in the practical diplomatics discussion that anyone wishing to teach diplomatics usefully needs a diplomatic cabinet `unumgänglich nöthig`.

That claim changes the status of collection-building. The cabinet is not merely a prestige display or a storehouse from which examples happen to be drawn. It is infrastructure required to produce trained perception.

A usable teaching cabinet must support:

- type comparison;
- original/surrogate calibration;
- recognition of material construction;
- exposure to variants;
- practice in reading and classifying;
- fraud/authenticity diagnosis.

This is a stronger category than `object-based teaching`.

---

## VIII. Relation to the earlier Heumann/Köhler provenance

The provenance correction elsewhere in the repository shows that Gatterer's apparatus combines material from Heumann, Köhler, Gatterer's own acquisitions, archive loans, and institutional gifts.

Pütter's teaching description explains why such heterogeneous provenance could be methodologically productive. The collection's epistemic value lies in the **relations it enables across classes of objects and surrogates**, not in a single pristine provenance.

Thus:

`multi-source collection provenance`
→ `organized comparison apparatus`
→ `trained surrogate/original discrimination`.

Do not infer that the teaching method itself was simply inherited from Heumann or Köhler. The concrete inherited materials and the later pedagogical arrangement are separate historical questions.

---

## IX. Article-level consequence

A better formulation than `Gatterer privileged originals` is:

> **Gatterer's teaching used originals to calibrate the interpretation of surrogates, so that printed, engraved, drawn and handwritten copies could subsequently carry more information for a trained observer than they did for an untrained reader.**

This gives the project a precise operation:

### `trained surrogate re-reading`

The object and its reproduction are not rival media. They alternate within a pedagogical circuit.

This also helps connect several previously separate cases:

- Eccard's engraved figures as portable comparison objects;
- Sagittarius/Schlegel reproductions whose errors required expert correction;
- Gatterer's diplomatic reproduction-error genealogy;
- 1769 proposition–proof adjacency;
- 1771 object pedagogy;
- 1799 diagnostic graphic comparison.

The common problem is **how absent or transformed evidence becomes inspectable without pretending that mediation disappears**.

---

## X. Evidence guard

1. `gleichsam originalisirt` describes the epistemic effect of training on the reading of copies; it does not mean copies literally become originals.
2. Do not reduce Pütter's method to `originals are more reliable`. Originals are used to calibrate and enrich later surrogate reading.
3. Casts, drawings, engravings and originals have different functional adequacies; do not collapse them into one generic category of image.
4. Pütter is describing Gatterer's teaching practice, not necessarily giving Gatterer's own exact theoretical vocabulary for every step.
5. The 1765 and 1788 collection sizes/descriptions represent different temporal states; do not back-project the 1788 apparatus into 1765.
6. Archive loans and institute-owned gifts must remain distinct from objects privately owned by Gatterer.
