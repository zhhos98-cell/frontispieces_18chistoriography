# Where may colour enter history? Schlözer's coloured facts and Gatterer's coloured tables, 1766–1772

Date: 2026-08-27

## 1. Why this changes the colour branch

The first colour-method sweep treated colour as a family of operations: encoding, decoding, copy finishing, relational syntax and classification. Schlözer's *Vorstellung seiner Universal-Historie* (1772) now adds a more precise actor-level problem:

> **The methodological issue may be not whether history is coloured, but where in the epistemic chain colouring is allowed to occur.**

This is stronger than a generic visuality argument because Schlözer himself uses colour while defining the legitimate work of historical method.

## 2. Schlözer, 1772: coloured tesserae, coloured facts, and historical method

Google Books exposes the key passage at pp. 44–45 of the first volume of Schlözer's *Vorstellung seiner Universal-Historie*.

Schlözer compares individual historical facts/events to the small coloured stones of mosaic painting. A skilled artist distributes and orders them so that a complete `Gemälde` appears to the eye. He then distinguishes the critical recovery of facts from their historiographical arrangement.

The crucial parenthetical criticism is brief but methodologically dense: the `Voltaires` make the facts themselves **or at least colour them** (`färben sie wenigstens`).

He then states that `Zusammenstellung` is the work of the historian and, in the continuation, that the special manner of this arrangement constitutes the `Methode der Universalhistorie`.

A minimal reconstruction is:

`Annalen / Denkmäler`
→ `Kritik extracts Facta`
→ `Facta remain evidentially constrained`
→ `historian selects / distributes / orders`
→ `Zusammenstellung`
→ `system / whole / Gemälde`.

The illicit operation is located earlier:

`Factum`
→ `made or coloured by historian/philosophical writer`
→ `evidentiary corruption / tendentious alteration`.

Thus colour is already functioning as an epistemological metaphor that distinguishes **composition** from **alteration of evidence**.

## 3. The key distinction: coloured facts versus colour in arrangement

This allows a sharper vocabulary.

### A. `colour_at_fact_layer`

Colour changes or embellishes what is claimed as a fact. In Schlözer's Voltaire aside this is suspect because criticism is supposed to recover facts from annals and monuments rather than manufacture or tint them.

### B. `colour_at_arrangement_layer`

Colour is applied after facts have been selected/criticised in order to expose relations among them. It can be redundant, diagrammatic or mnemonic without changing the underlying factual propositions.

### C. `colour_at_surrogate_layer`

Colour or a colour-code attempts to transmit a property of an evidentiary object or representation. Its legitimacy depends on whether the translation is source-grounded, decodable and sufficiently precise.

The existing corpus contains examples of all three problems.

## 4. Gatterer's `Synopsis`: literal pigment at the arrangement/display layer

Gatterer's *Synopsis historiae universalis* (1766; second edition 1769) is literally a coloured historical instrument. Two tables are described as `coloribusque illustratae`, although surviving copies vary because pigment was applied by hand.

Araújo 2015 already establishes the relevant prior art:

- the tables address the representation of historical simultaneity;
- line geometry gives durations and relations a stable diagrammatic form;
- pigment highlights political-cultural boundaries and major historical `revolutions`;
- coloured and uncoloured copies coexist;
- pigment is therefore cognitively useful without being fully invariant across copies.

The important comparison with Schlözer is not:

`Gatterer likes colour / Schlözer distrusts colour`.

It is:

`Schlözer: do not colour the Facta`
versus
`Gatterer: colour the displayed relations among historically organised units`.

On present evidence, Gatterer's pigment belongs mainly to the **arrangement/display layer**, not to the fabrication of the underlying facts.

## 5. A possible shared methodological boundary

This suggests a historically grounded distinction between:

`evidentiary content`
≠ `historiographical arrangement`
≠ `representational aid`.

Colour may be evaluated differently at each level.

A provisional pipeline is:

`source/object`
→ `criticism`
→ `fact/proposition`
→ `selection`
→ `arrangement / system`
→ `representation / overview`
→ `reader inspection`.

Colour operations can enter at several points:

- source/object colour may itself be evidence;
- monochrome code may transmit source/object colour;
- colouring a factual proposition may be distortion;
- colour in a table may make relations easier to inspect;
- colour in a copy may vary without changing the underlying line-based information;
- colour categories may classify bodies/territories/objects.

The methodological question becomes:

> **At which point does colour add inspectability without adding unsupported factual content?**

This is a much more precise form of `colour as method`.

## 6. Gatterer's heraldry creates a second test of the same boundary

A secondary transcription attributes to Gatterer's *Abriß der Heraldik* (1774), p. 12, an unusually relevant evaluation of Rink's additional hatch signs for `Naturfarbe` and `Eisenfarbe`.

Gatterer reportedly regards the first sign as superfluous but the second as instructive because he had learned from old German `Wappenbriefe` that heralds distinguished or used the relevant iron/white/silver terminology in a historically specific way. He also points readers toward a Köhler programme on the origin of heraldic hatchings.

If direct page inspection confirms the transcription, this is a very strong methodological instance:

`graphic colour code`
→ evaluated against `historical documentary usage`
→ code retained/rejected according to source warrant.

Here colour notation is legitimate not because the convention is elegant, but because its categories can be tested against historical evidence.

This would place heraldic colour coding between source criticism and surrogate design.

## 7. The 1772 Wappenbuch retrospective shows failure at the encoding layer

The Weigel/Raspe `Vorbericht` directly diagnoses older letter-coded tinctures as producing `Unordnung und Mißverstand` while also complaining about figures too small for clarity, insufficiently determinate blazon, lack of space for descriptions and changes in arms over time.

This is exactly the sort of case the new pipeline can describe.

The problem is not that colour information should be excluded. The problem is that a particular translation layer fails:

`semantic tincture`
→ `letter code`
→ `reader decoding`

becomes unreliable under conditions of compression and weak explanation.

The subsequent Wappenkalender/cumulative apparatus can therefore be studied as attempts to relocate chromatic information into more redundant channels:

`hatching + centralized decoder + prose blazon + optional pigment`.

## 8. Schlözer's mosaic metaphor is prior art, but the colour-location question remains open

André de Melo Araújo's 2015 chapter on Schlözer already analyzes the mosaic-painting analogy as part of the transition from an aggregate of special histories to a system of Universalgeschichte. It therefore cannot be presented as a newly discovered metaphor.

What remains more specific is the **joint reading of two meanings of colouring within the same methodological field**:

1. `farbichte Steinchen` — coloured units arranged into a whole;
2. `Facta färben` — illegitimate colouring/alteration of the facts themselves.

Placed beside Gatterer's physically coloured synchronistic tables and heraldic colour codes, this creates a new question about the **location of legitimate chromatic intervention in historical knowledge production**.

A targeted historiographical search has not yet located a study making this exact comparison.

## 9. Relation to the Gatterer–Schlözer controversy

The 1773 dispute is already controlled as a conflict over teaching, priority, plan, selection and the architecture of Universalgeschichte. It would be too strong to say that colour itself was a subject of the quarrel.

But Schlözer's 1772 method text now makes the colour branch much closer to the controversy than before.

The two programmes can be tested around a common sequence:

`Facta`
→ `selection`
→ `Zusammenstellung / Plan`
→ `overview of the whole`.

Schlözer explicitly defines the special form of `Zusammenstellung` as method and uses a mosaic/painter metaphor to describe it. Gatterer's tables materially externalize analogous ordering problems and sometimes add pigment to the graphic arrangement.

The defensible question is therefore:

> **Did the dispute over Universalhistorie also involve different judgments about where composition should remain conceptual/textual and where it could be delegated to material graphical operations?**

Colour is one exceptionally discriminating case within that broader media question.

## 10. A stronger formulation of the user's colour-method problem

The colour branch can now be formulated in three nested levels.

### Level 1: colour as information
How is chromatic information encoded, transmitted and reconstructed?

### Level 2: colour as operation
What does colour make easier to distinguish, compare, verify, remember or survey?

### Level 3: colour as epistemic jurisdiction
**Which layer of historical knowledge is allowed to be coloured?**

- source/object?
- fact/proposition?
- classification?
- arrangement?
- surrogate?
- explanatory visualization?
- copy finishing?

This third level is the most promising because it converts colour from a visual feature into a question about the jurisdiction and limits of historical method.

## 12. Current judgment

This is the first point in the colour branch where the eventual Göttingen controversy ceases to be merely an external endpoint.

Schlözer's own methodological vocabulary already makes `colouring` a boundary problem between fact and composition. Gatterer's historical practice meanwhile gives colour a literal material role in composition/overview and a source-testable coding role in heraldry.

The strongest working proposition is therefore:

> **The late-Enlightenment problem of historical colour concerned not only representation or perception, but the epistemic location at which difference could legitimately be added, encoded or made visible without corrupting the fact.**

This remains a working proposition pending the direct primary checks above.
