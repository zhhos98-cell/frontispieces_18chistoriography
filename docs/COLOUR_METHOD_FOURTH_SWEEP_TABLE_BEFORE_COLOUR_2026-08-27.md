# Colour as method — fourth sweep: the table before colour

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: active synthesis

## 1. Main change

The colour branch now needs to distinguish not only epistemic locations of colour but **successive knowledge operators**.

The decisive correction is:

> **The table has already done something to history before colour arrives.**

Colour cannot be evaluated as though it were applied to raw historical reality. In the Göttingen cases now under control, historical information has already passed through criticism, categorisation, commensuration, selection and graphical disposition before pigment or illumination makes a relation more salient.

## 2. Operator sequence

Current working sequence:

`source / report / object`
→ `criticism`
→ `Factum / warranted proposition`
→ `category / measure / commensuration`
→ `selection`
→ `Zusammenstellung / Plan / System`
→ `table / graphical disposition`
→ `colour / illumination / hatching`
→ `inspection / association / memory`
→ possible `copy-specific material state`.

This sequence is analytical. It is not claimed as one actor's explicit universal pipeline.

Every edge requires separate source control.

## 3. Distinct operators and failure modes

### `critical_operator`
Transforms documents, monuments or reports into critically constrained factual propositions.

Failure:
- invention;
- weak source criticism;
- rhetorical colouring of the Factum.

Schlözer's 1772 `Facta färben` warning is the negative control.

### `commensuration_operator`
Creates a common axis on which heterogeneous units can be compared.

Failure:
- false common measure;
- anachronistic developmental scale;
- categories that erase historical difference.

Herder's `Wo ist Maaß? Wo sind Data zum Maaße in so verschiednen Zeiten und Völkern?` pressure belongs here.

### `selection_operator`
Determines which historical units count for the universal whole.

Failure:
- arbitrary exclusion;
- hierarchy hidden as neutral relevance.

### `plan_system_operator`
Turns selected units into a connected whole.

Failure:
- relation asserted without sufficient historical warrant;
- imposed teleology;
- explanatory whole mistaken for recovered fact.

### `table_operator`
Externalises the Plan as rows, columns, compartments, synchronies, scales or other graphical relations.

Failure:
- flattening;
- false equivalence;
- apparent precision generated by geometry;
- visual order mistaken for historical order.

### `colour_operator`
Adds chromatic or colour-coded difference to an already organised field.

Possible operations:
- discriminate;
- mark boundary;
- mark duration;
- mark status or relation;
- guide decoding;
- accelerate inspection;
- support memory.

Failure:
- chromatic overstatement;
- perceptual certainty without epistemic warrant;
- copy variance;
- non-invariant hand application;
- illegible or ambiguous encoding.

### `reader_operator`
Turns graphical/chromatic presentation into inspection, recognition, association or memory.

Failure:
- mnemonic vividness mistaken for factual certainty;
- salience mistaken for importance;
- easy decoding mistaken for source fidelity.

## 4. Herder's objection belongs upstream of colour

Herder's 1772 review of Schlözer is now most useful not as an anti-table manifesto but as a pressure test of commensuration.

His questions about the one endpoint of human development, `Maaß`, and `Data zum Maaße` challenge the possibility of putting very different times and peoples onto one historical scale.

Thus the controversy can be represented as:

`heterogeneous history`
→ **what common measure?**
→ **what Plan?**
→ table / synopsis.

Colour enters only later.

This is why a perfectly clear colour system can remain methodologically problematic. If the preceding partition is weak, colour may make a weakly warranted distinction appear self-evident.

## 5. Schlözer's reply licenses downstream graphical delegation

In 1773 Schlözer's answer to Herder defends `gemahlte chronologische Tabellen` in children's instruction. The p. 327 example uses painted co-presence of Solomon's temple, Egyptian pyramids and Homer to stabilize `Synchronismus` through `Association der Ideen`.

The operation is downstream:

`historical units already accepted`
→ `pictorial co-presence`
→ `relation made perceptually available`
→ `memory`.

This does not answer Herder's upstream question of whether the units and synchronistic relation were selected under an adequate universal measure. It instead demonstrates what graphical form can do once a relation has been accepted.

## 6. Literal colour in 1775 is another downstream operator

The separate 1775 `Illumination der Tabelle` passage, presently controlled through a scholarly transcription, proposes making unequal duration of five world-historical periods visible to the young eye.

The sequence is:

`periodisation`
→ `temporal compartments / scale`
→ `illumination`
→ `visible length and duration`.

Colour does not create the periodisation. It renders the already constructed temporal relation perceptually immediate.

This distinction should become a permanent ontology rule:

`colour_semantics`
≠
`warrant_for_underlying_partition`.

## 7. Later Schlözer control: table as a general system technology

A later actor-level control shows that `Tabelle` was not merely a children's mnemonic for Schlözer.

In the preface to the 1793 *Allgemeines StatsRecht und StatsVerfassungsLere* / *StatsGelartheit*, reflecting a teaching system developed from the later 1770s onward, Schlözer confronts an enormous mass of apparently heterogeneous knowledge concerning state and government and asks whether it can all be brought into a `geschlossenes System, in eine Tabelle`.

He then says he had worked for years on this table, trying to make it as `natürlich` as possible and remove the `Künstliche`.

This later evidence should not be projected backward as proof of the precise meaning of `Tabelle` in 1772–1775.

Its value is comparative: it shows a stable Schlözerian aspiration to use tables to turn heterogeneity into system, and to make the resulting construction appear natural rather than artificial.

That creates an important later control for the colour project:

`heterogeneous knowledge`
→ `constructed table`
→ `naturalised system`.

A chromatic layer can participate in this naturalisation by making the system easier to see than the operations by which it was constructed.

## 8. Epistemic warrant versus perceptual Evidenz

The operator model forces a distinction between two forms of force:

### `epistemic_warrant`
Why should the historical category, relation or proposition be accepted?

Possible grounds:
- source criticism;
- documentary practice;
- stable disciplinary convention;
- explicit comparative evidence.

### `perceptual_evidence`
Why is the relation easy to see?

Possible mechanisms:
- geometry;
- line;
- proximity;
- table position;
- colour contrast;
- hatching;
- repeated legend.

A method may increase perceptual evidence without increasing epistemic warrant.

This distinction is central to the project because Gatterer's `Synopsis`, Wappenkalender hatching and Schlözer's proposed illumination all increase inspectability through different material operations.

## 9. Gatterer 1767 makes this distinction historically grounded

The distinction between warrant and perceptual Evidenz is not only a modern analytical convenience.

In the 1767 *Vorrede von der Evidenz in der Geschichtskunde*, Gatterer distinguishes two ways in which historical knowledge can produce Evidenz. Modern scholarship reconstructs them as combinable rather than mutually exclusive:

1. the historian exposes the principles / sources on which factual claims rest;
2. the historian narrates so `lebhaft und anschauend` that the reader becomes, as it were, a `Zuschauer` of the events.

The first concerns the evidentiary grounding of factuality. The second produces ideal presence and perceptual conviction.

Thus an actor-level distinction already exists between something close to:

`source_warrant`
≠
`presentational_Evidenz`.

Gatterer can combine them, but one is not reducible to the other.

This is the strongest conceptual hinge yet found for the colour project.

Colour, hatching, table geometry or graphical disposition can contribute to the second side — making relations available to the eye — while the historical categories and claims remain accountable to the first side.

A dangerous case is therefore:

`low source/relational warrant`
+
`high perceptual Evidenz`
→ apparent certainty.

A strong method instead seeks:

`source warrant`
+
`controlled arrangement`
+
`perceptual Evidenz`
without confusing the three.

## 10. Historiographical guard on Gatterer's Evidenz

This conceptual distinction is itself prior art. Daniel Fulda and subsequent scholarship have already analysed Gatterer's 1767 theory of Evidenz, including its combination of source demonstration and vivid, eyewitness-like presentation.

Therefore novelty cannot be claimed for discovering that Gatterer distinguishes proof and vividness.

The project-specific opening is narrower:

> **to use this actor-level distinction to follow how colour and colour-surrogates move between source warrant, encoding, historical system, perceptual Evidenz and material copy state across connected historical and auxiliary-historical practices.**

## 11. Heraldry becomes the clean control experiment

The heraldic branch is unusually useful because the semantic colour relation can be held relatively stable while its carrier changes:

`tincture`
→ verbal name
→ letter code
→ hatch code
→ monochrome decoder
→ prose blazon
→ optional hand pigment.

The 1772 publisher retrospective directly reports that one carrier — letter-coded tincture — could produce `Unordnung und Mißverstand`.

This permits an experimental question unavailable in the universal-history table:

> **If the semantic claim is held constant, what does changing the carrier do to reproducibility, ambiguity, compression and reader inspection?**

That makes heraldry a methodological control for the more difficult case in which Gatterer's synchronistic colours do not merely transmit literal object colour but mark abstract historical relations.

## 12. Historiographical boundary

Existing historiography already covers:
- Herder's critique of Schlözer's tables/synopsis/progress;
- Schlözer's aggregate/system and mosaic/tableau metaphors;
- the 1773 painted chronological tables;
- the 1775 illumination proposal;
- historical images as pedagogical instruments;
- Gatterer's colour as visual historical semantics;
- Gatterer's dual theory of source-grounded and presentational Evidenz.

Therefore novelty cannot sit at the level of `tables and images mattered`, `colour helped cognition`, or `Gatterer theorized vividness`.

The bounded opening after this sweep is:

> **a history of how successive operators transformed historical difference, how each operator acquired a distinct warrant, and how chromatic clarity could amplify the output of earlier acts of commensuration, classification and tabulation without automatically inheriting their epistemic justification.**

## 13. Relation to Gatterer–Schlözer

The `Species Facti` quarrel remains the decisive missing bridge.

The question is now sharper than a keyword search for `Farbe`:

> **Did either side treat Plan, table, synchronistic arrangement, graphical disposition or other implementation choices as part of the method that could be owned, borrowed or plagiarised?**

If yes, the colour project can connect representational operations to professional method-property without claiming that pigment itself was named in the quarrel.

If no, the two controversy surfaces should remain analytically connected but historically distinct.

## 14. New research questions

1. Does `Species Facti` distinguish an abstract `Plan` from its pedagogical/material implementation?
2. Does Gatterer's *Antwort* defend priority in tables, synchronistic structure, teaching sequence or only conceptual content?
3. Does Schlözer's `Plan` vocabulary treat graphical disposition as evidence of intellectual ownership?
4. When do Gatterer or Schlözer call an arrangement `natürlich`, `künstlich`, `deutlich`, `anschaulich`, `übersichtlich` or equivalent?
5. Can colour be shown to participate in the naturalisation of a system rather than merely its illustration?
6. When colour increases `Evidenz`, what source or system warrant remains separately available to police the chromatic assignment?

## 15. Current formulation

Long form:

> **The historical problem of colour begins before pigment: categories, measures and tables first make heterogeneous pasts commensurable; colour then redistributes salience, inspectability and memory within that constructed field. Gatterer's own distinction between source-grounded and presentational Evidenz shows why perceptual clarity cannot substitute for historical warrant. The methodological question is how each transformation was authorized and controlled.**

Short form:

> **Before colour orders history, history has already been ordered for colour.**
