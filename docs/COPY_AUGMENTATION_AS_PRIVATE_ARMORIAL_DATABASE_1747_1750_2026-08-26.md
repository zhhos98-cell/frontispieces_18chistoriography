# Copy augmentation as private armorial database, 1747–1750 — 26 August 2026

## Question

The Wappenkalender should not be treated only as a fixed publisher-issued sequence of frontispiece, armorial plates, genealogical tables and text. Two copy-level controls show owners converting individual volumes into **expandable armorial repositories** by inserting additional engraved heraldic material after publication.

The useful distinction is:

`publisher-issued apparatus ≠ copy-specific colouring/finishing ≠ later owner augmentation`.

## 1. 1747: more than one hundred additional heraldic copper engravings

Richard Bertling’s 1912 dealer catalogue describes a 1747 *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender* with the normal published apparatus, including a title copper and heraldic illustrations on 106 engraved plates. The same copy bore an ownership reading `A. W. von Arnstedt` and had been materially enlarged: **more than one hundred named eighteenth-century aristocratic heraldic copper engravings** were cut out and lightly attached to versos of existing plates.

This is not ordinary binding variance. The copy had become a larger visual comparison set whose scope exceeded the annual publication programme.

Evidence status: historical physical-copy catalogue description. Present location unresolved.

## 2. 1750: Maria Josepha copy, luxury finishing plus pasted additions

Venator & Hanstein, Katalog 128, lot 303 describes a 1750 calendar in an olive-green morocco binding with gold tooling and the armorial supralibros of **Maria Josepha of Saxony, Dauphine of France (1731–1767), mother of Louis XVI**. The issued apparatus is exceptionally well specified:

- engraved frontispiece;
- engraved medal plate;
- full-page engraved colour explanation;
- 108 full-page armorial copperplates with verso text;
- 74 numbered double leaves containing 135 genealogical tables;
- publisher advertisements.

The copy-specific finish is equally important. The 108 armorial engravings are described as **coloured and heightened with gold and silver**. In addition, numerous further coloured coats of arms were pasted onto otherwise blank pages.

The binding establishes an elite provenance association. It does not, by itself, establish that Maria Josepha personally ordered each insertion or that all additions were made at the same time as the binding. Those questions remain open.

## 3. Analytical consequence

Together the 1747 and 1750 copies expose a use-mode that is easy to erase in edition-level bibliography:

`serial publication → owned reference copy → available blank/verso space → inserted heraldic images → enlarged private comparison apparatus`.

The annual calendar was therefore capable of functioning as a **platform for accumulation**. Its printed genealogical tables and armorial engravings supplied an existing classificatory scaffold; owners could extend that scaffold with new visual witnesses rather than merely annotate the text.

The 1750 luxury copy adds another layer. Colour and metallic heightening convert standardized engraved plates into a more expensive, visually differentiated object, while pasted additions expand informational scope. Material enhancement and informational augmentation occur in the same copy.

This strengthens the repository’s broader vocabulary of extraction, recomposition and accumulation. A surviving copy is not a defective or embellished proxy for an edition. It can preserve evidence for how the serial apparatus was actually **used as an extensible information technology**.

## 4. Guardrails

- Never include pasted additions in the publisher-issued plate count.
- Never generalise one copy’s colouring or metallic heightening to the whole edition.
- An armorial supralibros establishes copy association/provenance evidence, not personal authorship of every intervention.
- Copy augmentation does not prove the inserted engravings came from the same Wappenkalender series unless each source sheet is independently identified.
- Treat the 1747 and 1750 cases as two copy-level controls, not evidence that augmentation was universal.

## 5. Next discriminating tests

1. Locate photographs or later provenance of the 1747 Arnstedt copy and identify a sample of the inserted engravings by source.
2. Locate images of the Maria Josepha 1750 copy, especially blank pages with pasted arms, to distinguish contemporary versus later insertion practices from paper, trimming, adhesive and colouring.
3. Compare inserted images against detached Wappenkalender sheets already circulating on the market; this may reveal whether annual plates were cannibalised to enlarge other copies.
4. Search other luxury/provenance copies for the same combination of colouring, metallic heightening and pasted additions.
