# Weigel terminal chronology and the 1755 Tyroff plate-state question — 26 August 2026

## Purpose

This note separates three issues that had begun to collapse into one another:

1. whether the old Weigel *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender* actually had a 1755 issue;
2. whether the series ended in 1757 or 1758;
3. whether the `M. Turoff fec. 1755` frontispiece reported in the 1762 Gatterer *Handbuch* is a new state/re-engraving of the older Müller–Tyroff `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica` composition.

The first two can now be bounded firmly. The third remains an image-level test.

## 1. The 1755 calendar exists and is the thirty-third issue

A detailed complete-copy description in Katalog 37 records:

> `Der Durchlauchtigen Welt zum drey und dreysigstenmal ... Geschichts- Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender ... 1755.`

Imprint:

`Nürnberg, A. Bieling für C. Weigels Erben (1754)`.

Collation reported:

- engraved frontispiece;
- engraved colour-explanation plate;
- one engraved medal plate;
- 109 full-page armorial engravings, text on versos;
- 79 numbered double-leaf genealogical tables;
- title, calendar and preface; publisher advertisements.

A second auction record independently describes a 1755 copy in the Christoph-Weigel-heirs imprint with more than one hundred copper engravings.

Therefore claims that the series skipped 1755 are false. The issue is physically attested and explicitly self-numbers as the **thirty-third**.

## 2. The old series ends in 1757, not 1758

The Deutsche Nationalbibliothek serial authority records:

- publication range: **1723–1757**;
- numbering: `1723 - 35.1757 nachgewiesen`;
- continuation: *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik*.

ZDB/VD18 digital records likewise terminate the old title at 1757.

The 1757 physical-copy description explicitly gives:

`zum fünf und dreysigstenmal` — the **thirty-fifth** issue.

A Venator & Hanstein catalogue note saying the calendar appeared `1723-1758` should therefore be treated as a secondary catalogue error, not as evidence for a lost thirty-sixth 1758 issue.

This closure also makes the sequence arithmetically coherent:

- 1755 = 33rd;
- 1756 = 34th;
- 1757 = 35th and terminal old-title issue.

## 3. Why 1755 now matters for the frontispiece genealogy

The 1762 Gatterer *Handbuch* copy in the National Library of Finland is catalogued with:

`Engr. front., signed 'M. Turoff fec. 1755'.`

This date now falls directly on a securely attested Weigel-calendar issue that itself had an engraved frontispiece.

The coincidence is potentially material rather than merely chronological. In 1755 Martin Tyroff was also supplying heraldic plates for Gatterer's *Historia illustris gentis Holzschuherianae*. Nineteenth-century heraldic literature explicitly attributes the armorial plates of that 1755 work to Tyroff as designer/engraver.

A bounded production-history hypothesis is therefore justified:

`older Müller–Tyroff Clio composition (visible by 1726; BM witness 1740)`
→ `possible Tyroff plate renewal / new state in or around 1755`
→ `1759 Gatterer first Handbuch uses Historia/Genealogia/Heraldica frontispiece`
→ `1762 Handbuch frontispiece signed M. Turoff fec. 1755`.

## 4. What is NOT yet established

Do not write any of the following as fact until the 1762 image is inspected:

- that the 1762 `M. Turoff fec. 1755` plate is the Clio composition;
- that a new copperplate was engraved in 1755 rather than an older plate being reworked/re-signed;
- that the 1755 calendar itself already carries the `fec. 1755` state;
- that the 1740 British Museum impression and the 1755/1762 impression derive from the identical copperplate.

The next decisive operation is an image-level comparison of:

1. BM 1740 `Historia Genealogia Heraldica` witness;
2. complete 1755 Wappenkalender frontispiece;
3. 1759 Gatterer pretitular Clio witness;
4. 1762 Gatterer frontispiece with reported `M. Turoff fec. 1755` signature.

Record composition, dimensions, signature wording/location, scratches, recutting, retouching and line-state differences.

Keep **serial chronology** and **plate genealogy** separate. The former is now closed at 35.1757. The latter has acquired a new high-value 1755 test but remains unresolved until direct image comparison.
