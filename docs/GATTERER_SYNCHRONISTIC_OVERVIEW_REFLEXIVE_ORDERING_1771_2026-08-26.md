## I. Why “simultaneity” is too generic

The 1771 overview should not be described only as a device that makes simultaneous events visible. Secondary transcription and reproduction show that it coordinates **three different histories** on one surface:

1. changing political / imperial structures;
2. epochal breaks in universal history;
3. changing historical knowledge and historiographical practice.

The strongest current formulation is therefore:

> The table synchronizes not only events with other events, but the history of the world with the history of knowing and writing that world.

This is a reflexive operation absent from a simple chronology.

---

## II. Three coupled registers

Martin Gierl's reproduction and transcription of the table allow the following controlled reconstruction.

### A. Political-system register

One register tracks ruling peoples, empires and systems of political dependence/subordination. Examples include the succession from Assyrians through Persians, Macedonians and Romans, followed in later periods by Germanic/Slavic, Arab, Mongol/Tatar and other formations.

This register therefore asks:

`what configuration of political power organizes the world in this period?`

It is continuous with the duration/power problem already visible in the 1766/69 `Durationem populorum, regnorum, civitatum` diagrams, but now compressed into a larger historical synopsis.

### B. Epoch / revolution register

A second register segments universal history through major transformations. Gierl's transcription includes markers such as:

- Creation / Fall / Flood;
- origin of peoples;
- migration period;
- rise of Arabs / Mongols and Tatars;
- Crusades;
- printing and fall of Constantinople;
- discovery of America;
- Reformation and Council of Trent;
- European balance and Peace of Westphalia;
- new philosophy in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

The important operation is not merely dating. These events function as **periodizing ruptures** that determine the architecture of the historical whole.

### C. Historiographical / epistemic register

The third register associates world-historical periods with changing forms of historical knowledge. The table's labels, as transcribed by Gierl, move roughly through:

- `historische Notwerkzeuge` / early auxiliary means;
- biblical and classical historiographers;
- chroniclers and charter/document writers (`Chronisten und Urkundenschreiber`);
- collectors, critics, learned scholars and pragmatic historians (`Sammler, Critiker, Gelehrte und Pragmatisten`).

This is the crucial reflexive feature. Historical knowledge itself is historicized and positioned synchronically beside political and epochal change.

## III. The visual operation

The table therefore does more than solve the problem:

`successive prose` vs `simultaneous events`.

It attempts a stronger coordination:

`political order`
+
`epochal transformation`
+
`historical knowledge regime`
→
`one synoptic field`.

The question for direct image inspection is whether these three registers are physically aligned strongly enough that the reader is invited to infer contemporaneity/correlation across them, or whether the relation is looser than later scholarly reproductions imply.

Until the original fold is inspected, avoid claiming a strict causal relation between a political epoch and its corresponding historiographical form. The current evidence securely supports **co-placement and synchronization**, not causal determination.

## IV. Difference from the inherited Clio frontispiece

This distinction is important for the article architecture.

### Clio / `Historia Genealogia Heraldica / Testes temporum et veritatis`

The inherited Müller–Tyroff frontispiece makes disciplines, instruments and witnesses co-present at the threshold of a publication.

Operation:

`disciplines + witnesses → threshold co-presence`.

It does not itself historicize those disciplines.

### 1771 synchronistic overview

The overview places successive forms of historical knowledge inside the same historical field as empires and epochal change.

Operation:

`world history + history of historical knowledge → reflexive ordering`.

The move is therefore not simply from “less visual” to “more visual.” It is from one visual operation to another:

- threshold co-presence;
- temporal/political duration;
- classified simultaneity;
- **reflexive synchronization of object-history and knowledge-history**.

## V. Relation to the 1766/69 Synopsis

The 1766/69 `Synopsis` already contains several distinct graphic grammars:

1. duration/power diagrams for peoples, realms and states;
2. classified event matrices organized by historical domain and century.

The 1771 overview appears to recombine these problems at a higher level of abstraction. It compresses political systems and epochal transitions while adding a historiographical register.

A useful controlled sequence is therefore:

`1766/69: how to place historical material and political duration on paper`
→
`1771: how to synchronize political structure, periodization and the history of historiography itself`.

This is not yet a claim of linear intellectual development. It is a comparison of graphic operations across adjacent works.

---

## VI. Article-level consequence

The strongest current claim is:

> **Gatterer's synchronistic tables did not merely overcome the successiveness of narrative. At least in the 1771 overview, the page became a device for locating historical knowledge itself inside universal history.**

This matters because the table is reflexive: the historian's own mode of work—collection, criticism and pragmatic synthesis—appears as the latest position in a historical sequence of ways of knowing the past.

That creates a methodological self-location without requiring the stronger claim that Gatterer regarded his own method as the teleological end of historiography.
