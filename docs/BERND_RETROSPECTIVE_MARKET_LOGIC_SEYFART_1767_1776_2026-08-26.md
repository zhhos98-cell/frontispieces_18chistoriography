# Bernd's retrospective market logic for the Seyfart Wappenkalender/Wappenbuch, 1767–1776 — 26 August 2026

## Evidentiary status

Christian Samuel Theodor Bernd's *Allgemeine Schriftenkunde der gesammten Wappenwissenschaft* (1830) is a **retrospective nineteenth-century account**, not a contemporaneous 1767 publisher statement. It can therefore supply a historically early explanation of the transition but cannot by itself establish Seyfart's or Raspe's original motive.

## Bernd's mechanism claim

Bernd's account, as transmitted in later heraldic bibliographic summaries, states that after Gatterer ceased to manage the calendar, Johann Friedrich Seyfart continued it to 1776. Bernd then gives a concrete market explanation: subscribers/buyers did not want to purchase the same armorial illustrations every year, so different arms were supplied in successive annual issues. He further states that the resulting armorial corpus comprised **571 Wappen**, with the images and their descriptive text separately numbered and designated so that they could be treated as a general Wappenbuch in their own right; special title pages were supplied for that purpose.

The exact German formulation most relevant to the present project is the claim that the `Abnehmer nicht jedes Jahr dieselben Wappenabbildungen kaufen wollten`, followed by the description of `571` arms, separately numbered/described, and made usable `als ein allgemeines Wappenbuch für sich`.

## Why this matters

This retrospective mechanism fits independently controlled object evidence unusually well:

- the 1767 Seyfart carrier preserves the annual `Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender` title while also participating in the separately titled *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch*;
- later surviving volumes/parts are organized as cumulative numbered armorial sequences rather than simple annual repetition;
- a complete four-volume 1767–1776 set is catalogued with 567 of 571 armorial plates, independently matching Bernd's total of 571;
- the same complete-set description reports only three repeated engraved frontispieces across four volumes, while the armorial corpus itself is designed for cumulative non-duplication;
- modern copy evidence shows that parts from 1767/1768 could later be detached and rebound as reusable armorial units, exactly the kind of modular afterlife made possible by separate numbering/title architecture.

The resulting model is not merely `serial becomes book`. It is more specific:

`annual subscription problem → avoid repeated armorial purchases → distribute non-duplicating numbered modules across years → provide cumulative title architecture → permit recombination as a general Wappenbuch`.

## Relation to the frontispiece problem

The frontispiece and the armorial sequence behave differently. Bernd's explanation concerns why **armorial content** changes rather than repeats, while surviving set descriptions report **repeated frontispieces**. That divergence is analytically valuable: the threshold/programmatic image could remain reusable stock even while buyers were being offered newly distributed armorial information each year.

This supports a two-layer production model:

1. **threshold stock**: recurrent Historia/Genealogia/Heraldica frontispiece compositions could be reused/re-hosted;
2. **subscription-value stock**: numbered Wappen plates were increasingly differentiated across annual deliveries and accumulated into a separately usable reference corpus.

The two layers therefore solve different publishing problems and should not be forced into one replacement chronology.

## Guardrails

- Treat buyer resistance to repeated images as **Bernd's 1830 retrospective explanation**, not a directly documented 1767 statement by Seyfart or Raspe.
- The independently corroborated number `571` strengthens Bernd's bibliographic accuracy about the finished corpus, but it does not automatically verify every causal statement he makes about publisher intent.
- Distinguish repeated frontispieces from non-repeating numbered Wappen plates. The evidence suggests different reuse logics for threshold image and substantive armorial content.
- Keep annual carrier, cumulative Wappenbuch title, individual numbered plate, and surviving bound copy as separate bibliographic/material levels.

## Next discriminating control

A contemporaneous preface, advertisement, subscription notice or publisher statement from 1767–1776 explicitly explaining the new numbering/title architecture would upgrade the market mechanism from retrospective explanation to contemporary production evidence. Until such a source is found, Bernd remains a strong early secondary control rather than primary motive evidence.
