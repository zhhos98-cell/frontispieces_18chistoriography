# Köhler 1734 copy-variation control — 26 August 2026

This control records a highly relevant actor-level statement about material variation in the heraldic plate corpus. It is not yet promoted to direct-primary status because the exact 1734 preface page has not been image-collated in the current research environment.

## 1. The statement

A later heraldic history by Gustav Adelbert Seyler quotes Johann David Köhler's 1734 preface to the Weigel edition of the Siebmacher/Helmers armorial. In Seyler's quotation, Köhler reports that a close comparison of **five Helmers copies** found that **no one copy agreed with another in the constitution and number of the arms**.

The quoted explanation is materially specific. Across copies/states:

- some arms had been removed from plates and improved versions inserted;
- previously empty shields had been filled with heraldic figures;
- additional arms had been inserted or added in different parts.

Seyler's transmitted wording begins:

> `man hat bey genauer Zusammenhaltung fünff Helmerischer Exemplarien befunden, daß keines mit dem andern in der Beschaffenheit und Anzahl der Wappen übereingetroffen ...`

and continues with the replacement, filling and insertion operations above.

Current source route:

- Johann David Köhler, preface to the 1734 Weigel armorial — original print digitally available in multiple carriers, exact page still to collate;
- Gustav Adelbert Seyler, later heraldic history, quoted via a modern transcription preserving the passage.

**Current evidence status:** `reported_primary_via_later_exact_quote`; upgrade only after direct inspection of the 1734 preface page.

## 2. Why this matters for the project

The statement is unusually close to the corpus's own witness discipline. It supplies historical actor-language for a rule that has otherwise been imposed as modern source criticism:

`same work/edition family ≠ invariant plate population`

Variation was not merely caused by later loss, rebinding or defective surviving copies. The production history itself included plate alteration and incremental insertion.

This bears directly on several current problems:

1. the c.1725/1740/1750 *Bequemes Hand-Buch* copies with 60, 81, 85, 105/106 and other armorial configurations;
2. the 1725 calendar/handbook 85/85 comparison;
3. the 1734 Weigel armorial and its relation to earlier calendar leaves;
4. late Seyfart/Wappenbuch copies with variable front matter;
5. the need to distinguish `same design`, `same copperplate`, `same impression`, `same issue`, and `same surviving bound copy`.

The strongest interpretive consequence is that copy-level variation is not peripheral noise. It is itself evidence for the way heraldic knowledge was maintained: correction could occur by physically changing the plate population.

## 3. Relation to the 1771 serialization control

The 1771 Seyfart preface directly states that the arms previously used in the Wappencalender were taken as a basis for the Wappenbuch and that successive divisions would be issued annually with the calendar.

Taken together — while keeping their evidence grades distinct — the 1734 and 1771 statements outline two complementary operations:

`plate alteration / insertion / correction within a work family`

and

`reuse / enlargement / annual delivery / cumulative rebinding across publication forms`.

This makes the project less a history of fixed iconographic editions than a history of mutable plate stocks and evidentiary reformatting.
