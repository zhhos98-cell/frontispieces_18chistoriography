# Interleaving as a heraldic working-space practice, 1740–1745 — 26 August 2026

## Controlled copy evidence

Two independently described physical copies of the Weigel/Köhler *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender* show that readers or binders could materially enlarge the printed object by interleaving it with writable paper and then use that extra space for manuscript additions.

### 1740 copy — Dorotheum, lot 110, 25 September 2018

Dorotheum describes a physical copy of the eighteenth issue, *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender auf das Schalt-Jahr 1740*, Nürnberg, Bieling for Weigel (imprint 1739), with engraved frontispiece, one copperplate, numerous armorial engravings, and 69 double-leaf units carrying 122 genealogical tables.

The decisive copy-level phrase is:

`Interfoliiertes Exemplar mit alten hs. Zusätzen.`

Thus the surviving copy was **interleaved** and contains **old manuscript additions**. The catalogue does not identify the interleaver, writer, date of annotation, or whether interleaving occurred before first use or later.

Source: Dorotheum, *Bücher und dekorative Grafik*, 25 September 2018, lot 110.

### 1745 copy — Tresor am Römer / current antiquarian description

A current physical-copy description for the twenty-third issue, calendar year 1745, reports the engraved frontispiece, 70 numbered double-leaf genealogical tables, portrait of Ferdinand IV and 102 full-page armorial engravings.

The copy is described as:

`durchschossenes Exemplar`

and also carries:

`Vereinzelte Marginalien von alter Hand`.

In eighteenth-century German book-trade/catalogue usage, `durchschossen` is the material equivalent relevant here: the printed leaves are interleaved with blank writing paper. The old marginalia show actual manuscript use of the enlarged writing surface, although the catalogue does not state that every annotation sits on an inserted leaf.

Source: Tresor am Römer, current antiquarian listing for the 1745 Wappenkalender; parallel Booklooker/Kiefer descriptions independently control the physical issue/frontispiece.

## Analytical consequence

These copies move the project's `working heraldic dossier` model earlier than the 1762 annotated Gatterer *Handbuch*. By 1740 and 1745, the annual could already be converted materially from a printed reference sequence into an **expandable writing environment**:

`printed calendar + genealogical/heraldic plates → inserted blank writing space → manuscript additions`.

This is structurally related to, but distinct from, the other controlled augmentation practices:

- 1747 Arnstedt copy: more than 100 additional printed heraldic cuttings attached to versos of existing plates;
- 1750 Maria Josepha-associated copy: publisher-issued armorial engravings coloured/metal-heightened plus additional coloured arms pasted into blank space;
- 1762 Württemberg noble-library copy: partly coloured hand-drawn arms, annotations and manuscript papers explaining arms and their origins;
- later private Sammelband: detached original leaves from different annual components recombined and missing material supplied by modern facsimile copies.

The 1740/1745 evidence identifies a particularly important operation: **space for future evidence was engineered into the book itself**. The object could be prepared in advance for accession, correction, comparison or updating rather than treated as a closed printed product.

## Guardrails

- Do not assume the publisher supplied interleaved copies as a standard issue state. Both controls are copy-specific descriptions.
- Do not date the manuscript additions merely from `alt`; this establishes historical rather than modern use, not a precise year.
- Do not identify the annotator without ownership/handwriting evidence.
- Do not infer that all interleaving was specifically intended for heraldic research. In these two copies, however, interleaving occurs inside a genealogical-heraldic reference apparatus and is accompanied by manuscript additions, making working-reference use a strong object-level interpretation.
- Do not use present plate counts from either catalogue as edition collation without comparing counting conventions and copy completeness.

## Relation to visual epistemic practice

The interleaved copies help explain why the Wappenkalender's visual and tabular structure matters epistemically. Arms and Stammtafeln were not only illustrations to be viewed. The book could supply a printed comparison grid around which readers inserted further information. The distinction between `printed evidence`, `reader-added evidence`, and `blank capacity for future evidence` is therefore materially visible in surviving copies.
