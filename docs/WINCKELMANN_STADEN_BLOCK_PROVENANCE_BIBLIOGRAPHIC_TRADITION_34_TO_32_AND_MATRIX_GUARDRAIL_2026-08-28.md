# Winckelmann–Staden block provenance: the `34 original blocks` bibliographic tradition, JCB's `32 + 2` refinement, and the matrix guardrail

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HISTORIOGRAPHICAL-PROVENANCE CHECKPOINT — A LONG BIBLIOGRAPHIC TRADITION TREATS THE 1664 IMAGE SET AS THIRTY-FOUR RECOVERED ORIGINAL STADEN BLOCKS; JCB/OPEN LIBRARY REFINES THIS TO 32 ORIGINAL-BLOCK IMPRESSIONS + 2 NON-STADEN EXCEPTIONS; OBERMEIER'S MATERIAL/ICONOGRAPHIC RECONSTRUCTION IS MORE CAUTIOUS AND SPEAKS ONLY OF SOME/A PORTION OF THE ORIGINAL BLOCK STOCK. THE THREE CLAIM LEVELS MUST REMAIN SEPARATE UNTIL PAIRWISE MATRIX COMPARISON.

## Result

The 1664 Staden-image problem now has **two different stemmata** that must not be collapsed:

1. a **material matrix stemma** — which 1557 woodblocks physically survived and were actually reprinted in 1664;
2. a **bibliographic attribution stemma** — how later catalogues and scholarship came to describe the 1664 image set as `34`, then `32 + 2`, original/non-original blocks.

The second stemma is historically important because the bulk provenance claim itself has a transmission history.

Current claim sequence:

`WINCKELMANN 1664 AUTHORIAL CLAIM`
→ all 34 woodcuts made at Staden's instigation

`OLDER BIBLIOGRAPHIC TRADITION`
→ 34 original Staden woodblocks rediscovered at Kassel and reused

`JCB / OPEN LIBRARY MODERN CATALOGUE REFINEMENT`
→ 32 images printed from original 1557 Marburg woodblocks + 2 images definitely not from Staden

`OBERMEIER MATERIAL/ICONOGRAPHIC CAUTION`
→ only `einigen` / `ein Teil` of the original Staden printing blocks demonstrably survived and were available to Winckelmann.

These are not equivalent evidence statements.

---

## 1. Winckelmann's own bulk-origin claim

The JCB/Open Library edition note reports that Winckelmann says on p.136 that all thirty-four woodcuts had been made at the instigation of Hans Staden.

Whatever the exact wording proves about production, this is an **authorial provenance/programme claim** internal to the 1664 host.

It does not by itself establish:

- physical survival of all 34 matrices from 1557;
- that all 34 compositions occur in the princeps;
- that all 34 impressions are from the same matrices;
- that later blocks were not retrospectively absorbed into the Staden programme.

Indeed JCB explicitly identifies pp.18 and 74 as non-Staden exceptions.

---

## 2. A long older bibliographic tradition turns the claim into a material-recovery story

Nineteenth-/early-twentieth-century bibliographic literature repeats a stronger material story.

Sabin's *Dictionary of Books Relating to America* describes the 1664 Winckelmann paraphrase as including thirty-four original woodcuts of the Marburg editions, the blocks for which Winckelmann found at Kassel.

An English Staden editorial introduction likewise states that Winckelmann, while working among the archives at Kassel, discovered thirty-four original woodblocks and used them again.

These controls matter because they show that the formula:

`34 ORIGINAL BLOCKS FOUND AT CASSEL`

predates the current JCB metadata and circulated as a bibliographic fact in its own right.

Current evidence status:

`HISTORICAL BIBLIOGRAPHIC TRADITION = CONTROLLED`.

What remains open is **where this tradition first arose and what evidence originally supported it**.

Potential ancestors include:

- Winckelmann's own p.136 wording;
- eighteenth-/nineteenth-century copy descriptions;
- direct comparison of 1557 and 1664 impressions;
- information about surviving printer/block stock in Kassel;
- later facsimile editors.

Do not treat repeated bibliographic transmission as independent physical verification.

---

## 3. JCB/Open Library introduces a materially sharper `32 + 2` model

The JCB-derived record says:

- 32 text illustrations accompanying the Staden narrative were printed from the original Marburg 1557 woodblocks rediscovered by Winckelmann;
- two further illustrations at pp.18 and 74 are definitely not from Staden's original work;
- p.168 and p.179 contain images in which names present on the original woodcuts have been eliminated.

This is a significant refinement over the older `34 original blocks` formula.

It implies:

`34 claimed Staden-origin images`
→ `32 original-block impressions`
+
`2 provenance exceptions`.

The JCB/Open Library record cites several authorities for the edition, including:

- JCB pre-1675 catalogue 3:122–123;
- Rubens Borba de Moraes, *Bibliographia Brasiliana*, pp.944–946;
- Richard N. Wegner, `Begleitschrift`, p.19, in the 1925 Staden facsimile;
- Karl Fouquet, `Die Ausgaben der Wahrhaftigen Historia 1557–1964`, p.32, in the 1964 facsimile;
- Alden, *European Americana*, 664/195.

However, the present sweep has **not yet recovered Wegner p.19 or Fouquet p.32 directly**, so it is not yet known which cited authority specifically supports:

- `32 original blocks`;
- the two non-Staden exceptions;
- the p.168/p.179 name-removal claim.

Therefore JCB's statement remains:

`HIGH-QUALITY CATALOGUE ATTRIBUTION`,

not yet a transparent reconstruction of its evidentiary derivation.

## 4. Obermeier independently imposes a stricter matrix-level boundary

Franz Obermeier's iconographic history of sixteenth-century Brazil states that Winckelmann had access to the blocks/plates of **some** woodcuts from Staden's editio princeps and inserted them at arbitrary places in his text:

`Winckelmann standen auch die Platten einiger Holzschnitte der editio princeps Stadens zur Verfügung`.

Elsewhere Obermeier describes the 1664 illustration relation as `Princeps (teilweise)` and says:

`wohl nur ein Teil der originalen Druckstöcke erhalten`.

These statements independently confirm genuine original-block survival and reuse, but they explicitly avoid a thirty-two- or thirty-four-block bulk identity claim.

Source:

- Franz Obermeier, *Brasilien in Illustrationen des 16. Jahrhunderts* / related iconographic study, discussion around the Staden reception and footnote on Winckelmann 1664.

Public searchable text currently exposes the relevant passage:

`Winckelmann standen auch die Platten einiger Holzschnitte der editio princeps Stadens zur Verfügung, die er in seinem Text an beliebigen Stellen eingefügt hat.`

This is materially decisive for evidence discipline.

## 5. The conflict is productive rather than something to smooth away

The evidence now supports three different questions:

### Programme provenance

Did Winckelmann present the image cycle as inherited from Staden?

**Yes, strongly.**

### Composition provenance

Do many/most of the 1664 Staden-section images derive compositionally from Staden's 1557 image cycle?

**Yes, strongly.**

### Matrix provenance

Were all 32 Staden-section images physically printed from the original 1557 blocks?

**Open at bulk level.**

At least some original matrices were genuinely reused; JCB catalogues 32 as such; Obermeier's material history uses narrower language.

Therefore the corpus rule must be:

`BULK COMPOSITION DERIVATION`
≠ `BULK SAME-MATRIX IDENTITY`.

---

## 6. Historiographical compression mirrors Winckelmann's own provenance compression

There is an analytically striking symmetry.

Winckelmann appears to compress a heterogeneous visual set into a single Staden-origin programme.

Later bibliography then compresses that programme into a physical recovery story of `34 original blocks`.

JCB partially decomposes the story into `32 + 2`, while Obermeier reopens the matrix count itself.

Thus provenance compression occurs at multiple historical moments:

`MIXED / UNCERTAIN VISUAL MATERIAL`
→ `1664 AUTHORIAL ORIGIN PROGRAMME`
→ `LATER 34-ORIGINAL-BLOCK BIBLIOGRAPHIC TRADITION`
→ `JCB 32 + 2 REFINEMENT`
→ `CURRENT MATRIX-BY-MATRIX REOPENING`.

This is itself a provenance history worth preserving in the repository.

Working label:

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC REIFICATION OF MATRIX PROVENANCE`.

## 7. Promotion rules going forward

For each 1557→1664 candidate pair:

### `same_composition_as`

May be assigned from clear iconographic identity.

### `probable_same_matrix_as`

Requires close dimensional/frame/lettering correspondence plus no evidence of recutting.

### `same_matrix_as`

Requires stable physical features such as:

- identical cut-line geometry;
- identical incidental nicks/breaks;
- identical malformed lines;
- wear development consistent with chronology;
- integral lettering identity;
- dimensions compatible with same block;
- distinctive defects not explainable by copying.

No bulk source statement, including a respected catalogue, substitutes for this item-level promotion rule when the project specifically asks matrix identity.
