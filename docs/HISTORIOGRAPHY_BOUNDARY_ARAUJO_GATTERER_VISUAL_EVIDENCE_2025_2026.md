# Historiographical boundary: Araújo on Gatterer, visual evidence and paratext — 2025–2026

## Why this boundary is necessary

Two recent studies by André de Melo Araújo directly occupy territory that this project must not redescribe as a novelty claim:

1. **2025** — “Evidentiary Authority as a System: Johann Christoph Gatterer and the Collective Making of Historical Knowledge in the Eighteenth Century,” *Berichte zur Wissenschaftsgeschichte* 48 (3): 203–226.
2. **2026** — “Epistemologia paratextual. Prova e autoridade no prefácio à Historia genealogica (1755) de Johann Christoph Gatterer,” *Revista de Fontes* 12 (23): 52–78.

Both center Gatterer's *Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum* (1755). Their combined scope makes several broad formulations unavailable as claims of originality for the present project.

## 1. What Araújo 2025 already does

The 2025 article explicitly reconstructs the **multiple production stages** of Gatterer's 1755 illustrated genealogy through material and medial analysis. It does not treat engravings as decorative supplements.

Its core claims and operations include:

- historical knowledge shaped by the media through which it was presented;
- emphasis on the engraved plates inserted in the volume;
- collective scholarly, artistic and editorial production;
- negotiation of graphic and intellectual authorship and authority;
- interplay among manuscript, letterpress, drawings, copperplate engravings and surviving artifacts;
- visual reproductions as carriers of evidentiary authority;
- comparison of reproduced charters with surviving originals;
- mistakes, omissions and format changes introduced by visual mediation;
- reconstruction of Gatterer's manuscript information system, pasted slips and internal cross-references;
- bookbinding/insertion of separately printed intaglio plates;
- specific analysis of plates XIII/XIV and their relationship to drawings, charters, seals and textual transcriptions.

The article also discusses the **1755 frontispiece itself**. Gatterer's preface is used to explain the central Holzschuher insignia and the symbolic elements depicted in the engraving.

Therefore the following generic propositions are already prior art:

- “Gatterer used images as evidence.”
- “Copperplate reproduction shaped historical evidence.”
- “Visual and verbal information formed an evidentiary system.”
- “The making of Gatterer's illustrated genealogy was collaborative.”
- “Media transformations could alter documentary evidence.”
- “Gatterer's frontispiece participated in an epistemic/evidentiary programme.”

## 2. What Araújo 2026 adds

The 2026 article moves from the whole editorial system to the **preface as an epistemic device**.

Its abstract explicitly centers:

- the missing-seal and mistaken-image cases in Gatterer's preface;
- the preface not as ancillary threshold but as a device that organizes and validates historical proof;
- paleographic and codicological analysis;
- comparison of manuscript and printed versions;
- graphic interventions and validation devices that shape/authenticate documentary evidence;
- the proposed category **“paratextual epistemology”**, meaning verbal and graphic operations at the margins of narrative that become central to scientific authority.

The bibliography notably includes both the **1752 Weigel Wappenkalender** and the **1759 Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik**, so the surrounding publication ecology is not wholly absent from Araújo's frame even though the article remains anchored in the 1755 Holzschuher case.

Accordingly, the present project should not claim that treating front matter/thresholds as epistemically active is itself new.

## 3. The defensible opening for the present project

The strongest opening is **longitudinal and material-bibliographic**, not a generic theory of visual evidence or paratext.

### A. Plate lineage across changing host publications

The project follows a recurring threshold composition across multiple publication regimes:

`1724 annual-event frontispiece`
→ `1725 unresolved title copper`
→ `by 1726 Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica Clio composition`
→ `1740 British Museum Müller–Tyroff witness`
→ `possible Tyroff 1755 renewal/state`
→ `1759 Gatterer Handbuch pretitular Clio`
→ `1764 Fortgesetzter Wappen-Calender`
→ later Seyfart/Wappenbuch copy-variable frontispiece ecology.

This asks not “how does one image authenticate evidence inside one book?” but:

> **What happens when a threshold plate survives, is recopied/re-engraved, migrates, or is omitted while the publication architecture around it changes?**

### B. Competing frontispiece genealogies

The project distinguishes at least two durable threshold lineages within overlapping Nürnberg heraldic print ecologies:

- **Eimmart cumulative Wappenbuch lineage** — Fürst heirs → Helmers → Weigel 1734;
- **Müller–Tyroff Clio annual-calendar/handbook lineage** — Weigel Wappenkalender → Gatterer Handbuch and related continuations.

This makes frontispiece history a problem of **host-publication regime and inherited plate stock**, not a single Gatterer case.

### C. Seriality and reformatting

The annual Wappenkalender materially recomposes heterogeneous information:

- rulers and portraits;
- armorial plates;
- genealogical tables;
- rotating historical modules;
- Thuanus/de Thou continuous history reformatted into monthly/day-organised `Geschichts-Calender`;
- publisher advertisements;
- recurrent frontispiece.

The 1771 Wappenbuch preface then explicitly describes the cumulative armorial work as based on preceding Wappencalender material and continued through annual additions.

This creates an actor-supported history of:

`serialisation → extraction → recomposition → cumulative reference work → renewed serial supplementation`.

### D. Copy-level material afterlives

The project follows surviving copies as historical states:

- missing frontispieces;
- detached plates;
- foreign frontispieces inserted by later owners;
- calendar leaves inserted into handbooks;
- extra armorial plates mounted onto original plate versos;
- divergent frontispiece counts across otherwise related late-series sets.

This scale differs from reconstructing the production of one authorial work. It asks how visual/evidentiary apparatus is reconfigured **after publication and across copies**.

### E. Publisher and plate-stock ecology

The project reconstructs the Weigel elder-widow/heirs succession, printer Bieling, Tyroff family/production relations, recurring publisher advertisements and possible stock transfer into Raspe/Gatterer formats.

The 1725 calendar/handbook `85 plate + title copper` coincidence is treated as a plate-stock comparison problem, not as bibliographic similarity.

## 4. Claims to demote or avoid

Do **not** use as central novelty claims:

- Gatterer made images evidentiary;
- frontispieces can be epistemic paratexts;
- eighteenth-century engraved reproductions mediate historical truth;
- manuscript and printed evidence interact;
- visual evidence is collaboratively produced;
- reproduction errors matter epistemically.

These can be used as established historiographical ground, with Araújo cited directly.

Likewise, avoid inventing a false opposition in which Araújo studies “text” and this project studies “images.” His 2025 article explicitly says the opposite and gives the engravings primary analytical weight.

## 5. Strong differentiation sentence

A defensible compact distinction is:

> **Araújo reconstructs how Gatterer's 1755 editorial system produced and authenticated evidence across manuscript, print and engraving. This project follows a different object scale: how engraved threshold and armorial apparatus moved between annual calendars, handbooks and cumulative Wappenbücher, acquired new host functions, and diverged across surviving copies over more than half a century.**

An even tighter formulation:

> **The unit here is not Gatterer's book but the migrating plate/witness occurrence.**

## 6. Consequence for the Clio frontispiece

The Clio plate should not be presented primarily as proof that an eighteenth-century frontispiece could do epistemic work. That proposition is now too broad and too close to existing work.

The more original questions are:

- when the composition first enters the Wappenkalender;
- whether 1755 marks a new Tyroff plate/state;
- why it moves from annual calendar to Gatterer handbook;
- how its function changes when the host shifts from serial to handbook/cumulative formats;
- whether it persists, disappears or is replaced in specific surviving copies;
- how its lineage differs from the older Eimmart Wappenbuch frontispiece;
- how repeated threshold imagery interacts with annual source recomposition, publisher advertising and plate-stock reuse.
