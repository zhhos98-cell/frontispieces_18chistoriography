# 1725 transition and plate-control pass — 26 August 2026

This pass resumes the bounded high-information queue without reopening generic discovery. It concentrates on five tasks: the unresolved 1725 Weigel title copper, copy-level variation in the related Weigel-widow handbook, direct control of Eccard's 1725 engraved apparatus through Döderlein's 1729 use, the Steidlin predecessor frontispiece series, and the later Seyfart repeated-frontispiece problem.

The main result is deliberately asymmetric. Several surrounding controls can now be strengthened, but the 1725 Clio identity itself remains unresolved. The transition must therefore still be written conservatively as:

`1724 different frontispiece → 1725 title copper present, identity unresolved → 1726 target Clio design directly visible`

## 1. 1725 Weigel Wappenkalender: presence closed, identity still open

The 1725 issue is securely identified as a physical/digital witness and its possession of a title copper is no longer in doubt.

Controlled anchors:

- BSB/MDZ object: `bsb10428127`;
- Google Books free digitisation: `JpZAAAAAcAAJ`;
- Antiquariat Emil Hirsch, catalogue 18 (4 Nov. 1930), lot 71: physical-copy description explicitly reports `Mit Titelkupfer`, twelve princely portraits, about one hundred coloured arms on 85 copperplates, plus a colour-key copperplate.

What remains missing is direct inspection of the title-copper image itself. Current public search layers expose the digital object and catalogue metadata but did not return the opening plate image. BSB's official MDZ documentation confirms that its digitised objects are exposed through IIIF Presentation manifests and per-page Image API services; the unresolved state here is therefore an access/routing problem in the present research environment, not evidence that the plate image is unavailable.

**Evidence rule:** do not infer the target design from the mere presence of a `Titelkupfer`. The 1725 witness remains `title_copper_present_identity_unresolved` until the actual opening plate is inspected or an independent copy-level image/description explicitly identifies `Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica. Testes temporum et veritatis`.

This also means that the target design's current earliest positive image-level witness remains the 1726 copy whose auction imagery visibly shows the Clio plate.

## 2. The 1725 handbook comparison must be copy/state-specific

Hirsch catalogue 18, lot 72, immediately follows the 1725 calendar and describes the undated Weigel-widow *Genealogisches und heraldisches Hand-Buch* as `(1725)` with a title copper and **85 engraved armorial plates**. The 85/85 coincidence with lot 71 remains a high-value plate-stock test.

A separate currently described copy of *Bequemes und zum Zeitungs-Lesen sehr dienliches genealogisches und heraldisches Hand-Buch* is materially different. The seller dates it c.1725 from the inclusion of Pope Benedict XIII and reports:

- **106 Wappenkupfer**;
- about 42 coloured/part-coloured;
- **107 Stammtafeln**, positioned directly after the corresponding arms;
- a terminal list of Christoph Weigel the Elder's copper-illustrated publications.

This variation is analytically useful. It makes the handbook/calendar question more precise but rules out treating `the handbook` as one fixed 85-plate object. The next comparison must therefore use individual copy/state identifiers and record plate sequence, captions, dimensions, verso tables, omissions, insertions, wear and colour state.

Safe inference:

`same publishing orbit + overlapping apparatus + one 85/85 historical-copy coincidence = plate-stock hypothesis`

Not licensed:

`same title/work family = same plate stock or same title copper`.

## 3. Eccard 1725: an additional digital witness and a direct figure-use control

The 1725 Eccard object-centred study can now be anchored to an additional BSB digital witness not previously listed in the repository's 21 August sweep:

- Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, **4 Num.rec. 33 d**;
- VD18 **14444186**;
- URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb10685202-3`;
- Nürnberg: Peter Conrad Monath, 1725;
- `[6] Bl., 56 S., [2] Bl.; Ill. (Kupferst.)`.

This joins the already recorded BSB witnesses `4 Bavar. 601` and `4 Asc. 954#Beibd.1` and provides another route for future image-level collation.

More importantly, the role of Eccard's plates is no longer only catalogue-inferred. Johann Alexander Döderlein's *Commentatio Historica de Numis Germaniae Mediae* (Nürnberg, 1729), p.119, directly records his encounter with Eccard's 1725 study and uses **Eccard p.45 / Tab. III / Fig. XXVI** while adjudicating a coin attribution. Döderlein identifies the Eccard figure as a representation of a coin associated with Heinrich, Duke of Saxony and Count Palatine of the Rhine, and changes his own interpretation after consulting it.

This upgrades one relation:

`Eccard engraved figure → later printed reference object in a concrete numismatic comparison`

The claim is publication- and figure-specific. It does not establish that Köhler borrowed from Eccard or Döderlein, nor does it establish a production relation between Monath and the Weigel shop.

The next image-level Eccard task is now narrower: inspect Tab. I–III and the text copper, identify signatures/engraver where visible, and map which views represent the casket and which represent coins.

## 4. Steidlin predecessor series: frontispiece apparatus predates Weigel, identity comparison still needed

The predecessor calendar ecology can be sharpened without collapsing it into the Clio lineage.

### 1718 Ostertag/Lotter issue

A surviving-copy description for the 1718 *Genealogisch-Heraldischer Calender* explicitly reports:

- an engraved frontispiece **by Johann Matthias Steidlin**;
- twelve half-page monthly engravings;
- thirty-five engraved armorial plates;
- one folding genealogical table.

This establishes a named frontispiece-producing agent and substantial visual apparatus before the Weigel Clio transition.

### 1720 Steidlin Staats-Calender

Heidelberg's complete digitisation of the 1720 *Genealogisch-heraldischer Staats-Calender* explicitly indexes a separate **Frontispiz** in the opening leaves and exposes IIIF 2.1/3.0 manifests for the volume. The frontispiece is the second indexed content unit after the binding; the direct page route resolves to the opening-image sequence before the title page.

The current pass has not yet obtained a stable image rendering of that page in this tool environment. Therefore no iconographic identity or continuity is asserted.

The series-level digital record confirms surviving/digitised issues for 1720–1721, 1723 and 1726. These should be compared as actual plate witnesses, not just bibliographic issues.

Revised predecessor question:

`What visual threshold apparatus did Steidlin use across 1718 → 1720 → 1723 → 1726, and does any material feature migrate into the separate Weigel 1724–26 transition?`

This is a comparison problem, not an influence claim.

## 5. Seyfart 1767–1776: repeated frontispiece identity still unresolved

The late-series problem remains open. Institutional and series-level records securely identify the 1767–1776 *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch* and its component volumes. Publicly surfaced 1767 images confirm armorial plate dimensions around 11.5 × 19 cm for several detached leaves, but no direct image or textual description located in this pass identifies the reported repeated frontispiece as the Müller–Tyroff Clio design.

The existing warning becomes stronger rather than weaker: surviving volumes/parts were bound in materially different configurations, so `1767`, `1768`, `1771`, etc. must be cited with a specific digital/copy identifier when comparing front matter.

Status remains:

`repeated frontispiece series reported → identity_unchecked`.

## 8. Working interpretation after this pass

The transition is becoming less like the invention of one image and more like a reorganisation inside an already dense visual-reference ecology. Steidlin calendars already paired frontispieces, monthly engravings, armorial series and genealogical tables. The Weigel calendar and related handbook show that the same broad kinds of apparatus could be serialised, recomposed and copied in different configurations. Eccard's 1725 Nürnberg study shows an old cabinet object and coins entering an engraved historical argument; by 1729 one of those engraved coin figures could be cited by Döderlein as a comparison capable of changing an attribution.

The Clio plate matters inside this ecology because it turns `Historia / Genealogia / Heraldica` into an explicit threshold statement about evidentiary domains. But the material history must still be reconstructed witness by witness. The 1725 title copper is therefore not a nuisance gap: it is the decisive test of when that explicit threshold formulation entered the Weigel serial.
