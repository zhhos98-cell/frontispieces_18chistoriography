## Reported p. 69 wording

The later heraldic bibliography transcribes Schmeitzel's notice as:

> `Zu Augsburg ist 1720. in 8. ein Genealogisch-heraldischer Calender heraus kommen, welcher ebenfals hier muß bemercket werden, weil in demselbigen etlich und dreyßig Wappen, deutscher und ausländischer Häuser, sauber in Kupfer gestochen erscheinen, aber die beygefügte Erklärung ist nichts werth.`

The orthography in the later transcription should not be silently normalized as diplomatic 1723 text until the original is inspected.

The two decisive contemporary evaluative phrases are therefore already controlled at secondary-transcription level:

- graphic execution: `sauber in Kupfer gestochen`;
- explanatory value: `die beygefügte Erklärung ist nichts werth`.

## Evidentiary significance

Schmeitzel separates at least two evaluative dimensions within the same calendar:

1. **graphic execution** — the roughly thirty-some German and foreign arms appear cleanly engraved;
2. **explanatory knowledge** — the attached explanation is judged worthless.

This is a strong contemporary control against treating visual finish, textual explanation, documentary reliability and historical truth as one undifferentiated quality.

The distinction is especially useful beside the Weigel Clio transition. An elaborate frontispiece or well-cut armorial apparatus cannot by itself establish the epistemic value of the historical/genealogical explanation attached to it. Contemporary readers could praise the copperwork while dismissing the prose.

## Relation to the competing-calendar ecology

The target of Schmeitzel's remark is the Augsburg genealogy-heraldry calendar of 1720, the same competing publication ecology represented by the directly digitized Steidlin *Genealogisch-heraldischer Staats-Calender*.

The Heidelberg 1720 digitisation confirms a complex modular book architecture: frontispiece, calendar matter, explanations, genealogical-heraldic sections, registers and two separate `Bericht an den Buchbinder` instructions. The series catalogue identifies Steidlin's Augsburg run as 1720–1721, 1723 and 1726, continuing the Regensburg/Ostertag ecology from 1717.

A separate surviving-copy control materially strengthens the **1723** point. Venator & Hanstein catalogue 128, lot 305 describes a *Genealogisch-Heraldischer Staats-Kalender auf das Jahr 1723* (Augsburg: J. J. Lotter, 1723), three parts in one volume, with:

- engraved frontispiece;
- **74 copperplate armorial plates**;
- **12 text coppers**;
- folding genealogical tables (`Faltstammbäume`).

Thus Schmeitzel's 1723 criticism of the 1720 predecessor is not a notice of an isolated failed experiment. By the year his *Einleitung* appeared, the competing Augsburg calendar ecology continued with a substantial engraved and genealogical apparatus.

Immediately before and during the rise of the Weigel calendar, a contemporary heraldic writer was therefore evaluating a durable competing genre by separable criteria:

`engraving quality ≠ explanatory value`.

This supports the corpus's broader distinction among:

- object/image quality;
- textual explanation;
- provenance/source status;
- comparative correctness;
- historical warrant.
