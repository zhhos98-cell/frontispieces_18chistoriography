# Clio plate-state control, 1739–1762 — 26 August 2026

This control pass isolates a narrow question that became visible while following the `Historia. Genealogia. Heraldica. Testes temporum et veritatis` frontispiece across the late Weigel calendar and early Gatterer handbook sequence:

> Does the Gatterer-era Clio witness represent continued use of the older Müller–Tyroff plate, or a Tyroff re-engraving / new plate state dated 1755?

The present evidence is sufficient to define the test, but not yet to identify the 1762 image as Clio without direct visual inspection.

## 1. 1739: direct signed Clio witness

A Hermitage catalogue description of the 1739 *Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender* records an engraved frontispiece and explicitly reads the signatures:

- `G. Müller del`
- `M. Tyroff sc.`

The same catalogue describes the volume as the seventeenth newly enlarged and improved issue, Nürnberg, `Christoph Weigels, des älteren, seel. Wittib`, printed by Lorenz Bieling.

This is stronger than a generic catalogue attribution: it is a copy-level reading of the engraved signature on the frontispiece itself.

## 2. 1740: independent institutional Clio control

British Museum object `1910,0906.2` independently identifies the target `Historia Genealogia Heraldica / testes temporum et veritatis` frontispiece as:

- engraved by Martin Tyroff;
- after Gabriel Müller;
- approximately 198 × 115 mm.

The 1739 Hermitage and 1740 British Museum witnesses therefore establish a stable signed Müller→Tyroff Clio state by the end of the 1730s.

## 3. 1755 calendar: the issue exists and carries a frontispiece

A historical complete-copy description in Antiquariat Emil Hirsch / catalogue 37 records the 1755 calendar as:

`Der Durchlauchtigen Welt zum drey und dreysigstenmal neu vermehrter und verbesserter Geschichts-, Geschlechts- und Wappen-Calender ... 1755`

Imprint:

`Nürnberg, A. Bieling für C. Weigels Erben (1754)`

Collation:

- engraved frontispiece;
- full-page engraved colour explanation;
- one engraved coin plate;
- 109 full-page engraved armorial plates, text on versos;
- 79 numbered double leaves with genealogical tables;
- publisher advertisements;
- described as complete.

This is important for two reasons.

First, it directly refutes the secondary claim that no 1755 issue appeared. With 1723 as issue 1, 1755 is arithmetically and bibliographically the **33rd** issue; 1756 is the 34th and 1757 the 35th.

Second, a frontispiece is physically present in the 1755 issue. Its image and signature have not yet been directly identified.

A recent Kiefer description labels a 1755 copy `zum vier und dreysigstenmal`, which conflicts with the historical complete-copy description and with the 1756/1757 numbering sequence. Treat the Kiefer ordinal as a modern catalogue error unless direct title-page evidence proves otherwise.

## 4. 1759: Clio passes into the Gatterer handbook at the Weigel→Raspe transition

A complete 1759 Gatterer *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik* copy printed/published in the Weigel/Bieling environment explicitly identifies the pretitular copper as:

`HISTORIA GENEALOGIA HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORVM ET VERITATIS`

Other 1759 copy descriptions record an engraved Martin Tyroff frontispiece.

This places the Clio threshold image inside the first Gatterer handbook phase, before the publishing identity has fully stabilised under the Raspische Handlung.

## 5. 1762: a frontispiece signed `M. Turoff fec. 1755`

The National Library of Finland / Finna copy of Gatterer's 1762 *Handbuch der neuesten Genealogie und Heraldik* records:

- Nürnberg: `In der Raspischen Handlung`, 1762;
- engraved frontispiece;
- signature read as **`M. Turoff fec. 1755`**.

The date is the key anomaly.

If direct image inspection shows that this 1762 frontispiece is the Clio composition, then the plate history must include a Tyroff state or re-engraving dated 1755. That would produce a provisional sequence:

`1739 signed G. Müller del / M. Tyroff sc.`
→ `1740 same target composition, BM attribution Müller→Tyroff`
→ `1755 possible new Tyroff state / plate`
→ `1759 target Clio in first Gatterer handbook`
→ `1762 frontispiece signed M. Turoff fec. 1755`

If the 1762 image is not Clio, the result is equally significant: the Gatterer handbook changed threshold image between 1759 and 1762.

## 6. Current evidentiary status

Established:

1. Clio is signed `G. Müller del / M. Tyroff sc.` in a 1739 calendar copy.
2. The target Clio composition is independently catalogued in 1740 by the British Museum as Tyroff after Müller.
3. A complete 1755 calendar exists, is the 33rd issue, and contains an engraved frontispiece.
4. The 1759 Gatterer handbook can carry the Clio composition as pretitular copper.
5. A 1762 Gatterer copy has an engraved frontispiece signed `M. Turoff fec. 1755`.

Not yet established:

- that the 1755 calendar frontispiece is Clio;
- that the 1762 frontispiece is Clio;
- that `fec. 1755` denotes a full re-engraving rather than retouching, replacement, copied state, or another Tyroff design;
- that the 1739/1740 and possible 1755 states derive from the same physical copperplate.

## 7. Decisive next image test

Inspect the frontispiece in the National Library of Finland / Google Books 1762 witness and record:

1. composition;
2. Latin inscription;
3. exact signature wording and position;
4. whether Gabriel Müller's `del.` survives;
5. dimensions if available;
6. any visible differences from the 1737 detached / 1739 / 1740 Clio witnesses.

If it is Clio and the old `G. Müller del.` is absent while `M. Turoff fec. 1755` is present, the strongest working hypothesis becomes a **Tyroff re-engraving / second plate of the Müller composition** rather than simple uninterrupted use of the same copper.
