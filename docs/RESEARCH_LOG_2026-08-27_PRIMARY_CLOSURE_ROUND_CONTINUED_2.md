## Falsification result — 1795 is not a heredity-empty intermediate state

Primary Latin text from the 1795 third edition of *De generis humani varietate nativa* changes the interpretation of the intermediate publication state.

### §39, p.106 — acquired-to-congenital conversion is explicitly posed

Blumenbach asks whether accidental or deliberate deformations and mutilations, especially when repeated through long series of generations, can over time become a kind of second nature so that an artificial effect becomes congenital conformation.

Kant's 1785 intervention is explicitly cited on the negative side of the dispute.

Operationally:

`artificial / accidental modification → repeated generational exposure → possible temporal stabilization → possible connate conformation`.

This is an open causal question, not an affirmative doctrine.

### §39, p.108 — propagation exists with variable constancy

Blumenbach says he adopts neither the affirmative nor negative side. He nevertheless presses the deniers to explain why artificial or accidental conformations could never propagate to descendants when other `notae gentilitiae` of unknown origin do propagate in families.

His examples include nose, lips and eyebrows, and he explicitly describes such characters as running through **more or fewer generations** with **greater or lesser constancy**. Organic diseases and defects of speech/pronunciation are invoked as parallel familial phenomena.

The correct 1795 persistence model is therefore:

`familial / gentilitial character → propagation across variable generational span → variable constancy`.

This is materially different from the 1797 rule of necessary inheritance.

### §40, p.109 — causal pluralism is actor-explicit

Blumenbach warns that degeneration can involve:
- several causes acting together;
- causes acting in contrary directions;
- different susceptibility and resistance of organic bodies/organs (`pronitas / renisus`);
- different immediate and mediated causal action.

Thus causal underdetermination and organ-specific susceptibility are not merely modern analytical reconstructions.

### §40, p.112 — differential persistence and causal mediation are actor-explicit

The primary indexed text now closes the next test.

Blumenbach argues that dark skin acquired/contracted in the torrid zone can persist under a changed climate more strongly than northern whiteness does when its bearers are transferred southward. Persistence is therefore not assumed to be symmetric under opposite directions of environmental transfer.

He then places stable `gentilitias et constantes craniorum formas` and `colores gentilitios oculorum` under more mediated causes that remain in large part unknown.

Safe closure:

- `actor_causal_pluralism = explicit`;
- `actor_organ_specific_susceptibility = explicit`;
- `trait_or_state_specific_persistence = explicit`;
- `directional_persistence_asymmetry = explicit`;
- `immediate_vs_mediate_causation = explicit`;
- `linear_trait_persistence_hierarchy = not established`.

Do **not** turn these passages into a clean `skin < eyes < skull` scale. The source establishes differential behaviour and causal mediation, not a single ordered hierarchy.

## Hard revision

Replace:

`1795 = anthropological recasting; hereditary persistence still unresolved`

with:

`1795 = public degeneration framework + explicit propagation problem + variable constancy + acquired-to-congenital conversion question + causal pluralism + organ-specific susceptibility + differential persistence; no controlled necessary-inheritance category gate`.

The 1795→1797 transition is therefore not:

`no heredity → heredity`.

It is:

`variable propagation / differential persistence → necessary reproductive persistence promoted to categorical jurisdiction`.

Key distinctions:

`propagation exists ≠ propagation is necessary ≠ persistence determines category membership`.

`causal propagation ≠ variable familial constancy ≠ necessary reproductive persistence ≠ categorical gate`.

## Source-use correction — Bendyshe 1865

A source trap was identified and corrected during this pass.

Thomas Bendyshe's 1865 *Anthropological Treatises* does not provide a clean full translation of the 1795 third-edition body. Bendyshe states that he translated the first edition as his base and attached selected later material. The 1795 table of contents, Banks letter and collection are useful controls, but body passages in Bendyshe must not automatically be labelled as 1795 third-edition translations.

Gruber 1798 follows the third edition more closely, but contemporary criticism of its translation quality makes the Latin primary text preferable whenever terminology matters.
