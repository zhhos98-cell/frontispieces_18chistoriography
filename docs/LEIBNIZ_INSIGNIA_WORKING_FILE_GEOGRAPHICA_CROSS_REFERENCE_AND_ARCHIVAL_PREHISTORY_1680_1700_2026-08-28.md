# Leibniz `Insignia` working file, `Geographica` cross-reference, and archival prehistory, c.1680–1700

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE OPERATIONAL / ARCHIVAL PREHISTORY — OFFICIAL LEIBNIZ-KATALOG QUERY CONTROLS 100 `Insignia`-TAGGED RECORDS; REPRESENTATIVE RECORDS PRIMARY-CATALOGUE/CRITICAL-EDITION CONTROLLED; FULL 100-RECORD TAXONOMY NOT YET RECONSTRUCTED

## Result

The strongest new finding is that `Insignia` was not merely an abstract category in Leibniz's library classifications and not merely the occasional filing label of a few heraldic excerpts.

The official Leibniz-Katalog currently returns **100 records** for the normalized `Ordnungsvermerk` query `insignia`.

The catalogue defines `Ordnungsvermerk` as Leibniz's ordering mark for the organization of his preparatory materials for the *Opus historicum*.

Therefore the 100 results expose a substantial **working-file infrastructure**.

They include, among other things:

- autograph excerpts from printed and manuscript historical works;
- short research notes;
- notes to be investigated further;
- letters received from collaborators;
- Leibniz's own drafts and extracts of correspondence;
- seals and seal descriptions;
- diplomas and privileges;
- coins and numismatic comparanda;
- arms, banners, shields and dynastic insignia;
- references to images and monuments;
- explicit cross-references to other filing domains.

The most important individual record is Leibniz-Katalog **ID 7139**, dated June 1690. It is filed under:

`Insignia ("Insignia")`

but its title is explicitly:

`[Verweis auf Geographica: Exzerpte aus Winkelmann]`.

After noting two walking lions of the Counts of Diez comparable to Brunswick insignia, Leibniz writes:

`liegen bey Geograf.`

This is a direct operational cross-reference between an `Insignia` note and `Geographica`.

It gives a concrete archival prehistory to the later compressed formula:

`Geographia. Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.`

The later formula must still be interpreted on its own textual and classificatory evidence. But the 1690 record shows that the proximity of geographical and heraldic material was already enacted in Leibniz's **physical/intellectual sorting practice**, years before the `contractior`.

Working model:

`historical research object`
→ `Insignia working file`
↔ `Geographica cross-reference`
→ `c.1693 classificatory redistribution of genealogy / insignia across History–Geography boundary`
→ `1695–98(?) compressed Geographia / Genealogica / Heraldica nesting`.

This is an archival-operational sequence, not a proved textual derivation of the later rubric from one filing label.

## 4. ID 7139: a direct `Insignia` → `Geographica` cross-reference

Leibniz-Katalog ID **7139**, `Ms 23, 47(1) Bl. 21r°`, dated June 1690, is titled:

`[Verweis auf Geographica: Exzerpte aus Winkelmann]`.

It carries Leibniz's ordering mark:

`Insignia ("Insignia")`.

The note concerns the arms of the Counts of Diez, comparing their two lions with Brunswick forms, and ends:

`Winkelman ex excerpt. Heßischer Chronick. liegen bey Geograf.`

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY AUTOGRAPH CROSS-FILE REFERENCE CONTROLLED THROUGH OFFICIAL CATALOGUE / AKADEMIEAUSGABE LOCATOR`.

This is the most important current operational control for the later History–Geography–Genealogy–Heraldry classification problem.

It demonstrates that in 1690:

`Insignia material`
↔ `Geographica filing location`

was not merely a later philosophical possibility. Leibniz's working notes actually cross-referenced the two domains.

### Interpretive consequence

The later `contractior` formula:

`Geographia. Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.`

should no longer be read only as an abstract taxonomic proposition.

It can be placed against a documented **sorting ecology** in which heraldic evidence could physically/intellectually be kept with geographical material.

Do not claim that ID 7139 caused or directly generated the later formula.

Safe label:

`DIRECT OPERATIONAL PREHISTORY OF THE HISTORY–GEOGRAPHY–HERALDRY BOUNDARY`.

## 6. `Insignia | Sigilla`: documentary role and status can be read from seals

ID **10282**, dated 1692, is an excerpt from d'Achery and filed:

`Insignia | Sigilla ("Insignia")`.

It discusses William the Conqueror's seal as a two-sided representation of political status:

- one side mounted as Norman count/duke;
- the other enthroned with sceptre as king of England.

This is valuable because `Insignia` here is not merely the history of graphic emblems.

The seal becomes a historical representation of **jurisdictional/person-status plurality**.

Working operation:

`sigillum`
→ `office / title / political status`
→ `historical inference`.

This broadens the later Heraldica problem beyond blazon taxonomy.

That proposition is currently a strong structural interpretation, not yet a demonstrated statement of authorial intention.

## 11. Consequence for the frontispiece project

The 1726 programme:

`HISTORIA / GENEALOGIA / HERALDICA / TESTES TEMPORUM ET VERITATIS`

can now be placed against a deeper prehistory with three analytically distinct levels:

### A. Working archive

`Insignia` as a large research file coordinating excerpts, correspondence, seals, diplomas, images, coins, arms, inquiry tasks and cross-file references.

### B. Classification

Genealogy and insignia/arms repeatedly redistributed at the History–Geography boundary and later compressed into `Genealogica & Heraldica`.

### C. Visual programme

Genealogy and Heraldry visually independentized beside Historia and material witnesses.

The research trajectory is therefore:

`archival sorting`
→ `taxonomic compression`
→ `visual coordination`.

No direct Leibniz → Weigel/Köhler transmission has been established.

But the frontispiece's conceptual architecture now has a much more concrete operational prehistory than a generic genealogy of “auxiliary sciences”.
