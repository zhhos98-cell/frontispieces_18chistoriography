## 1. Pp.611–612 — actor-explicit problem architecture

The opening now makes the central research problem primary-direct.

Meiners does not begin from a settled colour taxonomy. He poses a set of linked questions about:

- whether population colour depends more on climate/place of long residence or on descent;
- whether children's colour depends more on place of birth or `Blute der Eltern`;
- whether, and to what extent, colour is a secure sign of `Racen und Abkunft von Menschen`;
- how many `Hauptfarben` should be recognised;
- whether one or more of those principal colours can count as `Grundfarben`, understood as colours from which the others arose but which, according to the evidence then available, could not themselves be transformed into one another merely through external causes.

Meiners explicitly says these questions remain undecided and require exact and comprehensive examination.

This upgrades the opening to:

`colour description → causal discrimination (climate/descent) → genealogical/racial sign value → principal/original-colour reconstruction`.

The colour essay is therefore not merely descriptive. The inferential target `Racen und Abkunft` is actor-explicit at the outset.

## 2. P.613 — observer vocabulary and comparison-frame instability are actor-explicit

P.613 directly confirms that Meiners treats colour reporting itself as an evidence problem.

He notes that travel writers use colour terms such as white, yellow, brown, olive, `tanné`, black and related expressions inconsistently and without determinate extension. He also says that languages lack sufficiently precise expressions for innumerable gradations and that even credible observers can contradict one another in describing the same population.

Crucially, he recognises a comparison-frame effect: a writer may call one people white or yellow relative to another people, while a different comparison can produce another description.

He therefore treats the discrimination of climate effects from population mixture as a problem to be solved through critical handling of heterogeneous observations.

This makes the following primary-direct rather than merely Wilson-style analytical reconstruction:

`observer vocabulary instability + comparison-frame instability + contradictory reports → source criticism / calibration problem`.

Do not infer that Meiners solved this problem satisfactorily. The point is that the instability of the reporting layer was visible to the actor.

## 3. P.619 — local environmental differentiation remains inside the external-cause architecture

P.619 completes the previously missing leaf in the external-cause section. The page continues discussion of local winds and regional conditions, including cases in which winds or location-specific exposure affect heat and therefore colour differently even at similar latitude.

This confirms continuity with pp.614–618:

`latitude alone ≠ local effective climate`.

The causal field remains differentiated by winds, exposure, proximity and other local conditions rather than being reducible to a simple latitude-temperature rule.

No revision of the existing causal matrix is required; p.619 closes a carrier gap rather than falsifying the model.

## 4. P.671 — literal four-Hauptfarben list now primary-direct

The final missing classificatory leaf is now direct.

Meiners defines `Hauptfarben` as colours applying not merely to single peoples or individual human `Spielarten`, but to the inhabitants of whole or nearly whole parts of the world. He then recognises four principal colours in the present human species:

- white;
- yellow;
- red;
- black.

The page distributes these geographically across large world regions. It also treats brownish colour as a shade of white before continuing into the p.672 discussion of `Urfarben`.

P.672 had already established that white and yellow are the two `Urfarben` among these four `Hauptfarben`, with red and black treated as historically derived principal colours.

Therefore the full distinction is now primary-direct:

`four descriptive/geographical Hauptfarben = white + yellow + red + black`

while

`two historical/origin Urfarben = white + yellow`.

Thus:

`descriptive large-scale classification ≠ natural-historical origin classification`.
