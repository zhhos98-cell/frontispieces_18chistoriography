# Leibniz–Spener reception, `contractior` hierarchy, and correction of the Reimmann influence window, 1677–1701

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE CORRECTION — C.1693 GENEALOGY/INSIGNIA PREHISTORY ALREADY PRIMARY-CRITICAL-EDITION CONTROLLED; DIRECT LEIBNIZ RECEPTION OF SPENER 1677/1680 NOW CONTROLLED; IV.6 SYSTEMATIC CONTEXT CONTROLLED; EXACT N.72 AUTOGRAPH WORDING STILL REQUIRES DIRECT IV.6 TEXT/MANUSCRIPT INSPECTION

## Result

Two earlier working assumptions require correction.

First, **Reimmann 1694 can no longer be treated as a plausible explanation for the entry of genealogy/heraldic material into Leibniz's library classification**. The c.1693 Leibniz draft sequence already contains genealogy together with insignia/arms before Reimmann's first genealogy-history print:

- N.78: `Historia familiarum seu Genealogica, huc insignia et arma`;
- N.79: compressed family-history/genealogy + insignia wording;
- N.80: `Historia Genealogica ubi et de insignibus`;
- N.81: `Historiae migrationum, item Genealogiarum simul et insignium`.

The relevant problem is therefore no longer:

`Did Reimmann 1694 cause Genealogica/Heraldica to enter Leibniz's classification?`

That path is closed by chronology.

The surviving narrower questions are:

1. whether Reimmann 1694 contributed to later **compression, terminology, or re-description** inside a category already present;
2. whether any direct Reimmann reception can be established before the controlled 1701 Leibniz/Eckhart recension edge.

Current answer to (2): **not yet found**.

Second, a stronger pre-1694 reception environment has now been controlled: **Leibniz directly read, annotated and excerpted Philipp Jakob Spener's genealogical/heraldic works**. This is materially more relevant to the later shift from `insignia / arma` to the compact rubric `Heraldica` than a merely chronological Reimmann hypothesis.

The revised working sequence is:

`Spener genealogical/heraldic books directly in Leibniz's reading/excerpting environment, 1677/1680 → c.1690 object-level Spener excerpts → c.1693 Genealogica + insignia/arma classifications → 1695–98(?) contractior compression using Heraldica → Reimmann 1694 as parallel genealogy-historicization project → 1701 direct Leibniz/Eckhart Reimmann recension pipeline`.

This remains a **reception environment / candidate lexical-operational prehistory**, not a demonstrated causal chain from Spener to the wording of N.72.

## 2. IV.6 editorial reconstruction: later 1690s work systematizes rather than newly inserts these fields

The introduction to Akademieausgabe IV.6 treats N.72, N.77 and N.78 as a relatively straight continuation of the first-half-1690s classification drafts.

The editors explicitly state that the advance lies in conceptual/systematic framing while Leibniz **no longer changes the sequence of the individual fields** in a decisive way.

For N.77 (`De dispositione bibliothecae per ordinem facultatum`, 1696–97?), the editors reconstruct an explicit upper-level system:

`LITERAE HUMANIORES`
→ `PHILOLOGIA`
+
`HISTORIA`.

They then state that **Geography belongs to HISTORIA and that the mention of Genealogy and Heraldry shows that fields previously placed in Polyhistorica are also brought into HISTORIA**.

This is a decisive hierarchy control.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY CRITICAL-EDITION EDITORIAL/STEMMATIC CONTROL`.

## 3. How to read `Geographia. Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.`

The later print tradition of the `Idea Leibnitiana bibliothecae ordinandae contractior` gives the compact sequence:

`Historia civilis.`

`Universalis.`

`Geographia. Huc Genealogica, & Heraldica.`

`Historia Graeca, & Romana cum antiquitatibus.`

and so on.

The 1768 Dutens print has been directly inspected and controls this wording. Feller's 1718 *Otium Hanoveranum* is the earlier posthumous print witness identified by the Akademieausgabe.

Evidence status:

`PRIMARY POSTHUMOUS PRINT DIRECTLY CONTROLLED; EXACT N.72 AUTOGRAPH WORDING STILL PENDING`.

### Correct hierarchy

The Latin `Huc` should **not** be weakened into mere visual adjacency. Locally it means that Genealogica and Heraldica are referred **to this place/category**, i.e. to `Geographia` in the compressed list.

But this does not remove them from History.

The same compressed list is headed by `Historia civilis`, and the later N.77 systematic reconstruction makes the macro-level nesting explicit:

`HISTORIA`
→ includes `Geographia`
→ with Genealogy / Heraldry grouped in this historical-geographical neighborhood.

The safest current formulation is therefore a **dual nesting**:

`Historia / Historia civilis`
→ `Geographia`
→ `Genealogica + Heraldica`.

This resolves a false binary:

- it is too weak to say `Huc` has no hierarchical force;
- it is too strong to say Leibniz moved genealogy/heraldry **out of History and into Geography**.

The historically interesting object is the internal **History–Geography boundary architecture**.
