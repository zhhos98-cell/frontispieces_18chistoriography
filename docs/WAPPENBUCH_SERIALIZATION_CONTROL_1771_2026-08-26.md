# 1771 Wappenbuch serialization control — 26 August 2026

This pass directly controls the relation between the older annual *Wappencalender* and Johann Friedrich Seyfart's later *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch* using a fully digitised copy rather than inferring continuity from titles or plate counts.

## Direct digital witness

Universitätsbibliothek Tübingen, shelfmark **Fe 18-1**:

- *Der durchlauchtigen Welt vollständiges Wappenbuch. Erster Band in welchem auf hundert und acht und siebenzig Kupferplatten ...*;
- Nürnberg: auf Kosten der Raspischen Handlung;
- catalogue year: **1771**;
- URN `urn:nbn:de:bsz:21-dt-208196`;
- DOI `10.20345/digitue.20819`;
- OpenDigi supplies METS, IIIF, page-level PDFs/JPEGs and a structural table of contents.

## 1. Publisher's preface directly states reuse from the Wappencalender

The preface is unusually explicit about the material genealogy of the new Wappenbuch.

It says that the project took **“die bisher bey dem Wappencalender gewesenen Wappen zum Grunde gelegt”** — the arms previously used in the Wappencalender were taken as the basis.

This changes the evidentiary status of a major project proposition. The relation

`Wappencalender armorial apparatus → Wappenbuch armorial apparatus`

is no longer merely inferred from publisher continuity, overlapping titles, similar plate dimensions or shared subject matter. The 1771 publisher states the dependence directly.

This does **not** by itself mean that every later plate is the identical copperplate or impression from an earlier calendar. The preface establishes source/apparatus continuity at the programme level; individual same-plate claims still require material comparison.

## 2. The Wappenbuch was designed to be issued through the calendar rhythm

The same preface explains that the complete work would be assembled in four octavo volumes and that, because the arms could only be gathered gradually, they would be delivered in separate divisions and **“jährlich mit dem Wappencalender ausgegeben werden.”**

This is direct actor-language for the publication mechanism:

`annual Wappencalender delivery → successive armorial divisions → cumulative multi-volume Wappenbuch`

The later Wappenbuch is therefore not simply a replacement genre appearing after the calendar. It is materially produced through the calendar's annual distribution rhythm while being reorganised toward a cumulative reference work.

This provides strong primary support for the repository vocabulary:

- extraction;
- recomposition;
- de-serialisation into cumulative volumes;
- re-serialisation through annual delivery;
- accumulation.

## 3. The publisher frames the new work as an enlargement/completion problem

The preface says the earlier genealogical-heraldic handbook/Wappencalender had been continued annually and improved, especially through Gatterer, but users still wanted additional German and foreign princely/comital arms, larger and more orderly presentation, and more accurate descriptions.

The new Wappenbuch is therefore presented as a response to perceived incompleteness in an already successful serial apparatus, not as a clean break with it.

Working sequence:

`successful annual apparatus → recognised insufficiency → enlarged armorial collecting programme → staged annual delivery → cumulative Wappenbuch`

This helps explain why plate histories, calendar histories, handbook histories and later bound-volume histories repeatedly diverge.

## 4. Copy-level frontispiece variation is now directly visible at 1771

The Tübingen **Fe 18-1** structural front matter is:

`Einband → Spiegel → Vorsatz → Erklärung der Farben → Titelblatt → Vorbericht`

No frontispiece/title copper is listed in this digitised copy.

By contrast, a separate 1771 market copy of the second volume (Wissenschaftliches Antiquariat Thomas Haker, inventory 71332) is catalogued as:

- `(4), 194 Bl.`;
- **Mit Titelkupfer**;
- 193 engraved armorial plates.

Therefore:

`1771 publication year / Wappenbuch family ≠ invariant frontispiece presence`

At least at surviving-copy level, front matter varies. This is consistent with the repository's broader evidence for detached plates, missing frontispieces, rebound composites and inserted foreign frontispieces.

It also sharpens the Seyfart queue: the reported `3 wh. gest. Front.` in a complete 1767–1776 four-volume set cannot be projected onto every surviving part or volume. The three repeated frontispieces must be located in specific bound copies/volumes.

## 5. Direct implications for the article argument

This primary control upgrades the project's serial-material argument substantially.

The strongest defensible formulation is now:

> The later Wappenbuch did not merely succeed the Wappencalender. Its publisher explicitly took the calendar's existing arms as a basis and planned successive divisions to be issued annually with the Wappencalender before being accumulated into a multi-volume reference work.

The important transformation is therefore not a simple genre replacement:

`calendar → handbook → Wappenbuch`

but a circulation and reformatting process:

`serial plates + genealogical apparatus → annual issue context → extraction/enlargement → separately delivered divisions → cumulative reference volumes`

This is exactly the kind of material reorganisation for which the Clio threshold image matters: the image can persist, disappear, be detached or recur while the publication regime carrying the evidentiary apparatus changes around it.
