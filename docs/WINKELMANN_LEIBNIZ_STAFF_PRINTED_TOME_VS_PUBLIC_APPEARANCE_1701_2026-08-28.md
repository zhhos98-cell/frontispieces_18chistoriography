# Winkelmann 1701: Leibniz distinguishes a `Tome imprimé` from an `ouvrage` that might still `paroitre`

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: HIGH-VALUE POSTPRINT CIRCULATION / PROJECT-STATE CHECKPOINT — LEIBNIZ DIRECTLY STATES IN 1701 THAT HE HAD SEEN A PRINTED TOME OF WINKELMANN'S HESSIAN CHRONICLE WHILE STILL ASKING WHETHER THE WORK MIGHT ONE DAY APPEAR; STAFF'S ANSWER REMAINS TO BE RECOVERED

## Result

On **23 September 1701**, four years after the first five parts of Johann Just Winkelmann's Hessian Chronicle were printed at Bremen, Leibniz wrote to Kassel court official **Nathanael von Staff**:

> `Vous saurez sans doute, Monsieur, que feu Mr Winkelmann avoit composé en Allemand une Chronique de Hesse, dont j’ai vû même un Tome imprimé. N’y a-t-il pas d’esperance, que cet ouvrage paroitra un jour?`

Source:

- Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, *Sämtliche Schriften und Briefe*, Reihe I, Bd. 20, no. 284, Leibniz an Nathanael von Staff, Wolfenbüttel, 23 Sept. 1701, pp. 480–481.
- Critical-edition transmission: the dispatch is not preserved; the edition prints the 1738 Kortholt first edition as its copy text. The letter answers Staff no.225 and is answered by Staff no.354.

Official digital edition:

https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2438/I20B.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2

Independent early-print controls:

- Kortholt, *Epistolae ad diversos*, vol. 3 (1738), p.372 ff.;
- Dutens, *Opera omnia*, vol.5 (1768), p.523.

Both early printed witnesses preserve the key wording `un Tome imprimé` / `un Tome imprime`.

The sentence creates a historically important distinction internal to Leibniz's own wording:

`PRINTED TOME SEEN`
≠
`WORK HAS PUBLICLY / FULLY APPEARED`.

The exact reason for that distinction remains open.

---

## 1. The primary wording is `imprimé`, not `unpublished`

A current English web translation renders the sentence as if Leibniz had seen:

`an unpublished volume`.

That translation reverses the material state expressed by the French.

The controlled French is:

`un Tome imprimé`

= a **printed** tome/volume.

The 1738 Kortholt and 1768 Dutens editions independently preserve the same reading.

Therefore:

`LEIBNIZ SAW A PRINTED TOME = VERIFIED`.

Do not cite the English web translation for the print state without correcting it against the French source.

The translation site's editorial note further speculates that Leibniz was 'presumably' referring to volume 6. That is a modern inference, not a statement in the primary text and not required by the material bibliography.

---

## 2. `Tome imprimé` does not automatically mean the later sixth part

The standard 1697 first-edition state is already materially compatible with Leibniz's singular `un Tome`.

The Bremen first printing published **Parts I–V together as one continuously paginated physical volume**, approximately:

`10 preliminary leaves + 600 pages`.

Its title explicitly says that the project was composed in six parts but that only five were being published `vors erste`.

Thus a straightforward physical possibility is:

`un Tome imprimé`
=
`the single 1697 volume containing Parts I–V`.

This is at least as bibliographically natural as assuming that Leibniz meant a separate sixth-part print.

Current evidence does **not** establish which printed object Leibniz saw.

Keep distinct:

- the standard 1697 five-part volume;
- the unresolved undated 184-page `Sechster Theil` attachment reported in a later market copy;
- the complete posthumous 1754 sixth part.

Do not use the 1701 sentence to date the 184-page object or to prove a pre-1754 printed sixth-part state without object-level concordance.

---

## 3. The stronger point is the explicit `imprimé` / `paroitra` distinction

Leibniz's sentence itself juxtaposes two states:

1. he has seen `un Tome imprimé`;
2. he asks whether `cet ouvrage paroitra un jour`.

Whatever object he had seen, the wording shows that for Leibniz in 1701:

`something being physically printed`

was not sufficient for him to treat:

`the work as having appeared`.

Possible explanations include:

- he had seen an advance, proof, presentation, or otherwise restricted printed copy;
- the 1697 print existed but had limited or court-controlled distribution;
- he regarded the six-part project as incomplete because only five parts had appeared;
- he was unsure whether the printed first state would receive a wider/public release;
- `ouvrage` referred to completion of the larger project rather than merely the already printed tome;
- he lacked accurate information about the circulation/publication state after Winkelmann's death.

These are hypotheses to test, not interchangeable conclusions.

The direct evidence supports only:

`PRINTED-OBJECT AWARENESS`
+
`CONTINUED PUBLICATION/APPEARANCE QUESTION`.

---

## 4. This sharpens `publication state` into two distinct levels

The repository's current textual model distinguishes:

`project identity`
≠ `manuscript state`
≠ `reviewed/revised state`
≠ `publication state`
≠ `later completion state`.

The 1701 letter requires one further split:

`PRINTED OBJECT EXISTS / IS SEEN`
≠
`WORK IS PERCEIVED AS HAVING APPEARED`.

A refined sequence is:

`project state`
→ `temporary textual stabilization`
→ `printed-object state`
→ `distribution / public-appearance state`
→ `reception / awareness state`.

These levels can diverge.

This is directly analogous to the frontispiece corpus rule that a physical impression, its host, and its bibliographical/public identity cannot be collapsed into one event.

## 5. Relation to court-controlled printing and distribution

The repository already controls through Thomas Fuchs's reconstruction that Landgrave Karl financed the Chronicle's printing and that almost the entire edition went to Kassel for distribution, with Kassel assuming a leading role in the project.

That later reconstruction makes the 1701 distinction especially plausible as a **distribution/history-of-publication problem**, but it does not by itself explain Leibniz's precise knowledge state.

Safe relation:

`COURT-FINANCED / KASSEL-CENTERED DISTRIBUTION = CONTROLLED BY LATER ARCHIVAL SCHOLARSHIP`

alongside

`1701 LEIBNIZ HAS SEEN A PRINTED TOME BUT STILL ASKS WHETHER THE WORK WILL APPEAR = PRIMARY LETTER`.

Still unsafe:

`Kassel deliberately suppressed the 1697 edition until after 1701`.

No such conclusion is currently supported.
