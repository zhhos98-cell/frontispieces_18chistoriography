# Colour as method — sixth sweep: Gatterer, Meiners, Heeren and the jurisdiction of visible difference

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: active comparative synthesis

## 1. Main result

The project can now move beyond isolated colour cases without positing a single transferred `colour method`.

Araújo's reconstruction supplies a longitudinal Göttingen comparison in which the same broad visible property — bodily colour — receives sharply different epistemic powers.

The comparison is not primarily about attitudes toward colour. It is about the **jurisdiction of inference**:

- what counts as an observable difference;
- whether continuous variation can be discretised into stable categories;
- whether those categories may organise peoples;
- whether colour can bear genealogical, essential or causal inference;
- what happens when historical experience raises probability without supplying proof.

The emerging question is:

> **How much historical inference may a visible difference carry?**

## 2. Gatterer: descriptive permission without essential or genealogical entitlement

The Gatterer trajectory is now controlled through Araújo with exact primary loci, though the page images remain to be inspected directly.

### 1771 — non-essential difference

Gatterer, *Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie*, pp. 61–62, reportedly distinguishes outward human differences from `wesentliche` differences and calls them `zufällige Unterscheidungszeichen` / `blose Varietäten`.

Thus visible bodily difference does not by itself establish separate human essence or origin.

### 1775 — descriptive anthropography

Gatterer, *Abriß der Geographie*, pp. 4–5, nevertheless proposes a `Menschen- und Völkerkunde (Anthropographia und Ethnographia)` and a `Geographie der Menschenkörper nach Gestalt und Hautfarben`.

Thus colour can remain useful for a descriptive and classificatory task even after its essential authority has been restricted.

### 1789 — language outranks skin colour for origin

Gatterer, *Kurzer Begriff der Geographie*, vol. 2, p. 606, reportedly makes language the stronger evidence for the `Ursprung der Völker`. Skin colour persists where sufficiently precise and reliable linguistic information is unavailable.

This gives a warrant gradient:

`Hautfarbe as visible property`
→ descriptively useful
→ non-essential
→ weaker than Sprache for genealogical origin
→ fallback under data scarcity.

This is not an adoption-then-rejection story.

## 3. Meiners: visible colour promoted into stronger causal architecture

Araújo provides the sharpest actor contrast by setting Gatterer against Christoph Meiners.

Araújo states that Gatterer did not make accidental differences such as skin colour an `organisatorisches Prinzip` of his geographical and historical works, whereas the same feature in Meiners becomes a `causa essentialis` of differences within humankind.

The contrast matters methodologically because the observable property is not the main variable. The inferential entitlement changes.

In Meiners's *Grundriß der Geschichte der Menschheit*, Araújo also tracks a change between the 1785 and 1793 editions in the causal account of bodily colour:

- in 1785 climate is presented as the principal cause, with descent following once climatic action has taken effect;
- in 1793 parental blood/descent is promoted ahead of climate.

The exact claims belong to the historical racial anthropology of the period and should be handled as historically situated arguments rather than imported into modern biological categories.

For the colour-method project the important point is:

`visible colour`
→ category
→ inherited/causal explanation
→ claims about larger human differences.

This is precisely the kind of escalation the `inference_target` ontology is designed to record.

Evidence status remains `secondary_scholarship / probable` for the project dataset until the relevant 1785 and 1793 pages are directly controlled.

## 4. Heeren 1817: colour classification fails at the discretisation stage

Araújo's Heeren material introduces a new failure mode before causal inference begins.

A surviving student transcript by Heinrich von Gagern of Heeren's summer 1817 lecture on `Allgemeine Länder- und Völkerkunde`, later excerpted by Christoph Becker-Schaum, reports the following methodological problem:

- an exact classification of all peoples/types cannot be made;
- there are too many `Nuancen von Farben`;
- those nuances are insufficiently known;
- only coarse `Hauptvölker`, characterised by dominant colours, can be classified.

The source chain is:

`Heeren lecture, summer 1817`
→ `Heinrich von Gagern Kollegnachschrift`
→ manuscript preserved in Bundesarchiv holdings cited by Becker-Schaum
→ edited excerpts in Christoph Becker-Schaum 1993, pp. 307–319, relevant p. 311
→ quotation/reconstruction in Araújo 2012.

Evidence basis should therefore be encoded as `primary_derivative` plus secondary editorial control, not direct autograph or printed primary.

The methodological structure is:

`continuous visible variation`
→ too many poorly known nuances
→ unstable/low-resolution bins
→ no exact exhaustive classification
→ coarse categories only.

This is a **discretisation failure**, not yet an inference failure.

## 5. Heeren causal passage: probability is not proof

Araújo also quotes Heeren from *Historische Werke*, vol. 15, pp. 5–7. The controlled 1826 witness is the fourth revised edition of:

*Ideen über die Politik, den Verkehr und den Handel der vornehmsten Völker der alten Welt*, part III, `Europäische Völker`, first division, `Griechen`.

Heeren considers whether differences of bodily organisation observed alongside skin-colour differences might influence the faster or slower development of mental capacities. His formulation explicitly asks who can **prove** such influence, while allowing historical experience to increase its probability.

Crucially, in the continuation he refuses two stronger moves:

- historical experience should not prove absolute incapacity;
- the proposed bodily/colour relation should not count as the sole cause.

Thus:

`observed association`
→ historical recurrence
→ increased probability
≠ proof of mechanism
≠ absolute incapacity
≠ sole causal explanation.

This is an unusually clean actor-level control for `causal_underdetermination`.

### Chronology guard

Araújo discusses the larger *Ideen* project from its 1790s beginnings, but the quoted passage is cited from *Historische Werke*, vol. 15 (1826). Bibliographic control shows that vol. 15 is the `Griechen` division; an independently digitised edition of that division exists from 1812.

Therefore the project must currently encode:

- `1826 = controlled witness date`;
- `1812 = earlier edition of the same division exists`;
- `date when this exact causal wording first entered the text = unresolved`.

Do **not** label the quoted formulation `Heeren 1793` unless edition comparison proves it.

## 7. A new operator: discretisation before classification

The previous operator model began too late when it moved directly from observable property to category/classification.

The revised colour-classification chain is:

`visible chromatic variation`
→ `measurement / observation`
→ `discretisation / naming / binning`
→ `colour category`
→ `classification`
→ `genealogical / essential / causal inference`.

Each step can fail separately.

### Observation failure
- poor reports;
- surrogate distortion;
- insufficient comparative material.

### Discretisation failure
- continuous variation;
- too many nuances;
- unstable boundaries;
- coarse binning;
- nonstandard names.

### Classification failure
- categories do not map stably onto the units to be classified;
- descriptive convenience is mistaken for natural division.

### Inferential failure
- descriptive category is promoted to origin, essence or cause without sufficient warrant.

## 8. Heraldry becomes even more useful as a cross-domain control

The Heeren evidence makes the heraldic material methodologically stronger.

Heraldic tinctures operate in a finite conventional category-space. The domain is already discretised:

`tincture category`
→ stable verbal name
→ hatch code
→ decoder
→ reproducible transmission.

Human bodily colour, by contrast, presents itself as continuous and poorly standardised variation:

`visible variation`
→ uncertain measurement
→ too many nuances
→ unstable/coarse categories
→ restricted inference.

The contrast explains why two apparently similar phrases — “classifying by colour” — can describe radically different epistemic situations.

This comparison is analytical. It is **not** evidence that a heraldic colour method was transferred into anthropology.

## 9. Relation to colour-chart historiography

This new discretisation problem also creates a useful interface with existing histories of colour order and colour charts.

The project should not claim novelty for the general eighteenth-century problem of standardising colour categories. That field already has substantial prior art.

The historical-science opening is narrower:

> **What happened when the success or failure of colour discretisation determined which historical classifications and inferences could be made?**

That question can connect a monochrome heraldic decoder, a hand-coloured synchronistic table and bodily colour classification while preserving their different epistemic functions.

## 10. Four distinct colour failures now controlled analytically

The project now has at least four non-equivalent failure types:

1. **Schlözer — factual alteration**
   - colour intervenes at the fact proposition.
2. **Heeren 1817 — discretisation failure**
   - colour variation cannot be partitioned with sufficient precision.
3. **Gatterer — inference-target restriction**
   - visible colour can classify descriptively but lacks essential/genealogical authority.
4. **Heeren causal witness / Meiners contrast — causal overreach question**
   - observed bodily colour relations may be promoted, restricted or left underdetermined as causes.

A fifth remains in graphical media:

5. **display-to-evidence overreach**
   - a table or colour contrast makes a relation perceptually obvious without increasing the warrant of the underlying classification.

These should remain separate in the ontology.

## 12. Current strongest formulation

> **Colour became methodologically consequential not when it entered historical knowledge, but when observers decided how a visible continuum should be discretised, which categories were stable enough to use, and how far those categories could support description, genealogy, essence or cause.**

Compact form:

> **A visible difference has to become a category before it can become a historical explanation.**
