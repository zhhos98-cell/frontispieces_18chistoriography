## Final results

### A. 1785 anti-single-colour rule is primary-direct

1785 p.17, §2 directly gives the hierarchy:

`one Geschlecht/species`
→ `two Stämme`
→ `multiple Racen`
→ `Varietäten`
→ `Spielarten`.

Immediately afterward Meiners objects to using colour as the `einzige` or `vornehmste` mark for determining similarity and difference among peoples.

Status:

`1785 p.17 anti-single/principal-colour criterion = PRIMARY-DIRECT`.

### B. 1793 pp.59–60 retain the rule and add the Kant note

The second edition retains the same hierarchy and the same anti-single/principal-colour sentence.

The note attached to `Racen` now acknowledges that `Stamm` and `Race` lack fixed usage and refers to Kant's November 1785 essay in the *Berlinische Monatsschrift*.

Therefore:

`pre-existing Meiners hierarchy + pre-existing evidence rule + new Kant reference on Stamm/Race terminology`.

Status:

`1793 pp.59–60 = PRIMARY-DIRECT`.

Interpretive ceiling:

`Kant citation = selective/local module, not wholesale Kantianization`.

### C. The anti-isolated-body-part quotation is a modern locator error

The quotation stating that whole peoples cannot be judged from one bodily part, nor national bodily form from isolated bones, is not on *Grundriss* p.23 in either edition.

Direct carriers show:

- 1785 pp.23–24 concern descent/distribution of northern Asian and American peoples;
- 1793 p.23 is a methodological comparison of `Geschichte der Menschheit` and Universalhistorie.

The quoted wording is directly recoverable in Meiners, *Beyträge zur Naturgeschichte* (1790), pp.63–64, in the Blumenbach Online primary transcription, together with a provenance restriction on inference from Negro skulls.

The 2023 specialist volume's note `Meiners: Grundriß ... S.23` is therefore a source-locator error.

Final chronology:

`1785 colour insufficiency`
→ `1790 body-part/bone insufficiency + specimen provenance`
→ `1792 causal underdetermination and multi-trait/provenance triangulation`
→ `1793 retained colour insufficiency + revised taxonomy/terminology`.

### D. 1785 p.39 ↔ 1793 pp.84–85 is primary-direct and more precise than “climate replaced by descent”

Both editions place the passage in §14 on the `verschiedene Grad der körperlichen Stärke`.

1785 p.39 says climatic effects on the human body are highly indeterminate and non-uniform. It distinguishes strength from `Abhärtung`, then says `Abhärtung` is in some degree produced by exercise/life-way but more fundamentally an `Eigenthümlichkeit` of certain `Stämme, Racen und Klimate`.

1793 pp.84–85 retains climate as variable causation but rewrites the final distinction:

- `Abhärtung` = in some degree a consequence of exercise/life-way;
- `Härte` = an `Eigenthümlichkeit` of certain `Stämme und Racen`.

`Klimate` disappears from the latter predicate.

The exact revision is therefore:

`acquired hardening (Abhärtung)`
separated from
`group-attributed hardness (Härte)`
with
`Klima removed from the group-property predicate`.

This is a passage-level reorganisation of causal/property vocabulary, not evidence that environmental causation disappears globally.

Status:

`1785 p.39 ↔ 1793 pp.84–85 = PRIMARY-DIRECT`.

### E. Intensity/degree is actor language

The §14 heading/opening explicitly uses `verschiedene Grad der körperlichen Stärke`; the argument also uses `in gleichem Maße` and `in einem gewissen Grade`.

This justifies using intensity/degree as an analytical comparator. The CSMBR 2023 intensity summer school / Bigotti project remains a historiographical comparator only, not a direct genealogy.
