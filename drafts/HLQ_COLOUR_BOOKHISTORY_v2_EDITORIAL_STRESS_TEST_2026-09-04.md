## Historiographical positioning that survives stress test

Do not claim:

- colour printing has been neglected;
- hand-coloured copies vary;
- Gatterer first made historical colour cognitively meaningful;
- hatching was invented in eighteenth-century Nürnberg;
- Gatterer and Schlözer have never been juxtaposed;
- the 1773 professional quarrel was about colour.

The narrow position remains defensible:

**Histories of colour printing largely ask how colour was physically put onto paper. This article follows a connected eighteenth-century book history in which colour could also be communicated through monochrome signs, keys and prose, and then compares that practice with historical books whose advertised hand-applied colour varied from copy to copy.**

The most specific object-level novelty is not hatching. It is the recurrent `Erklärung der Farben` as a separable bibliographical component within a serial/cumulative reference-book lineage, including recurrence, copy loss, multi-channel decoding, and re-hosting.
