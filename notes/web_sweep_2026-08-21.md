# Web sweep — 2026-08-21

Working branch: `web-sweep-2026-08-21`

## Scope used for this sweep

Primary target: frontispieces attached to eighteenth-century works of historiography, broadly construed to include national history, universal history, ancient history, imperial/colonial history, and major translated/revised editions circulating in the eighteenth century.

The unit of evidence is the **edition/issue/copy**, not merely the author-work pair. A frontispiece can be added, removed, rebound into another volume, or change from allegory to author portrait between editions. Records therefore preserve volume placement and copy-state ambiguity wherever the evidence indicates it.

## Evidence labels

- **confirmed** — a catalogue, museum, auction collation, or digitized copy explicitly identifies a frontispiece.
- **needs copy-state check** — evidence is real but may be copy-specific, extra-illustrated, misbound, or not called for by the standard collation.
- **metadata incomplete** — frontispiece is confirmed, but exact edition/publisher/date or artist attribution still needs resolution.

## High-value confirmed clusters from first pass

1. **Rapin de Thoyras** — unusually rich diachronic sequence: original 1724 French allegory (Clio/Time), 1743 English portrait frontispiece, and Harrison's late-century allegorical/current-history plate dated 1784 and used in the 1789 edition. This is immediately useful for tracing how the visual authorization of the historian shifts across translations and re-editions.

2. **Smollett** — British Museum holds the vol. III frontispiece to the *Complete History of England*: Britannia, Liberty, Thames, imperial gift-bearers, and the inscription `Temporum Felicitas`. Strong case for frontispiece as a compressed argument about national prosperity and imperial order rather than simple decoration.

3. **Catharine Macaulay** — portrait material needs edition-level care. A first-edition set records a portrait inserted as a frontispiece in vol. I but explicitly calls the placement erroneous; the 1769 third edition is independently described with a portrait frontispiece to vol. I. Keep these distinct.

4. **William Robertson** — edition change is especially diagnostic. The 1769 first quarto *Charles V* had no illustrations; the 1772 octavo edition added four engravings including a frontispiece portrait of Robertson after Reynolds engraved by John Hall. *History of America* (1777) also varies sharply by issue/edition: J. Williams's octavo has an author portrait frontispiece and a separate folding Aztec pictograph plate, while a Strahan/Cadell/Balfour first-issue copy has a folding engraved frontispiece in vol. II that Sabin does not call for.

5. **Edward Gibbon** — portrait dated 1780 was issued with vol. II but is frequently bound at the start of vol. I. Treat placement as binding history, not as a stable feature of the textual edition.

6. **Raynal, *Histoire des deux Indes* (1780)** — major serial case: ten frontispieces in the 10-volume issue, with Raynal's portrait in vol. I and nine thematic scenes after Moreau le Jeune. Issue-level engraver attribution needs normalization. The 4to configuration differs from the 10-volume octavo and should be catalogued separately.

7. **German universal history** — Millot/Christiani and the Gatterer-related *Allgemeine Welthistorie* open a productive German strand. The Vienna Millot (1793–94) is described with a frontispiece in each of fifteen text volumes, engraved by Johann Caspar Weinrauch.

## Rule for subsequent additions

Do not collapse author-work records across editions. When evidence conflicts, preserve the conflict in separate rows and mark it for copy-state or issue-level verification.
