# Core research map: Nuremberg visual history → Gatterer → Schlözer

## 0. Scope lock

The project now treats the broad frontispiece census as comparative material. The main object is a much smaller chain:

1. the Nuremberg serial **Neu-eröffneter Historischer Bilder-Saal** and its engravings;
2. Johann Christoph Gatterer's Nuremberg formation and visual/evidentiary practices;
3. Gatterer's Göttingen tables, especially the **Synopsis historiae universalis** (1766) and synchronistic program (1771);
4. the **Gatterer–Schlözer controversy of 1773** over Universalhistorie, priority, teaching and method;
5. Clio/Historia only where personification clarifies what these different visual systems claim history to be.

---

## 1. Neu-eröffneter Historischer Bilder-Saal

### Serial architecture

The series began in Nuremberg in 1692 under Andreas Lazarus von Imhof and continued to 1782, eventually reaching seventeen numbered volumes (with several divided volumes/periods). Its stated program was universal history 'von Anfang der Welt biß auf unsere Zeiten', divided into periods and chapters and rendered through unusually extensive copper engraving.

The later chronology is central for this project:

- vol. 10: 1734–1743, published 1744;
- vol. 11: 1744–1749, published 1752;
- vol. 12: 1749–1756, published 1761;
- vol. 13: 1756–1760, published 1762;
- vol. 14: 1761–1765, published 1766;
- vol. 15: 1765–1770, published 1773;
- vol. 16: 1771–1775, published 1779;
- vol. 17: 1776–1777, published 1782.

This sequence overlaps almost exactly with Gatterer's transition from Nuremberg to Göttingen and with the 1773 Gatterer–Schlözer quarrel.

### A stronger point than 'the books were illustrated'

The surviving e-rara structure records explicit textual nodes explaining the engraved title/frontispiece:

- vol. 10: **Erklärung des Kupfer-Tituls**;
- vol. 11: **Erklärung des Kupfferblates**;
- vol. 12: **Erklärung des Kupfer-Tituls**.

This makes the image-text relation methodological rather than merely ornamental: the reader is given an engraved historical condensation and then an interpretive key.

### Nuremberg print personnel

- **Johann Michael Stock** (1737–1773), Nuremberg-born engraver, produced the engraved title for vol. 13 (1762) before moving to Leipzig in 1764.
- **Andreas Nunzer** is documented as producing at least one historical illustration for the series (e.g. Reichskammergericht material in vol. 9, 1735).
- The engraver network must be reconstructed volume by volume rather than treated as an anonymous 'Nuremberg style'.

### Immediate image task

For vols. 10–15, record for every engraved title/frontispiece:

- maker / designer / engraver;
- figures and labels;
- whether Clio, Historia, Fama, Time, Minerva, rulers, maps or battle scenes appear;
- relation between image and accompanying `Erklärung`;
- whether the plate gives chronology, simultaneity, hierarchy, causation or exemplary events;
- whether the same compositional formula is reused across volumes.

## 2. Gatterer in Nuremberg: 1752–1759

Gatterer was already teaching history and geography in Nuremberg from 1752 and became professor of imperial history and diplomatics at the Auditorium Aegidianum in 1756. His move to Göttingen in 1759 should therefore be treated against an existing Nuremberg ecology of visual historical print.

### Key object: *Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum* (Nuremberg, 1755)

This is especially important because it is not simply a text written before Göttingen. Surviving collations describe:

- engraved frontispiece / portrait material;
- engraved title;
- a dedication engraving;
- around nineteen engraved plates;
- numerous genealogical tables;
- engraved head- and tailpieces;
- a large documentary appendix.

The work helped establish Gatterer's reputation as an editor and critic of documents. It should be examined as an integrated arrangement of **portrait, genealogy, document, inscription and plate**, not as an isolated frontispiece case.

### Working question

Did Gatterer's later Göttingen 'scientific' visuality emerge by rejecting Nuremberg historical illustration, or by abstracting and reorganizing operations already familiar there: selection, juxtaposition, captioning, genealogy, periodization and visual compression?

At present the second possibility is more productive and should be tested rather than assumed.

## 3. Gatterer's Göttingen visual method

### *Synopsis historiae universalis* (1766)

Gatterer published six historical tables designed to make simultaneity and historical position visible. André de Melo Araújo treats the six folios as an attempt to solve the representational impasses of universal historical narrative through a new scientific visual semantics.

Martin Gierl describes Gatterer's tables as resembling an **open cabinet**: universal history is spatially decomposed into particular histories and smaller units. The problem is that such a cabinet freezes history; Gatterer's further methodological work attempts to make the system dynamic.

### What the tables do

The tables are not merely chronological aids. They:

- place facts in time and space;
- coordinate state history, church history and historia litteraria;
- display duration and simultaneity;
- visualize systems of political power;
- convert a mass of facts into a system visible 'at a glance'.

This gives a precise operation-level comparison with the *Bilder-Saal*: both solve an excess-of-history problem visually, but in different media and with different levels of abstraction.

### *Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie* (1771)

The 1771 work explicitly explains Gatterer's synchronistic tables and contains ten folded leaves. It should be read together with the plates, not as theoretical prose alone.

## 4. Gatterer–Schlözer, 1773

The quarrel has at least three separable layers.

### Institutional competition

Schlözer's Universalhistorie lectures drew students away from Gatterer. Contemporary/later accounts describe competition over lecture audiences and fees. This explains heat but not necessarily intellectual difference.

### Priority dispute

Their conceptions of Universalhistorie were close enough that accusations of plagiarism/priority became part of the quarrel. Schlözer appended a **Species Facti** to the second part of his *Vorstellung seiner Universal-Historie* (1773); Gatterer replied with *Antwort auf die Schlözersche Species Facti* (Göttingen, Dieterich, 1773), a 48-page pamphlet.

### Methodological problem

Both wanted something more systematic than an aggregate of facts. Current secondary literature describes:

- Gatterer: reconstruction of a **system of events**, causal nexus, synchronistic and diachronic ordering;
- Schlözer: an **ordered whole**, requiring a universal gaze and a capacity to select/omit facts once the total field has been collected.

The argument to reconstruct is therefore not 'scientific Gatterer versus narrative Schlözer'. The differences are subtler: selection, priority, lecture architecture, relation of whole to parts, synchrony/diachrony, and what counts as the proper visible/intellectual form of Universalhistorie.

### Primary-source reading protocol

For *Species Facti* and Gatterer's *Antwort*, extract every passage concerning:

- `Universalhistorie / Weltgeschichte`;
- `Methode`;
- `Plan`;
- `System`;
- `Zusammenhang / nexus`;
- `Tabelle / synchronistisch`;
- `Erfindung / Priorität / Eigentum`;
- lecture practice and student use;
- selection versus completeness of `Facta`.

Keep personal invective in a separate column so it cannot silently substitute for methodological evidence.

## 5. Clio: narrowed function

Do not run an autonomous Clio iconography survey now. Use Clio as a diagnostic inside this chain.

Questions:

1. In the *Bilder-Saal* title programs, is universal history personified by Clio/Historia, or by rulers/events/other allegories?
2. If Clio appears, does she **write**, **select**, **order**, **witness**, or **present** history?
3. Does the visual role later performed by tables and synchronistic diagrams correspond to operations once delegated to the muse?
4. Does Clio disappear as historical order becomes diagrammatic, or does she survive alongside the new tools?

A disappearance would be as meaningful as a survival.

## 6. Core hypothesis, version 0.2

The likely research object is not 'the decline of Clio' and not 'the rise of scientific history' in isolation. It is a change in the **media and operations by which a historical whole became visible**.

- The Nuremberg *Bilder-Saal* serializes the world into engraved historical scenes and explains its title engravings textually.
- Gatterer inherits a culture in which historical knowledge is already built through prints, tables, genealogy and documentary facsimile, then radicalizes abstraction in Göttingen: the historical whole becomes a synchronized visual system.
- The Gatterer–Schlözer controversy shows that this reorganization of Universalhistorie was contested at precisely the level of method, ownership and pedagogical authority.
- Clio matters when she lets us see which cognitive operations were once embodied in a personification before being redistributed into tables, diagrams, chronology, geography and criticism.

The project should now test this chain tightly rather than broaden the corpus.
