# frontispieces_18chistoriography

## Core question

This project is **not** a general census of eighteenth-century historical frontispieces. Its core is a narrower problem: how history was made visually orderable before and during the formation of Göttingen's new historical method.

The working axis is:

**Clio and visual personification → Nuremberg historical engraving and serial image production → Johann Christoph Gatterer's visual/synoptic construction of universal history → the Gatterer–Schlözer dispute over Universalhistorie and historical method.**

The larger frontispiece corpus remains useful only as a comparative control.

## Core actors and objects

### 1. Nuremberg visual history

- Andreas Lazarus von Imhof and the long-running **Neu-eröffneter Historischer Bilder-Saal** (Nuremberg, 1692–1782).
- Its changing engravers and title/frontispiece programs, especially the mid-century continuation.
- Johann Michael Stock's engraved title for vol. 13 (1762) and other Nuremberg engravers such as Andreas Nunzer where securely attributable.
- The problem is not illustration as ornament, but the conversion of universal/current history into a visible sequence of selected events.

### 2. Johann Christoph Gatterer

Gatterer's Nuremberg years (1752–1759) and Göttingen years must be treated as one trajectory rather than two disconnected careers.

Key objects:

- *Historia genealogica dominorum Holzschuherorum* (Nuremberg, 1755): documents, genealogical tables, portraits, engraved plates, and visual evidentiary order.
- *Synopsis historiae universalis* (Göttingen, 1766): six historical tables designed to render simultaneity, duration, states, and historical position visible at a glance.
- *Einleitung in die synchronistische Universalhistorie* (1771): explicit explanation of the synchronistic tables.
- Gatterer's teaching collections, maps, tables, diplomatic facsimiles and engravings as instruments of historical method.

### 3. Gatterer–Schlözer

The 1773 controversy is not background gossip. It is a source for competing claims over what Universalhistorie was, how it should be ordered, and who possessed methodological priority.

Primary texts:

- August Ludwig Schlözer, *Vorstellung seiner Universal-Historie* (1772–73), including **Species Facti**.
- Johann Christoph Gatterer, *Antwort auf die Schlözersche Species Facti* (Göttingen, 1773).

The institutional competition over lectures and fees matters, but the project should isolate the methodological content from the personal/institutional quarrel.

## Clio rule

Clio remains the iconographic core, but only where she illuminates this historical problem. Record:

- explicit identification as Clio versus generic *Historia*;
- what she does: writes, orders, records, presents, teaches, witnesses;
- what she is paired with: Time, Fame, Monarchy, Liberty, Mercury, chronology, genealogy, maps, portraits;
- whether she represents completed history or history still being made;
- whether the muse remains external to the historian or collapses into the historian's own authority.

Do **not** expand into a general Clio iconography corpus unless a case bears directly on the Nuremberg–Göttingen line.

## Working hypothesis

The central transformation may not be a simple passage from allegory to scientific method. Nuremberg visual history, Gatterer's tables, and the Göttingen controversy may instead belong to successive solutions to the same representational problem: **how to select, synchronize, authorize, and make visible a historical whole that cannot be narrated all at once.**

The *Bilder-Saal* distributes history across serial engraved scenes. Gatterer's tables compress historical multiplicity into spatial systems. Schlözer contests both priority and the proper organization of Universalhistorie. Clio can therefore be read not merely as a decorative muse but as one earlier figure for the operations later redistributed across tables, diagrams, chronology, geography, and source criticism.

## Immediate research priorities

1. Reconstruct the title/frontispiece and engraving program of the *Neu-eröffneter Historischer Bilder-Saal*, especially vols. 11–15 (1752–1773), which overlap Gatterer's Nuremberg-to-Göttingen transition and the 1773 dispute.
2. Identify engravers, publishers, image conventions, and repeated visual formulas in those volumes.
3. Compare this serial visual ordering with Gatterer's *Synopsis* (1766) and synchronistic tables (1771) at the level of operation: selection, simultaneity, sequence, hierarchy, causation.
4. Read the 1773 Gatterer–Schlözer texts closely for methodological vocabulary, claims of invention/priority, tables, ordering, and Universalhistorie.
5. Return to Clio only at points where the muse helps expose a change in what 'making history visible' meant.
